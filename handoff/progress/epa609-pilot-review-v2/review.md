# Independent pilot review — final stage

## Decision

Do not scale or approve the bank from this pilot. All 14 independently locked stem answers match the intended keys, and all 14 explanations support a defensible answer. This verifies selected answer/source coherence, not item quality across a bank. The raw options-only agreement is 9/14 (rules 3/4, equipment 3/4, service 3/4, YF 0/2). Preserve the raw choices, reasoning, and stage boundaries. Do not treat the small category counts as reliable comparative estimates or subtract subject knowledge from the result to manufacture a pass.

Most stage-1 choices honestly used domain knowledge or imagined stems. That does not erase later identifiable structural cues. R001 has component convergence; R009 gives away half its classification; R011 makes the answer the only live-performance option; R012 rules out its competing references in advance. These require changes even without a causal claim that they explain every options-only hit. No key flips or adversarial inversion of known guesses are recommended.

Source verification and exact URLs are recorded in source-pedagogy-notes.md. The explanations were read only after independent answers were locked. No mapping, private scoring file, full bank, authoring evidence or authoring scripts were inspected. Source record was disclosed during stage2, so the answer check is source-assisted, not a claim of unaided certification performance.

## Source precision

The purchase documentation, salvage reuse assignment, off-site common-ownership condition, top-off equipment exception and demonstration authority are accurately represented. Preserve their narrow scopes. R001 would benefit from explicitly teaching that the declaration is written. R009 must continue distinguishing purchase relief from training/certification and availability obligations. R011 does not establish mandatory historical job logs.

R002 correctly computes 12 mL and already teaches emptying the bottle before recovery. R006 uses legitimate phase-change reasoning supported by introductory physics and the refrigerant SDS, not a claim every spill has exactly constant temperature. R007 correctly permits recovery or re-evacuation. R008 distinguishes compatibility from other performance/approval claims. R012 applies to this component revision, not an unconditional hierarchy overriding OEM guidance. R013 leaves the actual pump instructions controlling.

R014 needs a small but real chronological repair. The Robinair manual describes clearing before the accepted-serial-number/shutdown display. Its current stem could suggest that acceptance display happens and clearing still remains. Ask what must complete after entering a valid code and before the shutdown prompt. The explanation itself is correct. For general EPA609 preparation, include the applicable manual context or use this as a clearly labeled equipment-specific application. Merely memorizing one model's UI order is not persuasive broad certification preparation.

## Per-item assessment and concrete repairs

### R001 — revise

Accurate under 82.42(b)(3); also make written-statement form and small-container scope explicit in teaching context. No wrong-key finding.

D is the intersection of the three most repeated components. Each distractor changes exactly one component from D, permitting convergence without regulatory knowledge.

Repair: Replace the near-identical composite list with a concrete proposed purchaser declaration and four parallel compliance determinations, or ask one disputed requirement at a time. Keep the real resale-only/purchaser identity rule; do not change the key to defeat guesses. A mixed-use statement, supplier-identity mistake and missing business address remain worthwhile teaching errors when not arranged as a visible answer template.

### R002 — retain

Correct arithmetic and fresh-oil requirement. Explanation appropriately says empty the bottle beforehand.

All three alternatives encode recognizable baseline/addition mistakes rather than random magnitudes.

Repair: Retain 20 minus 8 equals 12 mL, and keep the empty-before-recovery practice explicit. Do not imply that starting with oil in the collection bottle is the recommended procedure.

### R003 — retain_with_minor_revision

Correctly limits the desiccant recommendation to prolonged exposure. Does not claim every evacuation is ineffective.

Evacuation-only and pump-oil confusion are plausible; recycling recovered refrigerant is a weaker response to an uncapped circuit.

Repair: If improving difficulty, replace the weakest outside-circuit action with an alternative disposition of the existing desiccant, such as attempting to dry and reuse it under vacuum. Verify that misconception against the service context and avoid claiming an unsupported regeneration procedure.

### R004 — retain_with_minor_revision

Correct assignment under 82.34(d)(1)(ii)(B), with reclamation accurately separated.

Salvage-yard and independent-recycler alternatives test actual role confusion. The reclamation-facility option is partly eliminated by unreclaimed in the stem.

Repair: Keep the recovered-but-not-reclaimed scenario because it establishes the applicable legal route. Strengthen the fourth role alternative within that route, such as a separate recycling facility operated by the selling business. Explain that approved equipment and the recovery requirements still apply.

### R005 — revise

Accurate ownership chain: common equipment owner must also own or operate the extraction establishment. Other parties do not qualify merely by credentials or contract.

Vehicle ownership and machine-service-company ownership are remote distractions; same-owner choice is conspicuously relevant.

Repair: Use four plausible business ownership arrangements: common equipment owner operating the extraction site; shared brand but separately owned machines; recovery equipment owned by an independent contractor; common site operator leasing or contracting access without owning both machines. Phrase exact relationships carefully and recheck the ownership clause. Remove the section number from the question unless source lookup is intentionally being assessed.

### R006 — retain

Physics and SDS connection are sound. Nearly constant temperature is a stipulated scenario, not a universal spill guarantee.

Sensible warming is the strongest distractor; condensation/direction errors are still meaningful novice confusions.

Repair: Retain as a heat-transfer application. Teaching should explicitly distinguish refrigerant temperature from the skin losing energy. No need to obscure boiling or invent a different key; the phase-change observation is legitimate evidence the learner must interpret.

### R007 — retain_with_minor_revision

Correctly includes recovery or re-evacuation and requires both checks for remaining charge.

Continuing after passed vacuum check or retrying test dose are credible errors. Lubricant injection is less connected to this failure.

Repair: Replace the lubricant option with a plausible premature process reset or repeat-check action, while distinguishing it from source-permitted re-evacuation. Do not accidentally make re-evacuation a wrong answer. Maintain the failed pressurized-check context.

### R008 — retain

Supported distinction between material compatibility, cleaning performance, solvent removal, and vehicle-specific permission.

All alternatives represent different claims a technician might incorrectly infer from a product standard.

Repair: Retain. Explain that a compatibility result alone does not establish procedural approval or cleaning/removal outcomes; avoid suggesting J2670 approves every material in every system.

### R009 — revise

Explanation correctly preserves certification and distinguishes purchasing equipment from availability for opening systems.

Top-off-only in the stem directly removes both opening-system choices, leaving only the vehicle class decision.

Repair: Remove top-off-only from the stem and ask which described business is exempt from purchasing equipment under the specified provision. Keep the balanced four business profiles so both service scope and appliance class must be assessed. Explanation must still retain certification and avoid implying businesses opening systems qualify.

### R010 — revise

Correct low-area density explanation. Other physical facts do not account for pit accumulation.

Flammability is a strong competing safety focus. Temperature-pressure and ready evaporation are weaker distractors for the spatial question.

Repair: Use plausible alternative mechanisms for where released vapor accumulates or how ventilation placement addresses it, rather than unrelated true properties. For example compare heavier-than-air accumulation with mistaken assumptions that vapor necessarily rises, mixes uniformly, or stays at the release height. Keep options parallel and source-backed explanation of the actual mechanism; do not introduce hazardous procedural recommendations.

### R011 — revise

82.40(f) accurately supports an on-premises demonstration. Explanation properly avoids inventing a requirement to retain job logs.

One live demonstration against three endorsements/records creates a category-outlier cue; certified status makes training confirmation especially weak.

Repair: Use parallel actual verification procedures varying meaningful scope: demonstration at the business premises, mandatory retaking of an entire written test, third-party training demonstration, or document review as a substitute. Check the wording so it asks what this provision specifically authorizes rather than asserting the other acts could never be requested under any authority. Avoid three synonyms for paperwork versus one practical act.

### R012 — revise

Accurate for the described revised replacement instructions; not a universal ranking of all component instructions above every OEM directive.

Stem announces that existing data are obsolete and only A names the replacement. Earlier service bulletin is an especially transparent loser.

Repair: Present a genuine document-applicability decision: distinguish an original VIN-linked label, a current generic model manual, an older applicable-looking bulletin, and instructions explicitly applicable to the installed replacement part number. Remove the sentence that pre-declares every alternative obsolete. Let applicability and revision evidence establish the answer; retain enough detail for a unique answer without teaching a blanket authority hierarchy.

### R013 — retain_with_minor_revision

Accurate attribution of a typical hours-based interval to MACS with manufacturer instructions controlling.

Throughput is a useful neighboring-maintenance confusion; vehicle count is a credible but imprecise proxy; cylinder test date is weak.

Repair: Replace the cylinder-test date with a plausible pump-related maintenance record, such as calendar time since the last oil change, while keeping the question specifically about the cited hours-based schedule. Do not imply hours are the only basis for all pumps.

### R014 — revise

Explanation is substantively correct and explicitly model-specific. Manual has clearing followed by the accepted-serial-number and shutdown display; the current stem can be read as if acceptance display occurs before another clearing action. Capacity limit is a maintenance trigger, not the only permissible time to replace a filter.

Adjacent oil-warming/leak-check steps are credible sequence confusions. Code-entry detail risks testing model trivia rather than containment unless the relevant manual context is supplied.

Repair: Change the setup to a technician entering a valid replacement filter code, then ask what the machine must complete before instructing power-off/removal; do not say an accepted-code display precedes clearing. Prefer providing the relevant manual excerpt or an explicit model-specific training context. Tie the decision to clearing contained refrigerant before opening a connection and following prompts. Do not generalize this UI sequence across machines.
