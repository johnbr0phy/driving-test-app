This is distinct from a superficial longest-answer effect, but still weakens assessment of application.

## French: idiom and comprehension design

The French is generally grammatical, understandable and professionally appropriate. No broad translation-calque problem was found. The register is administrative and occasionally unnecessarily abstract for a driver-facing question, but this is not a factual defect. Specific improvements:

- R003: use the same document name, 'fiche de découverte', in option A; 'relevé de l'objet' is less natural and introduces a needless label substitution.
- R006: 'Quelle date borne ici l'usage' is stilted. Prefer 'Jusqu'à quelle date cette vignette peut-elle être utilisée ?'.
- R033: 'prestation de même périmètre' is professional jargon; 'prestation comprenant les mêmes services et frais' is clearer.
- R085: replace passenger with booking client in answer D, as noted above.
- R088: 'Les paroles observées' is unnatural; prefer 'Les propos entendus, avec l'heure et le lieu'.
- R118: wording names the expanded form of the desired acronym. It is valid recognition, but scarcely tests choice of legal structure. Give participant requirements instead.
- R156: question asks 'Quelle voie ... a changé ?' but answer says 'Cette voie ... est fermée'. Prefer asking the consequence for this first application, making question and response grammatically parallel.
- R187: 'période prévue' is understandable; 'dates de début et de fin' is more concrete if these are the intended data.
- R188: option A 'Au temps de l'exposition samedi' is awkward. Prefer 'Pendant l'exposition organisée samedi'.

The French passage set repeatedly uses the same discourse architecture: acknowledge a request, withhold confirmation, specify a condition, then reject an exaggerated inference. R025/R031/R035/R061/R074/R108/R120/R148/R150 especially cluster around pending status and conditional commitments. R021/R069/R136 additionally repeat caution against extrapolation or blanket rules. R003/R013/R020/R044/R045/R083/R101/R135/R152/R161/R169/R172/R188 provide more varied order, reference, summary, arithmetic and communication purposes.

These are not literal duplicates. However, repeated questions are often answerable by selecting the moderate, qualified option while alternatives say immediately, all, automatically, necessarily, or imply arbitrary cancellation. Remove that tone shortcut by giving four equally qualified, plausible summaries differing in the actual condition, person, date or action. For example R035 can compare 'existing trips maintained; extra vehicle pending', 'existing trips pending; extra confirmed', 'both pending' and 'both confirmed' in balanced language. R148 can compare actual combinations of group readiness and vehicle confirmation. Shorten filler sentences that do not contribute to referent, sequence, qualification or inference; excessive passage length currently creates the appearance of depth more than additional reading demand.

The scope explicitly omits free written production and spelling assessment. Those omissions must stay visible; multiple-choice passage recognition does not cover the full French expression competency.

## English: contextual A2 model assessment

All twelve English items were reviewed in context: R005/R007/R058/R075/R093/R122/R124/R151/R153/R164/R180/R190. English utterances are idiomatic and plausible for travel/service interactions. 'Boot' is standard British English; 'trunk' is a useful explained US equivalent, not an error. Polite May I/Shall I/Could you constructions, locations, times, receipts, baggage and heating are appropriate familiar-service material. R093's conditional concessive 'even if it takes longer' and R151's modal uncertainty are somewhat richer than bare vocabulary but remain reasonable short contextual reading practice around A2. This does not certify an entire course as A2.

Most tasks are translation or intent recognition; production, listening, oral interaction and repair are absent. Eight or so micro-skills cover little progression across four tests. R005's holiday well-wish and R164's waiting reply use stereotypical chauffeur scripts that options-only priors can recover. To make the stem necessary without unnatural distractors, offer four equally natural service replies differing in time, actor, quantity or direction, and make the preceding exchange disambiguate them. R075 and R180 can deliberately contrast offering to act, asking the passenger to act, and asking permission, but keep wording equally natural. R007/R124/R190 already distinguish actual location/object/time facts and should not be distorted merely because this model guessed them correctly in Stage1.
