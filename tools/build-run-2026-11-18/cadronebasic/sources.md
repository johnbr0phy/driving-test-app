# Sources for `cadronebasic` (Transport Canada Small Basic Exam, Pilot Certificate - Basic Operations)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the Canadian Aviation Regulations,
Transport Canada's knowledge requirements document TP 15263 and Transport Canada's own guidance.
No Transport Canada exam item, past question, flight-school quiz or commercial practice question
was used or reworded, and no school is named. Checked 2026-11-18.

## Exam format and outline (official)
- Transport Canada, "Small Basic Exam",
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-pilot-licensing/take-drone-pilot-online-exam/small-basic
  (fetched 2026-11-18, page modified 2025-11-04): 35 multiple-choice questions, 90 minutes (marked
  on the answers given if time runs out), 65% or higher to pass, minimum age 14, a 24-hour wait
  after a fail, retakes as often as wished, same fee each attempt, questions based on TP 15263
  ("not a study guide"). Passing gives the Pilot Certificate - Basic Operations.
- Transport Canada, "Knowledge Requirements for Pilots of Remotely Piloted Aircraft Systems, 250 g
  up to and including 150 kg, Basic and Advanced Operations - TP 15263", Fourth Edition 03/2025,
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/publications/knowledge-requirements-pilots-remotely-piloted-aircraft-systems-250-g-including-150-kg-basic-advanced-operations-tp-15263
  (fetched 2026-11-18, page modified 2025-04-01): the exam table (Basic 35 questions, 1 1/2 hours,
  65%; Advanced 50, 1 hour, 80%; Flight Reviewer 30, 1 1/2 hours, 80%), exams online only through the
  Drone Management Portal, English or French. The knowledge tables mark each topic Basic and/or
  Advanced; the bank uses only topics marked Basic (read from the table's tick marks): Aeronautics
  Act definitions, CAR 101.01, 102.01, 103.02, 601.14 to 601.22, 606.01, all of Part IX Divisions
  I to IV (900.01 to 901.59), TSB, FSS/FIC, airframes, batteries, data links, autopilots, motors,
  compass, altimeter, IMU, human factors, low-level meteorology (clouds, fog, turbulence, wind,
  icing, aviation weather web site), navigation basics (latitude, longitude, charts, CFS, UTC,
  sunrise and sunset, NOTAM, flight planning), flight operations and theory of flight. Topics
  marked Advanced only (Part IX Division V, controlled airspace procedures, METAR/TAF, MF/ATF
  procedures, ATC transponder theory, radiotelephony details) are left out except where a basic
  pilot must know the boundary (for example that controlled airspace is advanced).
- Transport Canada, "Getting a drone pilot certificate",
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-pilot-licensing/getting-drone-pilot-certificate
  (fetched 2026-11-18): passing the Small Basic Exam gives the basic certificate; certificates do
  not expire, but pilots must keep their skills current.
- Transport Canada, "Drone operation categories and pilot certificates",
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/learn-rules-you-fly-your-drone/drone-operation-categories-pilot-certificates
  (fetched 2026-11-18): micro under 250 g, small 250 g to 25 kg (basic and above), medium over 25 kg
  to 150 kg (advanced and above); minimum ages 14 basic, 16 advanced, 18 Level 1 Complex; what each
  certificate allows.
- Transport Canada, "Drone pilot study resources",
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-pilot-licensing/drone-pilot-study-resources
  (fetched 2026-11-18): lists TP 15263, the TC AIM, RPAS 101, the human factors handbooks and CARs
  Part IX as study resources.

## Law (primary)
- Canadian Aviation Regulations, SOR/96-433, consolidated text on Justice Laws,
  https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/FullText.html (fetched 2026-11-18,
  dcterms modified 2026-06-17, includes SOR/2025-70, SOR/2025-98 and SOR/2025-226): Part IX
  sections 900.01 (definitions: small and medium RPA, payload, VLOS, flyaway, sheltered
  operation), 900.06 to 900.09, 900.13 to 900.20 (registration), 901.11 to 901.50 (general rules:
  30 m from uninvolved people at any altitude for small RPA in VLOS, 400 ft (122 m) AGL and 100 ft
  above a structure within 200 ft, 3 NM from an airport and 1 NM from a heliport, 3 NM from a DND
  aerodrome, 12 hours after alcohol, up to five aircraft from one station, records 12 and 24
  months, incident analysis kept 12 months), 901.53 to 901.59 (basic operations: uncontrolled
  airspace, at least 30 m, age 14, TP 15263 exam, 24-month recency), 901.62 (advanced operations,
  used only for distractors and boundaries); CAR 101.01 (night, airport, heliport), 102.01(b.1)
  (indoor and underground), 103.02, 103.03, 601.15 and 601.17 (forest fire, 5 NM, 3,000 ft AGL),
  606.01; Schedule IV item 26 lists the basic exam charge, deliberately not used.
- Canada Gazette Part II, SOR/2025-70, "Regulations Amending the Canadian Aviation Regulations
  (Remotely Piloted Aircraft Systems - Beyond Visual Line-of-Sight and Other Operations)",
  https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors70-eng.html (fetched 2026-11-18):
  registered 6 March 2025; parts in force 1 April 2025 (Level 1 Complex exam, RPOC applications,
  micro drones at advertised events need an SFOC); the BVLOS, medium RPAS and SFOC requirements
  in force 4 November 2025. These are the rules used; the bank asks the basic rules as they stand
  after the change (basic now defined as uncontrolled airspace and 30 m at any altitude).

## Guidance (official)
- Transport Canada Aeronautical Information Manual (TC AIM, TP 14371), edition of October 1, 2026,
  RPA chapter, https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/aim-2026-2_rpa_en.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-18): micro-RPA weight rules, registration marking on the main body, 7-day
  notices, VLOS unaided (no binoculars; glasses allowed), radio line-of-sight and 2.4 GHz,
  airspace classes A to G, CYR and CYA, drone site selection tool colours, DAH and CFS every 56
  days, people in vehicles or buildings not counted for 30 m, visual scanning (20 to 30 minutes dark
  adaptation, 1 to 2 seconds to refocus, up to 10 seconds to see and avoid, 10 to 15 degree blocks,
  empty field myopia, fovea), IM SAFE, cannabis 28 days, GPS needs 4 satellites and can err by up
  to 75 ft in altitude, Calgary ASL example, night and civil twilight 6 degrees, aircraft position
  lights, 126.7 and 123.2 MHz, ROC-A, nautical and statute miles, VNC 1:500 000 and VTA 1:250 000,
  CFS Reg/Cert/Mil, aerodrome operators cannot forbid airspace use, records, CAR 901.49 list, TSB
  wording, tethered RPA, RPAS NOTAM decided by TC with NAV CANADA, Fisheries Act and marine
  mammals, national parks and bird sanctuaries.
- TC AIM, October 1, 2026, MET chapter,
  https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/aim-2026-2_met_en.pdf (fetched 2026-11-18):
  icing intensity and types (rime, clear, mixed), turbulence reporting, Beaufort wind scale.
- Transport Canada, "Command and control (C2) link tips for safe drone operations in urban
  environments",
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/learn-rules-you-fly-your-drone/tips-best-practices-drone-pilots/command-control-c2-link-tips-safe-drone-operations-urban-environments
  (fetched 2026-11-18): multipath in urban canyons, Fresnel zone and 40% obstruction, EMI sources
  (power lines, telecom towers, substations), dipole antennas perpendicular, patch antennas pointed.
- Transport Canada and Aerial Evolution Association of Canada, "RPAS 101: A General Knowledge
  Guide for Canadian RPAS Pilots" (joint production, linked by TC as a study resource and by the
  TC AIM), https://www.aerialevolution.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Nov-27-RPAS-101_EN-Final.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-18): systems (flight controller, ESC, IMU, GNSS, magnetometer calibration,
  barometer zeroed at launch, brushless outrunners, Kv x voltage), LiPo handling, IP ratings,
  launchers, time zones (EST UTC-5, PDT UTC-7), 24-hour time, landmark position reports, icing
  rule of thumb, 15 NM thunderstorm advice, emergencies, CRM, hazardous attitudes, Reason's
  model, TEM, automation, decisions in advance, diving and altitude. RPAS 101 dates from 2022, so
  only its systems, weather and human factors content is used; every rule comes from the current
  CARs instead (its $5 fee and older rule wording are not used).
- NAV CANADA / Meteorological Service of Canada, "The Weather of Ontario and Quebec, Graphic Area
  Forecast 33" (Local Area Knowledge manual), https://www.navcanada.ca/en/lawm-ontario-quebec-en.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-18): radiation, advection, frontal, steam and ice fog; sea and land breezes;
  anabatic and katabatic winds; mechanical turbulence; lee waves (wind within 30 degrees of
  perpendicular); stability; thunderstorm stages and air mass storms; freezing rain by warm
  overrunning.
- FAA, Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, chapter 5,
  https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak/07_phak_ch5.pdf
  and FAA Helicopter Flying Handbook, chapter 11,
  https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/helicopter_flying_handbook/hfh_ch11.pdf
  (both fetched 2026-11-18; government handbooks used for physics only, since TP 15263 lists theory
  of flight topics without a Canadian text): four forces in balance, angle of attack, stall at the
  critical angle regardless of airspeed, induced and parasite drag, load factor and bank, turn
  radius and airspeed, density and lift, vortex ring state, dynamic rollover's three factors.

## What could not be confirmed, and how the copy words it
- The exam fee: the TC exam page shows a blank fee; CAR Schedule IV lists a charge. Fees are left
  out of the bank, landing and facts.
- TSB reporting thresholds: the TC AIM says all occurrences in TSB Regulations 2(1) involving a
  drone must be reported, regardless of weight, while also listing the cases TSB investigates; the
  TSB Regulations text itself does not mention drones. No question asks a TSB threshold.
- Lapse rate: RPAS 101 gives about 2 degrees C per 1,000 ft and the TC AIM gives 3 and 1.5; no
  question asks a lapse rate.
- RPAS 101's 15 NM thunderstorm distance is guidance, worded as "RPAS 101 advises".

## Deliberately left out
- Fines and penalties, exam and registration fees, insurance amounts.
- Anything about the exam itself (question count, pass mark, retake wait) beyond the landing.
- Advanced, Level 1 Complex and BVLOS rules except as the boundary of basic operations.
- Microdrone best-practice guidance worded as law.
