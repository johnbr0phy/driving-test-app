# Red Seal Cook (carscook): sources

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the Red Seal Occupational Standard's
tasks and ranges, Health Canada food safety guidance and standard trade practice. No Red Seal
sample question, provincial study-guide item, textbook exercise or commercial practice question was
copied or paraphrased, and no training provider is named. Checked 2026-11-18.

  dairy alternatives, eggs and egg products 10 (5/5); G Pastas, noodles, stuffed pastas and
  dumplings 10 (6/4); H Grains, pulses, seeds, nuts and alternative proteins 11 (4/3/4); I Meats,
  game, poultry, game birds and variety meats 16 (8/6/2); J Fish and seafood 15 (7/8); K Salads
  and sandwiches 9 (3/3/3); L Specialty preparation 8 (3/2/1/2); M Sweet and savoury baked goods
  and desserts 10 (2/2/2/1/2/1). Question types: knowledge and recall 30 to 40%, procedural and
  application 55 to 65%, critical thinking under 10%. Acronyms provided at the sitting: AP, EP, PPE.

## Food safety figures (Health Canada)
- Safe internal cooking temperatures chart, https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/general-food-safety-tips/safe-internal-cooking-temperatures-chart.html
  (fetched 2026-11-18): ground beef, veal, lamb and pork 71°C; beef steaks and roasts medium-rare
  63°C, medium 71°C, well done 77°C; mechanically tenderized beef 63°C turned at least twice;
  poultry pieces and ground poultry 74°C, whole birds 82°C; egg dishes 74°C; fish 70°C; shellfish
  and crustaceans 74°C, discard shellfish that do not open; leftovers 74°C.
- Food safety tips for leftovers, https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/general-food-safety-tips/food-safety-tips-leftovers.html
  (fetched 2026-11-18): danger zone 4°C to 60°C; shallow containers to cool quickly; discard
  cooked food left at room temperature more than two hours; reheat to 74°C, bring gravies, soups
  and sauces to a rolling boil.
- Safe food storage, https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/general-food-safety-tips/safe-food-storage.html
  (fetched 2026-11-18): refrigerator 4°C or lower, freezer -18°C or lower; raw meat, poultry and
  fish kept separate on the bottom shelf.
- Food allergies, https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-allergies-intolerances/food-allergies.html
  (fetched 2026-11-18): priority allergens eggs, milk, mustard, peanuts, crustaceans and
  molluscs, fish, sesame, soy, sulphites, tree nuts, wheat and triticale.

## Technique facts
Cooking technique items (roux proportions, mother sauces and derivatives, mirepoix, stock and
fumet times, cuts, egg, pasta, grain, meat, fish, garde manger and baking methods) are standard
trade practice within the RSOS ranges listed above, stated at the level every Canadian cook
program teaches. Where sources commonly disagree on a figure (exact cut sizes in millimetres,
cooling time limits, sanitizer strengths), the item asks the principle instead.

## Could not confirm, or left out
- The Health Canada "Safely defrosting foods" page returned 404, so thawing items state only the
  general rule (no thawing on the counter; refrigerator or cold water).
- Commercial cooling time limits, hot-holding minimums, sanitizer strengths and food handler
  certification rules are set by each province's food premises regulation (Ontario's e-Laws page
  returned 403), so they are not asked; the landing says provincial rules vary.
- The Red Seal "Exam Preparation Guide" is the source of the 4-hour limit; individual
  jurisdictions may differ.
- Fees, booking, apprenticeship hours and retake rules: set by each province or territory, left out.

## Blueprint (Tigertest)
The real exam is 150 questions; each practice test is 50, one third of each MWA's count rounded by
largest remainder: A 5, B 4, C 4, D 4, E 4, F 3, G 3, H 4, I 5, J 5, K 3, L 3, M 3. Pools are four
times each count (200 in all), so every question appears in exactly one test. Five training sets
in MWA order, then four tests: 9 dashboard steps. Pass line 70%, the real pass mark.
