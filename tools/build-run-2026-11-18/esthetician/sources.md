# Sources for `esthetician` (NIC National Esthetics Theory Examination)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the NIC content outline and the
federal guidance below. No NIC sample question, past paper, school workbook item or commercial
practice question was copied or reworded, and no prep provider is named. A fact-and-answer scan
against `data/cosmetology-questions.json`, `data/barber-questions.json`,
`data/nail-technician-questions.json` and `data/mblex-questions.json` was run and the overlapping
items rewritten (tapotement, vibration and fibroblast items replaced). Checked 2026-11-18.

## Content outline and format (official)
- NIC, National Esthetics Theory Examination Candidate Information Bulletin (CIB), effective
  September 1, 2025, revision date March 1, 2026,
  https://prov-testing.github.io/nic_cib/esthetics_national_theory_english.html
  (fetched 2026-11-18; served by Prov, NIC's exam provider, the same host as the Nail Technology
  CIB reached from the "Candidate Information Bulletins" link on nictesting.org). Gave: 90
  minutes; 110 items, 100 weighted and counted in the score; four options with one correct;
  content outline with two weighted domains: Scientific Concepts 55% (microbiology; infection
  control levels and methods; standard precautions, blood exposure, SDS, handling chemicals;
  cells, tissues, organs and systems; histology and physiology of the skin, glands and
  functions; sebaceous and sudoriferous gland disorders, contagious diseases, inflammations,
  pigmentation, growths, skin cancers, primary and secondary lesions; hair structure, growth
  cycles, hirsutism and hypertrichosis; chemistry: ingredients, labeling, function, pH) and Skin
  Care and Services 45% (consultation and documentation including physician release; draping;
  Fitzpatrick skin analysis; contraindications; treatment protocol; cleansing, steaming,
  chemical and mechanical exfoliation, massage, extraction, masks, treatment, sun protection and
  home care; electrical equipment; makeup principles, products, application, bracing and
  infection control; aromatherapy, pre- and post-surgical treatments, ultrasonic; body wraps,
  scrubs and sunless tanning; lash and brow tinting, artificial lashes, lash lifting; waxing and
  tweezing; wellness; dermaplaning, nano infusion, microdermabrasion). References: Milady
  Standard Esthetics Fundamentals and Foundations 12th ed. (2020), Pivot Point Fundamentals:
  Esthetics (2022), supplemental Milady Standard Advanced Esthetics 3rd ed. (2026). The
  bulletin's eight sample questions were read for format only and not reused (their facts:
  dermatology, antiseptic, basal layer, anagen, consultation form at each treatment,
  telangiectasia, active melanocytes, steamer purpose, were avoided as keys).
- NIC, "NIC Exam Types", https://nictesting.org/nic-exam-types/ (read for the Nail Technology
  build the same day): exams are offered as theory, practical and written practical, run by
  test administration companies; languages are set by vendors and state boards.

## Pass mark (official state source)
- South Carolina LLR, Board of Cosmetology, "Examination Information",
  https://llr.sc.gov/cosmo/examins.aspx (fetched 2026-11-18): applicants must obtain a passing
  score of 75% on the NIC examination (theory and practical) for cosmetology, nail technology
  and esthetics. NIC publishes no national pass mark; each board sets its own, so the landing
  says "many boards use 75" and the app uses 75% as a readiness line.

## Federal guidance used in questions
- OSHA, "Bloodborne Pathogens and Needlestick Prevention", https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens
  (fetched 2026-11-18): bloodborne pathogens include HBV, HCV and HIV; the exposure control plan;
  engineering controls are the primary means of eliminating or minimizing exposure (29 CFR
  1910.1030).
- OSHA Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200 (standard text): SDS from manufacturers,
  importers and distributors; section 7 handling and storage, section 8 exposure controls and
  personal protection; workplace container labeling.
- FDA, "Cosmetics Labeling Guide",
  https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling-regulations/cosmetics-labeling-guide
  (fetched 2026-11-18): ingredients in descending order of predominance; ingredients at 1% or
  less in any order after the rest (21 CFR 701.3(f)(2)); drug and cosmetic products declare the
  active drug ingredient first (21 CFR 701.3(d)); soap defined as an alkali salt of fatty acids;
  products that clean and make other claims are cosmetics or drugs.
- FDA, "Alpha Hydroxy Acids", https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/alpha-hydroxy-acids
  (fetched 2026-11-18): AHAs exfoliate, effect depends on type, concentration and pH; FDA
  studies found increased UV sensitivity (18% more reddening, cell damage doubled), reversible.
- FDA, "Beta Hydroxy Acids", https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/beta-hydroxy-acids
  (fetched 2026-11-18): salicylic acid is the BHA most commonly used; citric acid usually called
  an AHA; glycolic acid increases sun sensitivity.
- FDA, "Allergens in Cosmetics", https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/allergens-cosmetics
  (fetched 2026-11-18): allergic contact dermatitis; inhaled fragrance can affect people with
  asthma; testing a small amount first.
- FDA, "Sunscreen: How to Help Protect Your Skin from the Sun",
  https://www.fda.gov/drugs/understanding-over-counter-medicines/sunscreen-how-help-protect-your-skin-sun
  (fetched 2026-11-18): broad spectrum SPF 15 or higher; reapply at least every two hours; SPF
  mainly measures UVB protection; broad spectrum covers UVA and UVB; water resistant labels state
  40 or 80 minutes; warning for products not broad spectrum or under SPF 15.
- FDA, "Sunless Tanners and Bronzers",
  https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/sunless-tanners-bronzers (fetched 2026-11-18):
  DHA darkens skin by reacting with amino acids in the surface; approved for external use only,
  not the lips, mucous membranes or the area of the eye; only products with sunscreen and an SPF
  protect from the sun.
- FDA, "Eye Cosmetic Safety", https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/eye-cosmetic-safety
  (fetched 2026-11-18): kohl unapproved and linked to lead poisoning; silver nitrate up to 4% in
  professional-use lash and brow color (21 CFR 73.2550); import alert on coal tar lash dyes;
  false lashes, extensions and adhesives are cosmetics; do not moisten mascara with water or
  saliva (adds bacteria, dilutes preservative).
- National Cancer Institute, "Melanoma Treatment (PDQ), Patient Version",
  https://www.cancer.gov/types/skin/patient/melanoma-treatment-pdq (fetched 2026-11-18): ABCDE
  signs; D, usually larger than 6 millimeters; E, evolving.

## Textbook facts
Microbiology terms, anatomy (skin layers, glands, facial bones, muscles and nerves), disorder and
lesion names, the Fitzpatrick scale, facial steps, massage movements, masks, galvanic poles,
devices, color theory and waxing technique are standard content of the NIC reference texts above
(Milady Standard Esthetics, Pivot Point Fundamentals: Esthetics). The textbooks are paid and were
not fetched; items keep to widely taught, stable facts.

## Could not be loaded
- CDC skin cancer symptoms page returned 403; NCI was used instead.
- FDA "Removing Hair Safely" and "Eyelash and Eyebrow Dyes" URLs returned 404; the lash and brow
  facts come from the Eye Cosmetic Safety page above.

## Left out on purpose
Training hours, license fees, renewal, scope-of-practice rules (lancets, dermaplaning, laser and
peel strength limits), each state's pass mark and the practical exam vary by state and are not
asked; the dermaplaning item says scope varies by state. No item names a product brand or
training provider. No item asks about the test itself.

## Blueprint (Tigertest)
Per 50-question test, Scientific Concepts 27 (54%): infection control and safety 6, anatomy and
physiology 5, skin histology 5, disorders and diseases 6, hair 2, chemistry 3; Skin Care and
Services 23 (46%): consultation and analysis 5, facial procedures 7, electrical and advanced
equipment 3, makeup 3, hair removal, lash and brow 3, body and other services 2. NIC weights only
the two domains; the split inside each is Tigertest's. Pools are 4x per test, so no question
repeats across the four tests. Five topic sets (36/48/44/36/36), 9 dashboard steps.
