# Sources for `nailtech` (NIC National Nail Technology Theory Examination)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the NIC content outline and the
federal guidance below. No NIC sample question, past paper, school workbook item or commercial
practice question was copied or reworded, and no prep provider is named. A fact-and-answer scan
against `data/cosmetology-questions.json`, `data/barber-questions.json` and
`data/mblex-questions.json` was run and the overlapping items rewritten. Checked 2026-11-18.

## Content outline and format (official)
- NIC, National Nail Technology Theory Examination Candidate Information Bulletin (CIB),
  effective September 1, 2025, revision date March 1, 2026,
  https://prov-testing.github.io/nic_cib/nail_technology_national_theory_english.html
  (fetched 2026-11-18; served by Prov, NIC's exam provider, and reached from the "Candidate
  Information Bulletins" link on nictesting.org,
  https://apps.provexam.online/sample-apps/cib_selection_app/). Gave: 110 items, 100 weighted
  (scored), 90 minutes; four options with one correct; content outline with weights:
  Scientific Concepts - Infection Control and Safety Practices 15%, Anatomy and Physiology 15%,
  Chemistry of Nail Products 10%; Nail Technology Procedures - Pre-service Process 5%, Nail
  Service Tools 13%, Manicure and Pedicure Services 18% (shaping, soak, cuticle, cleanse, buff,
  massage: effleurage, petrissage, friction, tapotement; polish; add-ons: paraffin, exfoliation,
  thermal), Nail Enhancement Services 20% (nail tip, acrylics, soft, hard and hybrid gels, powder
  dip), Post-Service Procedures 4%. References: Milady Standard Nail Technology 8th ed. (2020),
  Pivot Point Fundamentals: Nails (2022), Tammy Taylor Guide to Manicuring and Advanced Nail
  Technology Edition III (2022). The bulletin's sample questions were read for format only and
  not reused.
- NIC, "NIC Exam Types", https://nictesting.org/nic-exam-types/ (fetched 2026-11-18): Nail
  Technology is offered as a theory, practical and written practical exam; computer-based,
  practical and remote testing are run by test administration companies; foreign-language
  options are in the CIBs and set by vendors and state boards.
- NIC home page, https://nictesting.org/ (fetched 2026-11-18): links to the CIB app above.

## Pass mark (official state source)
- South Carolina LLR, Board of Cosmetology, "Examination Information",
  https://llr.sc.gov/cosmo/examins.aspx (fetched 2026-11-18): applicants need a passing score
  of 75% on the NIC theory and practical exams for cosmetology, nail technology and esthetics;
  Prov administers them. NIC itself publishes no national pass mark; each board sets its own, so
  the landing says "many boards use 75" and the app uses 75% as a readiness line.

## Federal guidance used in questions
- OSHA, "Nail Salons", https://www.osha.gov/nail-salons (fetched 2026-11-18): chemicals
  (toluene, formaldehyde, DBP, methacrylates), health effects, ergonomic and biological hazards.
- OSHA, "Nail Salons: Chemical Hazards", https://www.osha.gov/nail-salons/chemical-hazards
  (fetched 2026-11-18): exhaust ventilation may cut exposure by at least 50%; HVAC fan On;
  ventilated tables on, charcoal filters changed at least monthly; nitrile gloves for many
  chemicals, latex or vinyl for acetone; bottles capped; metal self-closing trash cans emptied
  daily; no pouring down the sink or toilet; goggles when transferring products; N95 for dust,
  not vapors; no eating in work areas; SDS near products.
- OSHA, "Nail Salons: Biological Hazards", https://www.osha.gov/nail-salons/biological-hazards
  (fetched 2026-11-18): disinfect foot basins after each client and at day's end; EPA-registered
  disinfectant per label; avoid clients with open or infected skin; bandage cuts.
- OSHA, "Nail Salons: Awkward Positions and Repetitive Motions",
  https://www.osha.gov/nail-salons/positions-motions (fetched 2026-11-18): raise the client's
  hand or foot, footrest, towel or foam pad on table edges, padded tool handles, breaks.
- FDA, "Nail Care Products", https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/nail-care-products
  (fetched 2026-11-18): MMA complaints and removal of 100% MMA monomer products; formaldehyde
  hardeners make nails brittle with frequent use; toluene being phased out; DBP the most common
  phthalate in polish (now in few); EMA judged safe with directions to avoid skin contact;
  flammable products; ventilation.
- OSHA Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200(g) (SDS sections, including first-aid
  measures), standard text.

## Textbook facts
Anatomy (nail parts, growth, bones and muscles of the arm, hand, leg and foot), disorder names,
massage movements, nail shapes, tools, polish, paraffin and enhancement technique are standard
content of the NIC reference texts above (Milady 8th ed., Pivot Point Fundamentals: Nails). The
textbooks themselves are paid and were not fetched; items keep to widely taught, stable facts.

## Left out on purpose
Training hours, license fees, renewal, scope-of-practice rules (credo blades, callus removal by
blade), each state's pass mark and the practical exam vary by state and are not asked. No item
names a product brand or training provider. No item asks about the test itself.

## Blueprint (Tigertest)
Per 50-question test: infection control and safety 8, nail and skin structure 4, disorders 4,
chemistry 5, consultation and post-service 4, tools 6, manicure and pedicure 9, enhancements 10
(the outline's 15/15/10/9/13/18/20, rounded). Pools are 4x per test, so no question repeats
across the four tests. Four topic sets (52/48/60/40), 8 dashboard steps.
