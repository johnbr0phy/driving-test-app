# Sources for `zak53` (South Africa K53 learner's licence test, Code 2)

All 200 questions are original. No official or commercial question bank was opened or copied.
Rules come from the National Road Traffic Act 93 of 1996 and the National Road Traffic
Regulations, 2000 (read through gov.za and Arrive Alive, the Department of Transport's road
safety site), the gov.za learner's licence service page, and the SADC Road Traffic Signs Manual
(sign classes and names, read through Wikipedia's summary of it). Signs and markings are described
in words (no images). All checked 2026-10-31 unless noted.

## Test format
- The real learner's test is 64 multiple-choice questions drawn at random (RTMC spokesperson,
  May 2026; verified by the integrator's fact-checkers for this batch, given in BRIEF.md).
- Section sizes 28 rules / 28 signs / 8 controls and the section pass marks 22/28, 23/28 and 6/8
  come only from study guides (e.g. autoviatest.com South Africa facts page, checked 2026-10-31,
  which also gives conflicting figures). The landing says "guides say" for all of these.
- Tigertest's mock tests: 4 x 50 in the brief's mix (22 rules, 22 signs and markings, 6 controls),
  40 to pass (80%), presented as Tigertest's own target.

## Licence facts
- gov.za, "Apply for a learner's licence"
  (https://www.gov.za/services/driving-licence/apply-learners-licence): Code 1 from 16 (up to
  125 cc), Code 2 (up to 3,500 kg GVM) from 17 on the date of the test, Code 3 from 18; learner's
  licence valid 24 months and "cannot be extended"; you may drive only supervised by a licensed
  driver; the test covers road signs, vehicle controls and rules of the road.
- Driving licence (Code B) from 18 (NRTA s15; Wikipedia "Driving licence in South Africa").
- Learner may not carry persons for reward, other than the accompanying person (NRTA, learner's
  licence conditions, via search excerpt of the Act).
- Avoided: licence card renewal after the 10-year approval (uncertain), any "zero alcohol limit"
  claim (not law), fees and fines.

## Alcohol
- Ordinary drivers: 0.05 g per 100 ml of blood, 0.24 mg per 1,000 ml of breath. Professional
  drivers: 0.02 g per 100 ml of blood, 0.10 mg per 1,000 ml of breath (NRTA s65; George Herald
  2026-02-23 traffic report and Citizen articles quoting the Act).

## Crashes
- Injury crash: report to police within 24 hours. Damage-only crash: by the next working day
  (NRTA s61; verified by the batch fact-checkers, BRIEF.md). Stop at once; give name and address
  to anyone with reasonable grounds to ask (s61).

## Speed and following
- General limits: 60 km/h urban, 100 km/h outside urban areas (not freeway), 120 km/h freeway
  (reg 292); goods vehicles over 9,000 kg GVM 80 km/h (reg 293). Arrive Alive, "Speed limits and
  the law", https://www.arrivealive.co.za/speed-limits-and-the-law
- Two-second following distance, more in poor conditions (K53 module "Following other vehicles",
  via acts.co.za search excerpt).
- Shoulder (yellow line): reg 298A, between sunrise and sunset, on a road with one lane each way,
  to let faster traffic pass when safe; not for overtaking (Carmag, TimesLIVE 2024-02-12,
  BusinessTech). The "150 m visibility" figure for the shoulder was not confirmed and is not used.

## Stopping and parking (regs 304 and 305)
- Arrive Alive, "Where are you not allowed to stop or park":
  https://carinsurance.arrivealive.co.za/where-are-you-not-allowed-to-stop-or-park-under-the-national-road-traffic-regulations/
  No stopping within 6 m of a tunnel, subway or bridge; within 9 m of the approach side of a
  pedestrian crossing; alongside another vehicle where the roadway is under 9 m wide. No parking
  outside urban areas on the roadway or within 1 m of its edge; within 9 m of a crossing; within
  5 m of an intersection (urban); within 1.5 m either side of a visible hydrant; left wheels more
  than 450 mm from the kerb; on a roadway under 5.5 m wide (unless one-way and signed); on traffic
  islands.

## Lights, phones, seatbelts, children
- Headlamps from sunset to sunrise and whenever persons and vehicles are not clearly visible at
  150 m; main beam off when it could dazzle (regs 157, 158, 180, acts.co.za excerpts).
- Hand-held phone ban, reg 308A (Arrive Alive, "Road legislation and using your phone when
  driving", https://www.arrivealive.co.za/Road-Legislation-and-using-your-phone-when-driving).
- Seatbelts, reg 213; child under 3 in an approved child restraint since 1 May 2015 (Western Cape
  Government, "Introduction of child restraint regulations",
  https://www.westerncape.gov.za/gc-news/70/36572; Arrive Alive seatbelt law page).
- Dead traffic lights treated as a four-way stop; first to stop goes first (EMPD via Citizen
  2022-03-09; K53 intersection module).

## Signs and markings
- Sign classes and names (control R1-R6 incl. 4-way stop, stop/go, pedestrian priority, give way
  to oncoming traffic; command R101-R140 incl. minimum speed, keep left, toll road, switch on
  headlamps; prohibition R201-R245 incl. R214 overtaking prohibited; reservation R301-R354;
  comprehensive R401-R403 freeway begins/ends and living street; selective restriction plates
  R501-R583 incl. day/night and time limits; warning W101-W365; markers W405-W414; information
  IN4-IN20; temporary signs on a yellow background): Wikipedia, "Road signs in South Africa",
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_signs_in_South_Africa (summarising the SADC Road Traffic
  Signs Manual).
- The no-overtaking sign shows two black cars (BRIEF.md, verified by the fact-checkers).
- Route prefixes N (national), R (regional), M (metropolitan); brown tourism signs.

## Deliberately avoided
- Shapes or colours of direction signs, no-stopping and no-parking line colours, chevron colours
  and the warning triangle distance (not confirmed from an official source).
- Fines, fees, demerit (AARTO) points, and the licence card renewal rule.
