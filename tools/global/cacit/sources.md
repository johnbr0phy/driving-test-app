# Sources for `cacit` (Canadian citizenship test)

All 200 questions are original. IRCC does not publish its test questions; none were copied from any
third-party practice site. Checked 2026-10-30.

## Study guide (current as of October 2026)
- IRCC, "Discover Canada: The Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship" (published 2009, revised
  2011, still the official guide; no replacement guide has been released as of 2026-10-30):
  https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/discover-canada.html
  Chapters used as categories: Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship; Who We Are; Canada's
  History; Modern Canada; How Canadians Govern Themselves; Federal Elections; The Justice System;
  Canadian Symbols; Canada's Economy; Canada's Regions.

## Test format
- IRCC "Study for the test" (page modified 2026-03-31):
  https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/test/study
  20 questions, multiple choice or true/false, 45 minutes, at least 15 of 20 to pass; topics rights
  and responsibilities, history, geography, economy, government, laws, symbols; all questions
  based on Discover Canada.
- IRCC "Test results and next steps": up to 3 chances to pass within the test period; after that a
  hearing with a citizenship official.
  https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/test/results.html
- Applicants aged 18 to 54 take the test (Citizenship Act knowledge requirement). Confirmed on canada.ca
  (eligibility page, 2026-10-30).

## Facts that changed after the guide was printed (explanations say so)
- 343 federal electoral districts from the 2023 Representation Order, first used in the April 2025
  general election (the guide says 308).
- CUSMA replaced NAFTA on 1 July 2020 (the guide says NAFTA).
- Canada is in the G7 (the guide says G8; Russia was suspended in 2014).
- The Sovereign is King Charles III (as of 2026); named only in one explanation, never in a key.
- Employers must allow three consecutive hours to vote (Canada Elections Act s. 132).

## Deliberate omissions
- No current Prime Minister, Governor General, premiers or MPs (they change). Capitals are asked
  for every province and territory instead.
- Population figures and language counts in the guide are dated, so no item asks for them.
