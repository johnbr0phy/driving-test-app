# aufirstaid sources (all checked 2026-10-30)

- training.gov.au, HLTAID011 Provide First Aid, Release 1, Assessment Requirements (document generated
  21 February 2025). https://training.gov.au/assets/HLT/HLTAID011_AssessmentRequirements_R1.pdf
  Used for: the knowledge evidence list that sets the eight categories (ARC guidelines, infection
  control and barrier devices, currency of skills, codes of practice, first aid kit contents, duty
  of care, own skills and limitations, consent, privacy, stress management, upper airway and
  positional change, duration and cessation of CPR, AED use and maintenance, chain of survival,
  access to emergency services, rate/ratio/depth, hand position, adult/child/infant differences,
  and conditions: allergic reaction, anaphylaxis, asthma, bleeding, burns, cardiac/chest pain,
  choking, diabetes, drowning, envenomation, eye injuries, fractures/dislocations/sprains/strains,
  head/neck/spinal, hypothermia, hyperthermia, minor wounds, nosebleed, poisoning, seizures, shock,
  sharps injuries, stroke); performance evidence for the landing (2 minutes uninterrupted CPR on an
  adult manikin on the floor and on an infant manikin, AED shock, verbal and written report).
- ANZCOR / Australian Resuscitation Council guidelines (official home https://resus.org.au/), read
  through a full-text student mirror https://survive-student-resource.pages.dev/ because the ARC site
  did not fetch. Guidelines used: 3 (unconscious victim, recovery position, pregnant on left side),
  4 (airway: back blows up to 5, then chest thrusts up to 5, alternate, avoid abdominal thrusts, no
  blind finger sweep, infant neutral head), 5 (breaths about 1 second), 6 (compressions: lower half
  of sternum, one third of chest depth, >5 cm adult, about 5 cm child, about 4 cm infant, two
  fingers for infants, 100-120/min, swap about every 2 minutes), 7 (AED; adult pads if no child pads),
  8 (CPR 30:2 all ages, do not interrupt to check, when to stop), 9.1.1 (bleeding: direct pressure,
  no elevation, tourniquet about 5 cm above, not over a joint, note time, do not remove; haemostatic
  dressings; embedded objects; nosebleed at least 10 minutes, help after 20), 9.1.3 (burns: 20 min
  cool running water, no ice, cling film, chemical burns brush off and flush, do not neutralise),
  9.1.5 (harness suspension: lay flat, no 30-min upright), 9.1.7 (crush: remove load if safe),
  9.2.1 (heart attack: rest, 300 mg aspirin unless allergic, ambulance if severe, worsening or
  >10 min, help with own angina medicine), 9.2.3 (shock: supine without leg raise, keep warm),
  9.2.4 (seizures), 9.2.5 (asthma 4 x 4 x 4 Australia, 6 x 6 x 6 NZ), 9.2.7 (anaphylaxis: lay flat,
  do not stand or walk, lateral thigh, second dose after 5 min), 9.2.9 (diabetes: 15-20 g glucose,
  about 200 mL juice, repeat after 10-15 min, follow with longer-acting carbohydrate, treat as low if
  unsure, leave insulin pump), 9.3.3 (hypothermia), 9.3.4 (heat exhaustion vs heatstroke, wrapped
  ice packs to neck, groin and armpits), 9.4.1 (snake bite: do not wash, cut, suck or tourniquet; do
  not kill or catch the snake), 9.4.2 (funnel-web PIT; redback cold pack, no PIT), 9.4.3 (bee sting
  remove fast; tick allergy kill in place), 9.4.5 (box jellyfish vinegar 30 s; bluebottle hot water
  20 min, no vinegar; Irukandji delayed onset), 9.4.6 (blue-ringed octopus PIT), 9.4.7 (stonefish
  hot water as tolerated, no PIT), 9.4.8 (PIT: 10-15 cm elasticised bandage, firm, splint, do not
  remove before hospital).
- ASCIA (Australasian Society of Clinical Immunology and Allergy) action plans, https://www.allergy.org.au/ :
  give adrenaline first, then asthma reliever, for sudden breathing difficulty in someone with known
  allergy and asthma; mild/moderate reactions: stay and watch; tick allergy: freeze, do not squeeze.
- National Asthma Council Australia first aid, https://www.nationalasthma.org.au/ : blue/grey reliever,
  spacer, reliever from a first aid kit may be used.
- Safe Work Australia, model Code of Practice: First aid in the workplace,
  https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/ : kit contents (no medication), first aider ratios (low risk
  1 per 50, high risk 1 per 25), CPR refresher yearly and first aid qualification every 3 years.
- Health departments' bushfire smoke advice (e.g. health.gov.au): stay indoors, P2/N95 masks.

All 200 questions are original. No RTO assessment, sample quiz or commercial bank was used.

Unconfirmed / judgement calls:
- First aider ratios (1:50 low risk) and "medication not in kits" are from the model Code of
  Practice as remembered; the safeworkaustralia.gov.au page was not fetched this session.
  Review 2026-10-30: secondary sources (state health and WHS guidance) agree on 1:50 / 1:25 / 1:10
  remote and on analgesics being left out of kits; the code PDF itself still did not fetch. Items
  soften to "as a starting point" and "painkillers", not "no medication at all".
- Heatstroke: ANZCOR 9.3.4 text read lists wrapped ice packs to neck, groin and armpits; some
  newer advice prefers cold-water immersion. The item asks only where ice packs go, so both agree.
- Box jellyfish vinegar is ARC's current advice although research has questioned it; kept as ARC.
- Infant compression depth keyed "about 4 cm" (ANZCOR 6), adult keyed "a third of chest depth".
- Pass mark: HLTAID011 has no national pass mark; RTOs set their own knowledge assessments
  (many require all items correct with re-attempts). 80% here is a readiness line, said so in copy.
- Heart Foundation 10-minute rule is cited as ARC 9.2.1 wording, which matches it.
