# aursa sources (RSA, SITHFAB021, Australia)

All questions are original, written by Tigertest. No RTO, regulator or commercial question bank was copied.

- SITHFAB021 Provide responsible service of alcohol, Release 1 (Service and Creative Skills Australia, generated 12 July 2024): elements and performance criteria 1.1 to 4.6. https://training.gov.au/TrainingComponentFiles/SIT/SITHFAB021_R1.pdf (checked 30 Oct 2026)
- Assessment Requirements for SITHFAB021, Release 1: performance evidence and knowledge evidence (harms, ways of assessing intoxication, at-risk groups, agencies, standard drinks, erratic drinking indicators, refusal communication, assistance, responsible delivery, harm minimisation). https://training.gov.au/TrainingComponentFiles/SIT/SITHFAB021_AssessmentRequirements_R1.pdf (checked 30 Oct 2026)
- Australian standard drink = 10 g alcohol (12.5 mL); volume (L) x %ABV x 0.789. Department of Health and Aged Care, "Standard drinks guide". https://www.health.gov.au/topics/alcohol/about-alcohol/standard-drinks-guide (from course knowledge; checked Oct 2026)
- NHMRC Australian Guidelines to Reduce Health Risks from Drinking Alcohol (2020): no more than 10 standard drinks a week and 4 on any day; under 18 and pregnancy/breastfeeding, no alcohol. https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/health-advice/alcohol (from course knowledge; checked Oct 2026)
- National Drug Strategy harm minimisation pillars (demand, supply and harm reduction). https://www.health.gov.au/resources/collections/national-drug-strategy
- State certification differences (NSW 5-year competency card and refresher; Qld no expiry; others vary): provider and regulator summaries, which conflict for Vic and SA, so the bank says only that some states require refreshers or extra training (checked 30 Oct 2026).
- Drink-driving limits (0.05 full licence; zero for learner and provisional; 0.02 heavy vehicles) are national in effect; stated as such.

Deliberately "varies by state" and never asserted: certificate validity and transfer, confiscating false ID, digital licences, minors' access to areas of a venue, signage wording, incident registers, unattended delivery rules, penalty amounts.

Not confirmed / judgment calls: the 80% readiness line (there is no national pass mark; RTOs generally require all knowledge questions to be answered correctly); "most states" for the intoxication definition (NSW wording, similar in Vic, Qld and others).
