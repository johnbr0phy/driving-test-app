# uksia sources (all checked 29 Oct 2026)

## The qualification and its exams
- Pearson BTEC Level 2 Award for Door Supervisors in the Private Security Industry, Specification Issue 2 (Sept 2023), first registration April 2021, QN 603/7281/3:
  https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/BTEC-Specialist-Qualifications/door-supervisors-in-the-private-security-industry/2021/specification-and-sample-assessments/btec-l2-award-for-door-supervisors-in-the-private-security-industry-spec.pdf
  - Unit 1 Principles of Working in the Private Security Industry: MCQ 72 questions, 110 min, pass 70% (50/72), plus section 9 statement (100%). LO1 industry and SIA, LO2 legislation, LO3 arrest, LO4 safe working, LO5 fire, LO6 emergencies, LO7 communication, LO8 record keeping, LO9 terror threats, LO10 vulnerable people, LO11 post-incident.
  - Unit 2 Principles of Working as a Door Supervisor: MCQ 50, 75 min, 70% (35/50). LO1 crimes, LO2 searching, LO3 drugs, LO4 evidence, LO5 licensing law, LO6 queues and capacity, LO7 equipment.
  - Unit 3 Application of Conflict Management: MCQ 20, 30 min, 70% (14/20), covering LO1-3; LO4 is practical.
  - Unit 4 Application of Physical Intervention Skills: MCQ 30, 45 min, 80% (24/30), covering LO1-3; LO4-5 practical.
  - GLH 52, TQT 61; conflict management must be taught before Unit 4 delivery.
  - The spec is the SIA's learning outcomes, the same for every awarding body; Pearson publishes question counts and pass marks openly.
- Pearson onscreen test list (NDS-2-01 72/110, NDS-2-02 50/75, NDS-2-05 20/30, NDS-2-06 30/45): https://qualifications.pearson.com/en/support/support-topics/centre-administration/onscreen-testing/onscreen-testing-for-btec-qcf-qualifications/door-supervision.html
- SIA specifications page (door supervision specs dated 2021; refresher spec 2024): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/read-the-specifications-for-sia-licence-linked-training
- SIA review of licence-linked qualifications, launched 5 Nov 2025, new qualifications mandatory spring 2027: https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sia-launches-major-review-of-security-licence-qualifications
- First aid requirement (EFAW or FAW, valid 12 months from course start; from Oct 2024 must cover catastrophic bleeding and airways): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sia-licensing-first-aid-training-requirement/first-aid-training-requirement-for-door-supervisors-and-security-guards
- Apply for an SIA licence (18+, fee £204, most licences 3 years): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-an-sia-licence
- Refresher training needed to renew from 1 April 2025: https://www.gov.uk/government/news/changes-to-the-training-you-need-for-an-sia-licence

## Law used in questions (England and Wales)
- Private Security Industry Act 2001 (s.3 offence, summary, up to 6 months and/or fine).
- Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 s.24A (any-person arrest, indictable offences, necessity reasons, constable not practicable).
- Criminal Law Act 1967 s.3; common law self-defence and Criminal Justice and Immigration Act 2008 s.76.
- Licensing Act 2003: four objectives; s.141 sale to drunk person; s.143 failure to leave (constable must help expel on request); s.146/149 under 18s; s.149(5) 16-17 beer, wine or cider with table meal. Scotland: Licensing (Scotland) Act 2005 adds public health as a fifth objective.
- Misuse of Drugs Act 1971: classes; s.8 occupiers. Nitrous oxide Class C from 8 Nov 2023; GHB moved to Class B in 2022 (the 2021 spec still lists it as C, so no item asks GHB's class). Ketamine avoided because a reclassification review was under way.
- Criminal Justice Act 1988 s.139 (folding pocketknife, cutting edge 3 inches or less; lock knives not folding).
- Equality Act 2010 (nine protected characteristics; direct and indirect discrimination), Human Rights Act 1998, UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018.
- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974; RIDDOR 2013; Health and Safety (Safety Signs and Signals) Regulations 1996.
- Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 (Martyn's Law): Royal Assent 3 Apr 2025, standard tier 200-799, enhanced tier 800+, SIA regulator, at least 24-month implementation (Home Office factsheet https://www.protectuk.police.uk/sites/default/files/2025-09/31.187_HO_Martyns%20Law_1_pager_Web_1.pdf). One item only.
- Counter-terrorism content (threat levels, Run Hide Tell, HOT, Four Cs, 100/200/400 m, 15 m radios, SCaN, ACT, Anti-Terrorist Hotline) as listed in Unit 1 LO9 indicative content; threat level definitions per MI5 https://www.mi5.gov.uk/threat-levels
