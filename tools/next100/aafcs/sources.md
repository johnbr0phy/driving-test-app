# Sources: AAFCS Hospitality, Nutrition, and Food Science (HNFS) exam (aafcs)

Every question is original. No AAFCS sample items, Pearson preparation-manual sample questions,
Pre-PAC bulletin sample items, review-course or commercial practice questions were opened for
writing or copied. Items were written from the competency statements and the primary sources below.

What "HNFS" is: the AAFCS professional competency exam in Hospitality, Nutrition, and Food Science,
the exam behind the CFCS-HNFS credential (Certified in Family and Consumer Sciences, HNFS
concentration) and one of three AAFCS exams Texas uses for FCS teacher certification (test 201).
It is not one of the high school Pre-PAC assessments: the closest Pre-PAC test is "Nutrition, Food,
and Wellness" (70 scored items, untimed), which is a different exam and is not modelled here.

## Outline (blueprint)

- AAFCS, Testing - HNFS (exam specifications, competencies and weights), fetched 2026-11-13:
  https://www.aafcs.org/credentialing/cert-testing/testing-hnfs
  Four standards: Foundations of the FCS Profession 15%, Careers 10%, Nutrition, Wellness, and
  Food Science 40%, Hospitality, Lodging and Food Service 35%, each with its competency list.
  Four-option multiple choice, one correct answer, some unscored research items; passing score a
  scaled 240 of 300. Tigertest blueprint per 50: foundations 7, careers 5, nutrients 7, diet and
  health 7, food science and labels 6, food safety 8, food preparation 5, hospitality operations 5
  (Tigertest's split inside each standard: 12 / 20 / 18).
- AAFCS, CFCS-HNFS credential page, fetched 2026-11-13:
  https://www.aafcs.org/credentialing/professional-certifications/cfcs-hnfs
  (credential, four competency areas, baccalaureate degree required for certification, 75 PDUs
  per three-year cycle; fees not used).
- AAFCS, FCS Body of Knowledge (2025 retooled version), fetched 2026-11-13:
  https://www.aafcs.org/about/about-bok
  Core principles (human needs, individual well-being, family resilience, community vitality),
  integrative elements (human ecosystems, lifespan progression), influencing elements (capacity
  building, global interdependence, resource development and sustainability, technology and
  innovation, wellness); well-being vs wellness; micro and macro environments.
- AAFCS, What is FCS? and About us, fetched 2026-11-13:
  https://www.aafcs.org/about/what-is-fcs (people-centered science, content areas, practice settings)
  https://www.aafcs.org/about/about-aafcs/about-us (Ellen H. Richards founded the American Home
  Economics Association in 1909).

## Law, standards and primary references (bank content)

- FDA Food Code 2022 page, fetched 2026-11-13: https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2022
  and the Summary of Changes in the 2026 Food Code, fetched 2026-11-13:
  https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/summary-changes-2026-fda-food-code
  (no change to cooking, holding, cooling, reheating, date marking or handwashing figures in 2026).
  Figures used: cold hold 41°F, hot hold 135°F; poultry 165°F instantaneous; ground meat 155°F for
  17 seconds; fish and whole-muscle meat 145°F for 15 seconds; reheating for hot holding 165°F
  within 2 hours; cooling 135 to 70°F in 2 hours, 41°F within 6 hours; date marking 7 days with day
  of preparation day 1; time as a public health control 4 hours; handwashing at least 20 seconds;
  exclusion for vomiting or diarrhea; no bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat food; test kits for
  sanitizer; poisonous materials stored away from food; receiving cold TCS food at 41°F.
- FDA, Food Allergies (FASTER Act; sesame the ninth major allergen from January 1, 2023), fetched
  2026-11-13: https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies
- FDA, How to Understand and Use the Nutrition Facts Label, fetched 2026-11-13:
  https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/how-understand-and-use-nutrition-facts-label
  (5% DV low, 20% DV high, 2,000-calorie basis, added sugars DV 50 g). Ingredient order by weight
  (21 CFR 101.4) and "low sodium" 140 mg (21 CFR 101.61) from the CFR.
- Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 (January 2026), fetched 2026-11-13:
  https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/ and https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf
  Used: sodium under 2,300 mg for ages 14 and up (1,200 / 1,500 / 1,800 for 1-3 / 4-8 / 9-13);
  saturated fat not over 10% of calories; no more than 10 g added sugar per meal; protein 1.2 to
  1.6 g/kg; dairy 3, vegetables 3, fruit 2, whole grains 2 to 4 servings in a 2,000-calorie
  pattern; added-sugar names; natural sugars not added sugars; avoid added sugars in infancy;
  pregnancy priorities iron, folate, iodine; older adults protein, B12, vitamin D, calcium;
  groups who should avoid alcohol; swap deep frying for baked, broiled or grilled; water and
  unsweetened drinks. MyPlate is not used (its fit with the 2025-2030 edition is unclear).
- US Department of Education, Perkins V, fetched 2026-11-13: https://octae.ed.gov/cte/legislation/perkins-v
  (Strengthening Career and Technical Education for the 21st Century Act, signed July 31, 2018).
- FCCLA, About, fetched 2026-11-13: https://fcclainc.org/about (national CTSO for FCS education,
  intracurricular, since 1945).
- Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition (150 minutes moderate activity a week
  for adults; 60 minutes a day for ages 6 to 17): https://health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines
  (not re-fetched in this build; long-standing federal figures).
- CDC adult BMI categories (18.5 to 24.9 healthy, 25 to 29.9 overweight, 30 and over obesity):
  https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/adult-calculator/bmi-categories.html (not re-fetched in this build).
- Commission on Dietetic Registration: graduate degree required for new RDN candidates from
  January 1, 2024: https://www.cdrnet.org/graduatedegree (not re-fetched in this build).
- Hotel metrics (occupancy, ADR, RevPAR) and food cost percentage are standard industry formulas;
  every number in those items is computed in the explanation.
