# NREMT Advanced EMT (AEMT) sources

All 200 items and all ten scenarios are original, written from the official specifications, the
national scope of practice model and the national model clinical guidelines below. Nothing is copied
or reworded from NREMT sample items, the NREMT Clinical Judgment sample packet, any prep company,
textbook question bank or app.

## Outline and format (fetched 2026-11-23)
- NREMT AEMT and Paramedic Certification Examinations page:
  https://www.nremt.org/Pages/Examinations/AEMT-and-Paramedic-Certification-Examinations (fetched
  2026-11-23). New AEMT exam from July 1, 2024 (old process ended June 30, 2024; ALS psychomotor exam
  discontinued); domains and ranges: Airway, Respiration and Ventilation 9-13%, Cardiology and
  Resuscitation 11-15%, Trauma 7-11%, Medical/Obstetrics/Gynecology 25-29%, EMS Operations 6-10%,
  Clinical Judgment 31-35%, pediatric items integrated throughout; technology-enhanced items (build
  list, drag and drop, options box); exam fee $159; given at Pearson sites.
- National Registry AEMT Examination Specifications (test plan PDF):
  https://www.nremt.org/admin/getmedia/ae90d9db-c974-49e1-a43b-f1dddd4ea365/AEMT-Test-Plan_Public_final
  (fetched 2026-11-23). Built from the 2019 National EMS Practice Analysis and its 2021 addendum;
  knowledge areas per domain; clinical judgment steps (recognize cues, analyze cues, define
  hypothesis, generate solutions, take action, evaluation) plus communication and leadership; fixed-length
  linear CBT, answer each item before moving on with no return; 135 items, 35 unscored pilot items,
  3 hours at Pearson VUE; item types multiple choice (one of four), multiple response (two or three of
  five or six), build list, drag and drop, options box, graphical and scenario-based; dichotomous
  scoring.
- AEMT Candidate Handbook, About the Examination: https://www.nremt.org/Handbooks/AEMT/About-The-Examination
  (fetched 2026-11-23): 135 questions with 35 unscored, 3 hours, score scale 100 to 1500 with 950
  passing, results usually within three business days, six attempts, 15 days between attempts,
  remedial education after three failures.
- AEMT Candidate Handbook, Certification Process: https://www.nremt.org/Handbooks/AEMT/Certification-Process
  (fetched 2026-11-23): $159 fee charged for each attempt; initial courses valid two years from
  completion; recertify every two years.
- NREMT Clinical Judgment Domain Sample Packet (March 6, 2024):
  https://www.nremt.org/admin/getmedia/730156db-c05d-40e8-8003-3f033bc9c6e9/Clinical-Judgment-Domain-Sample-Packet
  (fetched 2026-11-23). Read only to learn how scenarios are laid out (En Route, Scene and Post Scene
  tabs, a scenario panel beside each item). Never a source of items; see the similarity check below.

## Scope of practice and clinical standards (fetched 2026-11-23)
- National EMS Scope of Practice Model 2019, Including Change Notices 1.0 and 2.0 (NHTSA DOT HS 813
  151, August 2021), copy hosted by the Minnesota EMS Regulatory Board:
  https://mn.gov/oems/assets/National_EMS_Scope_of_Practice_Model_2021_%20%28002%29_tcm1221-744908.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-23; ems.gov returned 403). AEMT description and skill tables: supraglottic airway,
  CPAP, waveform capnography, tracheobronchial suctioning of an intubated patient, 12-lead acquisition
  and transmission (not interpretation), mechanical CPR device, IV and IO initiation, routes (IV, IO,
  IN, IM, SC, nebulized, sublingual), IV medications limited to analgesia, antinausea/antiemetic,
  dextrose, epinephrine, glucagon, naloxone and others by state protocol, sublingual nitroglycerin,
  aspirin, nitrous oxide, auto-injector antidotes. Paramedic-only skills (intubation, needle
  decompression, manual defibrillation, pacing, cardioversion, 12-lead interpretation, other drugs) are
  used only as wrong options or scope questions.
- NASEMSO National Model EMS Clinical Guidelines, Version 3.0 (March 2022), copy hosted by Iowa HHS:
  https://hhs.iowa.gov/media/198/download (fetched 2026-11-23; the nasemso.org PDF link returned an
  HTML page). A search on 2026-11-23 found V4 still in the input stage, so V3.0 is current. Used for:
  hypoglycemia (treat at 60 mg/dL or less, oral glucose 25 g, D10 preferred, 25 g field maximum, glucagon
  1 mg or 0.5 mg under 20 kg, insulin pump rule), opioid poisoning (airway and ventilation before naloxone,
  needle hazard, no vapor risk), anaphylaxis (epinephrine 0.3 mg IM at 25 kg or more, 0.15 mg under,
  repeat every 5 to 15 minutes, 20 mL/kg fluid, albuterol 2.5 to 5 mg for wheezing), chest pain (aspirin
  162 to 325 mg chewable non-enteric-coated, nitroglycerin 0.4 mg SL every 3 to 5 minutes if SBP over 100,
  no nitrates within 48 hours of a PDE5 inhibitor), cardiac arrest (30:2, 15:2 two-rescuer child, one
  breath every 6 seconds with an advanced airway, ETCO2 to monitor compressions), airway (breath every
  2 to 3 seconds in children, 6 to 8 mL/kg, EtCO2 35 to 45, permissive SpO2 of 90 percent or more in
  COPD), shock and sepsis (MAP = (2 x diastolic + systolic) / 3, MAP goal 65, up to 30 mL/kg in boluses),
  ventricular assist devices, general trauma (SBP target 90, head injury above 110, pediatric 20 mL/kg),
  extremity hemorrhage and amputated parts (dry in a bag, bag on ice), crush injury (saline before
  release, no lactated Ringer's), burns (dry dressing, Rule of 10), spinal care (collar criteria, no
  collar for penetrating neck injury, car-seat extrication), suspected stroke (last known well, glucose,
  severity screen signs, head up 15 to 30 degrees when SBP over 100, bring the witness), seizures (more
  than 5 minutes), childbirth (nuchal cord, prolapsed cord, breech, shoulder dystocia with McRoberts),
  neonatal resuscitation (ventilate under 100, compressions under 60 at 3:1), bites and envenomation
  (no tourniquet, ice, incision or suction), agitated patient (never prone), burns and smoke (high-flow
  oxygen for enclosed-space fires), ondansetron and prolonged QT, and the 2022 National Guideline for the
  Field Triage of Injured Patients reproduced as an appendix.

## Copyright and similarity check (2026-11-23)
The NREMT AEMT sample items site (AEMT-SampleItems.startpractice.com) is a script-only page and could
not be read; the test plan's Appendix A sample items are images. The Clinical Judgment sample packet
(26 image slides) was rendered and read. Its scenario is a patient in a dentist's office with an
insulin pump who is hypoglycemic after fasting, plus single items on a fall with raised intracranial
pressure, exertional chest pain and a capnography waveform after an argument. Our first draft had a
hypoglycemia scenario (insulin, skipped breakfast) and a capnography item on an anxious patient
breathing fast; both were replaced (a COPD scenario and a DKA capnography item). No item asks about a
patient fasting for a procedure, raised intracranial pressure or a waveform after an argument.

Every item was also compared with data/v2/emt, data/v2/paramedic, data/v2/nclex, data/v2/nclexpn and the
CPR, firefighter and first aid banks (stem word overlap over 0.35, or over 0.2 with the same key). Items
that repeated a sibling's fact and answer were rewritten with a different fact: aspirin dose (now the
preferred form), tourniquet steps (now wound packing), Parkland-style burn numbers (now Rule of 10 with
other weights), refusal steps, CPAP contraindication (now how CPAP works), ROSC signs (now obvious death),
SALT interventions (now ICS staging), nuchal cord (now breech), pelvic binder (now car-seat extrication),
fundal massage (now shoulder dystocia), delivery steps (now IV setup), nitroglycerin matrix (now 12-lead
selection), tension pneumothorax signs (now occlusive dressings), AED steps (now CPAP setup), ectopic
pregnancy (now miscarriage tissue), red-light intersections (now backing with a spotter), PCR error
correction (now objective wording), aortic aneurysm (now GI bleeding), stoma ventilation (now home
ventilator), febrile seizure (now sickle cell chest pain), standing orders (now the medical director),
delirium tremens (now intoxication mimics), START sorting and categories (now SALT), newborn steps (now
heart rate check).

## Blueprint
Per 75-item practice test, inside the NREMT ranges: airway 8 (10.7%), cardiology 10 (13.3%), trauma 7
(9.3%), medical, obstetrics and gynecology 20 (26.7%), EMS operations 6 (8%), clinical judgment 24 (32%,
four scenarios of six items). Pools (tests 1, 2 and drill-only): 8/8/6, 10/10/6, 7/7/6, 20/20/14, 6/6/6,
24/24/12 = 200. 75 items in 100 minutes keeps the real pace (135 in 180).

## Facts deliberately avoided
Fees inside questions, state-specific scope (every scope item follows the national model and says
"per local protocol" or "state law" where states differ), resuscitation drug doses, pediatric dextrose
by concentration other than D10, nebulized epinephrine for stridor, analgesic doses, TXA (not in the AEMT
column), field triage criteria printed only in the appendix image, and any 2025 AHA guideline change
that could not be read (cpr.heart.org returned 403).
