# AFOQT: sources

Every question, passage and table in this bank is original, written by Tigertest from the U.S. Air
Force's own description of the AFOQT subtests and from the primary science sources below. No
official AFOQT item, sample question from any AFOQT pamphlet, released form or commercial prep
item was used, quoted or paraphrased. Reading passages are invented practice texts; Table Reading
grids are generated numbers.

## Format (Form T, as the Air Force publishes it)
- Pearson VUE, "Air Force Officer Qualifying Test (AFOQT)" (the Air Force's test delivery page,
  Test Sponsor: U.S. Air Force): https://www.pearsonvue.com/us/en/afoqt.html (fetched 2026-11-14).
  516 multiple-choice questions, about five hours with breaks, every question must be answered.
  Subtests in order with time and items: Verbal Analogies 8 min/25, Arithmetic Reasoning 29/25,
  Word Knowledge 5/25, Math Knowledge 22/25, Reading Comprehension 24/25, Situational Judgment
  35/16, a 15-minute break, Self-Description Inventory 45/240 (not graded), Physical Science 10/20,
  Table Reading 7/40, Instrument Comprehension 5/25, Block Counting 5/30, Aviation Information
  8/20. Results are not raw percentages; subtests combine into composites for quantitative,
  verbal, academic aptitude, pilot, combat systems officer and air battle manager. Tests limited
  to two (three with an approved waiver). Free; voucher from a recruiter or test control officer.
  Content notes per subtest: Arithmetic Reasoning word problems (geometry, proportions, ratios,
  mixtures, integers, percentages, time and distance); Math Knowledge (algebra, geometry,
  arithmetic, inequalities, equations, absolute values, exponents); Physical Science (high-school
  forces, physical laws, simple machines); Table Reading (reading simple x-y tables); Aviation
  Information (flight mechanics, aircraft types, flight physics).
- Air Force ROTC Detachment 485 (Rutgers), "AFOQT": https://afrotc.rutgers.edu/afoqt
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Same 12 subtests, times and counts; scores are 1 to 99 percentiles against
  other examinees, not percent correct. Minimums Verbal 15 and Quantitative 10 (citing DAFMAN
  36-2664); rated candidates need more (chart not readable). Up to three attempts, retests no
  earlier than 90 days apart (citing AFROTCI 36-2011V3); the best composite across attempts is the
  score of record. Links the DAF-approved Form T preparation page on access.afpc.af.mil.
- AFPC, "AFOQT Information Pamphlet" (Form S era, file metadata 2005 to 2009, copy hosted by USF
  AFROTC): https://www.usf.edu/undergrad/documents/air-force-rotc/afoqt-information-pamphlet.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Used only for the answer format: multiple choice with four or five
  answers, one best answer, no penalty for guessing; Verbal Analogies "complete the analogy",
  Word Knowledge "closest in meaning to the capitalized word", Arithmetic Reasoning "most nearly
  correct" of five. Its sample questions were not used. Its composite table is for the older form
  (Navigator-Technical, Rotated Blocks, Hidden Figures, General Science) and is not used.

## Content sources
- NASA Glenn Research Center, Beginners Guide to Aeronautics:
  https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/four-forces-on-an-airplane/ (fetched
  2026-11-14): weight, lift, drag, thrust and their directions; balanced cruise.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/aircraft-rotations/ (fetched
  2026-11-14): roll, pitch and yaw axes through the center of gravity; ailerons, elevator, rudder.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/airplane-parts-function/ (fetched
  2026-11-14): wings, winglets, flaps, slats, spoilers, stabilizers, elevator, rudder, fuselage,
  engines.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/lift-equation/ (fetched 2026-11-14):
  L = Cl x density x V squared / 2 x area; dynamic pressure.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/factors-that-affect-lift/ (fetched
  2026-11-14): shape, size, velocity, inclination, mass of the flow, viscosity, compressibility.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/drag/ (fetched 2026-11-14): skin
  friction, form drag, induced drag and wingtip vortices; long thin wings have low induced drag.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/glide-angle/ (fetched 2026-11-14):
  tan(glide angle) = height / distance, related to the drag to lift ratio.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/turbine-engines/ (fetched 2026-11-14):
  thrust as reaction to accelerated gas; turbine engines need surrounding air; T-38 turbojets,
  C-130 turboprops, high-bypass turbofans for transports, afterburning low-bypass turbofans.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/earth-atmosphere-equation-english/
  (fetched 2026-11-14): troposphere to about 36,000 ft with temperature falling, pressure and
  density falling with altitude; standard sea level 59 F.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/aircraft-center-of-gravity/ (fetched
  2026-11-14): cg as the weighted average of component weights times distances; the aircraft
  rotates about the cg.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/bernoullis-equation/ (fetched
  2026-11-14): static plus dynamic pressure equals total pressure; pitot-static tube airspeed.
- https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/newtons-laws-of-motion/ (fetched
  2026-11-14): Newton's three laws, F = ma, action and reaction.
- NIST, "Definitions of the SI base units": https://www.nist.gov/si-redefinition/definitions-si-base-units
  (fetched 2026-11-14): the seven base units and their quantities.
- Physical Science items otherwise use standard high-school relations stated in the item (work =
  force x distance, power = work / time, kinetic and potential energy, mechanical advantage,
  density, pressure, Ohm's law, wave speed = frequency x wavelength). OpenStax Physics
  (openstax.org) was tried as a textbook reference and returned no table of contents.

## Left out on purpose
- Situational Judgment (16 items keyed by officer panels; no published key to write against),
  Instrument Comprehension, Block Counting (figures), and the Self-Description Inventory (not
  graded). The landing names all four.
- Airport signs, airspace, regulations and current aircraft inventories in Aviation Information:
  the FAA handbooks (faa.gov) returned 403, so aviation items stay on the flight physics and
  aircraft parts above.
- Composite formulas, percentile conversions and rated minimums (not readable from an official
  page), and anything about the test itself (counts, times, retests) in a question.
