# Sources for `afpsat` (AFP Service Aptitude Test)

All 200 questions are original. Reviewer sites and "AFPSAT past questions" pages turned up in
search results; none were opened for their questions and nothing was copied. Checked 2026-11-05.

## What could and could not be confirmed

The AFP's own sites could not be read this session: `afpsat.afp.mil.ph` does not resolve from this
network, `army.mil.ph` returned HTTP 403, `paf.mil.ph` HTTP 503. The AFP does not publish the
AFPSAT's item count, time limit, subtest split or passing score in any source found, so those are
**not stated as fact** anywhere in the bank, the landing or `examFacts`.

### Authoritative or near-authoritative (checked 2026-11-05)
- Gamos, J. N. M. and Usona, L. P. (2024). "Impact of the Respondent's Demographic Profile in
  Passing the Armed Forces of the Philippines Service Aptitude Test: An Analysis". Graduate Forum,
  Vol. 18 No. 1. https://ejournals.ph/article.php?id=35283
  - Abstract: the AFP uses the AFPSAT as "the first filter to ensure a high service aptitude within
    its ranks"; 530 applicants from all 17 regions in the first semester of 2023.
  - Reference list names the governing AFP circulars: AFP Circular 01 "AFPSAT" (10 January 2013),
    AFP Circular 02 "AFP Service Aptitude Test" (10 April 2015), AFP Circular 04 "AFPSAT System"
    (30 June 2020). Their contents were not available.
- RMN Networks, "Iskedyul ng AFPSAT sa Rehiyon Dos, Inanunsyo ng AFP" (22 April 2025).
  https://rmn.ph/iskedyul-ng-afpsat-sa-rehiyon-dos-inanunsyo-ng-afp
  - The AFP announced AFPSAT dates in Cagayan Valley (May and October 2025) alongside the Army
    Qualifying Examination (AQE) and the Special Written Examination (SWE) for Officer Candidate
    Course (OCC) and Candidate Soldier Course (CSC) applicants; applicants bring original documents
    (diploma or certificate, TOR or Form 137/138, PSA birth certificate, a government ID) and are
    told to contact the nearest Army Recruitment Office.
- Philippine Information Agency, Ilocos Sur recruitment drive (8 September 2023), as quoted in search
  results (page returned HTTP 503 when fetched): 60 applicants took the AFPSAT while 43 took the AQE
  and SWE. Shows the AFPSAT is the common entry test and the AQE/SWE are separate Army tests.

### Guides only (labelled "guides say" wherever used)
- supertutor.ph AFPSAT 2026 guide
  (https://supertutor.ph/resources/blog/gov-eligibility/afpsat-2026-complete-guide/), read
  2026-11-05: the AFPSAT is the unified written entrance test for officer and enlisted applicants
  to the Army, Navy and Air Force; the AFP publishes no item count, time limit or passing score;
  each service sets its own cut-off; coverage described by reviewers as verbal, numerical, logical
  and abstract reasoning, spatial and mechanical reasoning, and general information; slots come from
  each service's testing office after online registration; it quotes the AFPSAT portal on a
  six-month wait before a retake and on services being free.
- philippinego.com and open-exam-prep.com (search snippets): "150-item" test, "about 1 hour 20
  minutes". Not corroborated; not used as fact.
- Older Army recruitment notices quoted in search results (2019): Candidate Soldier Course
  applicants 18 to 26, single, at least 5 feet tall. Not current; not used.

## Tigertest's model (decisions, not facts about the real test)
- Four 50-question mock tests, 200 questions, every item used once (no spares), 70% target. The
  70% is Tigertest's target, not an AFP passing score.
- Split per test (Tigertest's own, following the coverage areas guides describe): verbal 17
  (vocabulary 7, grammar 6, reading 4), numerical 12 (number 6, problems 6), logical 7, abstract
  and spatial 4, general information 10 (Constitution and national defense 6, AFP, history and
  geography 4).
- Abstract reasoning on the real test uses figures; here it is written as number and letter series,
  codes, and spatial items described in words. The landing says so.

## Facts used in general-information items
- 1987 Constitution, checked 2026-11-05 at https://www.lawphil.net/consti/cons1987.html (the Official
  Gazette copy returned HTTP 403):
  Art. I (national territory, archipelagic waters); Art. II Sec. 3 (civilian authority supreme;
  AFP the protector of the people and the State; goal to secure sovereignty and integrity of the
  national territory), Sec. 4 (prime duty to serve and protect the people; Government may call
  upon the people to defend the State and require personal military or civil service), Sec. 2
  (renounces war as an instrument of national policy), Sec. 8 (freedom from nuclear weapons);
  Art. VI Sec. 23 (two-thirds of both Houses in joint session voting separately declare a state of
  war; emergency powers); Art. VII Sec. 18 (President as Commander-in-Chief; calling out the armed
  forces; 48-hour report to Congress; Congress may revoke by majority of all its Members voting
  jointly; Supreme Court review within 30 days), Sec. 16 (officers from colonel or naval captain
  need Commission on Appointments consent); Art. VII Sec. 21 (treaties need concurrence of two-thirds
  of all Members of the Senate); Art. XVI Sec. 4 (citizen armed force with military training; all
  citizens to undergo military training), Sec. 5 (oath to uphold and defend the Constitution;
  professionalism; insulated from partisan politics; no member engaged in partisan political
  activity except to vote; no active member appointed to a civilian post including GOCCs; Chief of
  Staff tour of duty not more than three years, extendable by the President in war or other
  national emergency declared by Congress; officers and men recruited proportionately from all
  provinces and cities as far as practicable); Art. XVIII Sec. 25 (foreign military bases, troops
  or facilities only under a treaty concurred in by the Senate).
- R.A. 8491, Flag and Heraldic Code of the Philippines (1998), checked 2026-11-05 at
  https://www.lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8491_1998.html: Section 10, red field on top
  in time of war, blue on top in peacetime. (A later revision of the code, if enacted, was not
  checked.)
- Commonwealth Act No. 1, National Defense Act (21 December 1935), which created the Philippine
  Army of the Commonwealth (AFP anniversary 21 December). General reference; not checked this session.
- R.A. 7077, Citizen Armed Forces of the Philippines Reservist Act (1991), checked 2026-11-05 at
  https://www.lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1991/ra_7077_1991.html: Section 12, First Category
  18 to 35, Second 36 to 51, Third above 51; Section 13, the Ready Reserve (mostly First Category)
  reinforces the regular force in war, national emergencies and local crises, the Standby Reserve
  is mobilized only in a national emergency or war, the Retired Reserve.
- AFP structure: Department of National Defense; General Headquarters at Camp Aguinaldo, Quezon
  City; three major services (Philippine Army, Philippine Navy with the Philippine Marine Corps,
  Philippine Air Force); Chief of Staff, AFP as the highest-ranking officer; core values Honor,
  Service and Patriotism; Philippine Military Academy at Fort Del Pilar, Baguio City; Medal of
  Valor as the highest military award; the Coast Guard under the Department of Transportation. General
  reference, not checked against an AFP page this session (the AFP sites did not load).
- History (general reference, not checked this session): Battle of Tirad Pass (2 December 1899,
  Gregorio del Pilar); fall of Bataan (9 April 1942, Araw ng Kagitingan, in a reading passage);
  Leyte landing (20 October 1944) and Lingayen Gulf (January 1945); PEFTOK in Korea (1950 to
  1955) and PHILCAG in Vietnam.
- Geography (general reference, not checked this session): Mount Apo highest peak, Mount Pulag
  highest in Luzon; Cagayan River longest river, Agusan longest in Mindanao.

## Fact-check pass (2026-11-05)

Each general-information and passage fact below was checked on 2026-11-05.

- 1987 Constitution, re-read at https://www.lawphil.net/consti/cons1987.html (2026-11-05): Art. VI
  Sec. 23(1) "by a vote of two-thirds of both Houses in joint session assembled, voting separately";
  Art. VII Sec. 18: report within forty-eight hours; Congress, voting jointly, by a majority of all
  its Members may revoke; the Supreme Court decides within thirty days of filing; martial law does
  not "authorize the conferment of jurisdiction on military courts and agencies over civilians where
  civil courts are able to function". Confirms AFPSAT-CIV items on war powers and martial law.
- AFP core values Honor, Service and Patriotism (AFPSAT-AFP-005): Presidential Communications
  Office release and press reports of the President's December 2024 AFP anniversary speech
  ("the values that define the AFP: Honor, Service, and Patriotism"), https://mirror.pco.gov.ph/?p=201698
  and https://ibctv13.com/pbbm-to-afp-keep-upholding-honor-service-patriotism/ (2026-11-05).
- Medal of Valor highest AFP award, Gold Cross below it (AFPSAT-AFP-006): AFP order of precedence as
  given at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Philippines_Medal_of_Valor and
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Cross_(Philippines) (secondary; AFP sites unreachable, 2026-11-05).
- Coast Guard (AFPSAT-AFP-001): R.A. 9993, Philippine Coast Guard Law of 2009, establishes the PCG as an
  armed and uniformed service attached to the DOTC (now the Department of Transportation), attached
  to the DND only in a war declared by Congress. https://chanrobles.com/republicacts/republicactno9993.php
  (2026-11-05).
- Marine Corps under the Navy (AFPSAT-AFP-002): Philippine Marine Corps official site,
  https://marinecorps.mil.ph/marine-forces ("naval amphibious force"); 2018 bills to separate it were
  not enacted (ABS-CBN, 21 March 2018). Checked 2026-11-05.

  under the reorganized DILG, https://batasnatin.com/laws/ra-6975 (2026-11-05).
- Camp Aguinaldo AFP GHQ, Camp Crame PNP national headquarters, across EDSA in Quezon City
  (AFPSAT-AFP-004): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Aguinaldo and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Crame
  (secondary, 2026-11-05).
