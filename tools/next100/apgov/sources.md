# AP U.S. Government and Politics (`apgov`): sources

All 201 items are original. No College Board released exam, Course and Exam Description sample question,
AP Classroom item or commercial prep item was used or paraphrased. Stimuli (27):
- Foundational document excerpts (7): Federalist Nos. 39, 51, 70 and 78, Brutus No. 1,
  the Emancipation Proclamation and Adam Smith's The Wealth of Nations, all public domain, lightly adapted
  (passages cut with "...", spelling modernized) and labelled "(adapted excerpt)". "Letter from a Birmingham
  Jail" is still under copyright and is described, never quoted.
- Practice texts (3): a student editorial, a political scientist's overview and a campaign memo, written for
  Tigertest and labelled "(practice text)".
- Data tables (11): hypothetical data, every title says "hypothetical data for practice". No real survey,
  turnout, veto or confirmation figure is asserted.
- Visuals (6): a map, two cartoons and three infographics, described in words (no images).
Required cases and laws named in questions are standard course content, described in our own words.

## Facts used in items (standard, stable)
- Senate cloture on legislation: three-fifths of senators duly chosen and sworn (Senate Rule XXII).
- Pocket veto: bill unsigned when Congress adjourns within ten days (Sundays excepted), U.S. Constitution Art. I, Sec. 7.
- Line-item veto struck down in Clinton v. City of New York (1998).
- Winner-take-all in 48 states and DC; Maine and Nebraska allocate by district.
- National Voter Registration Act of 1993 (motor voter); Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act of 2002; Hatch Act;
  Title VII (Civil Rights Act of 1964); Title IX (1972).
No officeholders, no party control of any branch, no dollar figures, no current-affairs items.

## Modelling choices
- Section I only, full length and pace: 55 questions in 80 minutes, one module, two practice tests, Test 2 held
  out of the drills. The free-response section is not modelled.
- Per test, in unit order: Unit 1 10 (18%), Unit 2 17 (31%: Congress and the presidency 9, courts and the
  bureaucracy 8), Unit 3 9 (16%), Unit 4 7 (13%), Unit 5 12 (22%: voting and parties 6, campaigns, groups and
  media 6). All inside the CED ranges. The real exam mixes units; Tigertest keeps them in order so each set stays
  together and the score by unit reads cleanly.
- Per test: 30 individual questions, five data sets (3, 2, 3, 2, 2 = 12), two text sets (4 on a foundational
  document, 3 on a practice text = 7) and three visual sets of 2 (6), matching the CED counts.
- Drill-only: 13 per domain (one set of 3 plus 10 individual).
- Score is percent correct.

people" as popular sovereignty (GED's "We the People", now the course's reason for listing the Gettysburg

override and amendment ratification fractions, Senate-only powers, Emancipation Proclamation scope, the
Civil Rights Act's commerce basis, Schenck, the War Powers Resolution, the Great Compromise, senator counts and terms.
