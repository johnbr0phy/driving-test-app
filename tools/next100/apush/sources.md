# AP U.S. History (`apush`): sources

All 201 items and all 51 sources (stimuli) are original. Every primary-style source is a practice text
written for Tigertest in the style of its time; its label ("A Boston merchant's letter, 1768 (practice
text)") describes the kind of writer, not a real person, and no real document is quoted. Historians'
interpretations are written for Tigertest too. No College Board released exam, Course and Exam
Description sample question, AP Classroom item or commercial prep item was used or paraphrased.
Laws, court cases, events and people named in questions are standard course content, described in our
own words.

## Format (checked 1 November 2026)
- College Board, AP Central, "AP United States History Exam":
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-united-states-history/exam
  - "a fully digital exam" in the Bluebook app.
  - Section I Part A: 55 multiple-choice questions, 55 minutes, 40%; "usually in sets of 3-4 questions",
    using primary and secondary sources, images, graphs and maps.
  - Part B: 3 short-answer questions, 40 minutes, 20%. Section II: document-based question (7 documents,
    1 hour recommended including a 15-minute reading period), 25%; long essay, 40 minutes, 15%.
  - The short-answer and long-essay questions are updated effective with the May 2027 exam ("give back
    time, reposition choice within the questions"); course content is unchanged. The page does not change
    the multiple-choice section. Exam date May 7, 2027 (not stated on the landing).
- College Board, "AP U.S. History Course and Exam Description", effective fall 2026 (c) 2026:
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-us-history-course-and-exam-description.pdf
  (downloaded 1 November 2026)
  - Exam overview (p. 497): 3 hours 15 minutes; 55 MCQ, 3 SAQ, 1 DBQ, 1 LEQ; weights and timing as above.
  - Unit weightings (p. 498): Period 1 (1491-1607) 4-6%; Period 2 (1607-1754) 6-8%; Periods 3 (1754-1800),
    4 (1800-1848), 5 (1844-1877), 6 (1865-1898), 7 (1890-1945), 8 (1945-1980) 10-17% each; Period 9
    (1980-present) 4-6%.
  - Section I Part A (p. 499): "55 multiple-choice questions typically appearing in sets of three to four
    questions, each with one or more stimuli, including primary texts, secondary texts, images ...,
    charts or other quantitative data, and maps"; "at least one set of paired text-based stimuli (in
    either the multiple-choice questions or one of the short-answer questions)".
  - Skills (p. 501): MCQs assess Skills 1 to 5 (developments and processes, sourcing and situation, claims
    and evidence in sources, contextualization, making connections through comparison, causation and
    continuity and change); argumentation is not assessed in the multiple choice.
  - Sample multiple-choice questions show four choices (A) to (D).
- No penalty for wrong answers: AP Students FAQ, "Will I lose points if I answer a multiple-choice question
  incorrectly?": https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/faqs/will-i-lose-points-if-i-answer-multiple-choice-question-incorrectly
- AP scores are reported 1 to 5; the composite-to-score conversion is set each year and not published as a
  fixed percent. Tigertest's goals are its own targets.

Not confirmed, so not stated: how many questions of each skill appear, and how many sets are paired texts

## Modelling choices
- Part A only, full length and pace: 55 questions in 55 minutes, one module, two practice tests,
  Test 2 held out of the drills. The short-answer, DBQ and long essay are not modelled.
- Per test, in chronological order: Period 1 3, Period 2 4 (domain `apushP12`, 7), Period 3 7, Period 4 8,
  Period 5 8, Period 6 7, Period 7 8, Period 8 7 and Period 9 3 (domain `apushP89`, 10). As shares of 55:
  5.5%, 7.3%, 12.7 to 14.5% each, 5.5%, inside every CED range.
- Every test item is in a stimulus set of 3 or 4 (15 sets per test); one set per test is two historians'
  interpretations (Test 1 causes of the Civil War, Test 2 the New Deal), and each test has one table.
  Drill-only items: per domain three sets (10 items) and three stand-alone items.
- Images, cartoons and maps are not modelled (text and small tables only).
- Tables use rounded figures, labelled as rounded (checked 1 November 2026):
  - Enslaved population, U.S. census totals: 1800 893,602; 1820 1,538,022; 1840 2,487,355, shown as 0.9, 1.5
    and 2.5 million. Confirmed in the 1800 column of the NCpedia census table
    (https://www.ncpedia.org/sites/default/files/documents/nchist_total-pop-til-1860.pdf) and the 1820 and 1840
    census articles citing the Census Bureau; some compilations give 1,529,012 (1820) and 2,482,798 (1840), which
    round the same.
  - Shares of European immigrants by region. 1881-1890, from the Treasury Bureau of Statistics table in The
    Statesman's Year-Book 1899, American edition, p. xcv
    (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Statesman%27s_Year-Book_1899_American_Edition.djvu/117): England and
    Wales 657,320, Scotland 149,809, Ireland 655,482, Germany 1,452,970, Norway and Sweden 568,362, Denmark
    88,132, Switzerland 81,988, Netherlands 53,701, France 50,464 (northern and western 3,758,396) against
    Austria-Hungary 353,719, Italy 307,309, Russia and Poland 265,088 (926,116): 80% and 20%. 1901-1910, from the
    DHS Yearbook "Immigration by region and selected country of last residence" decade series (as reproduced in
    the gnuplot demo data immigration.dat): northern and western Europe 1,910,035 of 7,387,494 European
    immigrants, about 26%; against the larger Europe total of some editions (8.1 million) about 24%. Shown as
    "about 80%" and "about 25%", with the table labelled approximate and rounded.
- Score is percent correct.
