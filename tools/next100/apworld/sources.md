# AP World History: Modern (`apworld`): sources

All 200 items and all 49 sources (stimuli) are original. Every primary-style source is a practice text
written for Tigertest in the style of its time; its label ("A Portuguese captain's report to his king,
1510s (practice text)") describes the kind of writer, not a real person, and no real document is quoted.
Historians' interpretations are written for Tigertest too. No College Board released exam, Course and
Exam Description sample question, AP Classroom item or commercial prep item was used or paraphrased.
Events, people, treaties and terms named in questions are standard course content, described in our own
words.

## Modelling choices
- Part A only, full length and pace: 55 questions in 55 minutes, one module, two practice tests,
  Test 2 held out of the drills. The short-answer, DBQ and long essay are not modelled.
- Per test, in chronological order: Units 1 and 2 5 each (domain `apworldU12`, 10), Unit 3 8, Unit 4 7,
  Unit 5 8, Unit 6 7, Units 7, 8 and 9 5 each (domain `apworldU789`, 15). As shares of 55: 9.1% for each
  small unit (range 8-10%), 12.7% or 14.5% for Units 3 to 6 (range 12-15%), inside every CED range.
  Units 7 to 9 share one domain so that stimulus sets can run across unit lines (a 1940s source asked about
  both the war and decolonization).
- Every test item is in a stimulus set of 3 or 4 (15 sets per test); one set per test is two historians'
  interpretations (Test 1 causes of the Haitian Revolution, Test 2 the rise of the gunpowder empires), and
  each test has one table (Test 1 the land-based empires and world population, Test 2 the Atlantic slave
  trade). Drill-only items: per domain three sets plus three stand-alone items (Units 7 to 9: four sets
  plus four stand-alone items).
- Images, cartoons and maps are not modelled (text and small tables only).
- Tables (checked 1 November 2026):
  - Enslaved Africans embarked, by period, rounded to 0.3, 1.9, 6.5 and 3.9 million: SlaveVoyages
    Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database estimates (https://www.slavevoyages.org/assessment/estimates):
    1501-1600 277,506; 1601-1700 1,875,631; 1701-1800 6,494,619; 1801-1866 3,873,580, which sum to the
    database's published total of 12,521,336 embarked. Reviewed 1 November 2026 against Boston University
    African Studies Center, "Global African Diaspora of Slave Origin", Table 1 (quarter-century totals from
    the SlaveVoyages estimates, https://www.bu.edu/africa/files/2023/11/Slave-Trade-Tables.docx.pdf), whose
    rows sum to 277,505 (1501-1600), 1,875,631 (1601-1700), 6,494,618 (1701-1800) and 3,873,581 (1801 on,
    the estimates ending in 1866), total 12,521,335; the one-unit differences are rounding between versions.
    The rounded 0.3, 1.9, 6.5 and 3.9 million are confirmed. The last row covers only 66 years, which the
    explanation of the "smaller total" item says.
  - World population, rounded: 1900 about 1.6 billion (UN, "The World at Six Billion", 1999, gives 1.65),
    1950 about 2.5 billion and 2000 about 6.1 billion (UN DESA, World Population Prospects,
    https://population.un.org/wpp/).
  - The land-based empires table is descriptive (ruling group, faith of the rulers, most subjects), standard
    course content.
- Score is percent correct.
- Key position spread: the build seeds each option shuffle from the stem plus a fixed salt ("h"), chosen
  so the keys spread evenly over A to D (54/47/48/51 after the 1 November review).

## Review (1 November 2026)
Sources rewritten because their wording or set-up echoed a famous real text: T1-S01 (Ibn Battuta on Mali's
Friday mosque and safe roads), T2-S02 (his generous sultan of Kilwa), T2-S03 (Bernal Diaz's first view of
Tenochtitlan), T1-S04 (Busbecq's "no distinction of birth"), D-S06 (Leo Africanus on Timbuktu's book trade),
T2-S07 (the 1635 sakoku edict, also moved to the 1640s since the Dutch went to Dejima only in 1641),
T2-S08 (Bolivar's Jamaica Letter of 1815, now a creole lawyer's council speech of 1810), D-S10 (Sieyes,
"What is the Third Estate?"), T2-S10 (Lin Zexu's letter to Queen Victoria), T2-S11 (Machemba's reply of 1890).
Period fixes: T2-S01 Cordoba (Castilian from 1236) replaced by Damascus; T1-S07 silver came from mines to the
east of China, not the west; T1-S12 dated 1915 (the Indian infantry left the Western Front at the end of
1915); D-S15 frozen meat (Argentine chilled beef came after 1900) and "young" for Argentina, not "small";
T2-S09 child hours made consistent with the 1833 Factory Act.

## Overlap check (1 November 2026)
Grepped the AP U.S. History, AP Psychology, AP Biology, GED, SAT, PSAT, ACT and Praxis banks and the US
civics bank for the same facts. Rewritten to avoid repeating them: why Britain industrialized first (GED
asks the same with coal and iron as the key; replaced by an urbanization item). Kept clear of the APUSH
Columbian Exchange source (maize and potatoes in Europe, horses on the Plains, smallpox): the drill source
here covers maize and cassava in Africa, sweet potatoes in China and sugar in the Caribbean. No item asks
a question the APUSH bank asks (mercantilism appears in both, as a definition here and through the
Navigation Acts there).
