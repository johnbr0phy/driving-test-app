# ASWB Bachelors bank sources

All 200 items are original, written for Tigertest from the outline and primary sources below. None is
copied from the ASWB guidebook's sample questions, ASWB's online practice test or any commercial prep
product.

## Content outline and format (exam owner)

- ASWB, "ASWB Examination Guidebook" (footer "08/2026"; exams from August 3, 2026),
  https://www.aswb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-Social-Work-Licensing-Exam-Guidebook-5.4.2026.pdf ,
  fetched 2026-11-19 as PDF and read as text. It gave:
  - Category table: the Bachelors exam requires a bachelor's degree in social work and covers "basic
    generalist practice of baccalaureate social work". The Associate and Bachelors exams share the same
    questions and content outline.
  - Bachelors/Associate content outline (guidebook pages 36 to 42): I. Values and Ethics 35% (IA Ethical
    principles and responsibilities, IB Ethical service delivery, IC Diversity and social justice);
    II. Assessment and Planning 33% (IIA Assessment concepts, IIB Assessment methods and techniques,
    IIC Assessment practices); III. Intervention and Practice 32% (IIIA Practice concepts, IIIB
    Intervention methods and techniques, IIIC Practice evaluation and research, IIID Supervision and
    administration), with the applied knowledge statements under each.
  - Format, the same for every category: two sections of 61 multiple-choice questions, 122 in all, of
    which 12 are unscored pretest questions and 110 count; four hours on a Pearson computer; "The exam
    may contain both three- and four-option questions."; free movement within a section, flagging and
    review.
  - Scoring: pass/fail, with the number of questions needed to pass set for each exam form; a failing
    report shows correct answers by content area.
  - Cognitive levels: the Associate and Bachelors exams have a larger share of recall questions than the
    Masters, Advanced Generalist and Clinical exams.
  - The guidebook's Bachelors sample questions (rapport with an anxious client, a couple discussing
    assisted living, unfinished tasks, a silent student, hopelessness in a housing complex) were read so
    that no item here repeats them.
  - Fees and the retake wait are in the guidebook but are not used.
- ASWB, "Blueprints for the 2026 competence assessments reflect significant structural modifications,
  minor content changes" (February 20, 2025), https://www.aswb.org/2026-blueprints/ , fetched
  2026-11-19: the move from four content areas to three, with values and ethics given the highest
  percentage in every category.

## Not modelled, and choices

- Three-option questions: every item here has four options.
- No fees, retake waits, state licensing rules or state reporting details; mandated reporting is asked
  only at the level all states share (reasonable suspicion, good-faith immunity, the agency
  investigates). Medical aid in dying, minors' consent ages and other state-by-state rules are left out.
- Tigertest's 70% pass line is a readiness target, not ASWB's per-form passing score.

## Lanes

Written against the ASWB Masters (`data/aswb-masters-questions.json`), ASWB Clinical
(`data/aswb-clinical-questions.json`), NCE (`data/nce-questions.json`), ADC (`data/adc-questions.json`)
and RBT (`data/rbt-questions.json`) banks. Every key was compared with their keys and every stem with
their stems; 32 first-draft items that repeated a fact and answer there (triangulation, homeostasis,
delirium, conservation, the Strange Situation, projection, displacement, Walker's cycle, open
questions, thought process, PHQ-9, SOAP notes, distress tolerance, disenfranchised and anticipatory
grief, test-retest reliability, palliative care, the U visa, medical aid in dying, the labor
trafficking passport sign and others) were replaced.
