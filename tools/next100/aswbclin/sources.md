# ASWB Clinical bank sources

- ASWB, "ASWB Examination Guidebook" (2026 Social Work Licensing Exam Guidebook, footer "08/2026";
  the cover note says it was published in August 2026 and reflects exams administered beginning
  August 3, 2026). Downloaded and read as PDF text on 2026-11-04 from
  https://www.aswb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-Social-Work-Licensing-Exam-Guidebook-5.4.2026.pdf .
  - Category table: the Clinical exam is for the "practice of clinical social work requiring the
    application of specialized clinical knowledge and advanced clinical skills"; typical eligibility is
    a master's degree in social work plus two years (or commensurate experience as defined by the
    jurisdiction) of experience in clinical settings. Many jurisdictions call the license LCSW.
  - Format (same for every category): 122 multiple-choice questions, 110 scored and 12 unscored
    pretest questions mixed in; four hours (240 minutes) on a Pearson computer, in two sections of 61
    questions; "The exam may contain both three- and four-option questions."
  - Scoring: pass/fail, with the number of questions needed to pass adjusted for each exam form, so
    no fixed percentage is published.
  - Clinical content outline (pages 66 to 72): I. Values and Ethics 36% (IA Ethical principles and
    responsibilities, IB Ethical service delivery, IC Diversity and social justice); II. Assessment
    and Planning 32% (IIA Assessment concepts, IIB Assessment methods and techniques, including use of
    the DSM and the mental status examination, IIC Assessment practices, including treatment planning
    and the use and side effects of common medications); III. Intervention and Practice 32% (IIIA
    Practice concepts, IIIB Intervention methods and techniques, naming CBT, DBT, MBSR, motivational
    interviewing and EMDR among evidence-based practices, IIIC Practice evaluation and research, IIID
    Supervision and administration). The guidebook's worked example says about 36 of the 110 scored
    Clinical questions are on Assessment and Planning and about 36 on Intervention and Practice.
  - Clinical sample questions (negative symptoms of schizophrenia, a 4-year-old's milestones,
    generalized anxiety after seven months of worry, a spouse calling about a client, a child with
    autism and social cues, an angry client convicted of assault): read so that no item here repeats
    them.
  - Fees and the retake wait are in the guidebook but deliberately not used.
- ASWB, "2026 blueprints" announcement, https://www.aswb.org/2026-blueprints/ (found 2026-11-02 by the
  Masters builder; confirms the move from four to three content areas).
- Prep-company blogs, found by web search on 2026-11-04 (used only to cross-check, not for facts): they
  describe the change from 170 questions (150 scored) in four content areas to 122 in three, and one
  gives the Clinical weights as 36/32/32, matching the guidebook.
- Content references for item facts: DSM-5-TR (2022), including criteria for schizoaffective,
  bipolar II, hypomania (4 days), delusional disorder, antisocial personality disorder (age 18,
  conduct disorder before 15), DMDD, adjustment disorder (within 3 months), PTSD with dissociative
  symptoms, peripartum onset (during pregnancy or within 4 weeks after delivery), illness anxiety,
  functional neurological symptom disorder, malingering, avoidant and obsessive-compulsive
  personality disorders, hoarding, opioid withdrawal, early remission (3 to under 12 months), major
  versus mild neurocognitive disorder, selective mutism, ARFID, Tourette's, the Glossary of Cultural
  Concepts of Distress (taijin kyofusho, susto), and the new codes for suicidal behavior and
  nonsuicidal self-injury; NASW Code of Ethics (2021); HIPAA Privacy Rule (psychotherapy notes,
  minimum necessary, authorizations); Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act; ASAM Criteria
  dimensions; FDA antidepressant boxed warning (under 25); SAMHSA's six principles of a
  trauma-informed approach (2014); Roberts's seven-stage crisis model; Stanley and Brown's safety
  planning intervention; Meyer's minority stress model; Sue et al. (2007) microaggressions; Gottman's
  four horsemen; Linehan's DBT (modes, chain analysis, DEAR MAN, opposite action); Miller and
  Rollnick (OARS, sustain talk); Foa (prolonged exposure); Resick (CPT stuck points); TF-CBT;
  PCIT; IPT problem areas; EFT stages; MBCT; Stoltenberg's integrated developmental model; CDC
  suicide rates by age and sex (men 75 and older highest: 40.7 per 100,000 in 2023 and 40.1 in 2024,
  NCHS Data Brief 572, https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db572.htm, checked 2026-11-04);
  Boss's ambiguous loss; command hallucination risk assessment.

Tigertest's pass line of 70% is a readiness target, not the official pass mark.

Lanes: the Masters bank (`data/aswb-masters-questions.json`) and the NCE bank
(`data/nce-questions.json`) share ethics, DSM-5-TR and theory. Every item here was checked against both
by stem and key; facts they already test (MDE 2 weeks, mania 1 week, GAD, panic, PTSD basics,
lithium toxicity, tardive dyskinesia, MAOI diet, Tarasoff, ASFA, ICWA, hospice prognosis, POLST,
Kadushin, parallel process, Tuckman, Yalom's factors, the miracle and scaling questions, CBT
restructuring, DBT's origin, EMDR for PTSD, Bowen, structural and strategic family therapy, single-
subject designs, formative evaluation and the common defense mechanisms) were left out, and four
near-repeats (fraudulent billing, the unsupervised supervisee, termination readiness, psychoeducation)
were rewritten to balance billing, a supervisor's dual relationship, lithium and NSAIDs, and expressed
emotion.

All 200 items are original, written for Tigertest from the outline above. None is copied from the
ASWB guidebook's sample questions, ASWB's practice tests or any commercial prep product.
