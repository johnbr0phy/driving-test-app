# Sources for `atvsafety` (ATV and snowmobile safety course test, state agencies)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the state course outlines, the state
safety course guide, the CPSC pages and the ASI tips booklet listed below. No course quiz, chapter
review, final exam, instructor test bank, state exam, online course item or commercial practice
question was used or reworded, and no training provider is named in a question. Checked
2026-11-23.

## Course content (official state outlines)
- Minnesota Rules 6102.0070, Education and Training Programs (ATV and OHM),
  https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/6102.0070/ (fetched 2026-11-23). The course must cover machine
  nomenclature, control familiarization, machine safety features, operating procedures, laws and
  rules, loading and towing, a code of ethics, safety hazards including hearing damage,
  environmental consequences, and written tests. This list sets the ATV categories.
- Minnesota Rules 6100.5500, Snowmobile Education and Training Programs,
  https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/6100.5500/ (fetched 2026-11-23). Same topic list for the
  snowmobile course (parts and controls, safety features, operating procedures, snowmobile and
  highway laws, loading and towing, code of ethics, hazards including hearing damage,
  environmental effects, performance and written tests). Both courses run under one state
  safety program with the same outline, which is why one bank covers both.
- Minnesota DNR, ATV safety training requirements,
  https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/vehicle/atv/index.html (fetched 2026-11-23). Ages and parts
  of the course (online course, hands-on class for ages 10 to 15, the certificate valid at 12,
  online only from 16, alternate ATV RiderCourse); used for the landing only.
- Minnesota DNR, ATV safety tips and golden rules,
  https://dnr.state.mn.us/safety/vehicle/atv/atv-safety-tips.html (fetched 2026-11-23). Golden
  rules (designated trails, gear, no paved roads, no impairment, passenger limits, fit, active
  supervision, factory seatbelts, take a course), the bicycle readiness sign, the four fit checks.
- Minnesota DNR, ice thickness guidelines, https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/thickness.html
  (fetched 2026-11-23). 4 inches for walking, 5 to 7 inches for a snowmobile or small ATV on new
  clear ice; white ice about half as strong.
- Pennsylvania DCNR, ATV safety, training and reporting accidents,
  https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dcnr/recreation/what-to-do/atv-riding/safety-training-and-reporting-accidents
  (fetched 2026-11-23). Youth course ages 8 to 15, the 7-day crash report, spacing between
  riders, buddy system, helmet rule; used as the example that rules vary by state.

## Snowmobile course text (official state guide)
- New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation, Snowmobile Unit,
  "New York State Snowmobile Safety Education Program, Instructor's Course Guide" (revised August
  2024), https://parks.ny.gov/documents/recreation/snowmobiles/SnowmobileSafetyInstructorGuide.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-23, teaching text of chapters 1 to 7 read). Used for: parts, controls and
  safety features (kill switch on the right bar, brake on the left, SSCC label, snow flap,
  throttle monitor), maintenance (wear bars over half worn, carbide runners with studs, belt
  1/8 inch narrower, sliders, toe-in darting, track tension, air box), storage, clothing layers,
  helmets (about five years, no paint), lens tints, spares, fueling and coolant, riding positions,
  body position on hills and turns, snow conditions (blowing snow, deep snow, slush, ice,
  heavy wet snow, three inches of cover), IASA trail signs (Stop Ahead 200 feet, Caution 50 to
  100 yards, object markers), trail hazards (guy wires), night riding, group riding (tail rider,
  3 to 4 sled lengths, road guards), sharing the trail (Ride Right, groomers, skiers, dog
  sledders, wildlife), towing a disabled sled, frostbite, hypothermia, snow blindness, getting
  lost, navigation by shadow stick and watch, trailering (walk-on loading, 60 percent ahead of
  the axle, coupler check, tie-downs, 1 psi per 10 degrees). New York's own laws (ages, insurance
  limits, registration, highway rules) are not asked. The guide contains chapter quizzes, a Basic
  Snowmobile Safety Review list and a 50-item final examination: none were used, and every item
  was compared with all of them afterwards (see below).

## ATV machine and federal rules (official)
- U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, ATV Safety Information Center,
  https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/ATV-Safety-Information-Center
  (fetched 2026-11-23). Safety tips (training, passengers, paved roads, alcohol, youth models
  under 16, protective gear) and death and injury figures (landing only).
- CPSC FAQ, All-Terrain Vehicles, https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/All-Terrain-Vehicles-ATVs (fetched
  2026-11-23). Category Y youth groups Y-6+, Y-10+, Y-12+ (under 16), Category T (14 with adult
  supervision or 16 alone), Categories G and S (16 and older), Type II two-up limited to
  Category G, children's-product rules for 12 and under, the ban on new three-wheeled ATVs
  (16 CFR 1420.4).
- CPSC, Guidance for Youth ATVs,
  https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/ATV/Guidance-for-Youth-ATVs
  (fetched 2026-11-23). Over 5 mph and about 75 pounds point to an ATV rather than a ride-on toy;
  the ANSI/SVIA 1 compliance label.

## ATV riding technique (industry booklet hosted by a state agency)
- ATV Safety Institute, "Tips and Practice Guide for the All-Terrain Vehicle Rider" (U.S. version,
  revised 10/20), hosted by Oregon Parks and Recreation Department at
  https://egov.oregon.gov/oprd/ATV/Documents/ATV-ASI-Tips-Guide.pdf (fetched 2026-11-23, read in
  full). Used for: machine differences, protective gear (helmet labels, eye protection markings,
  boots, open-face helmets, helmet off with landowners), pre-ride inspection (tire pressure 2 to
  6 psi with a low-pressure gauge, wheels, throttle snapping shut, brakes, engine stop switch, oil
  and fuel, loose parts, tool kit), starting, posture, shifting, braking, parking, turning,
  hills, traversing, obstacles, reading terrain, sand, mud and water, snow, road crossings,
  towing a trailer, buddy system, alcohol (impairment at 0.05 BAC), drugs, fatigue (stop at least
  every hour), registration, TREAD Lightly, the practice exercises. The booklet has no quiz.

## Read but not used for questions
- Idaho Department of Parks and Recreation, Idaho All-Terrain Vehicle Manual,

## What the real course test looks like
- Each state runs its own ATV and snowmobile safety courses (classroom, online, hands-on) and
  sets its own written test and pass mark. New York's snowmobile course, for example, needs 75%
  or better on its final exam (NY guide, permission statement). The 80% readiness line and the
  per-test split are Tigertest's.

## Left out on purpose
- One state's ages, insurance limits, registration rules, trail permits, speed limits and
  helmet laws; questions say these vary by state.
- Youth ATV speed limits (the ANSI/SVIA figures could only be read from secondary copies).
- Hand signals other than stop and left turn (state courses teach different right-turn,
  slowing and last-sled signals), so no hand-signal item was written.
- Fees and fines.

## Similarity check against official question material
- The NY guide's 70 chapter-quiz items were extracted and every bank item (stem plus options)
  was scored by word overlap against each. Eleven items with overlap of 0.22 or more were
  rewritten from a different angle with new wrong options (tow points, stopping on the trail,
  blowing snow, getting lost, wildlife, trailer loading, fueling, trail surface, Ride Right,
  choke, turning). After the rewrite the highest overlap is 0.25, on items asking a different
  fact. Checked 2026-11-23.
- Fact-and-answer repeats were checked against the boating, hunter, pcoc, nz-day-skipper,
  bowhunter, trapper, canadian-firearms, ontario-hunter-education and first aid banks; the white
  ice, ice escape and trailer safety chain items were replaced because trapper or boating ask
  them.

## Second review (reviewer, 2026-11-23)
- Downloaded the NY guide again and extracted every chapter-quiz item (91 multiple-choice items
  across the chapters and the final exam's 20), the final exam's 20 matching lines (parts and
  safety features, trail signs, hand signals), its 4 fill-ins and 6 true/false items, and the 13
  Basic Snowmobile Safety Review statements. Each bank item was scored three ways: stem plus key
  word overlap against each quiz item (stem and options), stem similarity (difflib), and shared
  option text, then every pair above 0.3 was read by hand for the same fact and answer.
- 13 items asked a quiz or final-exam fact with the same answer and were replaced with a
  different fact and new wrong options: snow cover minimum (final Q1), stopping place on the
  trail (final Q19), lenses after dark (final T/F 49), landowner permission (two items, ch. 1 Q7
  and final Q4), snow flap (final matching), ski runners (ch. 2 Q4), sliders (ch. 2 Q3), greasing
  the suspension (ch. 2 Q10), securing a sled on a trailer and hauling it backward (ch. 7 Q5, Q6),
  overdriving the headlight (ch. 5 Q7), and the blue-lens item that duplicated another bank item.
  One road-crossing item kept its key but lost two wrong options that echoed final Q10.
  After the rewrite no bank item asks a quiz or exam fact with the same answer; the remaining
  pairs above 0.4 share a topic only (kill switch location vs what it does, why not to tow by
  the ski tips vs where to tow from, which trail repairs to know vs what to carry).
- Repeats against every other bank (stem and key overlap, read by hand above 0.3): frostbite
  treatment (trapper), hypothermia above freezing (trapper), throttle snapping shut and locked
  wheels in a quick stop (motorcycle, Singapore RTT), painting a helmet (Greece motorcycle) and
  overdriving headlights (Alberta Class 7, Ontario G1) were replaced; the scarf item
  (NZ motorcycle) was replaced by a two-up suspension item.
