# South Australia firearms safety training (first licence) bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the Firearms Act 2015 (SA), the
Firearms Regulations 2017 (SA) and its Schedule 1 Code of Practice, SAPOL Firearms Branch pages
and the National Firearms Safety Code. None is copied, adapted or reworded from a SAPOL, TAFE SA
or club course assessment, a training provider's material, a practice quiz or any prep site. No
official question catalogue for the SA course is published, so there was nothing to compare
against for similarity; the course assessment's questions were not seen.

## The course (format)

- TAFE SA, Firearm Safety Training - Category AB, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.tafesa.edu.au/xml/course/sc/sc_T984450610.aspx
  SAPOL-approved firearm safety training. Entry needs a SAPOL approval letter; training within 6
  months of the letter. Four sections: what is a firearm; firearms and the law; safety with
  firearms and using firearms responsibly; hunting and animal destruction; plus range rules.
  Online learning with compulsory exercises (about 6 to 8 hours), then one in-class day of about
  8.5 hours with theory and assessments (about 6 hours) and practical assessments (about 2 hours).
  No pass mark, number of questions or written-assessment format is published.
- Format conclusion: the Registrar may require a course, a written examination and a practical
  examination (reg 28(1)); TAFE SA assesses theory and practical work on the course day. No count
  or pass mark is published, so Tigertest's 50-question tests and 80% readiness line are
  Tigertest's own, and the landing says so. The practical assessment is not modelled.

## Safe handling

- Commonwealth Attorney-General's Department, National Firearms Safety Code (2002), as published
  by NT Police, Fire and Emergency Services, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://pfes.nt.gov.au/file/download/35121
  Used for a small number of handling items (danger times, removing a firearm butt first,
  obstructions, walking in single file and line abreast, storage preparation, ammunition in
  transit, disposal, passing a firearm, air rifle ricochet). Most of the Code's facts are already
  asked by the NSW and Queensland banks, so items repeating them were replaced with SA law.

## Checked against sibling banks

- Every stem and key was compared, by word overlap and identical keys, with the NSW, Victoria,
  Queensland and WA firearms banks, NZ firearms, CFSC, CRFSC, the South African firearm bank and
  the hunter banks. 21 items that asked a fact those banks already ask were replaced. The items
  left with high overlap are SA law that happens to match another State's (category definitions,
  surrender on cancellation, police inspection), each written to SA's own provision.

## Left out on purpose

- Fees, expiation amounts and maximum penalties (they change and the course does not test them).
- SA hunting law under the National Parks and Wildlife Act 1972: the National Parks and Wildlife
  (Hunting) Regulations 2011 are shown as ceased on legislation.sa.gov.au, so no season, bag or
  permit details are asked beyond SAPOL's statement that DEW manages hunting permits.
- The practical handling assessment, range commands of particular clubs, and course logistics.
- Anything about the course assessment itself (no question asks about the test).
