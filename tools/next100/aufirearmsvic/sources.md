# Victorian firearm safety course test bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the Firearms Act 1996 (Vic) as in force
on 19 August 2026, the Victoria Police longarm firearm safety course material (the Firearm Safety
Code booklet, the course slides and the instructors manual) and the Victoria Police eligibility
page. None is copied, adapted or reworded from the course's knowledge tests, a training provider's
material, a practice quiz or any prep site. The course material contains no sample questions; the
slides list "Exam - Firearms Safety Rules" and "Exam - Law" as course items, and neither exam was
seen or used.

## Outline (course required by the Act, set by Victoria Police)

- Victoria Police, Eligibility requirements for firearm applications, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.police.vic.gov.au/firearm-licence-eligibility-requirements
  Eligibility (Victorian residence or work, 18 for an adult licence, 12 to 18 for a junior licence,
  fit and proper, genuine reason, safety course, non-prohibited, international police checks);
  Victoria Police sets minimum course requirements and approves independent instructors; the
  course leaves you with the Firearm Safety Code, safe handling principles and your
  responsibilities; at the end you sit a multiple-choice safety and law knowledge test, in
  person even if the course is online, with photo ID, and receive a certificate for the licence
  application; fees vary by provider and are not regulated by Victoria Police; fit and proper
  examples; prohibited persons (12 months after a finding of guilt up to 15 years after
  imprisonment; a final intervention order for its duration and 5 years after; an interim order
  does not make a person prohibited unless it has firearm conditions).
- Victoria Police and Firearm Safety Foundation (Victoria) Inc., Firearm Safety Code booklet,
  revised 2020, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.police.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-06/firearm-safety-booklet-2020.pdf
  Approved by the Firearms Consultative Committee and Victoria Police to help applicants prepare
  for the written test. The ten basic rules explained, carrying and field hazards, fences,
  semi-readiness, spotlighting from vehicles, hunting with vehicles, cleaning, correct
  ammunition, old shotguns, magazines, patterning, sighting in, actions, shot sizes, where and
  when to shoot, licensing and acquisition, disposal, transport, glossary, intervention orders,
  game licences. Its storage summary (hard wood or steel) predates the 2021 amendment of
  Schedule 4, so storage law is taken from the Act below.
- Victoria Police, Longarms Firearm Safety Course slides (revised with the Firearm Safety
  Foundation), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://police.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-06/Safety%20Course%20Powerpoint%20_0.pdf
  Course objectives (firearm information, safety rules, video, law, the safety rules exam and the
  law exam), the ten rules, safe direction definition, size and location for target
  identification, carries, law section (categories, junior licence, travelling, camping, loss
  within 24 hours, change of address within 14 days triggering a storage inspection, permit to
  acquire, registration, storage, private property, demand for licence by police or an authorised
  officer under the Conservation, Forests and Lands Act 1987, alcohol and drugs including
  prescription medicines, Game Licence for duck, quail and deer).
- Victoria Police, Firearms Safety Instructors Manual (marked DRAFT), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.police.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-06/safety%20manual.pdf
  Longarm course of about 180 minutes in three sessions and an examination; key teaching points
  used: visitors may not know the rules, car bodies give no protection, most accidents happen in
  or around the home, personal safety items and telling someone your plans, pellet count and
  pattern width examples. Used only for teaching points, never for anything legal.
- The course has no published topic weights, so the split is Tigertest's: Victorian law 31 of 50,
  safe use 19 of 50.

## Format

- No question count, time limit or pass mark for the knowledge test is published by Victoria
  Police in the documents read. The eligibility page says it is a short multiple-choice safety
  and law knowledge test sat in person at the end of the course; the course slides list a safety
  rules exam and a law exam. The 80% line is Tigertest's readiness target and the landing says
  so.
- Firearms Act 1996 s 17(1)(c)(iii) (adult licence: satisfactory completion of an approved course
  of firearms safety and a good knowledge of firearms law) and s 20(1)(b)(iii) (junior licence).

## Not confirmed and how the copy words it

- The Firearms Regulations 2018 (Vic) were not read, so nothing only the Regulations set is asked
  (fees, conveyance rules beyond the Act, approved range details, junior and visitor details).
- The test's question count, time and pass mark are not published, so never stated as fact.
- The 2020 booklet and the course slides give different danger ranges (for example airgun up to
  400 m in the booklet, 150 m in the slides), so no danger range is asked.
- The booklet says an interim family violence intervention order suspends a licence; the current
  Victoria Police page says an interim order does not make the respondent prohibited unless it
  has firearm conditions. Only the Victoria Police page's wording is used.

## Deliberately left out

- Fees, penalty units and maximum penalties, dealer business rules, handgun licence details,
  collectors and heirlooms beyond the basics, firearm prohibition orders, the uncommenced
  citizenship and background check provisions, game seasons and bag limits, and anything about
  the course or its test itself.
- Facts that differ between states: every legal rule is stated for Victoria only.
- Fact-and-answer pairs already used in the NSW (`aufirearmsnsw`), Queensland (`aufirearmsqld`),
  New Zealand (`nzfirearms`) and Canadian (`capal`, `crfsc`) firearms banks were checked by stem
  and key comparison, and the repeats found were rewritten (handing over an unchecked firearm,
  leaving an unfamiliar firearm alone, drinking only once firearms are put away, legal medicines
  slowing reactions, bullets through walls, the bolt out for travel, lever cocking, minimum
  danger ranges, the alternative storage test).
