# Sources: Australia security licence course test (CPP20218 Certificate II in Security Operations), `ausecurity`

Every question in this bank is original, written by Tigertest from the units of competency and the
laws below. No RTO assessment, state police question set, prep company item or past paper was read
or used. All sources fetched 2026-11-23.

## Law: arrest, force and trespass (primary legislation, readable)

NSW legislation (legislation.nsw.gov.au) and the NSW Police Security Licensing and Enforcement
Directorate pages returned HTTP 403 and a bot check to every automated reader on 2026-11-23, so
NSW law was NOT read and no item states a NSW-only rule. The bank teaches the principles that the
two state laws below share and the CPP units require, and words every state difference as
"varies by state". Two items are labelled "In Victoria" and use the Victorian Act text.

- Crimes Act 1958 (Vic), version 324 (in force 1 November 2026), authorised DOCX via
  https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/crimes-act-1958/324
  fetched 2026-11-23. s 458 (any person may arrest without warrant a person found committing an
  offence, for listed reasons, and take them before a court or deliver them to police), s 461
  (arrest on reasonable grounds stays lawful), s 462 (finds committing), s 462A (force not
  disproportionate to the objective, believed on reasonable grounds to be necessary).
- Criminal Code Act 1899 (Qld), Criminal Code, current whole-of-act HTML:
  https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-1899-009
  fetched 2026-11-23. s 546 (any person may arrest a person found committing an offence), s 552
  (take forthwith before a justice, or immediately deliver to a police officer), s 260 (breach of
  the peace: reasonable, proportionate force, detain only to hand to police), s 271 and s 273
  (self-defence and aiding in self-defence), s 277 (removing a person who wrongfully remains or is
  disorderly from premises with reasonable force, never grievous bodily harm), s 283 (excess force
  is unlawful).
- Private Security Act 2004 (Vic), version 027 (19 June 2025), authorised DOCX via
  https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/private-security-act-2004
  fetched 2026-11-23. s 34 (licences up to 3 years), s 128 (produce licence to police, an
  authorised person, or a person the holder is providing services to), s 131 (offence to employ
  unlicensed persons), s 138 (crowd controllers wear identification), s 141 and 142 (crowd
  controller register), s 176 (notify the Chief Commissioner of a criminal charge within 7 days).

## Counter-terrorism (Australian Government, nationalsecurity.gov.au)

- Australia's Strategy for Protecting Crowded Places from Terrorism (PDF):
  https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/crowded-places-subsite/Files/australias-strategy-protecting-crowded-places-terrorism.pdf
  fetched 2026-11-23. Definition of crowded places; hostile reconnaissance.
- Improvised Explosive Device (IED) Guidelines for Crowded Places (PDF):
  https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/crowded-places-subsite/Files/ied-guidelines-crowded-places.pdf
  fetched 2026-11-23. HOT principle (Hidden, Obviously suspicious, not Typical); no mobile phones
  or radios within 25 m; evacuate at least 100 m from an item and 500 m from a vehicle; no one in
  line of sight; VBIED indicators; record the item's details; not all unattended items are
  suspicious.
- Chemical Weapon Guidelines for Crowded Places (PDF):
  https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/crowded-places-subsite/Files/chemical-weapon-guidelines-crowded-places.pdf
  fetched 2026-11-23. Indicators (vapour, unexplained odour, dead animals, two or more people
  with the same symptoms, out-of-place sprayers); front-line staff: get people out, upwind and
  uphill, limit phone use, remove outer clothing into a sealed bag, wash with soap and water.
- Active Armed Offender Guidelines for Crowded Places (PDF):
  https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/crowded-places-subsite/Files/active-armed-offender-guidelines-crowded-places.pdf
  fetched 2026-11-23. Definition; Escape. Hide. Tell.; lockdown, invacuation, evacuation.
- Hostile Vehicle Guidelines for Crowded Places (PDF):
  https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/crowded-places-subsite/Files/hostile-vehicle-guidelines-crowded-places.pdf
  fetched 2026-11-23. Definition; attack methods (parked, encroachment, tailgating, penetrative
  impact, Trojan vehicle, duress against a guard).
- National Terrorism Threat Advisory System:
  https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/national-threat-level/threat-advisory-system
  fetched 2026-11-23. Five levels: Not expected, Possible, Probable, Expected, Certain.
- Current National Terrorism Threat Level:
  https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/national-threat-level/current-national-terrorism-threat-level
  fetched 2026-11-23 (Probable; National Security Hotline 1800 123 400). The current level is
  never asked, since it changes.
- What to do in an attack (Escape. Hide. Tell.):
  https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/what-can-i-do/what-to-do-in-an-attack
  fetched 2026-11-23.

## Format (landing and examFacts only, never questions)

- There is no single national exam. Each unit is assessed by the RTO (CPP Companion Volume and
  the units' Assessment Conditions: assessment in a security work environment or simulated one,
  by the RTO's assessor). No question count or pass mark is published; 80% is Tigertest's
  readiness line. State licensing bodies (NSW Police SLED, Victoria Police LRD, Queensland OFT,
  WA Police, SA CBS and others) set which units each licence class needs; that is "varies by
  state" in all copy.

## Left out on purpose

- Every NSW-specific rule (licence classes 1A to 1C, Security Industry Act 1997 sections, LEPRA,
  Inclosed Lands Protection Act), because the NSW sites could not be read.
- Fees, penalty units, licence terms other than the Victorian s 34 example, current threat level,
  liquor licence specifics (the RSA test `aursa` covers them), standard drink grams.
- The practical defensive-tactics and first aid assessments.

## Similarity and copyright check

No official question catalogue exists for this qualification (RTOs write their own assessments,
which were not read). Method: `python3 -I tools/next100/ausecurity/simcheck.py` compares every
stem, and every stem plus key, with the alberta-security, bc-security-worker, ie-psa-security,
malta-private-guard, nz-security, ontario-security-guard, psira-security, security-guard,
sia-door-supervisor, sira-security, gr-security, au-rsa and au-first-aid banks (difflib stem ratio
over 0.7 or word overlap over 0.5). First run 2026-11-23: 44 flags, of which 21 were the same fact
with the same answer (phonetic M, Roger, note-taking timing, notebook corrections, continuity,
duty of care definition, citizen's arrest equal to the public, hand to police, hazard and
dynamic risk definitions, blue mandatory sign, varied patrols, checkpoint scanners, bag-check
refusal, language barrier, first step with an upset patron, and others). All 21 were rewritten to
a different fact or angle. Final run: 14 flags, all on different facts or different answers
(for example Escape, Hide, Tell against the UK's Run, Hide, Tell).
