# Sources: BC Basic Security Training (BST) exam, `cabcguard`

Every question in the bank is original, written from the laws, regulation, registrar policy and
WorkSafeBC rules below. No BST exam item, JIBC course material, sample question, prep-site question
or prep book was read or used, even reworded. All sources fetched 2026-11-16 (WebFetch, or curl for
the long gov.bc.ca and bclaws pages, read as text).

## Official: the exam and the licence (JIBC on behalf of the registrar; Security Programs Division)

- JIBC, Basic Security Training (the program page): https://www.jibc.ca/program/basic-security-training
  - Fetched 2026-11-16. BST is two parts: BST Theory (SCRT-1651, 40 hours, in person or online,
    online self-paced with two months to finish) and BST Assessment (SCRT-1656, the final exam).
    Exam: 90 minutes, 75 questions, in person at JIBC New Westminster on the student's own laptop
    (online proctored exam possible outside the Lower Mainland); written within six months of the
    theory course; pass mark 70%; up to two attempts, then the theory course must be retaken.
    JIBC issues no BST certificate after December 15, 2025; the transcript is the proof. Topics named:
    professionalism and ethics, legal studies (laws and regulations governing security work),
    report writing, personal safety. Fees on the page are not used.
- JIBC course pages: https://www.jibc.ca/course/basic-security-training-theory and
  https://jibc.ca/course/basic-security-training-assessment
  - Fetched 2026-11-16. Theory: relevant legislation, legal authority, ethics and the roles of a
    security professional; the legal system; personal safety; follow-up procedures and documentation.
    Assessment: tests the BST Theory outcomes, with the focus on a security guard's legal authority to
    act and on public and personal safety always coming first; listed with "a two-hour time limit"
    (the program page says 90 minutes and 75 questions; the landing gives the program page's figures
    and says to check with JIBC). Assessment sessions are listed from November 2026 to March 2027, so
    the exam is current. The course pages show older codes (SCRT-1650 and SCRT-1655) than the program
    page; codes are not used anywhere.
- BC government, security worker training requirements:
  https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/security-services/security-industry-licensing/workers/training
  - Fetched 2026-11-16. BST through JIBC or an approved security training school; older training may be
    called Basic Standard Training Level 1 and 2; JIBC stopped issuing certificates after Dec 15, 2025.
- BC government, security worker licence and rules for licensed workers:
  https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/security-services/security-industry-licensing/workers
  and https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/security-services/security-industry-licensing/workers/rules
  - Fetched 2026-11-16. A licence from another province does not replace a BC licence; carry the
    physical licence (a photo is not acceptable), replace a lost one, never work with an expired one,
    show it on request except armoured car guards and liquor-establishment door security, hand it only
    to police or special provincial constables; report charges, convictions and address changes and
    keep working unless SPD says stop; self-employed contractors need worker and business licences;
    prohibited: badges, Sam Browne belts, tasers, pepper spray, batons; force includes blocking access,
    moving or restraining someone, handcuffs, weapons; record every use of force (SPD Form 0513 or
    equivalent) and give it to the manager.
- Registrar policy manual, licensing process and licence conditions:
  https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/security-services/security-industry-licensing/about/law-policy/licensing-process-licence-conditions-policies
  - Fetched 2026-11-16 (read in full as text). Used: 2.2.5 door security and volunteers; 2.6.1 English
    fluency (CLB 5); 2.6.6 BST is the training standard, JIBC trains and invigilates the exam on the
    registrar's behalf, former police and similar may apply directly; 3.2.1 Code of Conduct (13
    conditions, quoted); 3.3 restraints (only guards, Advanced Security Training within a year,
    requalify every 3 years, metal chain link handcuffs only); 3.4 armoured car firearms; 3.5 dogs
    (validation, annual requalification, six months for dual-purpose dogs); 3.6.4 guard under
    supervision (once only, 90 days, licensed guard readily available, 5:1 ratio, tell the client);
    3.7.1 peace officer condition; 3.7.2 use-of-force reports; 3.7.5 amber lights; 3.7.6 licence
    production exemptions; 3.7.7 vehicle markings, one white search light, no cage, siren or crash
    bumper, never hold or transport an arrested person.

## Not confirmed, and how the bank handles it

- JIBC publishes no topic weights. The blueprint (legal system 6, licensing 8, arrest 6, force 6,
  trespass and offences 6, conduct and privacy 8, reports 5, safety 5 per 50) is Tigertest's, built
  around the four topics JIBC names with legal authority weighted most, as the assessment page says.
- The BST curriculum itself (the JIBC course content) is not public. Report writing and personal
  safety items keep to general documentation and safety practice that follows from the rules above
  (Code of Conduct false statements, use-of-force records, PIPA accuracy, records retention,
  WorkSafeBC working alone and violence rules) and to JIBC's stated principle that public and
  personal safety come first. No figure in those items comes from anywhere else.
- The exam time differs between JIBC pages (90 minutes on the program page, a two-hour time limit on
  the assessment course page); the landing gives 75 questions and 90 minutes from the program page.

## Deliberately left out

- Fees, fines and administrative penalty amounts, ticket amounts, insurance amounts.
- Ontario rules (PSISA, Trespass to Property Act), which the `caguard` bank covers, and facts and
  answers already asked there (s. 494 headline powers, theft, robbery and B&E definitions, Ontario
  signage colours). Criminal Code items here ask different subsections or BC scenarios.
- Charter rights on arrest and case law on private arrest (not read; the Charter's reach to private
  guards is unsettled).
- Questions about the exam itself (length, pass mark, booking, attempts).
