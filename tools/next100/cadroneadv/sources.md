# Sources for `cadroneadv` (Transport Canada Advanced Exam, Pilot Certificate - Advanced Operations)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the Canadian Aviation Regulations,
Transport Canada's knowledge requirements document TP 15263, Standard 922, the TC AIM and ISED's
radiotelephone study guide. Transport Canada publishes no question bank, sample test or past paper
for the Advanced Exam, so there was no catalogue to screen against; no flight-school quiz,
commercial practice question or prep-app item was read or reworded, and no school is named.
Checked 2026-11-22.

## Exam format and outline (official)
- Transport Canada, "Take a drone pilot online exam: Advanced",
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-pilot-licensing/take-drone-pilot-online-exam/small-advanced
  (fetched 2026-11-22, page modified 2025-04-01): 50 multiple-choice questions, 60 minutes (marked
  on the answers given if time runs out), 80% or higher to pass, minimum age 16, a 24-hour wait
  after a fail with no limit on attempts, same fee each attempt (amount not shown), results right
  away in the Drone Management Portal, keep the reference number for booking the flight review,
  then pass a flight review with a flight reviewer to get the Pilot Certificate - Advanced
  Operations. TC strongly recommends a flight school first. Based on TP 15263 ("not a study
  guide").
- Transport Canada, "Knowledge Requirements for Pilots of Remotely Piloted Aircraft Systems, 250 g
  up to and including 150 kg, Basic and Advanced Operations - TP 15263", Fourth Edition 03/2025,
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/publications/knowledge-requirements-pilots-remotely-piloted-aircraft-systems-250-g-including-150-kg-basic-advanced-operations-tp-15263
  (fetched 2026-11-22, page modified 2025-04-01): exam table (Basic 35, 1.5 h, 65%; Advanced 50,
  1 h, 80%; Flight Reviewer 30, 1.5 h, 80%). Advanced topics: Part IX Division V (901.62 to
  901.75: certificate, recency, declarations, modified RPAS, controlled airspace, ATC compliance,
  established procedures near airports and heliports, EVLOS and sheltered operations, visual
  observers, flights under 30 m from people), EVLOS and sheltered operations (2 NM, 30 m,
  observer positioning and communication, C2 link stress, lost-link and return-to-home altitude,
  structures), CARs Part VI airspace, MF and ATF procedures (602.97 to 602.103), ATC services,
  METAR, TAF and aviation forecasts, transponder function and 901.46, radiotelephony with height
  versus altitude. The page text does not carry the Basic/Advanced tick marks, so the advanced-only
  topics were taken from the content itself and cross-checked with CAR Division V.
- Transport Canada, "Drone operation categories and pilot certificates",
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/learn-rules-you-fly-your-drone/drone-operation-categories-pilot-certificates
  (fetched 2026-11-22, modified 2025-11-04): what advanced allows (small and medium drones, near
  and over people, controlled airspace with ATC permission, sheltered, EVLOS; no BVLOS), Level 1
  Complex at 18 adds BVLOS; advertised events and drones over 150 kg need permission.
- Transport Canada, "Choosing the right drone for advanced operations",
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/learn-rules-you-fly-your-drone/choosing-right-drone-advanced-operations
  (fetched 2026-11-22, modified 2026-06-26): no declaration for basic operations; meeting one
  operation does not make a drone fit for all; which 922 sections are SAD only, PVD only or both;
  check the declared-drone list; invalidated declarations.

## Guidance (official)
- Transport Canada Aeronautical Information Manual (TC AIM, TP 14371), edition of October 1, 2026,
  RPA chapter, https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/aim-2026-2_rpa_en.pdf
  (downloaded 2026-11-22): 3.2.3 airspace classes (Class B from 12,500 ft, Class D around
  medium-sized airports, Class E control zones to 3,000 ft AGL and controlled for IFR), RPA
  restricted airspace moving to the DAH after 90 days; 3.4 advanced and Level 1 Complex operations:
  established procedure in the CFS PRO section or TC's generic procedure (contact the aerodrome
  operator, listen on the aerodrome frequency, land and reassess when aircraft are seen), NAV Drone
  flight authorizations and their contents, no SFOC above 400 ft in controlled airspace with ANSP
  authorization, aerodrome operators have no access to NAV Drone authorization details, small RPA
  without a declaration limited to the basic environment plus sheltered and EVLOS, medium RPA
  without a declaration cannot fly in Canada, Table 3.1 of declarations per operation, 922.07
  superseding 922.05 and 922.06, modifications and parachute minimum heights, PVD letters of
  acceptance and annual safety reports, 30-day notice of declarant changes, Level 1 Complex (18,
  20 hours of ground school, RPOC), recency activities and self-paced program kept by the pilot,
  failed recency exams do not invalidate a certificate, foreign-owned RPA need an SFOC-RPAS.
- TC AIM, October 1, 2026, MET chapter,
  https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/aim-2026-2_met_en.pdf (downloaded 2026-11-22):

  cloud type named, CAVOK not used and RMK used in Canadian TAFs, PROB30 and PROB40, WS group,
  P6SM, visibility in statute miles, BR at 5/8 SM or more, METARs on the hour, SPECI criteria,
  density altitude remark at 200 ft, PIREP winds converted to true, GFA issue and valid times, six

- TC AIM, October 1, 2026, RAC chapter,
  https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/aim-2026-2_rac_en.pdf (downloaded 2026-11-22):
  MF areas and CFS entries (frequency, distance, altitude), ATF on UNICOM or 123.2 MHz, aeroplane
  circuits at 1,000 ft above aerodrome elevation, transponder codes 7600 and 7700, Mode A 4096
  codes, Mode C, Mode S with extended squitter (ADS-B), SQUAWK IDENT, SSR-only coverage.
- TC AIM, October 1, 2026, COM chapter,
  https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/aim-2026-2_com_en.pdf (downloaded 2026-11-22):

- Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, "RIC-21 - Study Guide for the Restricted
  Operator Certificate With Aeronautical Qualification",
  https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/node/817

  three times, distress then urgency priority, 121.5 MHz, times in four digits with ZULU.

## What could not be confirmed, and how the copy words it
- Exam languages: the Advanced Exam page does not list them; TP 15263 says exams are in English
  or French. The landing says English or French, as TP 15263 does.
- Exam fee: not shown on the TC page; left out everywhere.
- TP 15263's Basic and Advanced tick marks did not come through in the fetched text; topics were
  chosen from what the document describes as advanced and from CAR Division V.
- PAN PAN's exact definition beyond "urgency signal" was not in the RIC-21 page text; the bank
  only uses its rank below distress.

## Deliberately left out
- Fines and penalties, exam, certificate and SFOC fees.
- Anything about the exam or the flight review itself (question count, pass mark, retake wait,
  flight review exercises, reviewer duties).
- Level 1 Complex and BVLOS rules beyond the edge of advanced operations; Standard 922.10 risk
  ratios.
- Facts already asked in `cadronebasic` (30 m and 122 m basics, 3 NM and 1 NM distances,
  registration, 12-hour alcohol rule, Class F codes, the CFS Cert entry, ROC-A, aircraft lights,
  launch-height arithmetic, the transponder waiver) and the ceiling layer pattern used in
  `part107`.
