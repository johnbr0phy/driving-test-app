# California Firearm Safety Certificate (FSC) test bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the California Department of Justice
Firearm Safety Certificate Study Guide and the Penal Code sections it summarizes. None is copied,
adapted or reworded from the DOJ written test, the study guide's chapter self-tests, a DOJ
Certified Instructor's material, a gun store quiz, a practice app or any prep site. The self-test
items in the guide were read only to see which topics it treats as testable and were not used.

## Outline (the study guide is the official syllabus for the test)

- California Department of Justice, Bureau of Firearms, Firearm Safety Certificate Study Guide,
  2025 edition (revised June 2025, the current English edition linked from the FSC page),
  fetched 2026-11-22:
  https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/fsc-study-guide.pdf
  The preface says the guide "provides the basic firearm safety information necessary to pass
  the test" and the introduction says the information needed to pass is in the guide. Chapters:
  1 Firearm Safety Rules (six basic rules, additional safety points); 2 Firearms and Children
  (owner responsibility, safe storage laws, Rules for Kids); 3 Firearm Operation and Safe Handling
  (revolvers, semiautomatic pistols, long guns, ammunition, physics of gunfire, calibers,
  dangerous range, malfunctions); 4 Firearm Ownership (training, manuals, cleaning, lead, repair,
  safety and storage devices, childproofing, Penal Code 25145 from January 1, 2026); 5 Prohibited
  Firearms Transfers and Straw Purchases; 6 Firearms Laws (sales and transfers, DROS, 10-day wait,
  age 21, private party and intra-familial transfers, proof of residency, FSC, safe handling
  demonstration, firearms safety device and roster requirements, new residents, privately
  manufactured firearms, concealed carry, common carriers, home, business and campsite, lethal
  force in self-defense, loaded firearms in public, large-capacity magazines, storage during
  prohibition, miscellaneous prohibited acts, criminal storage under Penal Code 25100 and the
  AB 53 changes, minors, persons ineligible to possess firearms, GVROs and DVROs).
  The guide says the safe handling demonstration steps "will not appear on the DOJ written test",
  so the bank asks how each firearm type works and how to check it is unloaded, never the
  numbered demonstration steps themselves.
- California DOJ, Firearm Safety Certificate page, fetched 2026-11-22:
  https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/fscinfo
  Lists the study guide editions (English revised June 2025; Spanish, Tagalog and Chinese
  revised February 2026) and says the certificate is earned by passing a written test on firearm
  safety, generally at participating dealers and private training facilities.

## Format (landing, FAQ and examFacts only; never asked in the bank)

- California DOJ, Firearm Safety Certificate frequently asked questions, fetched 2026-11-22:
  https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/fscfaqs
  30 true/false and multiple choice questions; pass at 75%, 23 of 30; $25 covers the test and
  the certificate and a second attempt with the same DOJ Certified Instructor, after 24 hours;
  replacement certificate $5 from the issuing instructor; valid five years from issuance;
  minimum age 18; California driver license or DMV identification card; exemptions such as a
  valid hunting license for long guns only, active, reserve or honorably retired military, CCW
  permit holders and POST 832 firearms training; loans of no more than three days with the lender
  present are generally exempt.
- The study guide (above) repeats the 75% pass mark, the five-year validity and the $25 fee, and
  cites Penal Code sections 31610 to 31670 for the FSC rules and 31700 for exemptions.
- The real test has true/false items; Tigertest's items all have four options, and the landing
  says so. The 75% line (23 of 30) is the real pass mark.

## Law used in questions (as summarized in the 2025 study guide)

- Penal Code sections cited in the guide and used for questions: 197 (justifiable homicide),
  246, 246.3, 247 (discharge), 417 and 417.3 (drawing or exhibiting), 626.9 (school grounds),
  171b to 171e (courts, Capitol), 171.7 (airplanes), 16850, 25400, 25605, 25610, 25640,
  26035, 26055, 26150 (concealed carry and the home, business and campsite), 25850, 26005,
  26040, 26100, 26350 (loaded firearms in public, open carry of an unloaded handgun, vehicles),
  23635 to 23690 (firearms safety devices), 23900 and 23920 (identification marks), 25100,
  25105, 25145 (criminal storage and secure storage, with AB 53 effective January 1, 2026),
  26845 (proof of residency), 26850 (safe handling demonstration), 27510 (under 21), 27545 and
  27590 (transfers through a dealer), 27560 (new residents, 60 days), 29010 (three self-made
  firearms a year), 29610 to 29655 (minors), 29800 to 29805 (prohibited persons), 29830 (storage
  during prohibition), 31610 to 31700 (FSC), 32000 and 32100 (handgun roster), 32310 and 32311
  (large-capacity magazines), 18150 to 18175 (GVROs); federal 18 U.S.C. 922(a)(6) and 922(d)
  (straw purchases) and 49 U.S.C. 46505 (aircraft).
- California Legislative Information, Penal Code section 31640 (and the FSC article),
  tried 2026-11-22: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=31640.
  The site returned 403 to automated fetches and would not render in the browser tool, so the
  statute text itself could not be read. Every legal rule in the bank is the DOJ's own summary in
  the 2025 study guide, which is what the written test is built on, and the explanations cite the
  Penal Code section the guide gives.

## Left out on purpose

- Fees, fine amounts and federal penalty figures (the $250,000 and 15 years in the guide), and
  the private party transfer fee cap.
- Anything about the test itself (question count, pass mark, retakes, fee, who gives it, how
  long the certificate lasts): these are on the landing only.
- The safe handling demonstration step lists, which the guide says are not on the written test.
- Rules the 2025 guide does not state: the one-handgun-in-30-days limit, ammunition purchase
  background checks, the excise tax, assault weapon definitions and county-by-county carry
  licensing details. Laws can change; the bank follows the June 2025 guide and the January 1,
  2026 storage changes it describes.
- Statistics quoted in the guide from third-party fact sheets.
