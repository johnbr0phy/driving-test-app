# CBEST (`cbest`): sources

All questions, passages, tables and excerpts in this bank are original, written for Tigertest from the CBEST
test specifications below. No CBEST practice test, released item, sample question or commercial prep material was
used or paraphrased. The official online practice tests listed on the preparation page (Reading, Mathematics,
Writing PDFs) were deliberately not opened.

## Test owner (checked 2026-11-14)
- California Commission on Teacher Credentialing (CTC) and Pearson (Evaluation Systems), CBEST test page on the
  CTC exams site: https://www.ctcexams.nesinc.com/TestView.aspx?f=HTML_FRAG/CA_CBEST_TestPage.html (fetched
  2026-11-14)
  - Three separately registered sections, all in English, computer-based at a test center or online proctored.
  - Reading: 50 multiple-choice questions, 1 hour 30 minutes; about 40% Critical Analysis and Evaluation and about
    60% Comprehension and Research Skills.
  - Mathematics: 50 multiple-choice questions, 2 hours; about 30% Estimation, Measurement, and Statistical
    Principles, 35% Computation and Problem Solving, 35% Numerical and Graphic Relationships. Calculators are not
    allowed.
  - Writing: 2 essays, 1 hour 30 minutes (one analyzes a situation or statement, one describes a personal
    experience).
  - Scores: each section scaled 20 to 80; passing is 41 on each section, or a total of 123 with no section below 37.
  - Some questions may be pretest items that do not count.

## Content outline (fetched 2026-11-14)
- CTC / Pearson, "California Basic Educational Skills Test (CBEST) Test Specifications" (Reading, Mathematics,
  Writing; copyright 2007 by the Commission on Teacher Credentialing), the document linked from the CBEST
  preparation materials page: https://www.ctcexams.nesinc.com/content/docs/CBESTUpdatedTestSpecs.pdf
  - Reading, Critical Analysis and Evaluation: compare and contrast ideas; supporting reasons and details; predict
    outcomes; author's attitude and viewpoint; relevance of facts to an argument; statements that strengthen or
    weaken an argument; persuasive techniques; fact versus opinion; logical assumptions; challenging statements;
    inconsistencies and differing points of view; audience; tone inappropriate to audience and purpose.
  - Reading, Comprehension and Research Skills: (A) general and specific ideas, sequence, outlines and graphic
    organizers, main idea and purpose, paraphrase and summary, facts and details, conclusions, inferences, implied
    relationships, word meaning from context and word parts, figurative and colloquial language, different
    interpretations, context, transition words; (B) table of contents, headings and index, where information is
    found, how a selection is organized, conclusions supported by a table or graph.
  - Mathematics, Estimation, Measurement, and Statistical Principles: U.S. customary units of length,
    temperature, weight and capacity; length and perimeter; estimating time for work tasks; estimating before
    computing; averages, ratios, proportions and percentiles of test scores; basic probability; interpreting
    stanines and percentiles.
  - Mathematics, Computation and Problem Solving: whole numbers; positive and negative numbers; fractions,
    decimals and percents; practical problems (e.g. invoice totals); equations with one unknown; whether enough
    information is given; alternative methods.
  - Mathematics, Numerical and Graphic Relationships: percent change; ordering numbers; less than, greater than and
    equal symbols; equivalent numbers and expressions; rounding; logical connectives (and, or, if-then) and
    quantifiers (some, all, none); missing table entries; tables, spreadsheets and bar, line and circle graphs.
  - Writing: two essays, a remembered experience and an expository analysis, scored for clarity, focus,
    development, conventions and logic. Not modelled here.
- CTC / Pearson, CBEST study guide test structure page:
  https://www.ctcexams.nesinc.com/Content/StudyGuide/CA_SG_obj_CBEST.htm (fetched 2026-11-14): test codes 095
  Reading, 096 Mathematics, 097 Writing; the same skill factors and approximate percentages.
- CTC / Pearson, CBEST preparation materials page:
  https://www.ctcexams.nesinc.com/TestView.aspx?f=HTML_FRAG/CA_CBEST_PrepMaterials.html (fetched 2026-11-14), used
  only to find the test specifications link.

## Credential context (checked 2026-11-14)
- California Commission on Teacher Credentialing, leaflet CL-667 "Basic Skills Requirement" (July 2021, page last
  updated October 2026): https://www.ctc.ca.gov/credentials/leaflets/basic-skills-requirement-%28cl-667%29 (fetched
  2026-11-14). The CBEST is one way to meet the basic skills requirement; passing standard 41 per section, or 37 in a
  section with a total of 123; passing scores do not expire; other routes (coursework, SAT/ACT, CSET: Multiple
  Subjects plus Writing Skills, AP, other states' exams) exist.

## Modelling choices
- Two practice tests of two sections each at half length and the real pace: Reading 25 questions in 45 minutes
  (real 50 in 90) and Mathematics 25 in 60 minutes (real 50 in 120), no calculator. Writing is two essays and is not
  modelled; the landing and scores say so.
- Per test: Reading 10 Critical Analysis and Evaluation (40%), 11 Comprehension and Context and 4 Research and
  Reference (60% together); Mathematics 8 Estimation, Measurement and Statistics (32%), 9 Computation and Problem
  Solving (36%), 8 Numerical and Graphic Relationships (32%). The split of the 60% between comprehension and research
  is Tigertest's own; the outline gives none.
- Four answer choices per question here. The specifications do not say how many choices the real test uses, so the
  copy does not claim a number.
- Scores here are percent correct per section. Pearson publishes no raw-to-scaled conversion, so no 20 to 80
  estimate is shown; the landing explains the real scaled passing standard.
- Units are U.S. customary, as the outline states. Graphs are given as tables or described in words.
- Left out: fees and registration rules, which can change, and the Writing essays.
