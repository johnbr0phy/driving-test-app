# Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test (CCAT) sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the content description below. None is taken
or reworded from Criteria's example questions (the opposite of LENGTHEN, the average of three numbers,
the plane flying 400 mph, the odd-one-out shape and the pattern box on its pages and PDFs), from any
released test, or from any prep site or app. Criteria's examples were read only to learn the item types.

## Format and content (the test owner, Criteria Corp)

- Criteria Corp, "What to Expect on the Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test (CCAT)" (candidate prep page):
  https://www.criteriacorp.com/candidates/ccat-prep (fetched 2026-11-22). 50 questions, 15 minutes,
  answer as many as you can; three question types, verbal, math and logic, and spatial reasoning; no
  calculator; the employer gets the raw score (number correct) and a percentile; no penalty for wrong
  answers; the average test taker gets 24 right and fewer than 1% answer all 50. Verbal covers precise
  word meaning, how words relate and how context decides the word; math and logic covers basic algebra,
  proportions and word problems; spatial covers rotating or flipping shapes, patterns and the odd one out.
- Criteria Corp, "Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test (CCAT Assessment)" (product page):
  https://www.criteriacorp.com/assess/cognitive-aptitude/criteria-cognitive-aptitude-test-ccat
  (fetched 2026-11-22). 50 items, very few people finish all 50 in the 15-minute limit; raw score out of
  50 and a percentile against the norm group; English (US) by default and English (UK); other languages
  go through the UCAT, a language-independent version; a dynamic test drawn from an item bank of
  thousands of items; used for mid- to high-level jobs.
- Criteria Corp, "Information Brief: Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test (CCAT)" (2026 PDF):
  https://www.criteriacorp.com/files/Criteria-InfoBrief-CCAT.pdf (fetched 2026-11-22). Up to 50
  questions in 15 minutes; three abilities, spatial reasoning, verbal ability, and math and logic;
  any device (phone, tablet, laptop, desktop), taken remotely and unsupervised; no two candidates get the
  same set of questions; reports give the raw score out of 50, a percentile and three sub-percentiles;
  employers compare the raw score with suggested score ranges by job family (the example Management
  range is 20 to 50), and may set their own; norm sample of 106,740 adults with a median of 24 and a
  standard deviation of 7; Cronbach's alpha 0.86; example items have five choices, A to E.
- Criteria Corp, "CCAT Score Report Guide" (PDF):
  https://www.criteriacorp.com/files/Criteria-ScoreReportGuide-CCAT.pdf (fetched 2026-11-22). 50
  multiple-choice questions with a 15-minute limit; very few people finish; Verbal Ability is tested
  "through sentence completion, vocabulary, and word comparison questions"; Math and Logic measures
  problem solving, arithmetic, logic and numerical reasoning; Spatial Reasoning uses object and pattern
  questions.
- Criteria Corp, "Score Report Data That Helps You Hire" (CCAT overview PDF):
  https://www.criteriacorp.com/files/Criteria-Overview-CCAT.pdf (fetched 2026-11-22). 50 questions,
  estimated time 15 minutes; report shows raw score, percentile, the three sub-scores and score ranges
  by position.

## How the bank follows it

Eight drill domains cover the three abilities Criteria names. Verbal ability: vocabulary (synonyms and
antonyms), word comparison (analogies) and sentence completion. Math and logic: arithmetic, word problems
(averages, proportions, rates, percents), number and letter series, and logic (syllogisms, orderings,
if-then). Spatial reasoning: rotation, mirror images, folding, odd one out and pattern rules, written in
words because the bank carries no pictures. Criteria publishes no per-area counts, so the per-test split
(vocabulary 6, analogies 6, sentence completion 6, arithmetic 6, word problems 8, series 5, logic 5,
spatial 8) is Tigertest's own. Each practice test is 50 questions in 15 minutes with five choices, like
the real test; difficulty is mixed through the test.

## Could not be confirmed, and how the copy words it

- No pass mark exists: employers compare the raw score with a range for the role. The copy says so and
  never calls a score passing.
- Whether difficulty rises through the test is not stated by Criteria, so the copy does not claim it.
- Retake rules are not on Criteria's pages; the copy says the employer decides.
- Criteria publishes no table from raw score to percentile beyond the norm median of 24 and the SD of 7,
  so Tigertest reports percent correct and the raw count, never a percentile.

## Left out

Criteria's example items; the UCAT and other Criteria tests (CBST, EPP, CLPT); the real test's picture
items (spatial items here are in words); any claim about specific employers' cut scores.
