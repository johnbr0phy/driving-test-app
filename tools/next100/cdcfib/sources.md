# Sources for `cdcfib` (CDCFIB recruitment CBT)

All 200 questions are original. No past paper, "CDCFIB past questions" booklet or commercial app
was opened or copied. Checked 2026-11-01 unless noted.

## Who runs the test, and what is (and is not) published

- Official sites: cdcfib.gov.ng showed only a maintenance page on 2026-11-01 ("Our portal is
  currently undergoing essential system upgrades"); cdcfib.gov.ng/about returned 404. The 2025
  recruitment ran on recruitment.cdcfib.gov.ng. So no official document stating the question count,
  time limit, subjects or pass mark could be read.
  https://cdcfib.gov.ng
- 2025 exercise timeline (news reports of Board statements): application portal 14 July to
  11 August 2025; shortlist published 30 October 2025; portal closed 7 November; online CBT
  12 to 19 November 2025, the third stage after application screening; then physical screening,
  document checks and medical (Nairametrics 26 Nov 2025; Arbiterz 12 Nov 2025).
  https://nairametrics.com/2025/11/26/cdcfib-2025-recruitment-over-200000-candidates-fail-computer%e2%80%91based-test
  https://arbiterz.com/?p=54640
- Format of the 2025 online CBT, from Board instructions as reported by Legit.ng and Nigeria
  Startup Act (Nov 2025): taken online at a scheduled slot on a laptop, desktop or smartphone with
  camera and microphone; portal opens 30 minutes before; selfie/photo verification; AI and live
  video proctoring; leaving the screen, switching apps or calls trigger violation alerts, and
  "Repeated violations will result in automatic failure"; a disconnected candidate may rejoin
  within the allotted time. Number of questions, duration and subjects were NOT stated.
  https://www.legit.ng/nigeria/1683028-2025-recruitment-cdcfib-releases-3-important-instructions-applicants-online-test
  https://www.nigeriastartupact.ng/cdcfib-cbt-guidelines-and-rules-as-exam-begins-november-12/
- Question count, conflicting and unofficial:
  - A guide (androidpols, Oct 2025) says 100 multiple-choice questions, options A to D, about 60
    minutes, equal marks, no negative marking, covering current affairs, general knowledge
    (Nigerian geography and history, basic science, logical reasoning, simple maths, English) and
    agency-specific topics. Another blog says 100 questions in 90 minutes.
    https://www.androidpols.com.ng/2025/10/how-2025-cdcfib-recruitment-cbt-will-be.html
  - Candidates quoted on the first day reported tests of 40 questions ("I only got to answer 24
    out of 40 questions") (Nigeria Startup Act, 12 Nov 2025).
    https://www.nigeriastartupact.ng/?p=15716
  - Conclusion: count, time, subject split and pass mark are unconfirmed. The landing says so.
- Results: Nairametrics (26 Nov 2025) reports the Board's score bands 0-40: 221,996;
  41-60: 229,155; 61-80: 278,543; 81-100: 142,697, and that 221,996 "failed". This suggests a score
  out of 100 with 40 or below counted as failing, but the Board published no pass mark. Not used
  as a figure in the bank or on the landing (it is one year's outcome).
- Scale: about 1.9 million applied and 171,956 were shortlisted for about 30,000 places
  (Arbiterz). Not used (changes each exercise).

Decisions: four 50-question tests, 70% target (Tigertest's own line, chosen above the reported
40-point failure band). Weights are Tigertest's own: English, numerical ability and reasoning
(the general aptitude core reported by guides) plus general knowledge in this builder's lane: the
four services and the Board, fire safety and first aid, Nigerian geography and environment. No
current affairs, office-holders, salaries, quotas or cut-offs.

## The Board and the four services
- The Board: began as the Customs, Immigration and Prisons Services Board under Decree No. 14 of
  1986, chaired by the minister of internal affairs; Customs was excised in 1992; Civil Defence
  added under the 2003 NSCDC Act; supervision of the Federal Fire Service transferred to it in
  March 2011; a 2022 bill sought to re-establish it under its current name. It handles
  appointment, promotion and discipline in the four services under the Ministry of Interior
  (search results summarising the CDCFIB about page, fmino.gov.ng and PLAC, read 2026-11-01).
  https://placng.org/i/president-muhammadu-buhari-transmits-bill-to-establish-board-of-the-civil-defence-correctional-federal-fire-and-immigration-services-to-the-national-assembly/
  https://law.ungovr.org/oversight/ng/civilian-review/cdcfib
- NSCDC (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-01): began May 1967 as the Lagos Civil Defence Committee; Corps
  name 1970 in most accounts (one secondary source dates the name to a 1984 decree; the NSCDC's own
  history says it became a national security outfit in 1984, so the bank says "1970 in most
  accounts" and asks no question keyed on the 1970 date); Act signed 28 June 2003, amended 2007; Ministry of Interior; protects critical national
  infrastructure and pipelines, crisis management and disaster response with the police; Agro
  Rangers created 2016 for farmers; ranks Commandant General down to Corps Assistant III.
  Licensing and supervision of private guard companies: NSCDC Act 2003 as amended 2007, s.3.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_Security_and_Civil_Defence_Corps
- NCoS (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-01): formerly the Nigerian Prisons Service; renamed by the
  Nigerian Correctional Service Act 2019 (signed 15 August 2019), which divides it into Custodial
  and Non-Custodial Services; Broad Street Prison, Lagos, opened 1872. Non-custodial measures
  (community service, probation, parole, restorative justice) and the "custodial centre" name are
  from the 2019 Act.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Correctional_Service
- NIS (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-01): separated from the Nigeria Police in 1958 as the Immigration
  Department; established by Act of Parliament on 1 August 1963; Immigration Act 2015 repealed the
  1963 Act and covers migrant smuggling; issues passports and CERPAC (Combined Expatriate Residence
  Permit and Aliens Card); machine-readable e-passports from 2007.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_Immigration_Service
- ECOWAS Protocol A/P1/5/79 on free movement: community citizens with valid travel documents may
  stay up to 90 days without a visa.
- FFS (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-01): began 1901 as the Lagos Fire Brigade; the Fire Service Act
  1963 made it a nationwide Federal Fire Service; firefighting, rescue, fire prevention and fire
  safety codes; Ministry of Interior, under the CDCFIB.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Fire_Service
- Heads: Commandant General (NSCDC); Controller General (NCoS, NIS, FFS). Titles only, no names.

## Fire safety, first aid and emergencies
- Fire triangle, extinguisher types (water for solids; CO2 for electrical; wet chemical for cooking
  oil), PASS, aim at the base, gauge zones, smoke and lifts: standard fire safety guidance as
  taught by fire services.
- First aid: danger first, recovery position, direct pressure, cool burns under running water
  about 20 minutes, adult CPR 30:2 (Resuscitation Council / ILCOR guidance).
- 112: Nigeria's toll-free national emergency number (NCC); FRSC's line is 122.
- NEMA (National Emergency Management Agency, established 1999); NIMET (weather); NIHSA (Annual
  Flood Outlook); NESREA (2007, environmental standards).

## Geography and environment
- 36 states plus the FCT; six geopolitical zones; Niger-Benue confluence at Lokoja; Chappal Waddi
  (about 2,419 m) in Taraba; Niger State largest and Lagos smallest by area; state capitals;
  Kainji on the Niger, Shiroro on the Kaduna; the Niger rises in Guinea, the Benue in Cameroon;
  harmattan roughly November to March; rainfall falling northwards; Olumo Rock (Abeokuta),
  Ikogosi (Ekiti), Yankari and Wikki (Bauchi), Obudu (Cross River); tin on the Jos Plateau.
  Standard atlas facts (Nigeria geography references), all static.
- Desertification and the Great Green Wall (Nigeria's National Agency for the Great Green Wall,
  2015); gully erosion in the south-east (Nanka, Agulu); gas flaring and oil spills in the Niger
  Delta; Lake Chad shrinking by about 90% since the 1960s; flooding from dam releases (Lagdo,
  Kainji) along the Benue and Niger. No year-specific flood figures.
