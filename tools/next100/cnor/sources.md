# CNOR (CCI) bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from CCI's own CNOR task and knowledge
statements and the public clinical and regulatory sources below. None is copied, adapted or
reworded from CCI's practice tests or study plan items, from AORN's Guidelines for Perioperative
Practice (paywalled, not read), or from any review book, course, app or prep site.

## Outline and format (Competency & Credentialing Institute)

- CCI, CNOR Candidate Handbook, "Updated 05/2026", version 5.2026 (PDF), fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://f.hubspotusercontent30.net/hubfs/2447632/Handbooks/CNOR%20Candidate%20Handbook.pdf
  200 multiple-choice questions, 185 scored and 15 unscored pretest; 3 hours 45 minutes; PSI
  test centers (Monday to Saturday, year-round) or Remote Secured Proctored Exam; raw score
  converted to a scaled score from 200 to 800, 620 to pass; a scaled score is not a percent.
  Subject areas and scored counts: 1 Pre/postoperative Patient Assessment and Diagnosis 15% (28);
  2 Individualized Plan of Care Development and Expected Outcome Identification 8% (15);
  3a Management of Intraoperative Activities, Patient Care and Safety 25% (46); 3b Management of
  Personnel, Services and Materials 9% (17); 4 Communication and Documentation 11% (20);
  5 Infection Prevention and Control of Environment, Instrumentation and Supplies 16% (30);
  6 Emergency Situations 10% (19); 7 Professional Accountabilities 6% (10). Eligibility: current
  unrestricted RN license; working in perioperative nursing; 2 years and 2,400 hours as a
  perioperative RN with at least 1,200 hours intraoperative (18 months and 2,400 hours for CFPN,
  CST, TS-C or military equivalent holders).
- CCI, CNOR Task & Knowledge Statements, "Updated 12/2023" (PDF), fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://2447632.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/2447632/Handbook%20Supplement%20Docs/CNOR%20Task%20&%20Knowledge%20Statements.pdf
  The task list each category follows (two identifiers, Universal Protocol, consent, medication
  reconciliation including herbal supplements, Aldrete score, pain assessment, NANDA and PNDS,
  advance directives and DNR; outcomes and plan of care; environmental hazards, skin prep,
  positioning, counts, specimens, implants and explants, medication labeling, wound
  classification, drains; supplies, cost containment, product evaluation, delegation, HCIR and
  visitor supervision, environmental stewardship; documentation, handoff, read-back, HIPAA,
  implant records, transfer of care; environmental cleaning, Spaulding, packaging, sterilization
  monitoring, attire by zone, hand hygiene and surgical scrub, loaner instruments; MH,
  anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest, trauma, hemorrhage, LAST, robotic emergencies, fire, disasters,
  active shooter; scope of practice, ANA Code, impaired and disruptive behavior, evidence-based
  practice, quality improvement, shared governance, professional organizations).
- CCI, CNOR credential page, fetched 2026-11-23: https://www.cc-institute.org/cnor/
  Same 200 questions, 3 hr 45 min and subject area percentages; pass or no pass; credential
  valid 5 years; continuous testing; 12-month eligibility, first attempt within 90 days, 30 days
  between attempts; $475 application with first attempt, $175 per retake (fees on the landing
  only, never in questions).
- CCI, 2024 CNOR and CFPN Job Task Analysis, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.cc-institute.org/cnor_cfpn_job_analysis/
  New CNOR exam content launches in 2027 (no date given) with six competency areas: plan of care
  42, intraoperative patient care and safety 49, personnel, services and materials 24,
  communication and documentation 20, infection prevention and control 33, emergency
  identification and management 17 items (185). The bank follows the current eight areas; the
  change is a freshness event.

## Clinical and regulatory sources read for this bank

- CDC/HICPAC, Guideline for Prevention of Surgical Site Infection, 1999 (Mangram et al.), full
  text copy hosted by SGNA, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.sgna.org/Portals/0/Issues/PDF/Infection-Prevention/17_CDC%20Surgical%20Site%20infections.pdf
  Hair removal only if it interferes, immediately before, with clippers; prep in concentric
  circles toward the periphery; 2 to 5 minute surgical scrub, hands up, short nails, no
  artificial nails or hand jewelry; exclude personnel with draining skin lesions; positive
  pressure, at least 15 air changes per hour with 3 fresh; doors closed; EPA disinfectant on
  visible soil; no special cleaning or closure after dirty cases; no tacky mats; flash
  sterilization only for immediate need; masks when sterile items exposed; caps; shoe covers not
  for SSI prevention; change soiled scrubs; closed suction drains through a separate incision;
  sterile dressing 24 to 48 hours; wound classes I to IV; hypothermia below 36 C.
- CDC/HICPAC, Guideline for the Prevention of Surgical Site Infection, 2017 (Berrios-Torres et
  al., JAMA Surg), copy hosted by VUMC, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.vumc.org/infection-prevention/sites/default/files/public_files/HICPAC%202017%20SSI%20Guidelines.pdf
  and CDC summary page https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/surgical-site-infection/index.html
  Glucose below 200 mg/dL, normothermia, alcohol-based skin prep, no extra prophylaxis doses after
  closure, preoperative bathing with soap.
- CDC/HICPAC, Guideline for Disinfection and Sterilization in Healthcare Facilities, 2008 (with
  updates), full text copy hosted by the University of Washington, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://depts.washington.edu/edgh/app-ipc/web/project-resources/Guideline_for_Sterilization_and_Disinfection.pdf
  Spaulding classes, cleaning before sterilization, chemical and biological indicators, event-
  related sterility, wet packs, low-temperature methods (hydrogen peroxide gas plasma, ethylene
  oxide with aeration), immediate-use limits, implants held for a negative BI, prion
  (Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease) processing.
- OSHA, 29 CFR 1910.1030 Bloodborne pathogens (eCFR), fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-Z/section-1910.1030
  Engineering and work practice controls, no bending or recapping of contaminated needles,
  hepatitis B vaccination offered within 10 working days of initial assignment.
- OSHA, Laser/Electrosurgery Plume and Hospitals eTool (surgical suite, smoke plume), read via
  search extract 2026-11-23: https://www.osha.gov/laser-electrosurgery-plume and
  https://www.osha.gov/etools/hospitals/surgical-suite/smoke-plume (no specific OSHA standard;
  local smoke evacuation recommended).
- FDA, surgical fire prevention (fire triangle, alcohol prep drying and pooling), read via
  search extract 2026-11-23: https://fda.gov/media/83903/download
- FDA, Reprocessing Single-Use Medical Devices: Information for Health Care Facilities, read via
  search extract 2026-11-23:
  https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/reprocessing-single-use-medical-devices-information-health-care-facilities
  Third-party and hospital reprocessors are held to the same requirements as the original
  manufacturer.
- The Joint Commission, Universal Protocol poster, read via search extract 2026-11-23
  (jointcommission.org returns 403 to automated fetches):
  https://www.jointcommission.org/-/media/tjc/documents/standards/universal-protocol/up_poster1pdf.pdf
  Mark the site when more than one location is possible; marked by the accountable practitioner
  who will be present; time-out before the procedure.
- WHO, Safe Surgery tools and resources, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/patient-safety/research/safe-surgery/tool-and-resources
  Checklist pauses at three points: before anesthesia, before incision, before the patient leaves.
- Haynes et al., A Surgical Safety Checklist to Reduce Morbidity and Mortality in a Global
  Population, NEJM 2009 (the WHO checklist study), checklist table read via search extract
  2026-11-23: https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/c5287a52-75bc-4131-9262-4c598fcde8cc/content
  Sign-out: the nurse confirms counts and that any specimen is correctly labeled with the
  patient's name.
- Rosenberg et al., Malignant hyperthermia: a review, Orphanet J Rare Dis 2015, PMC full text,
  fetched 2026-11-23: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4524368/
  Triggers (volatile agents, succinylcholine), rising end-tidal CO2 as an early sign, autosomal
  dominant, RYR1, dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg repeated, cooling stopped at 38.5 C, no calcium channel
  blockers.
- Cardona et al., World Allergy Organization Anaphylaxis Guidance 2020, PMC full text, fetched
  2026-11-23: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7607509/
  IM epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg to 0.5 mg in the anterolateral thigh, repeat every 5 to 15 minutes;
  perioperative triggers (neuromuscular blockers, chlorhexidine, latex).
- Local anesthetic systemic toxicity: awareness, recognition, and risk mitigation in the
  emergency department (2024), PMC full text, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11237253/
  Prodromal signs, airway first, lipid emulsion, bupivacaine most lipophilic, epinephrine 1
  microgram/kg or less.
- Positioning: Positioning patients for spine surgery: avoiding uncommon position-related
  complications, PMC full text, fetched 2026-11-23: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4133449/
  (chest roll under the chest, not in the axilla; ulnar nerve and pronation; brachial plexus);
  Critical biomechanical and clinical insights concerning tissue protection when positioning
  patients in the operating room: a scoping review, PMC full text, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7948884/ (pressure points, peroneal nerve, eyes).
- CISA, Active Shooter Pocket Card (May 2022), read via search extract 2026-11-23:
  https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/CISA-Active-Shooter-Pocket-Card-052022-508.pdf
  Run, hide, fight.

## Second review (2026-11-23)

- WHO Surgical Safety Checklist Implementation Manual (2009), PDF copy hosted by Leapfrog, read
  2026-11-23: https://www.leapfroggroup.org/sites/default/files/Files/WHO_SafeSurgeryChecklistImplementationManual.pdf
  Sign out: nurse verbally confirms specimen labelling ("read specimen labels aloud, including
  patient name"). Site marking: the surgeon performing the operation marks the site, usually with
  a permanent felt-tip marker, in cases with laterality or multiple structures or levels. This now
  supports CAR-031, CAR-032, CAR-033 (rewritten to the marker) and CAR-037 directly; the "visible
  after draping" item was rewritten because only a search extract supported it.
- FDA, Reprocessing Single-Use Medical Devices page, fetched 2026-11-23 (same URL as above):
  reprocessors of SUDs are held to the same regulatory requirements as the original manufacturer
  (MGT-005 confirmed).
- OSHA, Hospitals eTool, surgical suite smoke plume, fetched 2026-11-23 (same URL as above):
  evacuator in every room that generates plume, inlet within 2 inches of the site (CAR-009
  confirmed).
- CDC 2008 disinfection and sterilization guideline (UW copy above): burns from hot flash-sterilized
  instruments, cooled by air or immersion in sterile liquid such as saline (new INF-010); sterile
  items open to air become contaminated with time (new INF-031).
- LAST review PMC11237253 (above): aspirate before injection even with ultrasound, inject in small
  aliquots (new EMG-015).
- Positioning scoping review PMC7948884 (above): shear rises in Fowler and semi-Fowler positions
  (new CAR-019).
- Rewritten for repeats: CAR-019 (CST-PRE-019 peroneal nerve in lithotomy), EMG-015 (CST-PHM-012
  lipid emulsion), INF-031 (CST-PRE-015 first flap away from the body), INF-010 (CRCST SPD-PRP-039
  event-related sterility definition), CAR-003 (CST-INT-080 alcohol prep drying, now asks why).
- Widely taught OR practice kept without a public primary source because each key is the only
  defensible option: gown front sterile chest to field level (INF-032), joint count at a permanent
  relief handoff (CAR-029), industry reps give technical advice only (MGT-006), rapid robotic
  undocking drills (EMG-018), mask in the restricted zone (INF-026; CDC 1999 masks when sterile
  items are exposed), labeling medications on the field (CAR-034/035).
