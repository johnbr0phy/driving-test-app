# Sources for `corrections` (correctional officer entrance written exam, US, generic)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the subject areas and abilities the
official exam announcements below describe. Every rule, roster, log, passage and floor plan in a
question belongs to a fictional facility written for this bank; the items test reading, writing,
reasoning, math and direction skills, not any agency's real policy. No sample question from the
New York State test guide, no NYC DCAS item, no agency study guide and no commercial prep test or
app question was copied or reworded: the official guides were read only to learn which subject
areas and question types the written tests use. Checked 2026-11-23.

There is no national correctional officer exam. Each state, county, city or federal agency sets
its own hiring steps; many start with a multiple-choice written test. The bank models the subject
areas the official announcements below share.

## Exam owners (official)
- NYC Department of Citywide Administrative Services (DCAS), Notice of Examination, Correction
  Officer, Exam No. 6303 (amended April 29, 2026),
  https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dcas/downloads/pdf/noes/20266303000.pdf, fetched 2026-11-23. Gave:
  the multiple-choice test is taken at a computer terminal, ranks candidates on the eligible list
  and needs at least 70% to pass; it may test deductive reasoning, inductive reasoning, information
  ordering, mathematical reasoning, memorization, number facility, problem sensitivity, spatial
  orientation, visualization, written comprehension and written expression, each defined with a
  correction officer example (assigning tasks by rules, judging complaints, following orders in
  sequence, average miles per tour, remembering a room, totalling special meals, spotting rule
  breaks, finding the most direct route on a map, locating items after entering by another door,
  interpreting policies, writing reports with correct grammar, spelling and punctuation); only
  basic four-function calculators allowed; job duties (counts, searches for contraband, logbooks,
  escorts, supervising meals and visits, responding to incidents); appointment from age 21.
- New York State Department of Civil Service, "Test Guide for the Entry-Level Correction Officer
  Series", https://www.cs.ny.gov/testing/test_guides/CorrectionOfficer_EntryLevel_TestGuide.pdf,
  fetched 2026-11-23. Gave: a multiple-choice test with an overall time allowance of 3 hours in four
  subject areas: observing and recalling facts and information (study a picture or facts, then
  answer from memory, including true/false items); applying written information in a correctional
  services setting (all needed information is in the rules given); preparing written material
  (choose the clearest restatement of two or three sentences, and choose the best order for
  sentences of a paragraph); understanding and interpreting written material (answer only from the
  passage).
- New York State Department of Civil Service, "Correction Officer Trainee" overview,
  https://www.cs.ny.gov/testing/dec-instrument/cot-overview.cfm, fetched 2026-11-23. Gave: the
  current State Correction Officer Trainee examination is an online training and experience
  questionnaire, not a written test; candidates may retest every six months. Used on the landing
  to show that formats differ by agency.
- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, "Correctional Officers and
  Bailiffs", https://www.bls.gov/ooh/protective-service/correctional-officers.htm, fetched
  2026-11-23. Gave: duties (enforce rules, supervise people in custody, inspect for security
  problems, search people and property, report on conduct, escort and transport), a training
  academy after hiring, minimum ages usually 18 to 21, requirements vary by state and agency.

## Weighting (per 50-question test)
No owner publishes weights, so the split is Tigertest's, built on the NYS subject areas and the
NYC abilities: applying written rules 10, reading comprehension 8, preparing written material
(restatement, paragraph order, grammar, spelling and punctuation) 10, reasoning and problem
sensitivity (deductive, inductive, information ordering, spotting what is wrong) 10, math
(mathematical reasoning and number facility) 6, spatial orientation and visualization 6.
70% is used as the pass line because the NYC notice sets it; other agencies set their own.

## Not modelled
- Observing and recalling (memorization): it needs a picture or fact sheet studied for a set time
  and then taken away, which the practice engine does not have. The landing says so.
- Physical ability tests, medical and psychological exams, background checks, oral boards and
  the academy: described on the landing only as later steps.
- Any state's law, use-of-force statute, PREA details or agency policy: the real written tests
  give the rule in the question, so the bank does too.

## Left out on purpose
- Fees, salaries and test dates (they change every announcement).
- True/false items: written as four-option items, since the engine uses four options.

## Copyright check
The New York State test guide's sample items (a photo of inmates in a prison yard, a personal
grooming standards rule, an "information presentation" item about an officer who failed to take
precautions, a paragraph on choosing a corrections job, and a passage on transporting inmates)
were compared by hand and by keyword search with every item in this bank on 2026-11-23: no item
uses those topics, rules, sentences or option sets. NYC DCAS publishes no sample questions.
