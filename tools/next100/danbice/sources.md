# DANB Infection Control (ICE) bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from DANB's ICE exam outline and the
federal regulations and CDC guidance below. None is copied, adapted or reworded from DANB's or the
DALE Foundation's review course or practice test, the suggested textbooks, or any prep site, app
or book. DANB publishes no public question catalogue for the ICE exam, so there was no official
item list to check wording against; the bank was instead compared with the infection control
items already on Tigertest (see "Overlap check").

## Outline and format (Dental Assisting National Board)

- DANB, Infection Control (ICE) Exam Outline and References, effective 03/12/2025 (PDF), fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://danbsfprodassets.azureedge.net/assets/docs/default-source/exam-outlines/ice-exam-outline.pdf
  Component of the NELDA, CDA and COA certifications; no eligibility requirements. Domains:
  I Prevention of Disease Transmission 20% (infectious diseases, modes of transmission, patient
  safety, occupational risk; medical histories; hand hygiene; PPE selection, reuse, donning and
  doffing, disposal; aerosols, dental dams, evacuation), II Prevention of Cross-contamination 34%
  (treatment areas and labs, chemical disinfectants, surface barriers, tray setups with single-use
  devices, single unit dosing and aseptic retrieval, dental unit waterlines, evacuation lines and
  traps, equipment including radiography, impressions and appliances, waste), III Process
  Instruments and Devices 26% (transport, workflow, chemical agents, selecting the sterilizer,
  packaging and labeling, loading, storage; monitoring, interpreting results, malfunctions),
  IV Occupational Safety and Administration Protocols 20% (OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens and Hazard
  Communication standards, CDC guidelines, EPA and FDA rules; exposure control plan, breaches,
  quality assurance, sterilization logs, training records). 75 multiple-choice questions in
  60 minutes; test center or remote online proctoring; computer adaptive, the same share of
  questions from each domain for every candidate; scaled score 100 to 900, 400 to pass, neither
  number nor percent correct; modified Angoff standard setting. Suggested references: CDC 2003
  dental guidelines, CDC 2016 Summary, OSHA 1910.1030 and 1910.1200.
- DANB, 2026 ICE Application Packet (PDF, updated 05/15/26), fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://danbsfprodassets.azureedge.net/assets/docs/default-source/national-application-packets/ice-application-packet.pdf
  ICE exam fee $270 for traditional candidates ($265 active military), including a $75
  nonrefundable application fee; 60-day testing window after approval; offered in Spanish and
  Vietnamese; 2026 applications accepted through Dec. 31, 2026. Used for the landing only.
- DANB, ICE exam page, fetched 2026-11-23 (the page is rendered by script; only the navigation
  loaded, so no fact is taken from it): https://www.danb.org/exams/exam/ice
- DANB, 2026 Candidate Handbook (PDF), fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://danbsfprodassets.azureedge.net/assets/docs/default-source/candidate-handbooks/candidate-handbook.pdf?sfvrsn=61cda93e_19
  No limit on retakes of a failed exam; candidates reapply and pay the full fee each time.

## Federal regulation and CDC guidance (facts in questions)

- CDC, Guidelines for Infection Control in Dental Health-Care Settings, 2003 (MMWR 52 RR-17,
  PDF), fetched 2026-11-23: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/PDF/rr/rr5217.pdf
  Bloodborne risks (HIV 0.3%, HCV mean 1.8%, HDV needs HBV, no HCV vaccine); work restriction
  table (conjunctivitis until discharge ceases, herpetic whitlow until lesions heal, hepatitis A
  until 7 days after jaundice); conditions for worker-to-patient transmission; TB and airborne
  isolation rooms, NIOSH-certified N95 respirators fit-tested; surgical masks over 95% bacterial
  filtration; eyewear with solid side shields; reusable eyewear washed with soap and water;
  scrubs not PPE; long sleeves; dental dams and high-volume evacuation; surgical hand antisepsis
  2 to 6 minutes with persistent activity; liquid soap with hands-free dispensing; chipped nail
  polish; glove washing and wicking; FDA regulates gloves; barriers and the surface under them;
  end-of-day cleaning; housekeeping surfaces, walls, carpet, mops, fresh solution daily; blood
  spills and 1:100 bleach; regulated and general waste, double bagging, sanitary sewer; extracted
  teeth (return to patient, no incineration with amalgam, 10% formalin for 2 weeks); waterlines
  (biofilm, morning flushing, 20 to 30 seconds after each patient, manufacturer consultation,
  antiretraction valves, boil-water advisories and 1 to 5 minutes of flushing, sterile irrigation
  and bulb syringes, EPA drinking water standard); dental laboratory (cleaning, tuberculocidal
  disinfectant, written method, tamper-evident return, rag wheels, metal trays, articulators,
  pressure pots, PPE until disinfected); instrument processing (sections and partitions, holding
  solutions, automated cleaning, rinsing, no blind reaching, PPE, hinged instruments open,
  packaging for the method, FDA-cleared sterilizers, loading, drying, gravity and prevacuum steam,
  air removal test, chemical vapor with formaldehyde and its waste, bead sterilizers, liquid
  sterilants, ethylene oxide and handpieces, enclosed and event-related storage, dating and
  labeling); monitoring (mechanical, chemical, multiparameter indicators for steam only,
  biological indicators, implants quarantined, control BI, placement, mail-in services, positive
  spore test steps, loaner sterilizers, operator review, recall to the last negative BI, the
  conservative approach for non-steam methods, records kept per state and local rules).
- CDC, Summary of Infection Prevention Practices in Dental Settings: Basic Expectations for Safe
  Care, October 2016 (PDF on CDC Stacks), fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/40077/cdc_40077_DS1.pdf
  Infection prevention coordinator, written policies reassessed regularly (e.g., annually),
  training at orientation, with new tasks and at least annually; annual influenza vaccination
  offered; respiratory hygiene; hands away from face while in PPE; safe injection practices
  (alcohol on the septum, needle and cartridge for one patient, multidose vials discarded within
  28 days); single-use devices; reprocessing instructions near the reprocessing area and trained
  staff; the assessment checklist.
- OSHA, Bloodborne Pathogens, 29 CFR 1910.1030, eCFR text up to date as of 2026-11-19, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-Z/section-1910.1030
  (osha.gov returned 403 to automated fetches; the eCFR text was read through its versioner API.)
  Work practice and engineering control definitions; exposure control plan accessible and reviewed
  at least annually with input from frontline staff; hand-washing facilities or antiseptic
  cleanser; no shearing or breaking needles; banned activities; specimen containers; broken glass
  by mechanical means; reusable sharps; sharps containers (features, location, upright, replaced
  routinely, closed before moving); labels fluorescent orange or orange-red and red bags;
  laundry; written opinion within 15 days; source testing with consent; training at assignment
  and within one year; training records 3 years; sharps injury log; Appendix A declination.
- OSHA, Hazard Communication, 29 CFR 1910.1200, eCFR text up to date as of 2026-11-19, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-Z/section-1910.1200
  Signal words and Appendix C precedence; hazard statement definition; SDS section order;
  SDS with the initial shipment; SDSs accessible each shift; written program with a chemical
  list; portable containers for immediate use; labels not removed; training when a new hazard is
  introduced.
- EPA, Dental Office Category, 40 CFR Part 441, eCFR text up to date as of 2026-11-19, fetched 2026-11-23:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-N/part-441
  Amalgam separators meeting ISO 11143 with at least 95% removal; no oxidizing or acidic line
  cleaners (pH below 6 or above 8) in lines that discharge amalgam process wastewater.

## Left out or worded with care

- PPE removal order is not asked: the 2016 Summary points to the 2007 isolation guideline for it,
  which was not read; the bank asks only that hand hygiene is the final step.
- Ultrasonic solution change intervals, sterilizer cycle times and temperatures, and dry heat
  settings vary by manufacturer and are not asked.
- State rules (who may perform which duties, record retention years, waste disposal details) are
  not asserted; items say state and local rules decide.
- Fees, the passing score and exam format appear only on the landing and in examFacts.

## Overlap check

Every stem and key was compared, by word overlap and identical keys, with the 48 ICE items in the
DANB CDA bank (`data/danb-questions.json`), the 48 infection control items in the DANB RHS bank
(`data/danb-rhs-questions.json`), and with `crcst`, `cbspd-cspdt`, `hspa-cis`, `hspa-cer`,
`hspa-chl`, `cna`, `osha`, `nbdhe` and the UK Level 3 food safety bank (open PR). Items that asked
a fact those banks already key (chain-of-infection reservoir, biohazard label color, single
positive spore test retest, three empty-chamber cycles, indicator unchanged, sharps container
features, no reaching into reusable sharps, annual exposure control plan review, Danger as the
more severe signal word, SDS Section 4 and 8, 16 sections, SDS each shift, written program list,
disposal in Section 13, EPA registration number) were rewritten to a different fact. Facts the
DANB banks key (HBV vaccine within 10 working days, 30-year medical records, laundering, 500 CFU/mL,
weekly spore tests, Geobacillus, Spaulding categories, low and intermediate-level disinfectants,
barriers after each patient, spray-wipe-spray, overgloves, utility gloves) are not asked again.
