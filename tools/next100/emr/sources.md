# NREMT Emergency Medical Responder (EMR) sources

All 200 items are original, written from the official examination specifications, the national
scope of practice model, the national model clinical guidelines and the AHA guideline releases
below. Nothing is copied or reworded from NREMT sample items, any prep company, textbook question
bank or app.

## Outline and format (fetched 2026-11-23)
- NREMT EMR and EMT Certification Examinations page:
  https://nremt.org/Pages/Examinations/EMR-and-EMT-Certification-Examinations (fetched 2026-11-23).
  New EMR exam launched April 7, 2025 (last test date of the prior exam April 4, 2025), built from the
  2023 BLS practice analysis; domains and ranges: Scene Size-Up and Safety 19-23%, Primary Assessment
  37-41%, Secondary Assessment 4-8%, Patient Treatment and Transport 20-24%, Operations 10-14%, pediatric
  content integrated throughout; computer adaptive, at Pearson VUE or by OnVUE remote proctoring;
  technology-enhanced items (build list, drag and drop, options or check box); fee $88 per attempt; the
  state-approved BLS skills (psychomotor) requirement continues unchanged.
- National Registry Emergency Medical Responder Examination Specifications (test plan PDF):
  https://nremt.org/admin/getmedia/4657d30a-a955-43af-aa8f-a92388f0d9f4/EMR-Test-Plan_Public
  (fetched 2026-11-23, text extracted locally). Purpose; the job tasks under each of the five domains
  (plan before arrival, protect self and others, PPE, find patients, triage, request resources;
  communicate, general impression, consciousness, airway, breathing, circulation, chief complaint and
  life threats, baseline vitals, need for rapid treatment or transport; focused exam, interview, history
  and reassessment; manage airway, cardiovascular system, motion restriction, medication, special
  populations, transport and communication; equipment, inventory, documentation, responder well-being);
  CAT; 1 hour 45 minutes; minimum 90 and maximum 110 items including 30 unscored pilot items; item types
  multiple choice (one of four), multiple response (two or three of five or six, always three wrong),
  build list, drag and drop, options table; all scored all or nothing; passing standard set by a
  standard-setting study.
- EMR Candidate Handbook, About the Examination: https://nremt.org/Handbooks/EMR/Cognitive-Exam
  (fetched 2026-11-23): 90 to 110 questions, 30 do not count; score scale 100 to 1500 with 950 passing;
  results usually within two business days; three attempts, 15 days between attempts, a new full EMR
  course after three failures.
- EMR Candidate Handbook, Certification Process: https://nremt.org/Handbooks/EMR/Certification-Process
  (fetched 2026-11-23): $88 application fee for each attempt; initial course valid two years from
  completion; skills competency results valid 24 months; recertify every two years.
- NREMT Certification Examinations overview: https://nremt.org/Handbooks/Certification-Examinations/Overview
  (fetched 2026-11-23): one passing standard per level; on-screen calculator and tutorial.

## Scope of practice and clinical standards (fetched 2026-11-23)
- National EMS Scope of Practice Model 2019, Including Change Notices 1.0 and 2.0 (NHTSA DOT HS 813
  151, August 2021), copy hosted by the Minnesota EMS Regulatory Board:
  https://mn.gov/oems/assets/National_EMS_Scope_of_Practice_Model_2021_%20%28002%29_tcm1221-744908.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-23, text extracted locally). EMR description (initiates
  lifesaving care, relies on a larger response; may assist but must not be the highest level caring for a
  patient during ambulance transport; an EMT or higher must be in the patient compartment) and the skill
  tables. EMR column: oral airway, BVM, head tilt-chin lift, jaw thrust, mouth-to-barrier, mask, mouth,
  nose and stoma, manual removal of obstruction, oxygen by nasal cannula and nonrebreather, upper airway
  suction, CPR, AED, direct pressure, tourniquet, wound packing, cervical collar, manual cervical and
  extremity stabilization, extremity splinting, emergency moves, IM auto-injector and unit-dosed intranasal
  routes, auto-injector antidotes for chemical exposures, intranasal or auto-injector opioid antagonist,
  assisted uncomplicated delivery, manual blood pressure, eye irrigation. EMT and above only: nasal and
  supraglottic airways, CPAP, simple face and Venturi masks, pulse oximetry, mechanical CPR device, long
  spine board, seated SMR device, traction splint, mechanical restraint, epinephrine auto-injector, aspirin,
  oral glucose, inhaled bronchodilators, automated blood pressure, glucose monitoring, complicated delivery,
  patient transport. Every scope item says "under the national model" because states set the legal scope.
- NASEMSO National Model EMS Clinical Guidelines, Version 3.0 (March 2022), copy hosted by Iowa HHS:
  https://hhs.iowa.gov/media/198/download (fetched 2026-11-23). Used for: cardiac arrest (compressions at
  least 2 inches and less than 2.4 inches, 30:2 single rescuer, 15:2 for children with two rescuers, one
  breath every 6 seconds for an adult), extremity hemorrhage (commercial tourniquet on bare skin 2 to 3
  inches above the wound and not over a joint, a second tourniquet above the first if bleeding continues,
  mark the time, never loosen for pain, wound packing for junctional wounds), shock, opioid poisoning
  (ventilation before and after naloxone), spinal care and car-seat stabilization, burns, hyperthermia
  (cold-water immersion), hypothermia, childbirth and newborn care, seizures, suspected stroke (last known
  well, bring the witness), bites and envenomation, agitated
  patients, evisceration care (moist dressings), stinger removal, drowning, mass casualty triage
  (categories immediate, delayed, minimal, expectant, dead; SALT named as one accepted process).
- SALT Mass Casualty Triage (Sort, Assess, Lifesaving Interventions, Treatment/Transport), mcitriage.org,
  linked from ASPR TRACIE: https://mcitriage.org/salt (fetched 2026-11-23): walkers move to an area, the rest
  are asked to wave or move purposefully, those still are assessed first; lifesaving interventions within
  scope (control major hemorrhage, open the airway and give a child rescue breaths, chest decompression,
  auto-injector antidotes); individual assessment questions (breathing, obeys commands or purposeful
  movement, peripheral pulse, respiratory distress, hemorrhage controlled, likely to survive with current
  resources); expectant for patients not likely to survive with current resources.
  ASPR TRACIE entry: https://asprtracie.hhs.gov/technical-resources/resource/4680/salt-mass-casualty-triage-algorithm-sort-assess-lifesaving-interventions-treatment-transport (fetched 2026-11-23).
- American Heart Association news release, "Updated CPR guidelines tackle choking response,
  opioid-related emergencies and a revised Chain of Survival," October 22, 2025:
  https://newsroom.heart.org/news/updated-cpr-guidelines-tackle-choking-response-opioid-related-emergencies-and-a-revised-chain-of-survival
  (fetched 2026-11-23): alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts for a choking adult or child; for
  an infant 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts with the heel of one hand, no abdominal thrusts; opioid
  emergency algorithm and lay naloxone guidance.
- American Heart Association news release on the 2025 pediatric and neonatal guidelines, October 23, 2025:
  https://newsroom.heart.org/news/updated-cpr-guidelines-released-for-pediatric-and-neonatal-emergency-care-and-resuscitation
  (fetched 2026-11-23): infant compressions with two thumbs and encircling hands or the heel of one hand;
  the two-finger technique is no longer recommended.
- CDC, Sequence for putting on and removing PPE (Example 1 for removal: gloves, goggles or face shield,
  gown, mask or respirator, hand hygiene): https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/Toolkits-PPE-Sequence-P.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-23, text extracted locally).

## Copyright and similarity check (2026-11-23)
The test plan's Appendix A sample items (images on pages 7 and 8) were rendered and read: a pool rescue
asking whether to dry the chest for the AED or check a pulse first, a pacing, yelling patient (safe
distance and verbal de-escalation), a build list for an apneic patient pulled from a pool, normal vital
signs for a 2-year-old, and an options table of abdominal organs. Three first-draft items were close and
were replaced: the agitated-patient item (now a scope question on mechanical restraint), the wet-chest AED
item (now chest hair) and a drowning dispatch item (now a bathtub, about planning, not the sequence). No
item asks about a pool rescue sequence, de-escalation of a yelling patient, a 2-year-old's normal vitals
or abdominal organ locations.

Every item was compared with data/v2/emt, data/v2/aemt, data/v2/paramedic and the CPR, lifeguard,
firefighter, standard first aid (Canada), first aid at work (UK), Australian and New Zealand first aid
banks (stem word overlap over 0.35, or over 0.2 with the same key). Items that repeated a sibling's fact
and answer were rewritten with a different fact: TB respirator (now the mask on the patient), tourniquet
placement (now a second tourniquet), PCR error correction (now a late entry), oxygen cylinder setup (now
shutting oxygen down), adult compression rate (now the depth upper limit), rescue breathing rate (now the
pulse recheck), breathing check time (now checking the pulse at the same time), infant choking (now the
heel of one hand), pregnant choking and self-rescue (now signs the airway has cleared), AED pads on a child
(now mechanical CPR scope), child compression depth (now which hands), heat stroke (now cold-water
immersion), chemical eye flushing (now the direction of flow), chest recoil, compression pauses and rescuer
swaps (now leaning, a partner's pulse checks and why fatigue matters), the supine pregnant patient (now the
reason), newborn stimulation (now ventilation next), seizure care (now the position afterwards), snakebite
(now a bee stinger), amputated finger (now evisceration), forearm and collarbone splints (now rings and
watches), nosebleed and scalp bleeding (now an absent radial pulse and bandage tightness), blood splash
(now reporting the exposure), sharps (now why not to recap).

## Blueprint
Per 60-item practice test, inside the NREMT ranges: Scene Size-up and Safety 13 (21.7%), Primary
Assessment 23 (38.3%, split by Tigertest into responsiveness, airway and breathing 12 and circulation,
vital signs and priorities 11), Secondary Assessment 4 (6.7%), Patient Treatment and Transport 13
(21.7%), Operations 7 (11.7%). Pools (tests 1, 2 and drill-only): 13/13/14, 12/12/10, 11/11/10, 4/4/12,
13/13/20, 7/7/14 = 200. 60 items in 70 minutes keeps the real pace (90 items in 105 minutes). Fixed
linear forms stand in for the adaptive exam.

## Facts deliberately avoided
Fees inside questions, state-specific scope (every scope item follows the national model and says states
may differ), drug doses, AED energy settings, the 2025 AHA changes that could not be read in full
(cpr.heart.org returns 403), pulse oximetry and glucose values (not EMR skills), and the NREMT test itself.
