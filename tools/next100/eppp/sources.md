# EPPP Part 1 (Knowledge) bank sources

Every question in this bank is original, written by Tigertest from the content outline and the
primary sources below. No ASPPB sample question (Appendix F of the handbook), sample exam
(PEP/SEPPPO) item or prep-company question was copied or reworded.

## Outline and format (ASPPB, the exam owner)

- ASPPB, "The EPPP Candidate Handbook" (November 2026, file
  The-EPPP-Candidate-Handbook_FINAL-November-2026-1.pdf), linked from
  https://asppb.net/exams/asppb-examination-for-professional-psychology-eppp/eppp-handbook/ ,
  downloaded and read as PDF text, fetched 2026-11-22:
  https://asppb.net/wp-content/uploads/The-EPPP-Candidate-Handbook_FINAL-November-2026-1.pdf
  - Part 1 has eight content areas with weights: 1 Biological bases of behavior 10%,
    2 Cognitive-affective bases 13%, 3 Social and cultural bases 11%, 4 Growth and lifespan
    development 12%, 5 Assessment and diagnosis 16%, 6 Treatment, intervention, prevention and
    supervision 15%, 7 Research methods and statistics 7%, 8 Ethical, legal and professional
    issues 16%. Knowledge statements KN1 to KN71 (Appendix E), based on ASPPB's 2017 job task
    analysis.
  - Format: 225 items, 175 scored and 50 unscored pretest items; "three or four possible
    responses, only one of which is the correct answer" (Appendix E); 4 hours 15 minutes for the
    items, at Pearson VUE test centers; a French version (FEPPP) can be scheduled.
  - Scoring: raw scores converted to a 200 to 800 scale; ASPPB's recommended passing score is 500
    for independent practice (450 for supervised practice in some jurisdictions); each board sets
    its own standard. No percentage pass mark is published.
  - Retakes: no more than four times in any 12-month period; ASPPB recommends waiting about 90
    days; each licensing authority sets its own retake rules.
  - Transition: the integrated EPPP is scheduled to become operational in the fourth quarter of
    2027; from then Part 1 and Part 2 are closed to first-time takers; candidates who failed before
    then may retake until March 31, 2028; from April 1, 2028 only the integrated EPPP is offered.
- ASPPB, "Examination for Professional Practice in Psychology (EPPP) Content Areas",
  https://asppb.net/?p=235 , fetched 2026-11-22 (same eight domains, weights and KN statements).
- ASPPB, "ASPPB Announces Content Specifications for the EPPP Operational in Fall of 2027"
  (February 11, 2026), https://asppb.net/?p=2256 , fetched 2026-11-22: Part 1 and Part 2 given in
  2026 and 2027 use their published blueprints; beta test spring 2027.
- ASPPB, "The EPPP Is Changing: Here's What You Need to Know" (October 29, 2026),
  https://asppb.net/news/faq-integrated-eppp-beta-test-frequently-asked-questions/ , fetched
  2026-11-22: beta opens early 2027; the two-part exam retires within about 18 months.
- ASPPB, "EPPP Updated Timeline" (October 27, 2026), https://asppb.net/news/eppp-updated-timeline/ ,
  fetched 2026-11-22. Its PDF (EPPP-BLUEPRINT-LAUNCH-TIMELINE_10.21.2026.pdf) is an image with no
  text layer, so its dates were taken from the November 2026 handbook instead.
- An older handbook (May 2024), https://asppb.net/wp-content/uploads/EPPP-Candidate-Handbook.pdf ,
  fetched 2026-11-22, gives the same format, with "four possible responses" per item.

## Ethics and law

- American Psychological Association, "Ethical Principles of Psychologists and Code of Conduct"
  (2002, amended 2010 and 2016, effective January 1, 2017),
  https://www.apa.org/ethics/code/ethics-code-2017.pdf , fetched 2026-11-22 and read as text.
  Standards used: 1.02 (2010 human-rights amendment), 1.04, 1.05, 2.01(d), 2.03, 2.05, 3.05,
  5.05, 6.05, 6.06, 7.04, 7.06, 8.05, 8.07(c), 8.12(b)(c), 9.04, 9.09, 9.11, 10.02, 10.04, 10.07,
  10.08, 10.10. APA's Ethics Code Task Force is revising the code; no revision had been adopted
  when checked (web search, 2026-11-22), so items cite the 2017 code and avoid wording that a
  revision is likely to change.
- Court decisions named in items, from their published holdings: Tarasoff v. Regents of the
  University of California (Cal. 1976), Jaffee v. Redmond (U.S. 1996), Griggs v. Duke Power Co.
  (U.S. 1971), Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals (U.S. 1993). Duty-to-protect rules are
  stated as varying by state.

## Diagnosis

- American Psychiatric Association, DSM page, https://www.psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/dsm ,
  fetched 2026-11-22: DSM-5-TR is current; revised criteria and text are expected in early 2027.
  Diagnostic items use DSM-5-TR (intellectual disability needs intellectual and adaptive deficits
  with onset in the developmental period; delirium's disturbance in attention and awareness;
  autism spectrum disorder with severity levels).

## Checked against sibling banks

Before finishing, every key was compared with the keys of every other Tigertest bank, and items
whose stem and answer tested the same fact were rewritten (ASWB Bachelors, Masters and Clinical,
NCE, ADC, BCaBA, PANCE, the Philippine psychometrician and LET banks, NASM, SHRM-CP, RHIA,
Praxis PLT and AP Psychology). Psychometric numbers were changed where the psychometrician bank
used the same figures.

## Left out on purpose

Fees, score-transfer costs, state-specific licensing rules, retake waits and anything about the
test itself; current-affairs items; specific drug doses. Part 2 (Skills) and the integrated EPPP
are not modelled.
