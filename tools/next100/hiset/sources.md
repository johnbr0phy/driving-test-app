# HiSET (`hiset`): sources

All 288 questions and all of their passages, drafts, data tables and excerpts are original, written for Tigertest
from the HiSET content categories below. No HiSET practice test, sample question, released item, study companion
item or commercial prep material was used or paraphrased. One free practice test PDF on hiset.org was opened only
to confirm the answer format (four choices, A to D, and "No change" revision items on the Writing test); its
questions were not read for content. Historical documents quoted in Social Studies (the U.S. Constitution and its
amendments, the Declaration of Independence) are public-domain primary texts, quoted briefly and checked against the
National Archives transcriptions.

## Format and content outline (fetched 2026-11-14)
- PSI, "HiSET Test at a Glance (TAAG) Information Brief", (c) 2025 PSI Services LLC, PDF modified April 2026:
  https://hiset.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/taag.pdf
  - Language Arts - Reading: 65 minutes (English), 80 minutes (Spanish); 50 multiple-choice questions; Literary
    Texts 40%, Informational Texts 60%; process categories Comprehension, Inference and Interpretation, Analysis,
    Synthesis and Generalization; texts about 400 to 600 words (narratives, memoirs, essays, biographical sketches,
    editorials, poetry).
  - Language Arts - Writing: 120 minutes; 61 questions, 60 multiple choice plus 1 essay; multiple-choice content
    Organization of Ideas 22%, Language Facility 43%, Writing Conventions 35%; items are revisions of underlined or
    highlighted parts of drafts (letters, essays, newspaper articles, personal accounts, reports). The essay is
    evidence-based writing from a pair of texts, scored on a 1 to 6 guide.
  - Mathematics: 90 minutes; 55 multiple-choice questions; calculator neutral (a calculator is provided at the test
    center on request); Numbers and Operations on Numbers 19%, Measurement/Geometry 18%, Data
    Analysis/Probability/Statistics 18%, Algebraic Concepts 45%; a formula sheet is given, but d = rt, the
    Pythagorean Theorem and the quadratic formula are not on it.
  - Science: 80 minutes; 60 multiple-choice questions; Life Science 49%, Physical Science 28%, Earth Science 23%;
    most questions go with stimulus material describing investigations and results (graphs, tables, charts).
  - Social Studies: 70 minutes; 60 multiple-choice questions; History 35%, Civics/Government 35%, Economics 20%,
    Geography 10%; primary documents, cartoons, timelines, maps, graphs, tables and passages; fact, opinion and
    reliability of sources.
  - Each test "may contain some questions that will not count toward your score".
- PSI, "HiSET Test Taker Bulletin 2026", version 2.0, 1 June 2026:
  https://hiset.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/hiset_test-taker-bulletin.pdf
  - Five subtests (50, 60 + essay, 55, 60, 60 multiple-choice questions); computer or paper, at a test center or at
    home with a remote proctor; two free retests within 12 months of the purchase date.
  - Passing: each subtest scored 1 to 20; at least 8 on each of the five subtests, at least 2 out of 6 on the essay,
    and a total of at least 45 across all five. Writing scores cannot be mixed from different testing dates.
  - College and Career Readiness: at least 15 on a subtest (Writing: 15 total and at least 4 on the essay).
- PSI, HiSET "Get Your Scores" page: https://hiset.org/get-your-scores/ (fetched 2026-11-14): passing score 8 out
  of 20 on each subtest, at least 2 of 6 on the essay, a total scaled score of at least 45 out of 100.
- PSI, "HiSET Adult Educator Roadmap", September 2025:
  https://hiset.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/hiset_adult_educator_roadmap_sep2025-digital.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-14)
  - Exam overview: Reading 65 min (English) / 80 min (Spanish), Writing multiple choice plus one essay 120 min,
    Mathematics 90 min, Science 80 min, Social Studies 70 min, all multiple choice except the essay; total 7 hr 5 min
    (English).
- PSI, "HiSET Program Manual": https://hiset.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/hiset_program_manual.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-14)
  - Paper administration scripts: Mathematics 55 questions in 90 minutes; Science 60 in 80; Social Studies 60 in 70;
    Reading 50 in 65 (English); Writing 60 multiple choice and 1 essay in 120 minutes.
- PSI, Free Practice Test FPT9, Language Arts - Writing (half length):
  https://hiset.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/hiset_fpt9_en_writing.pdf (fetched 2026-11-14)
  - Opened for format only: four options (A) to (D), drafts with numbered paragraphs and suggestions for revision,
    "No change" options.

Not published by PSI, so not stated: how the 120 Writing minutes split between the multiple-choice part and the essay
(Tigertest sets its own pace), and the raw-to-scaled conversion for any subtest.

## Modelling choices
- Each subtest is a shorter timed section at roughly the real pace: Reading 20 in 26 min (real 50 in 65), Writing
  multiple choice 20 in 25 min (Tigertest's pace; the essay is not modelled), Mathematics 22 in 36 min (real 55 in
  90), Science 20 in 27 min (real 60 in 80), Social Studies 20 in 23 min (real 60 in 70). Two practice tests; Test 2
  is held out of the drills.
- Per test: Reading 8 literary / 12 informational (40/60); Writing 4 organization / 9 language facility / 7
  conventions (22/43/35 of 20); Mathematics 4 / 4 / 4 / 10 (19/18/18/45 of 22); Science 10 life / 5 physical / 5
  earth (49/28/23 of 20); Social Studies 7 history / 7 civics / 4 economics / 2 geography (35/35/20/10).
- All questions are single-answer with four choices, like the real test. Writing revision items keep "NO CHANGE"
  first in a fixed order. Reading passages are shorter than the real 400 to 600 words so a practice test fits a
  sitting.
- Scores: each section's percent correct is converted to an estimated 1 to 20 score with Tigertest's own curve, and
  the composite is the sum of the five (5 to 100). PSI publishes no conversion table, so these are labelled
  estimates everywhere; the Writing estimate leaves out the essay.
- Left out: the essay, current officeholders, fees (set by each state), state rules on who may test.
