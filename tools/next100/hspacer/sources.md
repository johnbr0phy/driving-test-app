# CER (HSPA) bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the official outline and the public
standards and guidance below. None is copied, adapted or reworded from HSPA practice exams, HSPA
lesson plans, the Endoscope Reprocessing Manual's review questions, released forms, or any
commercial review book or prep site.

## Outline (Healthcare Sterile Processing Association)

- HSPA, Certified Endoscope Reprocessor (CER) Exam Content Outline, Revised May 2022 (from the
  2021-2022 job task analysis), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://myhspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/CER_Exam_Content_Outline.pdf
  Seven sections with weights: 1 Microbiology and Infection Control 12%; 2 Endoscope Purpose,
  Design and Structure 10%; 3 Work Area Design 12%; 4 Endoscope Processing Steps 32% (point-of-use
  care through recall process, items A to U); 5 Endoscope Handling, Transport and Storage 16%;
  6 Endoscope Tracking, Repair and System Maintenance 10%; 7 Human Factors That Impact Endoscope
  Systems 8%. Section 4 is split in this bank into cleaning (items A to I) and disinfection,
  sterilization and documentation (items J to U). (The PDF's intro text says "four sections" and
  "Healthcare Leader", a copy-over from the CHL outline; the seven weighted sections are what it
  lists.)
- HSPA, Certified Endoscope Reprocessor (CER) certification page, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://myhspa.org/certification/certification-overview/certified-endoscope-reprocessor-cer/
  Standalone certification (CRCST not required); three months of hands-on endoscope reprocessing
  experience within the past three years; annual recertification by continuing education.

## Format (HSPA Certification Handbook, revised January 2026)

- HSPA, Certification Handbook, Revised January 2026, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://myhspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2024-Certification-Handbook.pdf
  CER exam: 150 multiple-choice questions, 125 scored and 25 unscored pretest questions mixed in;
  computer based at Prometric, no paper version; 3 hours, with an optional 15-minute tutorial
  before the clock starts; questions can be marked for review. Pass/fail is set by the number of
  scored questions answered correctly; the passing point was set by the Angoff procedure with the
  Beuk compromise and is not published as a percentage; result reports show section performance
  as At or Above, Below or Well Below, and HSPA does not give percentage scores. 120 days to sit
  the exam after approval. References HSPA names for the exam: HSPA Endoscope Reprocessing Manual,
  2nd ed (2022); ANSI/AAMI ST91 (2022); CDC Essential Elements of a Reprocessing Program for
  Flexible Endoscopes (2017); SGNA Standard of Infection Prevention in the Gastroenterology Setting
  (2024) and Guidelines for Use of High-Level Disinfectants and Sterilants (2017). The 70% line on
  Tigertest's tests is a readiness target, not HSPA's standard. Fees are never stated.

## Standards and guidance used in questions

- CDC HICPAC, Essential Elements of a Reprocessing Program for Flexible Endoscopes, fetched
  2026-11-15: https://www.cdc.gov/hicpac/media/pdfs/essential-elements-508.pdf
  The seven essential steps (pre-clean immediately at point of use per IFU; leak test after each
  use and before manual cleaning; meticulous manual cleaning within the IFU time, the most critical
  step; visual inspection with lighted magnification where needed; HLD or sterilization per IFU;
  storage that prevents recontamination, protects from damage and promotes drying, hanging
  vertically without coiling or touching the bottom, or in a cabinet designed for horizontal
  storage; documentation). Also: loaners inspected, leak tested and reprocessed before use;
  single-use devices not reprocessed except under FDA rules; hepatitis B vaccine and PPE access;
  scheduling and staffing to avoid delays and knowledge of IFU delayed-processing steps; competency
  per scope type; certification does not replace competency; water meeting manufacturer
  specifications; documenting scope and patient identifiers, procedure end time and manual
  cleaning start time, disinfectant concentration results and strip and chemical expiration,
  preventive maintenance and repairs, investigations of failures, retention per facility policy;
  inventory with unique identifiers and status; separate reprocessing space, one-way workflow,
  negative pressure in a separate manual cleaning room, HVAC suited to chemicals, separate hand
  sink, eyewash in the room but not at the decontamination sink, IFUs and SDS available;
  competencies at hire, periodically, with new scopes, equipment or chemicals and IFU changes, by
  direct observation; risk assessment items (including staffing for emergency procedures), AER
  validated for the scope, periodic audits; breach review by a multidisciplinary team.
- CDC, Disinfection and Sterilization: Summary of Recommendations, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/disinfection-sterilization/summary-recommendations.html
  Spaulding classes; flush and brush channels until no debris shows; discard enzymatic cleaner
  after each use; immerse completely and perfuse channels; FDA-cleared HLD; greater than 2%
  glutaraldehyde 20 minutes at 20°C; rinse, then 70% to 90% alcohol flush, then forced air;
  carrying case not for storage or transport; MEC tested at least daily of use and not used past
  reuse life; AER connectors attached, AER compatibility, conflicting IFUs resolved with both
  manufacturers; mechanical, chemical and biological monitoring, BIs at least weekly and with
  implants, Geobacillus stearothermophilus for steam; non-steam positive BI recall rule; FDA-cleared
  packaging; loose loading; peracetic acid items used immediately; prions the exception to
  standard sterilization.
- OSHA, Bloodborne Pathogens, 29 CFR 1910.1030, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1030
  Hepatitis B vaccine made available to employees with occupational exposure; no eating or drinking
  in work areas with likely exposure.
- OSHA, Medical services and first aid, 29 CFR 1910.151(c), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.151
  Quick drenching or flushing facilities where corrosive materials are used.
- OSHA, Hazard Communication, fetched 2026-11-15: https://www.osha.gov/hazcom
  Safety Data Sheets accessible to employees.
- FDA, Reprocessing of Reusable Medical Devices, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/reprocessing-reusable-medical-devices
  Reprocessing as a multistep clean then disinfect or sterilize process following labeling;
  duodenoscope and AER information pages.

## Could not be loaded or confirmed

- ANSI/AAMI ST91 (2022), the HSPA Endoscope Reprocessing Manual and the SGNA documents are sold or
  member-only and were not read. No question asserts an ST91 or SGNA figure (for example hang time
  limits, drying times or cabinet airflow numbers); hang time is asked only as a facility risk
  assessment decision, and cabinet and drying questions follow CDC.
- Some endoscope anatomy items (control head valves, bending section, light guide connector,
  biopsy port, venting cap) are general device knowledge stated at concept level with "per the
  IFU", since manufacturers' IFUs differ by model.

## Left out on purpose

- Fees, eligibility hours as numbers in questions, the exam's format and passing standard.
- Brand-specific settings, specific HLD products' times other than the CDC-cited glutaraldehyde
  figure, and anything repeated from the CRCST bank (data/crcst-questions.json), such as
  ultrasonic cleaning, OPA temperature handling, ST79 air exchange numbers and 5S.
