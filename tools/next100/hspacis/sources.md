# CIS (HSPA) bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the official outline and the public
regulations, standards and guidance below. None is copied, adapted or reworded from HSPA practice
exams, HSPA lesson plans, the Sterile Processing Instrument Manual or Instrument Resource Manual
review questions, released forms, or any commercial review book or prep site.

## Outline (Healthcare Sterile Processing Association)

- HSPA, Certified Instrument Specialist (CIS) Exam Content Outline, Revised October 2024 (from the
  2024 job task analysis), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://myhspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/HSPA_CIS_Content_Outline_2024.pdf
  Six sections with weights: I Decontamination Processes 12% (cleaning quality assurance, the
  cleaning toolbox of water, chemistry, PPE and implements, and specialized instrumentation such as
  ophthalmic, robotics, powered equipment, Ebola, prions, chemo and TASS); II Instrumentation
  Identification 32% (single-use, reposable and reusable; surgical and floor grade; specialty
  instruments and terminology; risk factors such as cement, magnetization and viscoelastic;
  materials such as nickel, titanium, aluminum and tungsten carbide; implants; marking methods;
  catalogs); III Instrumentation Inspection, Testing, Integrity and Assembly 20%; IV Preparation and
  Packaging 10%; V Disinfection and Sterilization Processes 6%; VI Quality and Information Systems
  20% (training, customer relations and process improvement, audits, tracking systems and UDI,
  loaned instrumentation, water quality with categories utility, critical and steam and examples of
  daily visual and monthly endotoxin checks). Linked from HSPA's Forms and Applications page
  (https://myhspa.org/forms-and-applications.html, fetched 2026-11-15). Section II is split in this
  bank into grades and materials (II.A, II.C), specialty instruments and terminology (II.B) and
  implants and identification methods (II.D, II.E); Section III into inspection and testing (A to
  D) and preventive maintenance and repair (E); Section VI into training, customer relations and
  audits (A to C) and tracking, loaners and water (D to F).
- HSPA, Certified Instrument Specialist (CIS) certification page, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://myhspa.org/certification/certification-overview/certified-instrument-specialist-cis/
  A secondary certification: current CRCST required and kept current; 200 hours of hands-on SPD
  experience, separate from CRCST hours; annual recertification by continuing education; the six
  knowledge domains with the October 2024 outline; computer based, no written version; questions
  can be marked for review.

## Format (HSPA Certification Handbook, revised January 2026)

- HSPA, Certification Handbook, Revised January 2026, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://myhspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026-Certification-Handbook.pdf
  CIS exam: 150 multiple-choice questions, 125 scored and 25 unscored pretest questions mixed in;
  computer based at Prometric, no paper version; 3 hours, with an optional 15-minute tutorial
  before the clock starts; questions can be marked for review. Pass or fail is decided by the
  number of scored questions answered correctly; passing points for CRCST, CIS, CER and CHL were set
  by the Angoff procedure with the Beuk compromise and are not published as a percentage.
  Eligibility: current CRCST plus 200 hours of hands-on SPD experience in the past 5 years (56
  decontamination, 55 assembly, preparation and packaging, 28 sterilization and disinfection, 20
  storage and distribution, 33 quality and information systems, 8 surgical procedure observation),
  documented by a supervisor; 120 days to sit after approval; annual recertification. The 70% line
  on Tigertest's tests is a readiness target, not HSPA's standard. Fees are never stated.

## Regulations, standards and guidance used in questions

- FDA, 21 CFR 878.4800 (manual surgical instrument for general use), 888.4540 (orthopedic manual
  surgical instrument), 882.4840 (manual rongeur), 886.4350 (manual ophthalmic surgical
  instrument), 874.4420 (ENT manual surgical instrument), 884.4530 (obstetric-gynecologic
  specialized manual instrument), 870.4500 (cardiovascular surgical instruments), 872.4565 (dental
  hand instrument), 888.3030 and 888.3040 (metallic bone fixation appliances and fasteners), read
  through the eCFR API, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H
  Instrument names and uses (rongeur biting bone of skull or spine; lid speculum, cystotome;
  tracheal bistoury as a long, narrow knife, mouth gag; amniotome, cervical dilators as bougies,
  tenaculum; amalgam carrier; implant alloys cobalt-chromium-molybdenum, stainless steel, titanium).
- FDA, UDI rule, 21 CFR 801.20 and 801.45, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-801/subpart-B
  Direct marking for devices used more than once and reprocessed before each use, exceptions where
  marking would interfere with safety or effectiveness or is not feasible.
- FDA, UDI Basics, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/unique-device-identification-system-udi-system/udi-basics
  Device identifier (labeler and version or model) and production identifier (lot, serial,
  expiration, manufacture date); GUDID holds only the DI.
- FDA, Reprocessing Medical Devices in Health Care Settings: Validation Methods and Labeling,
  guidance issued March 17, 2015 (Appendix E updated June 9, 2017), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.fda.gov/media/80265/download
  Point-of-use processing, thorough cleaning, disinfection or sterilization; soil markers such as
  total organic carbon and protein, spore log reduction not recommended for cleaning; final rinse
  water sufficient to remove endotoxins, tap water may contain endotoxins, AAMI TIR34 referred to;
  lubricants (oil and silicone may shield microbes); visual inspection with magnification and
  lighting, repeat cleaning or dispose, failure criteria corrosion, discoloration, pitting, cracked
  seals; reuse life limited to a number of cycles needs a tracking method; design features that
  challenge cleaning (lumens, hinges, junctions of insulating sheaths).
- CDC, Disinfection and Sterilization: Summary of Recommendations, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/disinfection-sterilization/summary-recommendations.html
  MEC testing of high-level disinfectants at least daily; FDA 510(k)-cleared packaging strong
  enough to resist punctures and tears; mechanical and chemical indicators each load, items not used
  if they indicate inadequate processing; BI at least weekly and with every implant load; single
  positive steam BI and non-implant items not recalled, non-steam positive BI items treated as
  nonsterile; avoid flash sterilization of implants unless unavoidable and not for convenience;
  cycle records with sterilizer, cycle and load number.
- CDC, Infection Control for CJD, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.cdc.gov/creutzfeldt-jakob/hcp/infection-control/index.html
  High infectivity tissue brain, spinal cord, eyes; low infectivity CSF, kidney, liver, lung and
  others; destroying heat-resistant instruments is safest; keep instruments moist; 1N NaOH methods
  before autoclaving; incinerate disposables.
- CDC, Infection Prevention and Control for VHF in hospitals, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.cdc.gov/viral-hemorrhagic-fevers/hcp/infection-control/index.html
  Dedicated medical equipment, preferably disposable.
- CDC, Sequence for Putting On and Removing PPE, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.cdc.gov/hai/pdfs/ppe/ppe-sequence.pdf
  Example 1 removal order: gloves, goggles or face shield, gown, mask, then hand hygiene.
- CMS, S&C 14-44 (Immediate Use Steam Sterilization in Surgical Settings), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/Provider-Enrollment-and-Certification/SurveyCertificationGenInfo/Downloads/Survey-and-Cert-Letter-14-44.pdf
  IUSS not a substitute for inventory; same cleaning as terminal sterilization; physical monitors
  and chemical indicators each cycle, BI at least weekly; implants processed by IUSS linked to the
  patient; IUSS limited to urgent need.
- ASCRS, AAO and OOSS, Recommendations Regarding Use of Enzyme Detergent for Cleaning Intraocular
  Surgical Instruments, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.ascrs.org/-/media/files/advocacy/recommendations-re-enzyme-final.pdf
  TASS risk factors (inadequate flushing of handpieces, enzyme detergents, ultrasound baths);
  enzyme detergent not required; sterile distilled or deionized final rinse because tap water may
  carry heat-stable endotoxin.
- OSHA, Bloodborne Pathogens, 29 CFR 1910.1030, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1030
  PPE provided by the employer at no cost to the employee.
- OSHA, Hazard Communication, fetched 2026-11-15: https://www.osha.gov/hazcom
  Safety Data Sheets accessible to employees.

## Could not be loaded or confirmed

- The references HSPA names for the exam (Sterile Processing Instrument Manual 2nd ed, Instrument
  Resource Manual, SP Technical Manual 9th ed, The World of Surgical Instruments, ANSI/AAMI ST79)
  are sold and were not read. No question asserts an ST79, ST108 or AORN figure (weight limits,
  water parameter targets, insulation test intervals).
- Instrument design conventions with no free primary text (gold handles for tungsten carbide
  inserts, black handles for extra-sharp scissors, satin and ebonized finishes, osteotome and
  chisel bevels, named instruments such as Penfield, Gelpi, Kerrison, Satinsky, DeBakey, Potts,
  Deaver, Hegar, the eraser test for rust versus stain, heat tint) are general instrument
  knowledge stated at concept level and worded "usually" or "often" where makers differ.
- Water categories, parameters and the daily visual and monthly endotoxin examples come from the
  HSPA outline itself; AAMI ST108 was not read, so no numeric targets are asked.
- myhspa.org's HTML pages showed a bot check to one fetcher; the PDFs and pages above were read by
  direct download.

## Left out on purpose

- Fees, eligibility hours as numbers in questions, the exam's format and passing standard.
- Chemotherapy-contaminated instrument handling (no free primary guidance read), and anything
  repeated from the CRCST, CER, CHL, CST and CBSPD banks (ratchet tap test, Kerrison index card
  test, scissor test media by length, the eraser stain answer, laser etching versus crevices,
  tip protectors, the 25-pound weight limit, pouch placement, cellulose and hydrogen peroxide, the
  -ectomy suffix, Balfour, the CJD keep-moist and spinal cord answers, the numeral 2 symbol, SDS
  access, loaner early arrival).
