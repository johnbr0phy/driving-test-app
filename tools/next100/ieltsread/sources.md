# IELTS Reading (`ieltsread`): sources

All 212 items and all 18 passages are original. Every passage was written for Tigertest on a
general-knowledge topic in the academic style the IELTS owners describe. Historical and science passages
keep to well-documented facts (Argand, Fresnel, the Svalbard vault, the Ideal X, Beaufort, FitzRoy,
Bjerknes, Richardson, Conté, the jiaozi, Stockholm's 1661 notes) and avoid precise figures that sources
disagree on; the researchers and studies quoted in the opinion passages (Merrow, Ostrowski, Anand,
Fogarty, Webb, Okafor, Henning, Pryce, Saleh, Brandt, Varela, Adair, Lindqvist, Karim, Tanaka) are
invented. Questions are answered from the passage only, as on the real test. No Cambridge IELTS book, official IELTS sample or practice test, British
Council, IDP or IELTS.org practice material, or commercial prep item was used or paraphrased. British
spelling throughout.

## Format and question types (owners: British Council, IDP IELTS, Cambridge University Press & Assessment)
- IELTS.org, "IELTS Academic format: Reading":
  https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading (fetched 2026-11-14)
  - 60 minutes, 3 sections, 40 questions, total text length 2,150 to 2,750 words; each correct answer
    receives 1 mark; scores reported as a band from 1 to 9 in whole or half bands.
  - Texts from books, journals, magazines, newspapers and online resources for a non-specialist audience,
    on topics of general interest to undergraduate or postgraduate students; narrative, descriptive or
    discursive/argumentative, at least one with detailed logical argument; technical terms come with a
    simple definition.
  - Question types: multiple choice; identifying information (True/False/Not given: "False" means the
    statement contradicts the text); identifying writer's views/claims (Yes/No/Not given); matching
    information; matching headings (more headings than paragraphs, none used twice); matching features;
    matching sentence endings (more endings than beginnings); sentence completion; summary, note, table and
    flow-chart completion (words from the text or from a list); diagram label completion; short-answer
    questions. Answers to several types follow the order of the text.
- IELTS.org, "IELTS General Training format: Reading":
  https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-general-training-test/ielts-general-training-format-reading (fetched 2026-11-14)
  - 60 minutes, 40 questions, 3 sections rising in difficulty: section 1 two or three short texts on
    everyday matters (notices, adverts, timetables), section 2 two workplace texts (job descriptions,
    contracts, training material), section 3 one long general-interest text; 2,150 to 2,375 words in all.
  - Question types: the Academic list less identifying writer's views and matching sentence endings
    (8 types as listed).
- IDP IELTS, "IELTS on computer: Reading preparation":
  https://ielts.idp.com/nepal/about/which-test-do-i-take/ielts-on-computer/reading-preparation (fetched 2026-11-14)
  - On computer: 3 passages, 40 questions, 60 minutes, questions shown beside the passage, answers typed
    on screen, no separate answer sheet and no extra transfer time.
- IELTS.org, "Updates to IELTS test delivery" (5 March 2026):
  https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/updates-to-ielts-test-delivery (fetched 2026-11-14)
  - From mid-2026 IELTS is no longer offered on paper; all tests on computer, exact timing by market; a
    Writing on Paper option in selected markets.

## Score conversion
- IDP IELTS, "IELTS Reading band scores" (Turkey, English):
  https://ielts.idp.com/turkey/results/scores/reading/en-gb (fetched 2026-11-14)
  - Academic raw score out of 40 to band: 39-40 = 9, 37-38 = 8.5, 35-36 = 8, 33-34 = 7.5, 30-32 = 7,
    27-29 = 6.5, 23-26 = 6, 19-22 = 5.5, 15-18 = 5, 13-14 = 4.5, 10-12 = 4, 8-9 = 3.5, 6-7 = 3, 4-5 = 2.5.
  - General Training: 40 = 9, 39 = 8.5, 37-38 = 8, 36 = 7.5, 34-35 = 7, 32-33 = 6.5, 30-31 = 6,
    27-29 = 5.5, 23-26 = 5, 19-22 = 4.5, 15-18 = 4, 12-14 = 3.5, 9-11 = 3.
  - The page calls these the average marks needed for each band and says actual scores may differ
    slightly between test versions. No penalty for wrong answers.
  - The Academic table is used for the band estimate (labelled an estimate). Raw 0 to 3 are not on the
    page: Tigertest maps 0 to 0, 1 to 1, 2 to 1.5 and 3 to 2.

## What is modelled and what is not
- Modelled: Academic Reading only, two full tests (3 passages, 40 questions, 60 minutes), plus drills by
  question type on 12 shorter drill-only passages.
- One mark per item, as on the real test, so every True/False/Not Given, Yes/No/Not Given and matching
  statement is its own question (not one grouped item) and the official raw-to-band table applies.
- Completion and short-answer questions on the real test are typed words from the passage; here they are
  dropdowns of words (cloze), which is easier. The landing and directions say so.
- Not modelled: General Training Reading (different texts and a stricter conversion; the landing gives its
  format), Listening (needs audio), Writing and Speaking (need a human examiner), diagram images (diagram
  label items describe the diagram in words), and "choose TWO" multiple-choice sets.
- Left out on purpose: fees, test dates, results timing, One Skill Retake rules and minimum bands asked by
  universities or visa bodies (they vary and change).
