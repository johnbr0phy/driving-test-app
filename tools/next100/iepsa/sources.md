# Sources: QQI Level 4 Security Guarding Skills for the PSA licence (Ireland), `iepsa`

All checked 5 November 2026. The web search budget for this workflow was used up before this
builder started, so every source below is a document fetched directly from its official URL.
psa.gov.ie could not be read (HTTP 503, then an expired TLS certificate), so the PSA's own pages,
its PSA 59 training requirements document and its TP1 requirements for training providers were not
read; the regulations the PSA made are read from the Irish Statute Book instead.

## Official (read this session)

### The qualification and its assessment

- QQI QSearch, award details for 4N20604:
  https://qsearch.qqi.ie/WebPart/AwardDetails?awardCode=4N20604
  Title Security Guarding Skills, code 4N20604, NFQ Level 4, EQF Level 3, minor award, 10 credits,
  published 05-09-2019, status Active. Counts towards the special purpose award 4S20606 Security
  Guarding (Level 4).
- QQI component specification for 4N20604 (PDF, 7 pages):
  https://qsdocs.qqi.ie//sites/docs/AwardsLibraryPdf/4N20604_AwardSpecifications_English.pdf
  Used:
  - Purpose: carry out private security guarding activities under supervision, in line with safety
    and health at work legislation and private security legislation.
  - Unit 1 Private Security Sector (learning outcomes 1.1 to 1.17): purpose and structure of the
    sector; legislation, regulations and standards; operational structure and quality management;
    role and responsibilities; prevention, protection and detection; Irish criminal, civil,
    equality, safety and health and environmental law; court proceedings and witness procedure;
    emergencies including terrorist and extremist actions and responding to an injured person;
    patrolling, access and egress, electronic aids and security hardware; documentation; alarms,
    warning devices, evacuation and preserving the scene; verbal and non-verbal communication,
    incident recording and reporting, conflict and aggression; on-site safety and fire equipment;
    confidential information; observation skills, communications equipment, challenging behaviour,
    crowd dynamics; presentation, attitude, teamwork and customer focus.
  - Unit 2 Security Guarding Skills (2.1 to 2.11): law on the role of security guards; patrols and
    their health and safety protocols; event security; crowd control; trespass; statutory powers
    of arrest; search and seizure, detention and arrest; confidentiality; key handling; retail
    security guard versus store detective; cash handling and movement in retail.
  - Assessment: Examination - Theory 50%, Skills Demonstration 50%; the learner must pass each.
    The assessor devises the instruments (examination papers, mark sheets). All learning outcomes
    must be assessed and achieved. Grading Pass 50-64%, Merit 65-79%, Distinction 80-100%.
  - Specific validation requirements: programmes must be consistent with the PSA's "Training
    Requirements for Guarding, Door and Event Security" (PSA 59, October 2016) and the PSA's
    "Requirements for Training Providers TP1"; learners whose first language is not English or
    Irish must meet the PSA's minimum CEFR B1 English requirement.
  So: no official question count, time or theory pass mark beyond the QQI Pass grade (50%) is
  published. Tigertest's four 50-question mock tests and 70% line are Tigertest's own target.

### Licensing law

- Private Security Services Act 2004 (No. 12 of 2004), revised (Law Reform Commission), as amended
  by the Civil Law (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 2011 and the Private Security Services
  (Amendment) Act 2021: https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2004/act/12/revised/en/html
  Used: s. 2 definitions ("door supervisor", "enforcement guard", "security guard" with paragraphs
  (a) in-house, (b) monitoring equipment, (c) supervising guards, (d) guard dog, (e) controlling
  movements at events; "remuneration"; "security service"); s. 6 (PSA established, independent);
  s. 8 (functions: control and supervise, licences, identity cards, register, standards,
  qualifications, complaints); s. 21 (application, references, Garda certificate);
  s. 22(3) (refusal: not fit and proper, under 18, non-compliance, fee unpaid), s. 22(5) (licence
  not transferable); s. 23(3) (licence stays in force while a timely renewal is undecided);
  s. 26 (refusal to renew, suspension, revocation); s. 27 (14 days for representations);
  s. 27A (temporary licence up to 6 months, one extension of up to 3 months, not for renewals);
  s. 29 (identity card carried, produced on request to the client, Garda or inspector);
  s. 30 (identity badge for prescribed categories); s. 32 (surrender on suspension, revocation,
  expiry or ceasing to provide the service); s. 33 (Private Security Register, free public
  inspection, on the website); s. 36 (notify convictions and pending proceedings); s. 37 (no
  unlicensed provision or holding out); s. 38 (no employing unlicensed persons; defence of a
  produced card); s. 39 (written complaints; revocation, suspension, reprimand, warning, caution,
  advice); s. 40 (Private Security Appeal Board, independent); s. 41 (appeal to the High Court on
  a point of law within 3 months). Fines and the licence period ("2 years or such longer period as
  may be prescribed") are not asked.
- Private Security (Licensing and Qualifications) Regulations 2022, S.I. No. 574 of 2022 (in force
  1 November 2022): https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2022/si/574/made/en/html
  Licence categories for individuals; "security guard (guarding)" defined (guarding property
  against loss, damage or waste by crime, fire, carelessness or flood; event work); qualification
  for that licence: QQI Level 4 minor award 4N20604 Security Guarding Skills or equivalent; door
  supervisor 4N20611 Door Security Skills. (Fees in reg. 7 are not used.)
- Private Security (Licensing and Qualifications) (Amendment) Regulations 2024, S.I. No. 204 of
  2024 (in force 8 May 2024): https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2024/si/204/made/en/html
  Adds the enforcement guard licence (QQI 4N22384 Enforcement Guard Skills); keeps 4N20604 for
  security guard (guarding).
- Private Security (Security Guard Temporary Licence) Regulations 2022, S.I. No. 573 of 2022:
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2022/si/573/made/en/html
  Temporary licence under s. 27A; training PSA 49:2020. Background only.
- Private Security (Identity Badge) Regulations 2024, S.I. No. 588 of 2024 (in force 1 November
  2024): https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2024/si/588/made/en/html
  The badge is the PSA identity card; prescribed categories include security guard (guarding)
  licence holders, except while it is necessary for the service that the public should not be
  aware of it, and security guard temporary licence holders.
- Private Security (Licensing and Standards) Regulations 2023, S.I. No. 140 of 2023:
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2023/si/140/made/en/html
  Contractor licence categories, including security guard (static), (event security), (alarm
  monitoring) and (CCTV monitoring), and the standards for each. Background only.

### Confirmed (kept, checked 2026-11-05)

- 112 and 999 (EMG-001, EMG-016, EVT-023): "In Ireland we have two emergency numbers - 999 and
  112." https://www.112.ie/ (checked 2026-11-05)
- District Court (REP-016, REP-018 rewritten): summary offences are tried by a judge without a
  jury, public order offences are always dealt with in the District Court, and murder and rape can
  never be dealt with there but are sent forward to a higher court.
  https://www.courts.ie/organisation-information/criminal-claims-in-the-district-court (checked
  2026-11-05). REP-016's explanation no longer names shoplifting or the higher courts.
- Oath or affirmation (REP-017, REP-020 rewritten): Oaths Act 1888 s. 1, an affirmation instead
  of an oath with the same force and effect.
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1888/act/46/enacted/en/print (checked 2026-11-05)
- Non-Fatal Offences Against the Person Act 1997 s. 18(1)(a) to (e) (POW-027 rewritten: force to
  protect another from injury or assault caused by a criminal act):
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1997/act/26/section/18/enacted/en/html (checked 2026-11-05)
- Criminal Justice (Public Order) Act 1994 s. 11 (POW-019 and POW-021 explanations now rest on
  ss. 11 and 13 rather than on "trespass is a civil wrong"):
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1994/act/2/section/11/enacted/en/html (checked 2026-11-05)
- Equal Status Act 2000 s. 4(1) (ROL-022 explanation: doing all that is reasonable to accommodate
  a person with a disability by special treatment or facilities):
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2000/act/8/section/4/enacted/en/html (checked 2026-11-05)
- Criminal Law Act 1997 s. 4 re-read: https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1997/act/14/section/4/enacted/en/html
- GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) storage limitation (ACC-013) and Art. 15 right of access from the controller
  (ACC-014, REP-014), secondary text of Regulation (EU) 2016/679:
  https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/ and https://gdpr-info.eu/art-15-gdpr/ (checked 2026-11-05)
- Euro banknote features (EVT-019): Europa series raised print, portrait watermark, portrait
  hologram and emerald number. Secondary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_banknotes (the ECB's
  https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/banknotes/current/security/html/index.en.html loads its text by
  script) (checked 2026-11-05)
- NATO phonetic alphabet, Golf and Kilo (PAT-011):
  https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/declassified_136216.htm (checked 2026-11-05)
- Procedure words (PAT-010, PAT-012): ACP 125 defines ROGER as "I have received your last
  transmission satisfactorily" and SAY AGAIN as a request to repeat. Secondary:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procedure_word (checked 2026-11-05). Fixed: PAT-010's stem said
  "received and understood"; it now says "I have received your message".
- Extinguishers (EMG-005, EMG-006, EMG-007): BS EN 3 colour code, CO2 a black panel, wet chemical
  canary yellow; European classes A solids, B flammable liquids, C gases, F cooking oils and fats.
  Secondary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_extinguisher (checked 2026-11-05)
- Before using an extinguisher, someone has called the fire service and there is a clear escape
  route; PASS, pull, aim low at the base, squeeze, sweep (EMG-008, EMG-009):
  https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/fire-extinguishers/ (checked
  2026-11-05)
- CPR and AED (EMG-015, EMG-016): check for danger, check response, call 999 or 112, check
  breathing, start CPR if not breathing normally and have a helper bring a defibrillator; severe
  bleeding (EMG-017): firm direct pressure with a dressing or clean cloth.
  https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/cpr/ and
  https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/severe-bleeding/ (checked 2026-11-05; UK guidance on the
  same European Resuscitation Council basis Ireland uses)
- Armed attack, escape first (EMG-014): Run, Hide, Tell ("Escape if you can"), UK NaCTSO guidance,
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/stay-safe-film (checked 2026-11-05). No Irish page
  was reached; the explanation now names it as an example of published advice.
- Bomb threats by email (EMG-010: do not reply to, forward or delete; report it) and suspicious
  items (EMG-011, EMG-012: never touch or move; evacuate and call the police):
  https://www.protectuk.police.uk/advice-and-guidance/risk/bomb-threats (checked 2026-11-05).
  No item tells guards to keep radios away from a device.
- Hostile reconnaissance (EMG-013): interest in entrances, exits, CCTV cameras and security
  features, filming: https://www.protectuk.police.uk/advice-and-guidance/risk/hostile-reconnaissance
  (checked 2026-11-05)
- Fire evacuation and people with disabilities (EMG-003, EMG-022, EMG-023): HSA, evacuation
  strategy must take account of people with disabilities, and fire-fighting equipment is for use
  in a fire's early stages without exposing staff to danger:
  https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/fire/emergency_escape_and_fire_fighting/ (checked 2026-11-05)

### Still good practice, not checked against an official source

These items test site procedure where the wrong options are plainly unsafe or unlawful; no single
official source sets them out, and none was reachable this session: notebook and report practice
(bound numbered notebook, single line through errors, 24-hour clock, head-to-toe descriptions,
scene log, continuity of evidence, REP-001 to REP-013, REP-019, REP-021 to REP-024); search
practice (consent or contract basis, same-sex searcher, person handles own items, POW-023 to
POW-026); retail practice (stop past the last payment point with continuous observation,
distraction theft, cash handling, floats, cash lifts, EVT-010 to EVT-018); event practice (crowd
density, ingress and egress, safety officer and control point, missing child, EVT-002 to EVT-009,
EVT-020 to EVT-022); access control practice (ACC-001 to ACC-012, ACC-016 to ACC-024); patrols
(PTZ, patrol tags, lone worker check-calls, keyholders, PAT-005, PAT-014, PAT-018, PAT-024);
evacuation details (roll call with the visitor log, meeting the fire service at an agreed point,
no lifts, EMG-002, EMG-004, EMG-019, EMG-021); prevention, protection and detection examples
(ROL-001 to ROL-003, only the principle is in the QQI specification). The PSA's own pages, PSA 59
and TP1 remain unread (psa.gov.ie unreachable).
