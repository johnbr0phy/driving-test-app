# Sources: ieqfa (QFA / Minimum Competency Code exam: regulation, ethics and core products, Ireland)

All checked on 5 November 2026 unless marked otherwise. Format facts go on the landing, the FAQ and
`lib/examFacts.ts`, never into the bank.

## The real exam (format)

- LIA, QFA | Certificate in Professional Financial Advice:
  https://www.lia.ie/courses/certificate-in-professional-financial-advice
  - Six modules: QFA Regulation, QFA Life Assurance, QFA Investment, QFA Loans, QFA Pensions and
    QFA Financial Advice (the capstone, taken last).
  - QFA Regulation covers investor protection measures and consumer contract regulations, best
    practice in client engagement, complaint handling and whistleblowing.
  - Each module is 5 ECTS credits. Most modules: a 2-hour online multiple-choice exam of 100 MCQs.
    QFA Financial Advice: a 2.5-hour exam of 3 case study questions.
  - Sittings in January, May and September (next exam January 2027, registration deadline
    13 November 2026, per the page).
  - The page says the qualification satisfies the Central Bank's MCC requirements for advising on
    and selling five of the eight retail product categories in the Code.
- LIA, About your exam: https://www.lia.ie/courses/exam-information/about-your-exam
  - LIA exams are held online with remote invigilation.
  - No pass mark, negative marking or grading bands stated.
- LIA, Exam regulations page and Level 7 regulations guide (FlowPaper viewer, text did not load):
  https://www.lia.ie/courses/exam-information/exam-regulations ,
  https://7eb90794.flowpaper.com/Level7ExamRegulationsGuide20242025/
  - **Pass mark not confirmed.** The 70% pass line in the config is Tigertest's readiness target,
    said so on the landing, in the FAQ and in examFacts.
- IOB, Qualified Financial Advisor (QFA) designation page: https://www.iob.ie/areas/qualified-financial-advisor
  - QFA is a professional designation accredited in all eight retail product categories of the MCC;
    "the first step towards a career as a financial advisor or financial planner". The IOB's route,
    its module names and its exam format were not on the pages read and are not stated anywhere.
- Not confirmed and therefore not stated: the pass mark, whether there is negative marking, the
  LIA's per-topic weighting inside the Regulation module, any language other than English, and
  how the QFA Board's exemption schedules apply. The landing names the LIA module as the model and
  says other MCC-recognised qualifications (IOB, Insurance Institute) set their own exams.

## Blueprint (Tigertest's)

Neither provider publishes topic weights. The nine categories follow the MCC 2017 Appendix 3
competencies "Legislation, regulation and compliance" and "Best practice" (life assurance product
table and the parallel tables for other products), plus the CPC 2025 structure, with a 20% share
for core product concepts (brief: "regulation and consumer protection with core product concepts").
Per 50: regulators 6, competence/F&P 6, CPC 8, contract and consumer insurance law 5,
complaints/compensation/data 5, AML 4, ethics 6, protection products 5, savings/pensions/credit 5.

## Law and regulation (bank content)

- Central Bank, Minimum Competency page: https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/how-we-regulate/authorisation/minimum-competency
  - MCC 2017 and MCR 2017 in effect from 3 January 2018; amended 2022, 2023, 2026 (S.I. 295 of 2026);
    from 28 July 2026 the scope covers staff of crypto-asset service providers.
- Unofficial Consolidated Minimum Competency Code, June 2026 (PDF, read in full for Parts 1 to 3
  and Appendices 1 to 3):
  https://www.centralbank.ie/docs/default-source/regulation/how-we-regulate/authorisation/minimum-competency/unofficial-consolidated-minimum-competency-code-june-2026.pdf
  - Scope (1.2); standards table (1.3); new entrants: training, supervision, first available sitting,
    four-year maximum, pro-rata adjustments (1.4); grandfathering exclusions (1.5); prescribed script
    (1.6); CPD 15 hours, 1 hour per function, 1 hour ethics, no carry forward, shortfall made up next
    year if none in past five years, 8 hours a day and 4 per topic, accreditation by recognised
    qualification providers (1.7); records (1.9); MiFID: qualification plus six months' experience
    (1.3, 2.2.1); product design key person (2.2.4); Central Bank does not take part in course design
    or exams; NFQ level 7 and CPD with ethics for new recognised qualifications (3.1, 3.3);
    Appendix 1 product categories (consumer credit excludes credit cards, overdrafts, overrunning,
    high cost credit, housing loans); Appendix 2 specified functions; Appendix 3 regulatory
    competencies (CCPC, IDR 2018 intermediaries, Distance Marketing Regulations 2004, AML Act 2010,
    Investor Compensation Scheme, FSPO, Data Protection and Equal Status Acts).
- Central Bank, Consumer Protection Code 2025 pages:
  https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/consumer-protection/consumer-protection-code-regulations ,
  https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/consumer-protection/consumer-protection-code
  - Published 24 March 2025, took effect 24 March 2026 after a 12-month implementation period.
- Standards for Business Regulations 2025 (S.I. 80 of 2025, Central Bank Reform Act 2010 section 17A):
  https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/consumer-protection/consumer-protection-code/section-17a-regulations/standards-for-business
  - The list beginning "secure its customers' interests; act with honesty and integrity; ...".
- Consumer Protection Regulations 2025 (section 48), Part 1:
  https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/consumer-protection/consumer-protection-code/section-48-regulations/part-1-preliminary-and-general
  - Definition of "consumer in vulnerable circumstances".
- Part 2: https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/consumer-protection/consumer-protection-code/section-48-regulations/part-2-general-consumer-protection-requirements
  - Reg 16 knowing the consumer (incl. sustainability preferences, capital security); reg 18
    statement of suitability (signed, dated, durable medium); reg 19 exemptions; regs 20 to 21
    commission; ch. 3 vulnerability (assistance, training); ch. 4 digitalisation (regs 38 to 41, no
    preselected "read and understood" options); ch. 5 (warning statements boxed, bold, font; one
    month's notice of changes to services; plain language for technical terms; key information not
    obscured); ch. 7 regulatory disclosure statement wording (reg 71); ch. 8 unregulated activities
    (separate webpage, prescribed warning, reg 72 to 73); ch. 9 advertising (regs 74 to 77);
    ch. 10 contingent selling and bundling (regs 90 to 94); ch. 11 errors (6 months, analysis every
    6 months, refunds, regs 96 to 98); ch. 12 complaints (regs 101 to 106: written by post and
    electronically, 5 working days acknowledgement, 20 working day updates, 40 working days, decision
    within 5 working days, procedure on websites, 5-working-day resolution logged); ch. 13 calls and
    visits (regs 108 to 114, 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. Monday to Saturday); ch. 14 records 6 years (reg 117).
- Part 4 (insurance):
  https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/consumer-protection/consumer-protection-code/section-48-regulations/part-4-insurance
  - Differential pricing (reg 315); automatic renewals (regs 324 to 327); no claims discount terms
    (reg 343); surrender value and secondary market (reg 345); claims: decision within 5 working days
    with reasons and appeals mechanism (reg 364), at least 10 working days to accept an offer (365),
    payment within 10 working days (366).
- Central Bank, Individual Accountability Framework:
  https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/how-we-regulate/individual-accountability-framework
  - Central Bank (Individual Accountability Framework) Act 2023; SEAR (executive PCF roles from
    1 July 2024, Duty of Responsibility); Common Conduct Standards (all CF roles) and Additional
    Conduct Standards (PCF and CF-1) from 29 December 2023; firms certify CF and PCF holders under the
    Certification Regulations from 8 January 2024; ASP action directly against individuals.
- FSPO, Make a complaint: https://www.fspo.ie/make-a-complaint/ (free; give the provider a chance first)
- FSPO, What are the time limits?: https://www.fspo.ie/make-a-complaint/what-are-the-time-limits
  (6 years from the conduct; long-term financial services also within 3 years of becoming aware,
  conduct on or after 1 January 2002 and service not expired more than 6 years; pension provider
  conduct on or after 13 April 1996).

## Fact-check, 5 November 2026 (claims re-checked against these sources, all read 2026-11-05)

Web search was unavailable for this pass (session search budget used up), so only pages fetched
directly were read. Items not confirmed are listed at the end.

- Central Bank Reform Act 2010 as revised (Law Reform Commission):
  https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2010/act/23/revised/en/html
  - s23(1): no appointment to a PCF unless the Bank has approved it in writing; s23A gives an ECB
    approval route for significant supervised entities (IEQFA-MCC-020, REG-008 in part).
  - s53E Common Conduct Standards: honesty and integrity; due skill, care and diligence; cooperate
    in good faith and without delay with the Bank; best interests of customers, fairly and
    professionally; proper market conduct standards (IEQFA-REG-014, ETH-018; ETH-018's key changed
    from "Openly and cooperatively" to the statutory "In good faith and without delay").
- Central Bank, Fitness and Probity: https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/how-we-regulate/fitness-probity
  - Three standards: competent and capable; honest, ethical and acting with integrity; financially
    sound (IEQFA-MCC-021).
- Central Bank, Individual Accountability Framework (above): firms certify CF and PCF holders;
  frequency not stated, so not asked (IEQFA-MCC-022).
- Central Bank (Supervision and Enforcement) Act 2013 as revised:
  https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2013/act/26/revised/en/html
  - s38(2): a person in a PCF must, as soon as practicable, disclose to the Bank information about
    breaches of financial services law (IEQFA-ETH-012).
- Protected Disclosures Act 2014 as revised: https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2014/act/14/revised/en/html
  - Amended by the Protected Disclosures (Amendment) Act 2022 (No. 27 of 2022), mostly from
    1 January 2023; prescribed persons operate external reporting channels (IEQFA-ETH-011; the
    Central Bank's listing as a prescribed person was not re-read).
- Consumer Protection Code 2025, Part 2 (URL above): reg 56, "broker" only where principal regulated
  activities are on a fair analysis of the market; reg 58, tied to one producer must be disclosed.
  IEQFA-MCC-023 was rewritten to this rule: the old "tied insurance intermediary acts under the
  insurers' full responsibility" definition could not be confirmed (EUR-Lex IDD text did not load).
- Consumer Insurance Contracts Act 2019 as revised: https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2019/act/53/revised/en/html
  - s8(3) specific questions, no general questions; s8(5) plain and intelligible language;
    s8(7) answer honestly and with reasonable care; s9(3)-(4) negligent misrepresentation remedy
    reflects what the insurer would have done; s9(5) fraud, avoid the contract; s19(3) basis of
    contract clauses invalid (IEQFA-LAW-005 to LAW-009).
- Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman Act 2017 as revised:
  https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2017/act/22/revised/en/html ; FSPO About us: https://www.fspo.ie/about-us/
  - Opened 1 January 2018; ss27-28 dissolve the FSO Bureau and the Pensions Ombudsman and transfer
    their functions; s60(4) may direct compensation (cap s60(5)); s62(1) decisions binding subject
    to appeal; s64 appeal to the High Court (IEQFA-RED-002, RED-005, RED-006).
- Deposit Guarantee Scheme: https://www.depositguarantee.ie/ (banks, building societies and credit
  unions authorised by the Central Bank; administered by the Central Bank) (IEQFA-RED-007).
- Investor Compensation Company DAC: https://www.investorcompensation.ie/ (fund of last resort for
  customers of authorised firms) (IEQFA-RED-008).
- Central Bank, Insurance Compensation Fund page:
  https://www.centralbank.ie/regulation/industry-market-sectors/insurance-reinsurance/insurance-compensation-fund
  (the 1% levy stops on 1 January 2027). High Court approval of payments was not confirmed, so it was
  removed from IEQFA-RED-009's explanation.
- Criminal Justice (Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing) Act 2010 as revised:
  https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2010/act/6/revised/en/html
  - Financial institution includes insurance intermediaries for life assurance; s26 beneficial owner
    by reference to 4AMLD art 3(6)(a) (more than 25%); s30A(3) business risk assessment documented;
    s33(8) no service where CDD not completed; s37(10) PEP family members and close associates;
    s42 report to FIU Ireland and the Revenue Commissioners as soon as practicable; s49 tipping off;
    s54(6) staff training; s55 records at least 5 years (IEQFA-AML-001 to AML-010, AML-014, AML-016).
- Central Bank mortgage measures:
  https://www.centralbank.ie/financial-system/financial-stability/macro-prudential-policy/mortgage-measures
  ("an essential element of the Central Bank's macroprudential policy framework") (IEQFA-REG-005).
- Pensions Authority: https://www.pensionsauthority.ie/ (regulates occupational pension schemes,
  trust RACs and PRSAs; Pensions Act 1990) (IEQFA-REG-006).
- CCPC, Money: https://www.ccpc.ie/consumers/money/ (impartial information and comparison tools;
  enforces competition and consumer law; keeps a credit intermediaries register, so explanations no
  longer say it authorises no firms at all) (IEQFA-REG-001, REG-007).
- Central Bank, unauthorised firms: https://www.centralbank.ie/consumer-hub/unauthorised-firms
  (check the Registers; warning list; make sure it is the genuine authorised firm) (IEQFA-REG-016, REG-017).
- GDPR (EUR-Lex): https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng (Art 5(1)(c), 9(2)(a), 12(3),
  33(1)); DPC: https://www.dataprotection.ie/ ("the Irish supervisory authority for the GDPR")
  (IEQFA-RED-011 to RED-015).
- Equal Status Act 2000 as revised: https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2000/act/8/revised/en/html
  (s3(2) grounds incl. the Traveller community; s5(2)(d) actuarial or statistical data, not gender)
  (IEQFA-RED-016).
- Distance Marketing Directive 2002/65/EC Art 6(1) (EUR-Lex): https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2002/65/oj/eng
  (14 calendar days, 30 for life assurance and personal pensions; transposed by S.I. 853 of 2004)
  (IEQFA-LAW-016, LAW-017).
- CCPC, Life insurance: https://www.ccpc.ie/consumers/money/insurance/life-insurance/ (cancel within
  30 days; Section 72 policy pays inheritance tax) (IEQFA-LAW-018, PRO-005).
- CCPC, Car insurance: https://www.ccpc.ie/consumers/money/insurance/car-insurance/ (at least
  third-party cover is a legal requirement) (IEQFA-LAW-019).
- HIA glossary and LCR: https://www.hia.ie/consumer-information/glossary ,
  https://www.hia.ie/information/lifetime-community-rating-lcr (community rating, open enrolment,
