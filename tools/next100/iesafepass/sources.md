## Official: the programme (SOLAS)

- SOLAS, Safe Pass: https://www.solas.ie/construction-lp/safe-pass
  - "A mandatory one-day health and safety awareness programme" for "all construction workers in
    Ireland - including apprentices and trainees", under the Construction Regulations 2013.
  - Delivered by SOLAS-approved organisations and tutors (register at constructioncourses.ie).
  - Assessment: "The assessment is not a written test; it is a multiple-choice test. The tutor reads
    each question twice and the candidates tick one of the possible answers." SOLAS does not publish
    the course assessment's question count or pass mark.
  - Cards valid for four years; renewal by attending the course again, or by the alternative renewal
    computer test at an approved Pearson test centre: 50 questions, 45 correct to pass, 45 minutes.
  - Legislation cited: Construction Regulations 2013, the 2005 Act, General Application Regulations 2007.
  - Free revision portal and mock test at safepassrevision.ie; a 500-question revision book in the
    learner's SOLAS online account (not used: Tigertest's items are original).
  - The page did not mention a blended or online format; the brief's "blended course since 2021" was
    not confirmed this session, so the landing says "one-day course" only.
- constructioncourses.ie, Safe Pass information: https://www.constructioncourses.ie/Home/InformationSafePass
  - Alternative renewal test: computer based at a Pearson VUE centre, 50 multiple-choice questions,
    45 minutes, 45 correct to pass, results printed on the day; "designed to allow those who have
    previously completed the classroom-based programme" to renew.
  - The 13 learning units of the Tutor Led Programme (the bank's blueprint): Introduction of Site
    Safety; Legislation and Site Safety; Site Accident Reporting; Introduction to Risk Assessment;
    Risk Assessment for Electricity; for Excavations; for Heights; Behaviour-Based Safety; Site Safety
    and Construction Equipment; Site Safety and Construction Vehicles; Personal Health and Welfare;
    Noise and Vibration; Personal Protective Equipment.
  - Tutor uploads results to the National Construction Schemes Database; card to the tutor about
    five working days later; cards valid four years.
- SOLAS FAQ: https://www.solas.ie/faq-s : participants must be 16 on the day and have an Irish PPS
  number (non-nationals working temporarily need a process number; UK nationals can use their NI number).

## Official: law and HSA guidance (used in the bank)

- Safety, Health and Welfare at Work Act 2005 (No. 10 of 2005), Irish Statute Book:
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2005/act/10/enacted/en/print
  - s. 8 employer's general duties "so far as is reasonably practicable"; s. 13 employee duties
    (comply and take reasonable care; not under the influence of an intoxicant to a dangerous extent;
    submit to appropriate, reasonable and proportionate tests if reasonably required; cooperate; no
    improper conduct; attend training; correct use of articles and PPE; report defects); s. 19 hazard
    identification and risk assessment; s. 20 safety statement based on the risk assessment; s. 25
    employees select a safety representative; s. 27 protection against penalisation; s. 66
    improvement notices and s. 67 prohibition notices.
- Safety, Health and Welfare at Work (Construction) Regulations 2013 (S.I. 291 of 2013):
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2013/si/291/made/en/print
  - Reg 4(2): Schedule 4 (safety awareness card) applies to craft and general construction workers,
    persons undertaking on-site security work, and others prescribed; 4(3) excludes certain services
    installers. Reg 10: client notifies the HSA where work lasts longer than 30 working days or
    exceeds 500 person days. Reg 19: PSCS coordinates Safe Pass and CSCS card arrangements.
    Reg 23: site safety representative where more than 20 persons are normally employed; may
    investigate accidents, dangerous occurrences and complaints and accompany inspectors.
    Reg 25: every contractor ensures workers hold a valid safety awareness card, CSCS card for
    Schedule 5 tasks, and site-specific induction where Reg 4(3) applies. Reg 26: safety officers
    where a contractor has more than 20 persons on a site or 30 in construction work. Ban on throwing
    or dropping materials from a height. Vehicles and earthmoving plant operated only by a competent
    person aged 18 or over unless training under supervision. Schedule 5 CSCS tasks include
    scaffolding and tower crane operation.
- Safety, Health and Welfare at Work (General Application) Regulations 2007 (S.I. 299 of 2007):
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2007/si/299/made/en/print
  - Reg 81: circuits for portable equipment over 125 V AC (up to 1,000 V) protected by RCDs of 30 mA
    or less, including generator circuits. Reg 103: top guard-rail at least 950 mm, gaps no more than
    470 mm, suitable toe-boards. Reg 114: ladders only for low-risk, short work (or where features
    cannot be altered), firm stable surface, prevented from slipping, protruding above the landing,
    handhold kept. Reg 115: fragile surfaces. Reg 119: a construction working platform with a fall of
    2 m or more not used unless inspected within the previous 7 days, and after exceptional
    circumstances. Noise: lower action value 80 dB(A), upper 85 dB(A), limit 87 dB(A). Vibration:
    hand-arm action 2.5 m/s², limit 5 m/s²; whole body 0.5 and 1.15 m/s² (A(8)). Manual handling:
    avoid with mechanical aids where possible. Schedule 1 hand signals: STOP is the right arm pointing
    upwards with the palm forward; END is both hands clasped at chest height.
- HSA, Accident and Dangerous Occurrence Reporting:
  https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/accident_and_dangerous_occurrence_reporting/
  - Report injuries where the employee cannot do normal work for more than three consecutive days,
    excluding the day of the accident; fatal accidents immediately to the HSA or Gardaí, formal
    report within five working days; non-fatal accidents and dangerous occurrences within ten
    working days.
- HSA, Excavations FAQ: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/your_industry/construction/construction_faqs/excavations/
  - Inspection at least daily while people work there; over 2 m deep, at the start of each shift;
    after events affecting stability and falls of material; every 7 days when nobody works there;
    AF3 report; support may be needed even under 1.25 m; Safe System of Work Plan (SSWP); contact
    ESB, Bord Gáis (now Gas Networks Ireland) for services.
- HSA, Noise FAQ: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/physical_agents/noise/noise_-_frequently_asked_questions/
  (80/85/87 dB(A); hard to hear normal conversation at 2 m suggests over 80 dB(A)).
- HSA, HAV risk assessment: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/physical_agents/vibration/hav_risk_assessment/
  (avoid or lower exposure; anti-vibration gloves not counted as a control; report numbness and tingling).
- HSA, Vibration at Work: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/physical_agents/vibration/vibration_at_work/
- HSA, Work at Height: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/work_at_height/ (definition: any place where a
  person could be injured if they fell, including at or below ground level; collective measures first).
- HSA, Employees' duties: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/employees_duties/ (vehicles cause the greatest
  number of workplace deaths; duties listed).
- HSA, Safe Pass FAQ: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/your_industry/construction/construction_faqs/safe_pass/
  (who needs Safe Pass: craft workers, apprentices, general workers, working chargehands, on-site
  security, delivery drivers on site, students on placement; not office staff, PSDP/PSCS, architects,
  supervised visitors, canteen staff; Safe Pass does not replace the employer's training duty).
- HSA, Sun Protection: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/sun_protection/ (skin cancer the most common
  cancer in Ireland; outdoor workers at higher risk).

## Fact-check pass, 5 November 2026 (2026-11-05)

Web search was unavailable this pass (budget used up), so every source was opened directly by URL
(curl or WebFetch). Checked 2026-11-05 unless stated.

- Safety, Health and Welfare at Work Act 2005, Irish Statute Book (as above), checked 2026-11-05:
  s. 66 improvement notice (states the opinion and the provision, and directs the person "to remedy the
  contravention ... by a date specified in the notice"); s. 67 prohibition notice (served where an activity
  "involves or is likely to involve a risk of serious personal injury"; it shall "prohibit the carrying on of
  the activity concerned until the matters ... are remedied"; takes effect immediately on receipt in the
  cases s. 67(5)(a) covers). The brief's "ss. 65 and 66" was off by one: s. 65 is the improvement plan.
  s. 8(5): safety measures must "not involve financial cost to his or her employees" (IESP-PPE-001).
  Used by IESP-LAW-022, IESP-LAW-023.
- Safety, Health and Welfare at Work (Reporting of Accidents and Dangerous Occurrences) Regulations 2016
  (S.I. 370 of 2016), inserting Schedule 15 into the 2007 Regulations:
  https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2016/si/370/made/en/print (2026-11-05). Dangerous occurrences
  include "the collapse of, the overturning of, or the failure of any load-bearing part of any lift or
  lifting equipment" and loss of load from a vehicle or mobile machine causing a risk of injury. A crane
  simply dropping its load was not clearly listed, so IESP-ACC-006 now asks about a crane overturning.
- General Application Regulations 2007 (S.I. 299 of 2007), as above, checked 2026-11-05:
  Reg 38 roll-over protection and a restraining system on ride-on mobile work equipment (IESP-VEH-011);
  Reg 48(d) lifting machinery clearly marked with its safe working load, 48(j) not used beyond it except
  when tested (IESP-VEH-018); Reg 49 lifting accessories marked with the characteristics essential for safe
  use (IESP-VEH-016); Reg 52(1)(c) examination by a competent person after a repair or alteration relevant
  to safe operation (IESP-VEH-023); Schedule 1 Part B thorough examination intervals, lifting accessories
  including chains, ropes, rings, hooks and shackles 6 months, crane 12 months (IESP-VEH-021, -022);
  Reg 62 provision of PPE (IESP-PPE-001); Schedule 9 signs: prohibitory round, red edging and diagonal;
  warning triangular, black pictogram on yellow; mandatory round, white pictogram on blue; emergency
  escape and first-aid rectangular or square, white on green (IESP-PPE-003, IESP-ELE-020).
- Construction Regulations 2013 (S.I. 291 of 2013), as above, checked 2026-11-05: Schedule 5 CSCS tasks
  include telescopic handler, tractor/dozer, mobile and crawler crane, articulated and site dumper, 180°
  and 360° excavator and mini-digger operation (IESP-VEH-003); Reg 35(2)-(3) every person given a
  protective safety helmet, each marked to identify the person it was issued to and not issued to anyone
  else until cleaned and disinfected (IESP-PPE-004, IESP-PPE-005, both rewritten from unconfirmed
  replacement and sticker claims); welfare: sanitary conveniences (Reg 101), washing facilities with
  "hot and cold or warm running water", shelter with tables and seats for meals, drying wet clothing and
  "an adequate supply of potable drinking water" (IESP-HLT-018).
- An Garda Síochána, Contact us: https://www.garda.ie/en/contact-us/ (2026-11-05): "In an emergency always
  call 999/112" (IESP-ACC-009, IESP-ELE-017).
- HSA, Fire Extinguisher - Safety Checks information sheet:
  https://www.hsa.ie/media/riop52fh/103525-hsa-2-fire-extinguisher-data-sheet.pdf (2026-11-05):
  solids: water, foam, multi-purpose powder; flammable liquids: foam; electrical fires: carbon dioxide;
  gases: dry powder with specialist advice (IESP-ACC-018). The I.S. EN 3 colour bands were not found on
  an official page, so IESP-ACC-017 now asks which extinguisher suits flammable liquids.
- HSA, Guide to the General Application Regulations 2007, Part 3 Electricity:
  https://www.hsa.ie/media/nfcd00ni/gen_apps_electricity.pdf (2026-11-05): reduced low voltage (not over
  125 V AC) required for portable equipment on construction sites; transformer midpoint earthed "to reduce
  the potential level of shock" (IESP-ELE-003); Reg 85: "An isolator-locking device must be used" and
  isolated conductors "proved dead by testing ... before work commences" (IESP-ELE-018); secondary
  injuries, e.g. "falling from a ladder after a mild shock" (IESP-ELE-016, rewritten: no official source
  for non-conductive ladders was found).
- HSA, Overhead Power Lines: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/electricity/powerlines_and_cables/overhead_power_lines/
  (2026-11-05): "Anyone in the cab may be in less danger"; the HSA-approved code is published by ESB Networks.
- ESB Networks / HSA, Code of Practice for Avoiding Danger from Overhead Electricity Lines (v2.1, 2019):
  https://media.esbnetworks.ie/media/docs/default-source/publications/code-of-practice-for-avoiding-danger-from-overhead-electricity-lines.pdf
  (2026-11-05): crossing points with height-restricting goalposts of rigid non-conducting material
  (IESP-ELE-013); after contact "stop work, remain calm and stay in the cab", call ESB Networks 1800 372 999,
  jump clear with feet together only if fire or another hazard forces you out (IESP-ELE-014); consult the
  network owner (IESP-ELE-015). ESB Networks construction safety: https://www.esbnetworks.ie/safety/safety-tips/construction-safety
- Gas Networks Ireland, Dial Before You Dig: https://www.gasnetworks.ie/home/safety/dial-before-you-dig/
  (2026-11-05): Gas Networks Ireland operates the gas network and supplies pipe maps before digging (IESP-EXC-017).
- HSA, Using Ladders Safely information sheet: https://www.hsa.ie/media/khfdozzm/using_ladders_safely_information_sheet.pdf
  (2026-11-05): "The correct angle for a ladder is 75 degrees or the 1 in 4 rule" (IESP-HGT-013; landing tip).
- HSA, Guide to the General Application Regulations 2007, Part 4 Work at Height:
  https://www.hsa.ie/media/rzfifybc/gen_apps_work_at_height.pdf (2026-11-05): access ladders "should project
  at least one metre beyond any landing and be secured", angle 4:1 (IESP-HGT-014); rescue plan in place
  before work starts; suspension trauma from hanging motionless in a harness; personal fall protection
  securely attached to anchors (IESP-HGT-022 to IESP-HGT-024).
- HSA, Code of Practice for Access and Working Scaffolds (2023): https://www.hsa.ie/media/0tlazdod/scaffolds-cop-2023.pdf
  (2026-11-05): mobile tower castors "should always be locked, except when moving the tower"; "the tower
  should not be moved with workers or materials anywhere on the tower" (IESP-HGT-017).
- HSA, Leptospirosis: https://www.hsa.ie/eng/topics/biological_agents/specific_biological_agents_diseases/leptospirosis/
  (2026-11-05): Weil's disease spread by rat urine, often via water; enters through cuts and the mucous
  membranes; construction workers at risk; wash and cover cuts (IESP-HLT-012).
- HSA, Stop and Think Asbestos flyer: https://www.hsa.ie/media/zl5lrpsk/asbestos_flyer.pdf (2026-11-05):
  "If you uncover any hidden material or dust you suspect may contain asbestos, stop work and get competent
  advice" (IESP-HLT-013).
- HSA, Silica Dust employee information sheet: https://www.hsa.ie/media/uvgptibc/csp-silica-info-sheet-print-5dec22.pdf
  (2026-11-05): water suppression, on-tool extraction; silicosis and cancer; FFP3 or P3 masks that fit
  correctly (IESP-HLT-014).
- HSA, Guidelines on Occupational Dermatitis: https://www.hsa.ie/media/kdvp133s/health_dermatitis.pdf
  (2026-11-05): wet cement is a skin irritant in construction, chromates in cement a sensitiser; alkalis
  can burn the skin (IESP-HLT-020).
- HSA, A Guide to Respiratory Protective Equipment:
  https://www.hsa.ie/media/ab3likf5/103525-hsa-7-respiratory-protective-brochure-copy-1.pdf (2026-11-05):
  fit testing "recommended ... for all tight fitting respirators"; "Tight fitting face piece respirators
  must not be worn by individuals who have a beard or moustache"; hoods or helmets instead (IESP-PPE-010;
  IESP-PPE-011 rewritten from the unconfirmed stubble claim).
