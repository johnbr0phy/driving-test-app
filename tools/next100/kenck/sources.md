# kenck sources (checked 2026-11-05)

## Read from the Nursing Council of Kenya's own documents
- NCK Examination page (https://nckenya.com/examination/), read 2026-11-05: "The licensure
  examination is administered through regional examination centers." It "consists of 4 individual
  papers for basic cadres and 3 papers for other cadres"; each paper "administered over the course
  of three hours"; question types are multiple-choice, short answer and long answer questions, long
  answers responding to "brief health-care scenarios"; candidates must satisfy the Board of
  Examiners on each paper; a maximum of 4 resits. This page is older than the schedule below and
  conflicts with it on paper count and length; the landing follows the dated schedule.
- NCK "Instructions and schedules for 2026 and 2027 licensure examinations"
  (https://nckenya.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Examination-instructions-and-schedule-August-November-2026-May-2027.pdf,
  Word file dated 3 July 2026), text extracted 2026-11-05:
  - "Each paper is allotted a duration of two (2) hours"; morning sessions start at 9.00 a.m. and
    afternoon sessions at 2.00 p.m.
  - Candidates must attend a rehearsal in person and complete two mock exams "in the examination
    system" before the main exam; only "the designated online examination application" may be open
    on the PC; the exam is taken at a designated examination centre (remote sitting only on written
    request). So the exam is online, computer-based, at regional centres.
  - Two papers (Paper 1 at 9 to 11 am, Paper 2 at 2 to 4 pm) for: Kenya Registered Community Health
    Nurse - Basic [KRCHN B], Kenya Registered Nurse Midwife [KRN/M], Kenya Registered Nurse/Mental
    Health Nurse [KRN/MH], Bachelor of Science in Nursing [BScN], Bachelor of Science in Midwifery
    [BScM]. Kenya Registered Nurse [KRN] is listed as "Two Papers". Post-basic specialties sit one paper.
  - Series: 12 to 14 August 2026 (results 30 September 2026), 11 to 13 November 2026 (registered
    nurse papers 12 November for KRCHN, 13 November for BScN and KRN/M; rehearsals 10 November;
    results 11 December 2026), 19 to 21 May 2027.
- Not published by NCK (searched 2026-11-05): number of questions per paper, the pass mark, and a
  topic blueprint. A third-party guide also says no public numeric pass mark was found. The landing
  and examFacts say so; 60% is Tigertest's readiness target, not an NCK figure.
- Digiproctor's NCK page (https://digiproctor.com/nck) describes the exam as run on its platform
  "in hybrid test mode" in 15 centres across Kenya (vendor page, not used on the landing).

## Blueprint used (Tigertest's, labelled as such)
The registered nurse cadres (KRCHN, BScN, KRN/M) cover general nursing, midwifery and community
health. Per 50-question mock test: fundamentals 7, pharmacology and dosage 4, medical-surgical 9,
midwifery and newborn 8, reproductive health 3, child health and IMCI 6, community health and PHC 6,
mental health 3, law, ethics and leadership 4. The split is Tigertest's; NCK publishes none.

## Kenyan clinical and legal references used for items
- Kenya malaria guidance (test, treat and track; AL first line; parenteral artesunate for severe
  malaria, 3 mg/kg under 20 kg, 2.4 mg/kg otherwise; IPTp-SP from 13 weeks; LLINs at first ANC),
  in line with WHO Guidelines for malaria.
- Kenya TB programme (GeneXpert as initial test, 2RHZE/4RH, TB preventive therapy for child contacts,
  pyridoxine with isoniazid).
- Kenya HIV prevention and treatment guidelines (TDF/3TC/DTG preferred first line, viral load
  monitoring, cotrimoxazole prophylaxis, CrAg screening in advanced disease, same-day ART in
  pregnancy, infant DNA PCR at 6 weeks).
- Kenya immunisation schedule: BCG at birth; pentavalent, OPV and PCV at 6, 10 and 14 weeks;
  measles-rubella at 9 and 18 months; vitamin A from 6 months every 6 months to 59 months; HPV for
  10-year-old girls since October 2019. A second IPV dose added from November 2025 is not asked.
- WHO IMCI (danger signs, fast breathing cut-offs, oral amoxicillin for chest indrawing, Plan B
  75 mL/kg, zinc 10 to 14 days), WHO SAM management (MUAC below 11.5 cm, RUTF, F-75, ReSoMal).
- WHO 2016 ANC model (eight contacts, adopted by Kenya), WHO Labour Care Guide (fetal heart every
  5 minutes in the second stage), WHO PPH guidance (oxytocin 10 IU IM, tranexamic acid within
  3 hours, NASG, uterine balloon tamponade), Pritchard magnesium sulphate regimen, chlorhexidine
  7.1% cord care (Kenya newborn policy), tetracycline eye ointment.
- Kenya Essential Package for Health levels 1 to 6; Kenya Community Health Policy (one community
  health promoter per about 100 households; a community health officer supervises, per the Act); Primary Health
  Care Act, 2023 (CHVs renamed community health promoters).
- Law: Nurses and Midwives Act, Cap 257; Constitution of Kenya 2010, Article 43 and the Fourth
  Schedule; Children Act, 2022; Data Protection Act, 2019; Prohibition of Female Genital Mutilation
  Act, 2011; Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (Control) Act, 1994; Tobacco Control Act,
  2007; Post-Rape Care form (Ministry of Health) and P3 form (police).
- Mathari National Teaching and Referral Hospital (Kenya's psychiatric referral hospital).

## Fact-check, 2026-11-05
- Chlorhexidine digluconate 7.1% gel (delivering 4% chlorhexidine) for newborn cord care: adopted
  by Kenya's Ministry of Health in 2013 (expert panel, Kenya Essential Medicines List) and set out
  in the MOH 2016 guideline; described as the national recommended practice. Kiambu County study,
  PMC, checked 2026-11-05: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12176292/ and
  https://afenet-journal.org/evaluating-effectiveness-of-chlorhexidine-digluconate-7-1-in-comparison-to-alternative-cord-care-practices-in-kiambu-county-kenya-a-cross-sectional-study-on-neonatal-outcomes/
  (WHO's recommendation: chlorhexidine for cord care where neonatal mortality is high). Confirmed.
- Supervision of community health promoters: the Primary Health Care Act, 2023 (No. 13 of 2023,
  in force 2 November 2023) defines a "community health officer" as "a trained health professional
  who is assigned to supervise the community health promoters under their community health unit",
  and s 10(1) has that officer assign households to each promoter; a community health unit is up to
  1,000 households. Bill text (as passed in these definitions):
  https://r4d.org/wp-content/uploads/Kenya-Primary-Health-Care-Bill-2023.pdf ; Act on Kenya Law:
  https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2023/13/eng@2023-11-24 , checked 2026-11-05.
  FIXED: the CHN item said community health assistants supervise promoters (the older Community
  Health Strategy wording); it now asks for the Act's community health officer, and no option names
  community health assistants, so the item has one defensible answer.
- About 100 households per promoter: Kenya News Agency and press on the 2023 CHP rollout
  ("ten promoters with 100 households each ... 1,000 households ... form a Community Health Unit"),
  https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2023-09-25-ruto-flags-off-100000-community-health-promoters-kits ,
  checked 2026-11-05. Kept.
