# LLQP sources (checked 2026-11-04)

## Exam format (landing, FAQ, examFacts only; never in the bank)
- Insurance Council of British Columbia, "Taking the LLQP Exam In-person":
  https://www.insurancecouncilofbc.com/llqp/exam-information-and-registration/about-the-exam/in-person-exams/
  The Harmonized LLQP exam is multiple choice, four options, one best answer; 75 minutes per module;
  each module has five unscored pilot questions; Life Insurance, Segregated Funds and Annuities, and
  Accident and Sickness Insurance have 35 questions scored out of 30; Ethics and Professional
  Practice (Common Law and Civil Code) has 25 questions scored out of 20.
- Service NL (Newfoundland and Labrador), memo "LLQP Modular Exams", November 1, 2017:
  https://www.gov.nl.ca/dgsnl/files/insurance-pdf-memo-llqp-2018.pdf
  "The passing mark per module is 60%." Modular exams; each module written and passed separately.
- AMF (Quebec), "Pilot exam questions":
  https://lautorite.qc.ca/en/becoming-a-professional/insurance-of-persons-and-group-insurance-of-persons/examinations/pilot-exam-questions
  Pilot questions since September 1, 2018, not identified in the exam, time stays 75 minutes.
  Quebec writes the Civil Code version of the Ethics module.
- CISRO, LLQP pages: https://www.cisro-ocra.com/en/llqp/ (curriculum and sample questions; the
  curriculum PDFs did not load from the script, so module topics follow the module names and the
  provincial pages above).
- Guides (not official) say: 60% on each module, scores not averaged; Life and A&S needed for a
  life licence with A&S; Seg Funds module needed to sell segregated funds; a pass window of about
  12 months after the first module in many provinces (varies by province: left off the landing).

Modules (four): Life Insurance; Accident and Sickness Insurance; Segregated Funds and Annuities;
Ethics and Professional Practice (Common Law, or Civil Code in Quebec). An accident and sickness
only licence needs the A&S and Ethics modules (varies by province; left as "varies").

## Content sources for the bank
- CCIR/CISRO Segregated Funds Guidance and Notice, November 19, 2025:
  https://www.cisro-ocra.com/Documents/View/2767 (guidance), /2769 (notice), /2765 (news release).
  Used: two-business-day right to cancel a seg fund contract or deposit, Fund Facts and
  Information Folder, chargeback schedule at most 36 months, 10% chargeback-exempt withdrawal,
  60 days' notice to owners of a fund merger or closure, leveraging-strategy suitability.
- CLHIA guideline on individual variable insurance contracts (seg funds): maturity and death
  benefit guarantees of at least 75% of deposits, maturity at least 10 years after the contract
  or deposit (common knowledge in the industry; stated without a guideline number).
- Provincial Insurance Acts (common-law provinces, uniform life insurance provisions, e.g.
  Ontario Insurance Act Part V): insurable interest and written consent, two-year contestability
  except fraud, misstatement of age, reinstatement within two years, beneficiary designations
  (revocable unless irrevocable is filed with the insurer), family-class beneficiaries and
  creditor protection, payment for minors, 30-day grace period.
- Civil Code of Quebec, arts. 2415 ff. (insurance of persons): spouse designated as beneficiary is
  irrevocable unless stated otherwise (art. 2449); creditor protection for the policyholder's
  married or civil union spouse, descendants and ascendants (art. 2457). Used only as
  "In Quebec" items.
- Income Tax Act (Canada): policy gain on disposition = proceeds minus ACB (s. 148), NCPI reduces
  ACB, rollover to a child where the child is the life insured (148(8)), CDA credit for
  corporate-owned death benefits, deemed disposition at death, RRSP/RRIF rollover to spouse,
  RRSP maturity by the end of the year the annuitant turns 71, spousal RRSP three-year
  attribution, prescribed annuity taxation, taxation of wage-loss replacement benefits
  (6(1)(f)), employer-paid group term life as a taxable benefit.
- Revenu Quebec: employer contributions to private health plans are a taxable benefit in Quebec.
- Employment and Social Development Canada: EI sickness benefits up to 26 weeks (since
  December 18, 2022), one-week waiting period, 55% of insurable earnings; CPP disability for a
  severe and prolonged disability.
- Canada Health Act: five criteria (public administration, comprehensiveness, universality,
  portability, accessibility).
- RAMQ: basic prescription drug insurance is mandatory in Quebec; a person eligible for a group
  plan with drug coverage must join it.
- CCIR/CISRO Guidance: Conduct of Insurance Business and Fair Treatment of Customers (2018) and
  Incentive Management Guidance; FINTRAC obligations for life insurance companies, brokers and
  agents under the PCMLTFA (client identification, record keeping, suspicious transaction
  reports, no tipping off); PIPEDA consent principles; OmbudService for Life and Health
  Insurance (OLHI); Assuris; OSFI.

No amounts that change yearly (contribution limits, YMPE, thresholds) and no fees are used.
