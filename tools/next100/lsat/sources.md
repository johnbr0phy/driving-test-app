# LSAT: sources

Every question and passage in this bank is original, written by Tigertest from LSAC's own
description of the skills each section tests. No LSAC PrepTest, official sample question, LawHub
item or commercial prep item was used, quoted or paraphrased. Passages are invented practice texts.

## Format (all from LSAC)
- LSAC, "Specifications: LSAT and LSAT Argumentative Writing":
  https://www.lsac.org/lsat/register-lsat/accommodations/specifications-lsat-and-lsat-argumentative-writing
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Four 35-minute multiple-choice sections: one Reading Comprehension, two
  Logical Reasoning, one unscored variable section that validates new questions. A 10-minute
  intermission between the second and third sections. LSAT Argumentative Writing: unscored,
  online with remote proctoring, 15 minutes of prewriting analysis then 35 minutes to write; a
  completed writing sample must be on file for a score to be released. Notes that the format
  changes from August 2027.
- LSAC, "LSAT test format": https://www.lsac.org/lsat/taking-lsat/test-format (fetched 2026-11-14).
  Three scored sections and one unscored section; the unscored section is Reading Comprehension or
  Logical Reasoning and can come at any point; Argumentative Writing gives 50 minutes in all.
- LSAC, "Reading Comprehension": https://www.lsac.org/lsat/taking-lsat/test-format/reading-comprehension
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Four sets of five to eight questions; three or four single passages, and a
  set can be two related shorter passages (comparative reading: generalization and instance,
  principle and application, point and counterpoint). Passages from the humanities, social
  sciences, biological and physical sciences and law. Questions on main idea or primary purpose,
  stated information, inference, meaning in context, organization, author's attitude.
- LSAC, "Logical Reasoning": https://www.lsac.org/lsat/taking-lsat/test-format/logical-reasoning
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Skills: parts of an argument and their relationships; similarities and
  differences between patterns of reasoning; drawing well-supported conclusions; reasoning by
  analogy; misunderstandings or points of disagreement; how additional evidence affects an
  argument; detecting assumptions; identifying and applying principles; identifying flaws;
  identifying explanations. The drill families in this bank follow that list.
- LSAC, "Logical Reasoning sample questions", read only for the format, never for content:
  https://www.lsac.org/lsat/taking-lsat/test-format/logical-reasoning/logical-reasoning-sample-questions
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Each question is based on a brief passage; choose the response that most
  accurately and completely answers the question; five lettered choices, (A) to (E).
- LSAC, "LSAT Scoring": https://www.lsac.org/lsat/lsat-scoring (fetched 2026-11-14). Scale 120 to
  180; the score is based on the number answered correctly, converted to the scale; no deduction
  for incorrect answers; a score band and percentile rank (against the previous three testing
  years) on the report.
- LSAC blog, "Starting August 2027, the LSAT will look a little different" (Susan L. Krinsky,
  26 October 2026):
  https://www.lsac.org/blog/starting-august-2027-lsat-will-look-little-different-still-test-same-skills-same-ways
  (fetched 2026-11-14). From August 2027 the unscored questions are embedded: Reading Comprehension
  first (52 minutes, six passages, 40 questions, of which four passages and 27 questions are
  scored), then two Logical Reasoning sections (45 minutes, 32 questions, 25 scored), with a
  10-minute break after each of the first two sections. The current four-section format stays
  through the June 2027 test. This gives the scored counts used here: 25 per Logical Reasoning
  section and 27 Reading Comprehension questions on four passage sets.
- LSAC LawHub, "Redesigned official LSAT PrepTests available now":
  https://app.lawhub.org/article/redesigned-official-lsat-preptests-available-now (checked
  2026-11-14 via search). Analytical Reasoning (logic games) was replaced by a second scored
  Logical Reasoning section from the August 2024 LSAT; the LSAC specifications page above confirms
  the current two Logical Reasoning, one Reading Comprehension structure.

## Modelling choices
- A practice test is the three scored sections at the real 35 minutes each: Logical Reasoning
  (two 25-question timed parts), the intermission, then Reading Comprehension (27 questions on
  three single passages and one comparative pair). The unscored variable section and Argumentative
  Writing are not modelled. Section order on the real test varies.
- Five answer choices on every question, like the real test.
- Scaled score: Tigertest's own curve() estimate from total raw correct out of 77 to 120 to 180.
  Not an LSAC conversion table (each form has its own); labelled an estimate everywhere.
- Logical Reasoning drill families (Tigertest's grouping of LSAC's skill list): argument structure
  (main conclusion, role of a statement, method, disagreement); assumptions (necessary and
  sufficient); strengthen, weaken and explain; flaws; inference (must be true, most strongly
  supported); parallel reasoning and principles. Reading Comprehension: main point and structure;
  detail and inference; comparative reading.
- Per test: structure 8, assumption 9, strengthen/weaken/explain 10, flaw 9, inference 7,
  parallel and principle 7 (50 Logical Reasoning, two parts of 25). LSAC publishes no per-type
  counts; this spread is Tigertest's.

## Not confirmed or left out
- Question counts per section are not on LSAC's current-format pages; the 25/25/27 scored counts
  come from LSAC's own August 2027 announcement, which says the scored content is unchanged.
- Fees, test dates, delivery (remote or test centre) and accommodations are not stated in the
  bank or copy; the landing tells readers to check LSAC.
- Analytical Reasoning (logic games) is not included: it left the LSAT in August 2024.
