# Medication Aide (MACE) bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the official content outline and
the primary sources below. None is copied, adapted or reworded from MACE sample questions in any
candidate handbook, released items, practice exams, or any commercial review book or prep site.

## Outline (Credentia, which now develops and owns the MACE)

- Credentia, Washington Medication Assistant (MACE) handbook: National Medication Aide
  Certification Examination (MACE) Content Outline (last updated 2025-03-31), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://kb.wamace.credentia.com/en/article/national-medication-aide-certification-examination-mace-content-outline
  I. Authorized Duties 16% = 8 questions (A. building relationships, B. delegation, C. role of
  MA-C: permitted duties, restrictions/limitations, D. specific legal and ethical issues,
  E. location and use of resources and references: nurse, pharmacist, physician, package/drug
  insert, drug reference manuals). II. Medication Administration, Observation and Reporting
  60% = 30 questions (A. administering and charting: orders, documentation, storage, disposal;
  B. safety and rights of medication administration; C. routes; D. factors affecting how the
  body uses medication; E. classifications by body system and action; F. rights of individuals;
  G. causes and reporting of medication errors; H. reporting of symptoms and side effects;
  I. reporting any change from the client's normal condition). III. Medication Concepts and
  Measurements 24% = 12 questions (A. concepts: terminology and abbreviations, dosage range,
  actions and implications, therapeutic and other side effects such as idiosyncratic,
  paradoxical, antagonist, precautions, interactions; B. forms: liquid, solid and semi-solid;
  C. measurements). The 8 + 30 + 12 add to 50, the scored items.
- Credentia, Colorado MACE handbook, The Medication Aide Certification Examination Overview
  (last updated 2025-12-30), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://co-mace.credentia.com/en/article/the-medication-aide-certification-examination-overview
  Same three areas and percentages (the page prints "60% = 3 questions" for area II, a typo for
  30). The online written examination has 60 multiple-choice questions and two hours; candidates
  are told when 15 minutes are left.
- NCSBN, NNAAP and MACE page, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.ncsbn.org/exams/nnaap-and-mace.page
  "NCSBN no longer develops and owns the content for the NNAAP and the MACE"; questions on
  content and scheduling go to Credentia. The MACE is "typically administered to nurse aides who
  choose to receive additional training to become certified medication aides." The older NCSBN
  MACE test plan page and content outline PDF (ncsbn.org/mace-test-plan,
  ncsbn.org/public-files/MACE_Content_Outline.pdf) now return 404; search snippets of that PDF
  give 60 items, 50 scored and 10 pretest, the same split as Credentia's 8/30/12 of 50.

## Format (Credentia state handbooks)

- Credentia, Washington MACE Exam Overview (last updated 2025-05-21), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://kb.wamace.credentia.com/en/article/exam-overview
  60 multiple-choice questions in English, online proctored, scored immediately.
- Credentia, Washington Exam Results (last updated 2026-01-06) and Colorado Score Reporting
  (2025-12-31), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://kb.wamace.credentia.com/en/article/exam-results and
  https://co-mace.credentia.com/en/article/score-reporting
  Results in the candidate's Credentia account within 24 business hours; a failing report gives
  diagnostic information; a new fee for each retake; three attempts in these states' rules.
  No passing score or percentage is published, so Tigertest's 70% is a readiness line. Retake
  limits, waits and eligibility are state rules and are only described as varying by state.

## Practice content used in questions

- NCSBN, Medication Assistant-Certified (MA-C) Model Curriculum, adopted by the Delegate
  Assembly August 9, 2007, fetched 2026-11-15 (PDF, 11 pages):
  https://www.ncsbn.org/public-files/07_Final_MAC.pdf
  The curriculum the MACE content is based on. Used for: supervision by a licensed nurse; the
  MA-C definition excluding parenteral routes and nasogastric, gastrostomy and jejunostomy
  tubes; acts that shall not be delegated (conversion or calculation of dosage, assessment of
  need for or response to medication, nursing judgment on PRN medications); not administering
  when the nurse is unavailable or the client is unstable; no verbal or telephone orders;
  elements of a complete order (signed, legible, drug, dose, route, time, frequency); MAR,
  controlled substance log, storage area, room, cart and tray; disposal of outdated,
  contaminated or unused medication; the MA-C does not convert dosages; apothecaries', metric
  and household systems, grain (weight) and minim (volume); liquid, solid and semi-solid forms
  (suspension needs shaking, time-released and coated forms not crushed, scored and unscored
  tablets); terminology (generic and brand), standardized abbreviations and those no longer to
  be used, dosage range, action, implications, therapeutic and side effects, precautions,
  contraindications, allergic reactions and anaphylaxis, adverse reactions, tolerance,
  interactions (grapefruit juice), additive (synergistic), antagonist, idiosyncratic and
  paradoxical effects; the three safety checks and six rights (client, drug, dose, route, time,
  documentation); preparation steps (wash hands, review pulse and blood pressure medications,
  identify, introduce, explain, glove if necessary, position, do what you explained, wash hands,
  special considerations, document); causes of errors and reporting to the nurse with an
  incident report; reporting changes in temperature, pulse, respirations, blood pressure, pain,
  urine output, mental status and activity; initials and time on the MAR, circling and
  documenting refusals; delegation is client and situation specific, cannot be redelegated, and
  the aide speaks up and asks for training; the nurse keeps overall responsibility and sets the
  level of supervision, nursing administration validates credentials, trust is central; rights
  of individuals (confidentiality, privacy, individuality and autonomy, respectful communication,
  wishes, refusal, being informed); abuse types, duty to report, negligence, fraud, theft and
  diversion; routes (oral, buccal, sublingual, metered-dose inhaler, nebulizer, nasal, eye, ear,
  topical, dressings, soaks, transdermal, rectal and vaginal suppositories); factors (age, size,
  family traits, diet, disease, psychological issues, gender and basal metabolic rate, dosage);
  classes by body system (antimicrobial, cardiovascular, dermatological, endocrine,
  gastrointestinal, musculoskeletal, neurological, nutrients, respiratory, sensory, urinary).
- FDA, Disposal of Unused Medicines: What You Should Know, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.fda.gov/drugs/safe-disposal-medicines/disposal-unused-medicines-what-you-should-know
  Take-back sites and mail-back envelopes first; flush only drugs on the FDA flush list;
  otherwise mix (uncrushed) with dirt, cat litter or used coffee grounds, seal in a plastic bag,
  and scratch out personal information on the empty container's label.
- The Joint Commission, Official "Do Not Use" List (PDF dated 9/14/18):
  https://www.jointcommission.org/-/media/tjc/documents/resources/patient-safety-topics/patient-safety/do_not_use_list_9_14_18.pdf
  Returned 403 to automated fetches on 2026-11-15; its entries were checked from the indexed text
  of that PDF: U (write "unit"), QD (write "daily"), QOD (write "every other day"). Only those
  three are asked. The MA-C model curriculum itself tells aides not to use abbreviations that
  should no longer be used and points to this list.

## Left out on purpose

- What a medication aide may do differs by state (some states allow more routes or tasks than
  the model curriculum). Every scope item says "under the model curriculum" and one item tells
  the aide to follow the state's own rules; no state's rule is asserted.
- No dosage calculations or conversions (the model curriculum says the MA-C does not convert
  or calculate dosages), no drug doses, no fees, no eligibility hours and no retake rules.
- Specific drug names are limited to acetaminophen and metoprolol as examples; no item asks a
  dose or a brand-to-generic match.
- Written against data/cna-questions.json, data/v2/nclexpn/questions.json and
  data/ptcb-questions.json (and the ccma, cma and rma banks): items that repeated a fact and
  answer there (financial abuse by a relative, removing the old patch, NPO, the antipyretic
  definition, leaving pills at the bedside, reporting a pain rating) were rewritten.
