# NAPLEX (`naplex`): sources

All questions in this bank are original, written for Tigertest from NABP's public content outline and
candidate bulletin and from primary US sources (federal law and regulation, FDA labeling and safety pages,
CDC guidance). No NABP material that contains questions was used beyond reading the names of its formats:
the four sample items in NABP's 2020 "Competency Statements and Sample Questions" PDF and the two sample items
printed in the 2025 content outline were read only to confirm item formats and are not reproduced,
reworded or used as topics (no item here asks about their drugs or calculations). No Pre-NAPLEX, NAPLEX
Advantage, past or leaked exam content and no commercial review book or question bank (RxPrep, UWorld,
Kaplan, APhA review or others) was read or used. Drugs are named by generic name only. Patient cases are
fictional.

## Content outline (fetched 2026-11-14)
- NABP, "NAPLEX Content Outline. Effective for all NAPLEX exams beginning May 1, 2025" (PDF, (c)2025),
  fetched 2026-11-14: https://nabp.pharmacy/wp-content/uploads/NAPLEX-Content-Outline.pdf
  - Five content domains, weights as a share of the 200 scored questions: 1 Foundational Knowledge for
    Pharmacy Practice 25% (50); 2 Medication Use Process (Prescribing, Transcribing and Documenting,
    Dispensing, Administering, and Monitoring) 25% (50); 3 Person-Centered Assessment and Treatment Planning
    40% (80); 4 Professional Practice 5% (10); 5 Pharmacy Management and Leadership 5% (10).
  - Subdomains used as item `skill` labels and to split the drills: 1A pharmaceutical science (pharmacology;
    PK, PD, pharmacogenomics; pharmaceutics); 1B compounding (nonsterile, sterile); 1C calculations (nine
    kinds, from patient parameters to PK parameters); 1D drug development; 1E research design and
    biostatistics; 1F drug information resources; 2A prescription and order interpretation (names and classes,
    indications and dosing, dosage forms, boxed warnings and REMS, safety and effectiveness); 2B therapeutic
    substitution (formularies, shortages, biosimilars); 2C immunization (scheduling, contraindications,
    storage, administration, adverse reactions); 2D handling, storage, stability and disposal; 3A medication
    and allergy history and reconciliation; 3B health histories and screening; 3C patient conditions
    (pathophysiology, appropriateness, interactions, errors and omissions, ADRs, toxicology, adherence);
    3D monitoring (goals, safety, effectiveness); 3E patient education; 3F OTC and supplements; 3G devices and
    self-monitoring tests; 4A ADE and error reporting (MedWatch, VAERS); 4B public health (tobacco cessation,
    antimicrobial and opioid stewardship, screenings); 4C social determinants of health; 4D ethics;
    5A operations; 5B inventory (recalls, shortages); 5C quality improvement (MUE, RCA, CQI);
    5D mentorship and preceptorship.
- NABP blog, "Breaking Down the NAPLEX Content Outline" (posted 2025-04-23), fetched 2026-11-14:
  https://nabp.pharmacy/news/blog/breaking-down-the-naplex-content-outline/
  - Outline released October 1, 2024, in force from May 1, 2025; same five domains and weights.

## Format (fetched 2026-11-14)
- NABP, NAPLEX/MPJE Candidate Application Bulletin (online edition dated March 2026), Section 3 "What Is the
  NAPLEX?", fetched 2026-11-14: https://read.nxtbook.com/nabp/bulletin/naplex_mpje_bulletin/what_is_the_naplex.html
  - 6-hour exam of 225 questions in a computerized fixed form; 200 scored, 25 unscored pretest questions
    spread through the exam. Most questions are scenario-based with a patient profile or medical record;
    stand-alone questions are mixed in. Questions must be answered in order, none can be skipped, and an
    answer cannot be changed after moving on. Two optional 10-minute breaks; total appointment 6 hours 45
    minutes. Retakes: 45-day wait, 3 attempts in 12 months, 5 attempts in all.
- Same bulletin, Section 10 "Taking Your Exams":
  https://read.nxtbook.com/nabp/bulletin/naplex_mpje_bulletin/taking_your_exams.html
  - On-screen calculator with scientific and 5-function modes; "many of the calculations on the NAPLEX will
    require the on-screen scientific calculator". No personal calculators.
- Same bulletin, Section 11 "Exam Results and Other Outcomes":
  https://read.nxtbook.com/nabp/bulletin/naplex_mpje_bulletin/exam_results.html
  - Results are reported as Pass or Fail, with no scaled score; failing reports show relative performance in
    the five content areas; unanswered questions are scored as incorrect.
- NABP Support Center, "What does the scaled score and domain level information mean?", fetched 2026-11-14:
  https://nabp.pharmacy/help/scaled-score-and-domain-level-meaning/
  - The NAPLEX scale runs 0 to 150 and "a scaled score of 75 or higher represents a passing score"; scaled
    scores are no longer provided for the NAPLEX itself.
- NABP, NAPLEX Exam Day Information page, fetched 2026-11-14:
  https://nabp.pharmacy/programs/examinations/naplex/test-day-information/
  - Confirms 6 hours, 225 questions, fixed form, pass or fail, 5 attempts.
- NABP, "NAPLEX Competency Statements and Sample Questions" (2020 PDF), fetched 2026-11-14, read for item
  format names only: https://nabp.pharmacy/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/NAPLEX-Competency-Statement-Sample-Questions.pdf
  - Formats: multiple choice, multiple response ("select ALL that apply"), constructed response (numeric
    entry with a rounding instruction), ordered response (all options must be used) and hot spot (click a
    region of an image). Hot spot is not modelled (the bank has no images).

## Law, regulation and clinical references (fetched 2026-11-14)
- DEA Diversion Control Division, "Controlled Substances - Alphabetical Order" (Orange Book list),
  fetched 2026-11-14: https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/schedules/orangebook/c_cs_alpha.pdf
  - Schedules used: oxycodone, hydrocodone, methylphenidate, amphetamine, lisdexamfetamine, codeine (single
    entity) II; buprenorphine, ketamine, anabolic steroids III; alprazolam, lorazepam, tramadol, zolpidem,
    carisoprodol, phenobarbital, modafinil, phentermine, suvorexant IV; pregabalin, lacosamide,
    brivaracetam, cenobamate V. Marijuana stays Schedule I in this list; the rescheduling proceeding is
    not asked about.
- FDA, "Disposal of Unused Medicines: What You Should Know" (content current as of 2024-10-31), fetched
  2026-11-14: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/safe-disposal-medicines/disposal-unused-medicines-what-you-should-know
  - Take-back first, then the flush list only for listed drugs, then household trash mixed with an
    unappealing substance in a sealed bag, with the label scratched out.
- FDA, "Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies | REMS" (content current as of 2025-05-20), fetched
  2026-11-14: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/risk-evaluation-and-mitigation-strategies-rems
  - What a REMS is and that only a few medicines need one; the olanzapine pamoate post-injection delirium
    sedation syndrome REMS (certified facility, 3 hours of observation).
- CDC, "Vaccine Storage and Handling" (HCP page), fetched 2026-11-14:
  https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/storage-handling/index.html
  - Digital data loggers with a backup, do not use vaccine after an excursion until the manufacturer or
    public health confirms it, use only the supplied diluent, never freeze diluent, light-sensitive vaccines.
- CDC, Adult Immunization Schedule by age (2025, addendum July 2, 2025), fetched 2026-11-14:
  https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/adult-age.html
  - Read to see which schedule details are in flux; see "Left out" below.
- CDC, "CDC Clinical Practice Guideline for Prescribing Opioids for Pain, United States, 2022" (MMWR Recomm
  Rep 2022;71(3)), fetched 2026-11-14: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/71/rr/rr7103a1.htm
  - Nonopioid therapy first, immediate-release opioids at the lowest effective dose when started, as-needed
    use for acute pain, naloxone offered, caution with benzodiazepines.
- National Library of Medicine, DailyMed (FDA-approved prescribing information), checked 2026-11-14:
  https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/
  - Drug facts in the questions (boxed warnings, contraindications, key interactions, monitoring, storage)
    are the long-standing ones in current US labeling.

- FDA, "Drug Disposal: FDA's Flush List for Certain Medicines", fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.fda.gov/drugs/disposal-unused-medicines-what-you-should-know/drug-disposal-fdas-flush-list-certain-medicines
  - The list includes "any drug that contains the word fentanyl"; flush only listed drugs and only when no
    take-back option is readily available.
- FDA, "Recalls Background and Definitions", fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.fda.gov/safety/industry-guidance-recalls/recalls-background-and-definitions
  - Class I, II and III definitions; a market withdrawal is a minor violation not subject to FDA legal action.
- FDA, Cordarone (amiodarone) prescribing information (Reference ID 4336449), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2018/018972s054lbl.pdf
  - Warfarin: prothrombin time up 100% after 3 to 4 days; reduce warfarin by one-third to one-half and
    monitor. Digoxin: reduce by half or stop.
- DailyMed, bumetanide injection (Fresenius Kabi), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a007f3aa-3977-4be3-a9a2-db3d458bca58
  - "1 mg bumetanide has a diuretic potency equivalent to approximately 40 mg furosemide." The common
    clinical reading is oral furosemide; IV furosemide works at about half the oral dose.
- Drug information resources: primary (original research), secondary (indexing and abstracting services),
  tertiary (review articles, textbooks, compendia), the standard classification taught in US PharmD programs.

## Could not be loaded or confirmed
- ISMP's error-prone abbreviations list and the Joint Commission "Do Not Use" list returned 403 to the
  fetcher. Abbreviation items keep to the long-standing core (U, IU, trailing and leading zeros, qd and
  qod, MS and MSO4) and say "error-prone" rather than citing either list's wording.
- USP general chapters 795 and 797 returned 403. Compounding items are written at concept level (laminar
  airflow, ISO classes named only as "ISO Class 5 primary engineering control", hand hygiene, garbing
  order principles) and never quote a beyond-use date table.

## Deliberately left out
- Immunization ages and dose counts that ACIP changed or was reviewing in 2025-2026 (COVID-19, hepatitis B
  birth dose, RSV ages, pneumococcal and zoster starting ages). Immunization items cover storage, live
  vaccine contraindications, administration technique and VAERS only.
- State pharmacy law (the MPJE covers it), fees, retake rules and anything about the exam itself.
- Prices, dollar thresholds and brand names.
