# NCPT (NCCT) bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from NCCT's official detailed test plan and
the primary standards below. None is copied, adapted or reworded from NCCT practice tests, NCCT
review material, released items or any commercial review book or prep site.

## Outline (National Center for Competency Testing)

- NCCT, National Certified Phlebotomy Technician (NCPT) Detailed Test Plan, Effective January 2025
  (EX-0513, 3 pages), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.ncctinc.com/Documents/NCPT_2025.pdf
  100 scored items and 25 unscored pretest items, three hours; 92% standard four-option multiple
  choice and 8% alternative items (drag and drop, multi-select, hotspot). Content categories and
  scored items: 1 Infection Control and Safety 15; 2 Preparing for Specimen Collection 25
  (A Equipment Selection 12, B Patient and Site Preparation 13); 3 Specimen Collection 30;
  4 Collection Complications and Troubleshooting 15; 5 Law and Ethics 15. Every task statement and
  the 28-item Essential Knowledge Base were used category by category.
- NCCT, PT Comparison to Previous Detailed Test Plan (2025), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.ncctinc.com/Documents/PT%20Comparison%20to%20previous%20DTP%202025.pdf
  The old plan had 125 scored items in six categories; the 2025 plan cut it to 100, added tasks on
  patient education, venipuncture, consent and preventing legal action, and deleted quality
  control records, order entry, arterial site selection and special non-blood fluids (CSF and
  the like), so the bank leaves those out.
- NCCT, Forms and Documents page, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.ncctinc.com/forms-and-documents
  Lists the NCPT eligibility criteria, detailed test plan, comparison, executive summary and the
  Candidate Handbook.
- NCCT, Phlebotomy Technician Eligibility Criteria, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.ncctinc.com/Documents/PT%20Route.pdf
  Pathways: high school, current student, graduate within five years of an NCCT-authorized
  program (Registered Apprenticeships included), one year of full-time experience, military,
  instructor; a Critical Skills Competency Form (50 venipunctures and 10 capillary punctures in the
  2025 plan, per the comparison document); California and Louisiana candidates check state rules.

## Format (NCCT Candidate Handbook, revised January 2026)

- NCCT Candidate Handbook, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.ncctinc.com/Documents/CandidateHandbook.pdf
  NCPT: 3 hours, computer based, four-option multiple choice and innovative item types,
  100 scored and 25 pretest items. English only. No penalty for guessing. Scores reported from
  200 to 720 with every pass point scaled to 575, set by a criterion-referenced panel standard;
  results right after the exam. NCCT test site or remote testing. Re-examination: 30 days after a
  first and second failure, 90 days after a third, then 180 days with an appeal and proof of
  remediation. Fees are never stated by Tigertest. NCPT is NCCA accredited.

## Practice standards and law used in questions

- WHO guidelines on drawing blood: best practices in phlebotomy (2010), publication page
  fetched 2026-11-16: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241599221
  PDF fetched the same day:
  https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/10682984-b7a0-4e4b-bd32-0cbc7d22136b/content
  Used for: identification and asking about fainting and allergies; median cubital vein first,
  basilic risk (artery and nerve), no needle where veins branch; tourniquet 4 to 5 finger widths
  above the site, released before withdrawal and before two minutes; 70% alcohol, 30 seconds and
  let dry, povidone iodine raising potassium, phosphorus and uric acid; do not retouch the site;
  anchor with the thumb below the site; 30 degrees or less; arm extended, not bent; no draw from
  an existing IV site; do not push a syringe plunger; tube table (citrate full draw, sodium heparin
  for lithium, EDTA full draw, ACD for HLA and paternity, fluoride/oxalate for glucose); gauge
  choice and haemolysis with small gauges; capillary site table (heel birth to about 6 months,
  middle and ring finger, across fingerprint lines), avoid posterior heel, 1.5 mm finger depth to
  8 years, wipe the first drop, do not squeeze, capillary order haematology, chemistry, blood bank;
  one puncture per lancet; tourniquets as an MRSA source; label before the patient leaves with
  name, number, date of birth, date and time; leak-proof bag with the form outside.
- CLSI, Order of Blood Draw: Tubes and Additives (blog), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://clsi.org/about/blog/order-of-blood-draw-tubes-and-additives/
  GP41 order: blood culture, sodium citrate, serum (with or without clot activator or gel),
  heparin, EDTA, sodium fluoride/potassium oxalate; the same order for syringes; a plastic serum
  tube with clot activator may interfere with coagulation testing.
- OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1030
  No bending, recapping or removing contaminated needles except by a one-handed or mechanical
  method where required; no shearing or breaking; sharps containers closable, puncture resistant,
  leakproof, labeled, upright, close to use, not overfilled; hepatitis B vaccine offered within 10
  working days at no cost, declination statement; immediate confidential post-exposure evaluation
  at no cost; written opinion within 15 days; training at assignment and at least annually;
  sharps injury log; exposure control plan reviewed at least annually; wash hands after removing
  gloves; no eating or drinking in exposure areas; fluorescent orange or orange-red biohazard
  labels, or red bags.
- CDC, Clinical Safety: Hand Hygiene for Healthcare Workers, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html
  Sanitizer of at least 60% alcohol; soap and water when hands are visibly soiled and in
  C. difficile outbreaks; hand hygiene immediately after removing gloves.
- CDC, Transmission-Based Precautions, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/basics/transmission-based-precautions.html
  Contact: gown and gloves on entry, removed before leaving, dedicated equipment; droplet: mask on
  entry; airborne (tuberculosis, measles, chickenpox): N95 respirator, AIIR.
- HIPAA minimum necessary standard, 45 CFR 164.502(b), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.502
  Reasonable efforts to limit PHI to the minimum necessary; not applied to disclosures to a
  provider for treatment or to the patient.
- FDA, CLIA Categorizations, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/ivd-regulatory-assistance/clia-categorizations
  FDA (CDRH) categorizes tests as waived, moderate or high complexity; uncategorized tests count as
  high complexity.
- American Hospital Association, The Patient Care Partnership, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.aha.org/other-resources/patient-care-partnership
  The plain-language brochure that replaced the AHA Patient's Bill of Rights: high quality care, a
  clean and safe environment, involvement in care, privacy, preparing to leave, help with the bill.
- The Joint Commission, National Patient Safety Goals Effective January 2026 for the Laboratory
  Program, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/3c7a110c215943bc80d9ce87e9d9ee9d
  NPSG.01.01.01: use at least two patient identifiers when collecting blood samples and other
  specimens; the room number or physical location is not an identifier; label containers in the
  presence of the patient.
- Frequently Asked Questions on NFPA 704 (NFPA's FAQ, as hosted by the Cambridge, MA fire
  department), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.Cambridgema.gov/-/media/Files/firedepartment/lepcinfo/704_FAQs.pdf
  Blue for health (nine o'clock), red for flammability (twelve), yellow for instability (three),
  white for special hazards (six); ratings 0 to 4.
- Standard textbook facts the test plan's Essential Knowledge Base names (tort definitions,
  laboratory departments, basic vascular anatomy, urine, stool, sputum and swab collection,
  chain-of-custody steps, specimen handling for light and temperature). CLSI GP41, GP42 and GP44
  themselves are paid documents and were not read; facts that rest only on them (heel depth
  figures beyond WHO's, exact inversion counts, serum separation times) were left out, and
  inversion is asked only as "follow the manufacturer".

## Bank decisions

- Per 50-question test, from NCCT's 15/12/13/30/15/15 of 100: Infection Control and Safety 8,
  Equipment Selection 6, Patient and Site Preparation 7, Specimen Collection 15 (blood collection
  procedures 8; point-of-care, special and non-blood collections, processing and delivery 7),
  Collection Complications 7, Law and Ethics 7. The split inside Specimen Collection and the
  rounding are Tigertest's.
- Tigertest's 70% line is a readiness target; NCCT's 575 is a scaled score, not a percent.
- Alternative item types (drag and drop, multi-select, hotspot) are written as single answer.
- No question asks about the exam itself; no fees; state rules (scope of practice, minors'
  consent) are "varies by state"; no provider or course named.
- Duplicates: written against data/phlebotomy-questions.json (NHA CPT), data/rpt-questions.json,
  data/ascp-pbt-questions.json and data/ncma-questions.json and scanned for repeated stems and
  fact-and-answer pairs; matches were rewritten.
