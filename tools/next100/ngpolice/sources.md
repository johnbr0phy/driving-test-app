## Who runs the recruitment, and the latest exercise
- Police Service Commission (PSC) home page, news list (read 2026-11-01): 11 Dec 2025 announced the
  recruitment of 50,000 constables into the Nigeria Police Force, portal opened, "PSC, NPF commit to
  a seamless process"; 26 Jan 2026 over 400,000 applications, deadline extended two weeks;
  24 Apr 2026 "written examination" scheduled for 28-30 April 2026, warning against scams.
  https://www.psc.gov.ng/
- Federal Ministry of Information (fmino.gov.ng), 26 Apr 2026, "Police Recruitment: Written
  Examination Holds 28th-30th April, 2026 As PSC Warns Against Scams" (a Delta State Police Command
  press statement): PSC in collaboration with the NPF; written examination 28-30 April 2026 at
  designated centres in the 36 states and the FCT; for General Duty and Specialist applicants who
  passed the physical and credentials screening; invitation card from www.npfapplication.psc.gov.ng
  from 24 April 2026; bring the card, a pen and the NIN slip; recruitment is free. Does not state
  the number of questions, time or subjects.
  https://fmino.gov.ng/police-recruitment-written-examination-holds-28th-30th-april-2026-as-psc-warns-against-scams/
- News Express, 11 Dec 2025, step-by-step guide to the 2025/2026 recruitment (reproducing the
  advertisement): applications 15 Dec 2025 to 25 Jan 2026 at https://npfapplication.psc.gov.ng;
  Nigerian by birth; General Duty 18 to 25 years with five credits including English and
  Mathematics; Specialists up to 28 with four credits and trade experience; height and chest
  minimums; NIN required; stages: online application, physical and medical examinations, aptitude
  tests, background checks.
  https://newsexpressngr.com/news/287092/step-by-step-guide-on-how-to-apply-for-police-20252026-job-recruitment
- Blueprint, 15 Feb 2024, "PSC shortlists 171,956 applicants for JAMB CBT" (PSC press statement):
  for the 2024 exercise the PSC's Police Recruitment Board had JAMB conduct the computer-based
  aptitude test for General Duty applicants (5 March 2024); successful candidates then had a medical
  examination before training; specialists took practical tests. Shows the test is a CBT, and that
  the delivery body can change from one exercise to the next.
- Blueprint and Newstimes (April 2026, via search, 2026-11-01) call the 28-30 April 2026 test a

  https://blueprint.ng/recruitment-police-announce-date-for-aptitude-test/

## Format: what is and is not confirmed
- NOT published by the PSC or NPF: the number of questions, the time, the subject split or a pass
  mark. Guides (open-exam-prep.com and similar, via search, 2026-11-01) describe the CBT as English,
  mathematics, general knowledge and current affairs, with figures that disagree: "about 60
  questions in 30 to 45 minutes", "60 to 100 questions in 45 to 60 minutes", "about 100 questions
  in roughly one hour". Labelled "guides say" on the landing.
- Tigertest's model: four 50-question mock tests, 70% (35 of 50) as Tigertest's target, weighted
  English 15, mathematics 14, reasoning 5, civics/Constitution/rights/policing 16 per test. Current
  affairs are left out on purpose (they date within a year); the landing says so.

## Law used in the GK questions
- Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (with amendments), English text at the
  Constitute Project (read 2026-11-01): s.1 supremacy; s.3 states and FCT; s.4-6 legislative,
  executive and judicial powers; Senate three per state plus one for the FCT, House of
  Representatives 360; s.35(2) right to remain silent until consulting a legal practitioner or
  person of choice; s.35(3) informed in writing within 24 hours; s.35(4) release if not tried
  within two months (custody) or three months (bail); s.35(5) "reasonable time" one day where a
  court is within 40 km, otherwise two days or longer; s.214 Nigeria Police Force, no other police
  force; s.215 IGP appointed by the President on the advice of the Nigeria Police Council, a
  Commissioner of Police for each state appointed by the Police Service Commission, Force under the
  command of the IGP, Governor's directions and referral to the President; s.216 Council
  delegation and consultation; Third Schedule: Nigeria Police Council (President as chairman,
  every Governor, PSC chairman, IGP; general supervision, advising on the IGP), Police Service
  Commission (power to appoint to offices other than IGP, discipline and dismiss), INEC (chairman
  plus twelve National Electoral Commissioners; elections, party registration, voter
  registration); First Schedule FCT six area councils; Fifth Schedule asset declarations to the
  Code of Conduct Bureau. Chapter II fundamental objectives and duties of the citizen (assist lawful
  agencies in maintaining law and order); Chapter III citizenship by birth, registration and
  naturalisation; Chapter IV rights (life, dignity, liberty, fair hearing, private and family life,
  thought/conscience/religion, expression, assembly and association, movement, freedom from
  discrimination); s.9 amendment (two-thirds of each chamber and two-thirds of state Houses of
  Assembly); s.46 High Court enforcement. The 774 total (768 LGAs + 6 FCT area councils) follows
  the First Schedule.
  https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Nigeria_2011
- Police Act 2020 (assented September 2020): repealed the Police Act Cap. P19 LFN 2004 (which
  began as the 1943 Police Ordinance); section 4 general duties (prevention and detection of crime,
  protection of life and property, public safety and order, enforcement of laws); community
  policing provisions (sections 113 to 119). Via PLAC comparison and legal commentary found by
  search; no section numbers of the Act are cited in the questions.
  https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Comparison-between-the-Repealed-Police-Act-1943-and-the-New-Police-Act-2020.pdf
- Administration of Criminal Justice Act 2015: no arrest in place of a suspect; restraint or
  handcuffs only where violence or escape is feared, for safety, or on a court order. Cited by name
  only. Anti-Torture Act 2017 named in one explanation.
- 112 as Nigeria's toll-free national emergency number (NCC Emergency Communications Centres).
- Nigeria Police Academy at Wudil, Kano State (degree-awarding; Regular Course 7 commissioned as
  ASPs, Nov 2025, per VON and Blueprint via search, 2026-11-01); NPF statement that police bail is
  free; rank order Constable, Corporal, Sergeant, Sergeant Major, Inspector (Police Regulations
  reg. 273 as cited by ThisDay, 1 Oct 2018:
  https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2018/10/01/nigeria-58-the-nigerian-police-from-independence);
  CP, AIG, DIG, IGP.
- 112: NCC national emergency code since 2018, routed through Emergency Communication Centres to
  police, FRSC, NSCDC, fire and ambulance; adopted as the unified national emergency number at the
  157th National Economic Council meeting, 30 April 2026; nationwide rollout roadmap ordered Sept
  2026 (fmino.gov.ng, saharareporters.com 8 Sept 2026, via search 2026-11-01). The question says
  only that 112 is the toll-free national emergency number.
