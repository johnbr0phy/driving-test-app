- FSBPT, NPTE Candidate Handbook, version 2025.02, read directly:
  https://FSBPT.org/Portals/0/documents/free-resources/NPTE_Candidate_Handbook.pdf
  PTA exam: 4 sections, 180 multiple-choice questions (45 per section), 4 hours of testing time,
  4 hours 30 minutes total appointment (scheduled 15-minute break after section 2, tutorial, survey).
  40 of the PTA items are unscored pretest items. Each section is not separately timed. Some items
  include a graphic or video; scenario-based questions (2 to 5 per shared scenario, up to 35 on the
  PTA exam) appear at the end of each section. One correct answer per question, no penalty for wrong
  answers. Scaled scores 200 to 800; the passing standard is a scaled score of 600 in every
  jurisdiction. Computer-based at Prometric, on fixed dates four times a year (January, April, July,
  October).

- CMS, Medicare Benefit Policy Manual, Chapter 15, section 220.3 (documentation requirements for
  therapy services): the clinician writes a progress report at least once every 10 treatment days;
  assistants may write elements of progress between reports but not the progress report itself.
  https://www.cms.gov/Regulations-and-Guidance/Guidance/Manuals/Downloads/bp102c15.pdf

## Bank decisions

- Per 50-question test: 8 / 12 / 11 / 9 / 3 / 3 / 4, the outline midpoints scaled to 50. The small
  body systems (integumentary, lymphatic, metabolic and endocrine, GI, GU) and system interactions are
  one category ("Other Systems & System Interactions"); safety, professional responsibilities and
  research are one category.
- Tigertest's 70% pass line is a readiness target, not FSBPT's standard (a scaled 600, whose raw
  equivalent changes by form).
- Graphic, video and scenario items are written as text scenarios; no images.
- PTA scope only: the PTA carries out and adjusts interventions within the PT's plan of care, collects
  data, reports changes to the PT; evaluation, plan of care changes and discharge are the PT's. PTA
  supervision rules (on-site or general, ratios) vary by state and are not asserted.
- Clinical figures used: ABI below 0.9 suggests PAD; resting BP above about 200/110 holds exercise;
  SpO2 below about 88% with activity calls for rest; phase I cardiac rehab HR within about 20 bpm of
  rest; heart rate reserve (Karvonen) example 220 - 58 = 162, reserve 88, 50% = 118; Berg maximum 56
  (about 45 or less linked to fall risk); TUG over about 13.5 s linked to fall risk in community
  older adults; 5.07 (10 g) monofilament for protective sensation; pre-exercise glucose below about
  100 mg/dL on insulin needs carbohydrate, glucose above about 250 mg/dL with ketones holds exercise
  (ACSM/ADA exercise guidance); 1 MHz ultrasound for deeper tissue, 3 MHz superficial; hot packs 6 to
  8 towel layers; NMES off time longer than on time.
- No exam-admin questions, no fees, no prep provider named.
- Dedupe: stems compared by word overlap with every bank in data/ and data/v2/emt (nasm-cpt, cna,
  cet, cma, ccma, cpr, first-aid, psw, gulf-nursing, nbdhe and others). Items repeating a fact and
  answer were rewritten: warfarin INR (now a head strike on warfarin), nitroglycerin first aid (now
  nitroglycerin and orthostatic dizziness), 220 - age target HR (now heart rate reserve with less
  common numbers), age of walking (now order of milestones), hypoglycemia first aid (now
  hyperglycemia with ketones), supine in pregnancy (now diastasis recti), paper chart correction
  (now Medicare progress reports). Review pass (2026-11-03): a parallel-bars head strike replaced the
  gym trip that echoed NASM-PRO-004; weak distractors replaced on four items.

All 200 items are original, written for Tigertest from the FSBPT outline. None is copied from FSBPT's
PEAT, sample questions or any commercial review.
