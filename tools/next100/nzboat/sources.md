# Sources: Coastguard Boating Education Day Skipper, New Zealand (`nzboat`)

Every question is original, written by Tigertest from the official sources below. No Coastguard
assessment, sample, quiz item or commercial practice question was used or reworded (the five quiz
questions on Coastguard's old Day Skipper page were read only to confirm the course's scope; some
rules they cover are asked here, but none of their wording is reused).

## Course outline and format (exam owner)
- Coastguard Boating Education, "Day Skipper Course | Classroom and Online", fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.boatingeducation.org.nz/courses/1/day-skipper/
  Course purpose: general knowledge to operate a pleasure craft in familiar waters in daylight at an
  introductory level. Learning outcomes: components of a boat and its equipment, basic boat handling,
  use of navigational charts, basic tide and weather terminology, navigational marks, Maritime Rules
  and Regulations, causes of and preventive actions in emergencies, common marine knots. Delivery:
  classroom (4 weeks including 2 days in class), virtual classroom (16 hours of online tutoring) or
  online self-directed (4 weeks), each "including an online assessment"; Day Skipper Unit Standards
  option is 8 weeks. Internal Coastguard Education certificate only, not recognised by NZQA. Domestic
  learners 16 and over; certificates sent within 4 weeks. No question count, time limit or pass mark
  is published. The bank's categories follow these eight outcomes; four tests of 50 and the 80% line
  are Tigertest's target and are labelled so everywhere.
- Coastguard Boating Education, Domestic Learner Handbook, 18 March 2026 (PDF), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.boatingeducation.org.nz/downloads/downloads/Domestic_Learners_Handbook_18_March_2026_Spread.pdf
  Assessments are completed at the end of each course; if an assessment does not meet the required
  pass level the learner gets feedback and a second attempt at the questions they got wrong, then
  must re-enrol if still unsuccessful. No numeric pass level given. Landing copy only.
- Coastguard Boating Education FAQ, fetched 2026-11-16: https://www.boatingeducation.org.nz/faq/
  No licence is legally required for a recreational vessel; Day Skipper is the minimum recommended
  training; except in an emergency, anyone operating a VHF or SSB marine radio must hold an operator's
  qualification; call signs and MMSI explained.
- Coastguard New Zealand, old Day Skipper page (archived copy of www.coastguard.nz, 3 October 2024),
  fetched 2026-11-16 via the Wayback Machine:
  https://web.archive.org/web/20241003221937/https://www.coastguard.nz/education/day-skipper/
  Units: The Boat (terminology, equipment), Navigation, Rules & Regulations, Emergencies, Knots; the
  LIFES sequence (Lifejackets, Investigate, Fix, Emit a distress signal, Stay with the boat). The live
  coastguard.nz pages return HTTP 403 to automated fetches.

## Law and rules
- Maritime New Zealand, Maritime Rules Part 91 Navigation Safety Rules, MNZ Consolidation
  1 November 2016 (copy of the MNZ PDF), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.karapirorowing.com/site_files/21887/upload_files/Part91-maritime-rule(1).pdf?dl=1
  91.4 PFDs (carriage, wearing when there is danger, towed persons), 91.5 under-15s and 10 knots,
  91.6 5 knot rule (50 m, 200 m of shore or structure, 200 m of Flag A, body over the bow or side),
  91.7 wake, 91.8 observer aged 10+, 91.9 no towing sunset to sunrise, 91.10 and 91.11 access lanes,
  91.12 reserved areas, 91.13 anchoring, 91.14 navigation aids, 91.15 Flag B 200 m, 91.16 under
  500 GT in harbour areas, 91.17 rivers, 91.18 Flag A, definitions (Flag A at least 600 mm by 600 mm).
  maritimenz.govt.nz HTML pages return 403; its /media/ PDFs load (below), but no newer Part 91
  consolidation PDF URL could be found, so only rules unlikely to have changed are asked.
- National lifejacket wearing rule (second review, 2026-11-15): the Maritime Transport (Lifejackets
  on Recreational Craft) Amendment Bill passed in September 2026 and took effect on 9 October 2026:
  everyone on a recreational craft 6 m or less must wear a properly fitted lifejacket while
  underway (not at anchor, made fast to the shore or aground), subject to Part 91's existing
  exceptions. Reported by Yachting NZ (3 Sep 2026, https://www.yachtingnz.org.nz/node/38106) and
  1News (9 Oct 2026, https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/10/09/what-you-need-to-know-as-new-rules-for-lifejackets-start-today/);
  the amended Part 91 text was not reachable (legislation.govt.nz and maritimenz.govt.nz return 403).
  NZBOAT-LAW-010 and LAW-011 were rewritten for it; the 2016 consolidation above predates it.
- Maritime New Zealand, Maritime Rules Part 22 Collision Prevention, MNZ Consolidation
  31 March 2021, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.maritimenz.govt.nz/media/vu5bafi0/part22-maritime-rule.pdf
  22.5 to 22.19 conduct (lookout, safe speed, risk of collision, action, narrow channels, sailing,
  overtaking, head-on, crossing, give-way and stand-on, responsibilities, restricted visibility),
  22.20 to 22.30 lights and shapes, 22.32 to 22.35 sound signals.
- Maritime New Zealand, Maritime Rules Part 23 Operating Procedures and Training, consolidation
  1 December 2025, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.maritimenz.govt.nz/media/jj2perei/part23-maritime-rule.pdf
  Appendix 3 distress signals (red flares, orange smoke, arms raised and lowered, continuous fog
  signal, Mayday, DSC on VHF channel 70, EPIRB).
- Maritime New Zealand, Safer Boating Forum newsletter, June 2026, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.maritimenz.govt.nz/media/kxmpk0ze/sbf-newsletter-june-2026.pdf

## Regional council navigation safety guidance
- Hawke's Bay Regional Council, "Boating in Hawke's Bay" (harbourmaster booklet, PDF), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.hbrc.govt.nz/assets/Document-Library/Harbourmaster/HBRC-BoatinginHB-Booklet.pdf
  Skipper duties, essential equipment (extinguisher sizes, fuel thirds, anchor 1.5 kg per metre,
  chain at least boat length and 200 x anchor weight, rope 5 x depth), 5 knot rule, give way
  summary, lifejacket types 401 to 406 and 71 N, causes of fatalities and kill cords, VHF use and
  the Mayday procedure, beacons, flares, phones, big ship blind spot (up to 1 km), IALA A lateral,
  cardinal, special and preferred channel marks, sector lights and leads (Napier inner harbour,
  variation about 22 degrees E), lights, jet skis, towing, paddle craft, dive Flag A, heavy weather
  handling, towing another boat (25 m rope, double fuel), knots, power loading, trip reports,
  MetService forecasts and the 25 knot advisory, chart symbols and datums. Hawke's Bay's lifejacket
  wearing bylaw (6 m or less) is asked only as a Hawke's Bay rule.
- Bay of Plenty Regional Council, "Buoys, marks and beacons and reserved areas", fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://boprc.govt.nz/living-in-the-bay/boating-in-the-bay/being-safe/buoys-marks-and-beacons-and-reserved-areas

  channel marks Fl (2+1); isolated danger, safe water and special mark purposes; cardinal marks;
  reserved areas and ski lanes; dive flag.
- Taranaki Regional Council / Port Taranaki, "Know before you go" (PDF), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.trc.govt.nz/assets/Documents/Environment/Coast/KnowB4YouGo-Nov2019.pdf

## Official confirmation of the lifejacket wearing rule (read 2026-11-16)
- Maritime NZ, "Lifejacket wearing rule amendment", https://maritimenz.govt.nz/recreational-craft/maritime-transport-lifejackets-on-recreational-craft-amendment-bill/ (last updated 8 October 2026; read in a browser because automated fetches get 403). "From 9 October 2026, everyone on board recreational craft of 6 metres in length and under will have to wear lifejackets while those craft are underway. The new rule will apply everywhere in New Zealand." Underway: not at anchor, attached to a dock, aground or attached to the shore. Supports NZBOAT-LAW-010 and NZBOAT-LAW-011.
