# NZ Drone (Part 101) bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the Civil Aviation Authority of New
Zealand's rule text, transport instrument and guidance listed below. None is copied, adapted or
reworded from any pilot qualification course quiz, approved training organisation material,
CAA sample question, commercial prep site or the US FAA Part 107 bank (data/part107-questions.json).

aviation.govt.nz HTML pages sit behind a bot wall that returned empty pages to automated reads;
the CAA's PDFs on the same host were read in full (text extracted from the PDF files).

## Rule text in force on 2026-11-15

- CAA, Civil Aviation Rules Part 101, Gyrogliders and Parasails, Unmanned Aircraft (including
  Balloons), Kites, and Rockets - Operating Rules, CAA Consolidation 22 December 2025, fetched
  2026-11-15:
  https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/rules/consolidations/Part_101_Consolidation.pdf
  First in force 5 April 2025 (Civil Aviation Act 2023); Amendment 1 (25/CAR/02) effective
  22 December 2025 revoked and replaced 101.7, 101.11, 101.15, 101.203 to 101.215 and inserted
  101.17 (transport instruments). Used: 101.1 (subject to 102.21), 101.3 definitions (RPA includes
  radio controlled model aircraft, excludes control line and free flight model aircraft; shielded
  operation within 100 m of and below the top of an object; aerodrome = promulgated in the AIPNZ),
  101.5 (Part 47 registration does not apply), 101.7 (restricted areas and MOAs need the
  administering authority's approval; danger areas need the operator to establish safety),
  101.9 (never in a low flying zone), 101.11 (controlled airspace needs prior ATC authorisation
  unless shielded), 101.12 (airspace knowledge before each flight, or direct supervision by a
  person who has it), 101.13 (all practicable steps to minimise hazards), 101.15 (no dropping
  of objects that creates a hazard), 101.17 (transport instruments are secondary legislation
  made by the Director), 101.201 to 101.215, 101.202 (approved person or organisation and its
  six functions).
- CAA, Civil Aviation Transport Instrument CATI 101-1, Civil Aviation (Operation of Unmanned
  Aircraft, Control Line Model Aircraft and Free Flight Model Aircraft) Transport Instrument,
  initial issue, in force 22 December 2025, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/rules/transport-instruments/CATI-101-1.pdf
  2.1 control lines no longer than 30 m; 2.3 aerodromes (4 km of the boundary; pilot
  qualification from an approved person or organisation, or supervision, or a Part 61 or 149
  licence; ATC authorisation at a controlled aerodrome; agreement with the operator, an observer
  and 400 ft at an uncontrolled aerodrome; shielded exception outside the boundary, at most
  33 ft above the shield and behind a barrier able to arrest the flight; free flight launched
  downwind of the active runway); 2.5 airspace (consent of persons below; consent of the
  property owner or occupier; 400 ft AGL; observe the surrounding airspace; above 400 ft only in a
  danger area designated for it, or more than 4 km from an aerodrome, in Class G and notified for a

  sight (corrective lenses only; FPV only with a trained observer in direct communication;

  days without night flying, orientation lighting and one aircraft at a time); 2.13 give way to and
  remain clear of all manned aircraft on the ground and in flight; 2.15 25 kg limit and the 15 to
  25 kg construction, inspection and operation approvals.

## Guidance (acceptable means of compliance and CAA safety publications)

- CAA, Advisory Circular AC101-1 Revision 2, Remotely Piloted Aircraft Systems (RPAS): Operating
  in compliance with Part 101 Rules, 22 December 2025, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/rules/advisory-circulars/AC101-1a-Remotely-Piloted-Aircraft-Systems.pdf
  Part 101 covers recreational and commercial use; no pilot qualification is needed unless an
  unshielded operation is within 4 km of an aerodrome boundary; consent over people may be verbal
  or written; consent over property from owner or occupier, informal or contractual, and one
  occupant may consent for others; public land through the council or DOC; council bylaws are not
  overruled; land or move away at once if a manned aircraft approaches; Airshare is the preferred
  way to apply for controlled airspace, and approval covers the airspace only; as much notice as
  possible to aerodrome operators; night lighting (glow sticks fine, strobes unsuitable, lights are
  not for collision avoidance); shielded examples (trees, buildings, pylons); airworthiness (no
  standards in Part 101: fit for purpose, manufacturer's manual, pre-flight checks, battery
  programmes, rotor guards); LiPo batteries are not dangerous goods while powering the aircraft but
  may be when carried as cargo; Appendix 1 hazard list (crowds even with consent, stock and wildlife
  including marine mammals, roads, weather and visibility, unreliable 2.4 GHz areas, pilot fitness,
  return-to-home not set).
- CAA, Flying your drone safely brochure, revised June 2026, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.aviation.govt.nz/assets/publications/brochures/caa-flying-your-drone-safely-web.pdf
  Preflight checks, 120 m (400 ft), keep clear of fog and cloud, consent rules including private
  lakes, boats and vehicles, council or DOC for parks, reserves, beaches and national parks;
  breaches can lead to prosecution; qualification is part of flying within 4 km of an aerodrome.
- CAA, GAP New Zealand Airspace booklet (2020), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://aviation.govt.nz/assets/publications/gaps/gap-new-zealand-airspace-web.pdf
  Airspace classes used in NZ (A, C, D, G); CTRs are the only controlled airspace touching the
  surface; Class C and D need an entry clearance; special use airspace types and chart designators
  (R, M, D, V, B, L); restricted areas often protect wildlife, temporary ones on Police request;
  MOAs may be H24; danger areas have a using agency and no administering authority; VHZ day VMC
  only; MBZ at busy uncontrolled aerodromes and tourist airspace; LFZ surface to 500 ft for flight
  training; military low flying down to 250 ft AGL; AIP, NOTAMs, VNCs. Drone section: stay 4 km from
  helipads including hospital and sightseeing helipads; controlled airspace extends beyond 4 km.
- CAA, How to report occurrences (GAP), fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://avsec.govt.nz/assets/publications/gaps/how-to-report-occurrences.pdf
  Notify an accident or serious incident as soon as practicable on 0508 ACCIDENT; report online at
  aviation.govt.nz/report; recreational Part 101 operators need not report occurrences involving only
  their own aircraft, but may report a significant one (control failure, battery fire) as an
  aviation-related concern; Part 102 operators report as their exposition says; err on the side of
  reporting; the CAA never turns away a report.
- Airshare (run for the CAA by Airways New Zealand), drone rules page, fetched 2026-11-15:
  https://www.airshare.co.nz/rules
  Summary of the Part 101 rules; ATC authorisation in controlled airspace through the AirShare app;
  stay 4 km from anywhere aircraft land or take off; FPV needs an observer; see aircraft with your
  own eyes, not through binoculars, a monitor or a headset.

## Format

The CAA publishes no exam for Part 101. A pilot qualification is issued by an approved person or
organisation under rule 101.202, each setting its own course and assessment; no question count,
time or pass mark is published. The 80% line here is Tigertest's readiness target and the copy
says so.

## Pending changes (not asked)

- A February 2026 government regulatory briefing (MFR2026-026, regulation.govt.nz, a scanned image
  PDF that could not be read as text) proposes changes to Parts 101 and 102 for agricultural drone
  spraying. Because rule 101.15(c) (no aerial spraying, topdressing or vertebrate toxic agents under
  Part 101) is under reform, the bank does not ask about it.
- A draft further revision of AC101-1 was out for consultation in 2026; the current Revision 2 text
  is used.

## Conflicts and how the bank handles them

- Part 101's definition of a shielded operation says "below the top of" the object, while CATI 101-1
  (2.3(b), 2.7) and AC101-1 allow up to 33 feet (10 m) above the shield for the aerodrome and
  controlled airspace exceptions. The bank asks the 33 ft figure only in those two contexts and
  uses "within 100 m of the object" for the definition.
- Airshare's rules page still says unshielded night flying needs Part 102; CATI 101-1 2.11 now
  allows it under Part 101 with approved night training. The bank follows CATI 101-1.

## Deliberately left out

Fines and infringement fees, Part 102 costs, course prices, named training providers, privacy law
beyond the consent rules Part 101 states (the Privacy Act is not part of Part 101), aerial spraying
(pending reform), weather numbers not in CAA text (wind limits are the manufacturer's).
