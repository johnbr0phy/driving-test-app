# nzensf sources (checked 2026-11-15)

All questions in this bank are original. None is taken, reworded or adapted from the Council's
sample questions, past papers, prep sites or any question pool. The Council's 2025 sample paper
was opened only to confirm the format (part names, four options); its questions were not used.

## Exam owner: Nursing Council of New Zealand (Te Kaunihera Tapuhi o Aotearoa)

### Format
- Handbook for pre-registration nursing programmes, May 2022, Section 7 State Final Examination,
  https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/Public/Education/Exam%20and%20Candidate%20info/Handbook%20for%20pre-registration%20nursing%20programmes%20May22.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15):
  - 7.1.3 "The enrolled nurse examination consists of 90 multiple-choice questions."
  - 7.1.4 questions are categorised under the domains of competence and "a section on medication
    calculations" (EN domains then: professional responsibility, provision of nursing care,
    interpersonal relationships, interprofessional health care, medication calculations).
  - 7.1.5 "a candidate must achieve an overall pass of 70% across the whole Exam."
  - 7.2.1 held on or about the third Tuesday of March, July and November.
  - 7.2.2 "Candidates for the enrolled nurse examination are provided 90 minutes."
  - 7.4.1 results are issued pass or fail; 7.5.2 sample questions are on the Council's website.
- State Final Examination Frequently Asked Questions (PDF, dated October 2024),
  https://nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/State%20Final%20Examination%20Frequently%20Asked%20Questions%20(FAQ).pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15): "There is no required pass rate per category of the examination. The
  examination is graded on an overall percentage." "Candidates have three attempts to pass the
  State Final Examination." Up to 25 minutes extra time in exceptional circumstances.
- Sample questions for the enrolled nurse state final examination 2025 (PDF),
  https://nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/Public/Education/State%20Final%20Examinations/EN%20sample%20exam%20questions%202025%20v2.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15, format only): two parts, "Part A: Medicine Calculations" and "Part B:
  Nursing Knowledge"; "The multiple-choice questions have four options. Choose one answer";
  "Answer all questions as if you were working in the capacity of an enrolled nurse."
- Internationally qualified nurses' theoretical examination handbook, version 1.4, 1 September
  2025, https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/IQN%20Theoretical%20Examination%20Handbook.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15): the IQN enrolled nurse examination is 90 multiple-choice questions, Part A
  Medication safety 9 questions in 25 minutes, Part B Nursing knowledge 81 questions in 110
  minutes; Part A covers "medication and fluid safety questions including medicine
  calculations"; Part B questions "are closely linked to the Council's registered nurse or
  enrolled nurse standards of competence"; a calculator is provided in the software. This is the
  IQN exam, not the State Final; it is used only for the 9 and 81 split, labelled as such.
- Pearson VUE, Nursing Council New Zealand page, https://www.pearsonvue.com/us/en/ncnz.html
  (fetched 2026-11-15): both examinations have Part A Medication Safety and Part B Nursing
  Knowledge (IQN delivery).

### Scope and standards (what Part B is written from)
- Enrolled nurse scope of practice, January 2025 (PDF),
  https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/Public/Nursing/Enrolled%20nurse/Scope-practice-EN-2025.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15): ENs "partner with people receiving health and/or disability support
  services to initiate and monitor care through nursing assessments, care planning,
  implementation and evaluation of care", "must work with access to, and seek when appropriate,
  guidance from a registered nurse or other registered health practitioner", "may include a
  leadership or coordination role".
- Standards of competence for enrolled nurses, February 2025 (PDF NCNZ031),
  https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/Public/Nursing/Enrolled%20nurse/NCNZ031-Competencies-EN-11.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15): five pou: Māori health (1.1 to 1.4); cultural safety (2.1 to 2.5,
  "culturally safe care which is determined by the recipient"); whanaungatanga, partnership and
  communication (3.1 to 3.5, seek guidance, direction and coordination, escalate); pūkengatanga
  and knowledge-informed practice (4.1 to 4.9: assessment, deterioration, medicines, IPC,
  sustainability); mana hautū, professional accountability and responsibility (5.1 to 5.7:
  Code of Conduct, documentation, risk, continuing competence). Glossary: kawa whakaruruhau,
  whakapapa-centred care, social determinants.
- FAQs enrolled nurse scope of practice 2025 (PDF),
  https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/Public/Nursing/Enrolled%20Nurse/FAQs%20enrolled%20nurse%20scope%202025.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15): the scope took effect 20 January 2025; RNs "provide support and guidance
  to enrolled nurses"; ENs must recognise a change in condition and escalate; "The Nursing
  Council does not give advice on whether individual tasks are within a nurse's scope"; whether
  an EN gives IV antibiotics depends on the organisation's policies.
- FAQs changes to direction and delegation 2025 (PDF),
  https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/Public/Nursing/FAQs%20changes%20to%20direction%20and%20delegation%202025.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15): "Enrolled nurses can provide direction and coordination, as appropriate,
  to other health workers"; "Registered nurses are responsible and accountable for directing and
  delegating to members of the healthcare team"; pou were formerly called domains.
- Code of Conduct for nurses (June 2012 booklet),
  https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/common/Uploaded%20files/Public/The%20Code%20of%20Conduct/Code%20of%20Conduct%20Booklet%20full.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-15): eight principles (dignity, cultural needs, partnership, safe and

  such as 4.4 work within your limits, 4.8 clear and accurate records, 6.9 intervene to stop
  unsafe practice, 7.7 no loans or bequests, 7.13 professional boundaries.
- Code of Conduct page, https://www.nursingcouncil.org.nz/Public/Nursing/Code_of_Conduct/NCNZ/nursing-section/Code_of_Conduct.aspx

## Clinical content
Clinical items (calculations, assessment, deterioration, acute and long-term care, mental
health, child and maternal health) are written from standard nursing knowledge as taught for
the Diploma in Enrolled Nursing and checked against the scope above: the EN assesses, plans,
gives care, monitors, recognises change and escalates to the RN or other health practitioner,
and works within organisational policy. Units are New Zealand's: mmol/L, micrograms written in
full, mL, kg, °C. Rounding is stated in every calculation stem.

## Deliberately left out
- Any NZEWS score values or thresholds (the HQSC vital sign chart page could not be loaded:
  https://www.hqsc.govt.nz/our-work/hospital-care/deteriorating-patient/new-zealand-early-warning-score-vital-sign-chart/
  returned 404 on 2026-11-15), so deterioration items use plain vital sign trends and escalation.
- Fees, APC costs, immunisation schedule dates, Pharmac funding rules and anything that changes
  often; named officeholders.
- Questions about the examination itself.

## What is Tigertest's, not the Council's
- The domestic State Final split between Part A and Part B is not published; the 9 and 81 used
  here is the IQN enrolled nurse examination's, and the 15 plus 75 minute timing is Tigertest's
  split of the handbook's 90 minutes.
- Part A here asks for a typed number (the real Part A is four-option multiple choice), so a
  learner has to work each answer out rather than recognise it.
- The Part B topic split is Tigertest's, built around the five pou; the Council publishes no
  blueprint for the 2025 standards.
- The handbook is dated May 2022 (it predates the 2025 scope); its 90 questions, 90 minutes and
  70% overall are used because no newer domestic description could be found on the Council's
  public pages, and the 2024 FAQ agrees the exam is graded on an overall percentage.
