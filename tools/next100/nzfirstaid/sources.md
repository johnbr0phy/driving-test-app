# Sources for `nzfirstaid` (New Zealand workplace first aid: NZQA skill standards 41052 and 41053, formerly unit standards 6401 and 6402)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the NZQA skill standards, the ANZCOR
guidelines they require, and the New Zealand resources the standards list (Hato Hone St John, Stroke
Foundation NZ, Heart Foundation NZ, the National Poisons Centre). NZQA and the standard-setting body
publish no question catalogue, sample test or past paper for these standards: assessment is a
practical, simulated incident run by each accredited provider. So there was no official catalogue to
screen against. No training provider's quiz, workbook question or app item was read or reworded, and
no provider is named in any question. Checked 2026-11-23.

## Standards (official)
- NZQA, Skill standard 41052 "Administer first aid", Level 2, 1 credit, version 1,
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/41052.pdf (fetched 2026-11-23). Registered
  27 November 2025, replaces unit standard 6401, planned review 31 December 2030. Outcome: assess a
  person's condition, manage injury and medical condition. Assessment must comply with the ANZCOR
  guidelines, uses provider-developed simulated incidents and must include at least one injury
  (burns, fractures, dislocations, soft tissue injuries, poisoning) and one medical condition (heart
  attack, asthma exacerbation, stroke, seizure, diabetic emergency, severe allergic reaction).
  Graded Achieved only. Prerequisite 41053. Resources: ANZCOR, Asthma NZ, Epilepsy NZ, Hato Hone
  St John, Heart Foundation, Stroke Foundation NZ.
- NZQA, Skill standard 41053 "Administer basic life support", Level 2, 1 credit, version 1,
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/41053.pdf (fetched 2026-11-23). Replaces unit standard
  6402. Outcomes: collapsed person unresponsive and not breathing normally (DRSABCD, CPR, AED,
  adult or child and infant), foreign body airway obstruction (adult or child, and infant), severe
  and life-threatening bleeding (must consider a tourniquet), shock (recovery position).
  Definitions: adult 8 years or over, child 1 to 8, infant under 1 (these are the standard's
  assessment definitions; ANZCOR's own airway guideline defines a child as 1 year to puberty, so no
  question turns on the 8-year line except AED pads, where ANZCOR uses it too).
- NZQA, Skill standard 41051 "Manage first aid during emergency situations", Level 3,
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/41051.pdf (fetched 2026-11-23). Replaces 6400; lists
  situational hazards (electrocution, water, fire, chemicals, bodily fluids, weather, road crashes,
  gas) and major injuries (crush, spinal, head, burns, internal bleeding). Used for the scene and
  crush/spinal items; the bank is built on 41052 and 41053.
- NZQA, Review summary, Field Health, November 2025,
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/summaries/2025/nov25/revsumnov25-16.html (fetched 2026-11-23):
  Toitū te Waiora developed skill standards 41051, 41052 and 41053 to replace unit standards 6400,
  6401 and 6402 (review category C); the old unit standards expire on 31 December 2027; providers'
  consent to assess carries over.
- NZQA, Change report, Republication of First Aid Skill Standards, April 2026,
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/summaries/2026/apr26/revsumapr26-21.html (fetched 2026-11-23):
  "First Aid as a Life Skill" removed from the assessment specifications; the ANZCOR guidelines
  (published in NZ by the New Zealand Resuscitation Council) are the reference.

## Where sources disagree and what the bank does
- Asthma: ANZCOR's New Zealand plan is 6 puffs, one at a time with 6 breaths each, wait 6 minutes,
  repeat; Hato Hone St John repeats one puff and 6 breaths with no wait. The bank asks only what both
  share (one puff at a time, 6 breaths, spacer, sit upright, call 111), never the wait time. The
  Asthma Foundation NZ site now redirects to cardiacandrespiratory.org.nz with no first aid page.
- Raising a bleeding limb: ANZCOR finds no evidence and notes possible harm; St John says raise it.
  The standards require ANZCOR, so the bank keys ANZCOR and says so.
- Seizure: ANZCOR says ambulance if not awake within 5 minutes; St John says 10. The bank asks only
  the seizure length of over 5 minutes, where both agree.
- Shock: the current ANZCOR guideline (December 2025) says lie the person down and does not mention
  raising the legs, so no item keys a leg raise for shock.

## Could not be read, and left out
- WorkSafe New Zealand's first aid guidance (worksafe.govt.nz) and the Health and Safety at Work
  (General Risk and Workplace Management) Regulations 2016 on legislation.govt.nz both blocked
  automated reads on 2026-11-23. So the bank has no questions on a PCBU's legal first aid duties,
  first aider numbers or kit contents; the landing describes the workplace link in general terms only.
- No fees, course prices, refresher intervals or certificate validity (set by providers and
  employers, not by NZQA).
- Format: there is no national exam, question count or pass mark. Each provider assesses the
  standards with simulated incidents, graded Achieved. Tigertest's 80% is a readiness line.
