# Sources for `nzforklift` (New Zealand forklift operator certificate theory: NZQA skill standards 40735, 40736 and 40737, formerly unit standards 10851 and 10852)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the NZQA skill standards, New Zealand
law (Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, the General Risk and Workplace Management Regulations
2016), NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi's F endorsement rules, and published lift-truck safety codes
for the operating practice the standards ask about. NZQA and the standard-setting body publish no
question catalogue, sample test or past paper for these standards: each registered forklift
trainer or accredited provider assesses the knowledge and the practical skills itself. So there was
no official catalogue to screen against. No training provider's quiz, workbook question or app
item was read or reworded, and no provider is named in any question. Checked 2026-11-23.

## Standards (official, the assessment outline)
- NZQA, Skill standard 40735 "Operate a forklift safely", Level 3, 4 credits, version 1,
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/40735.pdf (fetched 2026-11-23). Registered
  24 July 2025, replaced unit standard 10851, planned review 31 December 2030. Outcomes: describe
  safe operating features (standards, rules and codes; forklift physics and operating parameters;
  fault reporting; consumables), prepare to start (pre-operation inspection, environment check,
  start-up), operate safely (forward and reverse, stack and de-stack, manoeuvre round obstacles,
  park, shut down). Defines operating parameters (nominal and rated capacity, load centre, load
  charts, centre of gravity, stability triangle and pyramid, mast tilt, side shift, fork
  positioners, speed, rear end swing, axle loading, derating, exclusion zones), consumables, load
  plate and rated capacity. Indicative content: pre-operation inspection (consumables, leaks,
  damage, operational safety), start-up (seatbelts, three points of contact, driving position),
  environment check (at, above and below ground hazards, visibility, weather, operator fit to
  operate), park and shut down, load calculation. Learners 15 or older on enrolment. Says the
  standard does not entitle the holder to operate on a public road: a full licence of the right
  class with the F endorsement is needed.
- NZQA, Skill standard 40736 "Operate a forklift fitted with an attachment", Level 3, 3 credits,
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/40736.pdf (fetched 2026-11-23). Replaced unit
  standard 10852; prerequisite 40735; side shift and working platforms are not accepted as
  attachments for its assessment; permanently fixed, removable and hydraulically operated
  attachments; attachments usually have their own capacity plate; the PCBU must train operators
  on specialised attachments used at their workplace.
- NZQA, Skill standard 40737 "Operate a forklift and perform a task with a working platform",
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/40737.pdf (fetched 2026-11-23). Prerequisites 40735
  and unit standard 23229 (safety harness); trial lift, mast vertical and forks centred, tilt
  controls locked out where practical, harness with full restraint lanyard on the platform's anchor
  point, communication throughout, platform safe working load against the truck's rated capacity,
  rescue procedures, post-operation check and fault reporting.
- NZQA, Expiring unit standards 10851 and 10852 version 5,
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/units/pdf/10851.pdf and
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/units/pdf/10852.pdf (fetched 2026-11-23): replaced by 40735 and
  40736; last date for assessment 31 December 2027; a registered forklift trainer issues a
  certificate as proof of training; refresher training every three years is recommended, with
  details in WorkSafe's Approved Code of Practice for Training Operators and Instructors of Powered
  Industrial Lift Trucks (Forklifts) (1995).

## Law (official)
- Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, version as at 5 April 2025,
  https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2015/0070/latest/DLM5976660.html (read in a browser
  2026-11-23; automated fetches get a 403): s 22 reasonably practicable, s 24 notifiable incident
  (fall or release from a height of any plant, substance or thing), s 34 PCBUs with the same duty
  consult, s 36 primary duty of care (including workers whose work the PCBU directs), s 44 officers'
  due diligence, s 45 worker duties, s 46 other persons at a workplace, s 55 preserve sites, s 56
  notify by the fastest possible means, s 57 records at least 5 years, s 58 engage with workers,
  s 83 right to cease or refuse unsafe work.
- Health and Safety at Work (General Risk and Workplace Management) Regulations 2016,
  https://www.legislation.govt.nz/regulation/public/2016/0013/latest/DLM6727530.html (read in a
  browser 2026-11-23): reg 6 hierarchy of control measures, reg 9 information, supervision,
  training and instruction (readily understandable), reg 18 worker's PPE duties, reg 24 working
  under raised objects, reg 25 falling objects and exclusion zones, reg 46 and reg 47 young persons
  (no worker under 15 drives self-propelled mobile plant over 700 kg; the 12-year exception covers
  only trained workers on agricultural tractors).
- NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi, Applying for a forklift (F) endorsement,
  https://www.nzta.govt.nz/driver-licences/getting-an-endorsement/getting-an-endorsement-dfrtw/applying-for-a-forklift-f-endorsement
  (read in a browser 2026-11-23): F endorsement needed to drive a forklift on a road; class 1 up
  to 18,000 kg loaded, class 2 over; the broad definition of road (wharves, beaches, riverbeds,
  road shoulders, a factory yard with no gate); approved course certificate alone is not enough;
  apply at a driver licensing agent with a full licence or two forms of ID, the certificate and an
  eyesight check; the endorsement expires with the driver licence.

## Operating practice (published codes the standards' "accepted industry guidance" covers)
- HSE (Great Britain), L117 Rider-operated lift trucks: Operator training and safe use, Approved
  Code of Practice and guidance, third edition 2013, https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/l117.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-23): actual capacity and the capacity plate, attachments and derating,
  counterweight and battery as counterweight, restraints, traffic routes, road humps, gradients,
  marking, lighting, key control and parking, LPG and refuelling, explosive atmospheres, trailers
  and bridge plates, curtain-siders, battery charging and changing, pre-shift checks (fork
  positioning locks), integrated and non-integrated platforms. Used for practice only, never for
  New Zealand law.
- OSHA (United States), 29 CFR 1910.178 Powered industrial trucks and Appendix A Stability,
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.178 and
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.178AppA (fetched
  2026-11-23): stability triangle, line of action, moments, load centre and the capacity ratio,
  lateral stability of an unladen truck, travel on grades with the load upgrade, travelling with
  the load trailing when it blocks the view, crossing rails diagonally, no riders, arms and legs
  inside the running lines, nobody under raised forks, parking with wheels blocked on an incline,
  no horseplay. Used for physics and practice only.
- HSE, GS6 Avoiding danger from overhead power lines, https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/gs6.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-23): assume a line is live; stay in a vehicle that has touched a wire.

## What could not be read, and how the bank handles it
- WorkSafe New Zealand's forklift pages and its Approved Code of Practice for Training Operators
  and Instructors of Powered Industrial Lift Trucks (Forklifts) sit behind an Imperva CAPTCHA
  (worksafe.govt.nz) or did not resolve (registers.worksafe.govt.nz) on 2026-11-23. Nothing was
  done to get past the check. So no question asks an ACOP-only rule (trainer registration,
  certificate wording, instructor requirements); the three-year refresher appears only as NZQA
  states it, a recommendation, and is not asked. NZECP 34 electrical safe distances (also on
  WorkSafe's site) are not asked.
- No national exam, question count or pass mark exists: providers assess the standards and grade
  Achieved. The 80% line on the landing is Tigertest's readiness target.

## Deliberately left out
Fees (the F endorsement fee), fines and penalty amounts, imperial figures from the US standard,
the US 25-foot "unattended" rule, gradient percentages, the ACOP's details, and anything about the
assessment itself.

## Repeats with sibling banks
Stems and keys were compared with `forklift`, `auforklift`, `nzheavy`, `nzfirstaid`, `auwhite`,
`auwah`, `osha`, `osha30`, `cscs` and `iesafepass` (word overlap and key similarity). Fifteen items
asking the same fact with the same answer (primary duty, five-year records, load centre
definition, 600 mm load centre, travelling in reverse with a blocked view, ramps, pedestrians,
LPG cylinder changes, battery PPE, cracked forks, power lines and others) were rewritten from a
different angle or replaced.
