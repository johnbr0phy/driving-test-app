# NZ security guard Certificate of Approval sources (checked 2026-11-16)

All 200 questions are original. None is copied or reworded from NZQA assessment material,
provider workbooks, sample questions or prep sites.

## The training a security guard needs (owner: PSPLA, Ministry of Justice; standards: NZQA)
- PSPLA, "Training & skills", fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.justice.govt.nz/tribunals/licences-certificates/pspla/training-and-skills/
  Crowd controllers, property guards and personal guards must complete the minimum training before
  applying for a certificate of approval (COA): unit standards 27364, 27360 and 27361, or skill
  standards 40146, 40145 and 40147 (12 credits together). The NZQF New Zealand Certificate in
  Security (Foundation) (Level 3) or (Advanced) (Level 4) is treated as meeting them. A temporary
  COA lets you work while you train: about 10 days to issue, lasts 3 months, apply again if training
  is not finished. Training is done once and is not repeated at renewal. Remote monitoring (the
  monitoring officer class) is outside the minimum training. Overseas training can be assessed for
  equivalence. On completion you upload your NZQA record of achievement and get a new badge.
- NZQA skill standard 40146 v1 (registered 29 August 2024, review 31 December 2029), "Describe
  foundation knowledge of the security industry", Level 3, 4 credits, replaced unit standard 27364,
  fetched 2026-11-16: https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/40146.pdf
  Outcomes: responsibilities under the Private Security Personnel and Private Investigators Act 2010
  (COA vs licence, tasks of crowd controller, property guard and personal guard, production and
  visible wearing of the COA, updating details with the PSPLA); legislation on controlling entry,
  excluding or removing people, lawful force, lawful search, proof of age on licensed premises,
  arrest or detention, privacy (Crimes Act ss 34, 41, 42, 48, 52, 56, 62; Summary Offences Act
  ss 3 and 39(3); Sale and Supply of Alcohol Act 2012; Trespass Act 1980 ss 3 and 4, "occupier";
  Oranga Tamariki Act s 5(1)(b)(i); Privacy Act 2020); health and safety (HSWA ss 30, 44, 45; GRWM
  Regulations reg 21 remote or isolated work; hazards: isolation, environment, human factors,
  slips, trips, falls, faulty equipment, traffic, hazardous substances, bodily fluids); first line
  response to emergencies and security incidents (111 or 105, handover information, evacuation
  routes and assembly points, security breach, suspicious behaviour, site lockdown, alarm
  activation); reporting (when, where, what, who/how; 24-hour clock; phonetic alphabet; verbal,
  written or digital reports); customer service and interpersonal communication. Graded Achieved.
  Learning and assessment "weighted towards gaining theoretical knowledge".
- NZQA skill standard 40145 v1 (29 August 2024), "Explain conflict management in a security
  context", Level 3, 4 credits, replaced 27360, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/40145.pdf
  Conflict cycle (escalation to de-escalation; baseline, trigger, passive resistance, assertive and
  aggressive behaviour); triggers; situational, environmental (time of day, weather, persons present)
  and human factors (challenging behaviour, mental illness, alcohol or drug use); situational
  awareness; distance, space and body positioning; equipment and backup; limiting factors (physical,
  mental health, site instructions, company policy); TENR (threat, exposure, necessity, response),
  OODA (observe, orient, decide, act), AWOCA (ask, why, options, confirm, act); tactical withdrawal;
  lawful force reasonable, necessary, proportionate; push/pull to make distance; interview stance to
  ready stance; stacking and splitting; teamwork; post-incident reporting, duty of care, support
  systems (backup staff, equipment, phone, radio, hand signals, Employee Assistance Programme,
  self-care); definition of assault. Assessed with realistic scenarios; graded Achieved.
- NZQA skill standard 40147 v1 (29 August 2024), "Manage and resolve conflict situations in a
  security context", replaced 27361, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/skills/pdf/40147.pdf
  Practical: four role-play scenarios (compliance situation; escalated angry customer; escalated
  then lawful force; escalated then tactical withdrawal); communication techniques (active
  listening, reflecting, rephrasing, summarising, questioning, options and consequences); summon
  support; post-incident reporting. Assessed off-job by observed role play; modelled only as
  knowledge.
- Unit standards 27364, 27360, 27361 (expiring, replaced by the skill standards above), fetched
  2026-11-16: https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/units/pdf/27364.pdf (used only to confirm the
  replacement).

## Assessment format (landing, FAQ and examFacts only; never in the bank)
- Every standard is graded Achieved by the accredited provider that delivers it. The NZQA
  documents set the outcomes and say 40146 and 40145 are knowledge-weighted and 40145 and 40147 are
  assessed with scenarios; 40147 is a practical role play. No national question count, time limit
  or pass mark is published by NZQA or the PSPLA. Tigertest's 50-question mock tests and 80%
  readiness line are Tigertest's own target and are labelled so (the nzlcq pattern).

## The law and practice behind the questions
- PSPLA, "Licence or certificate?", fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.justice.govt.nz/tribunals/licences-certificates/pspla/who-needs-a-licence-or-certificate/licence-or-certificate/
  Company licence, individual licence for a sole trader, COA for employees and contractors; who
  needs which; bar staff need a COA only if they screen entry, keep order or remove people; staff
  who only make drinks or clear tables do not. (Fine amounts deliberately not asked.)
- PSPLA, "Work you need a licence or certificate for", fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.justice.govt.nz/tribunals/licences-certificates/pspla/apply-or-renew/who-needs-a-licence-or-certificate/work-you-need-a-licence-or-certificate-for/
  The nine classes of work and what each covers (crowd controller, property guard, personal guard,
  security technician, security consultant, document destruction agent, repossession agent, private
  investigator, monitoring officer).
- PSPLA, "Replacements, updates and duties" and "Renew", fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.justice.govt.nz/tribunals/licences-certificates/pspla/replacements-updates-and-duties/
  https://www.justice.govt.nz/tribunals/licences-certificates/pspla/apply-or-renew/renew/
  Replace a lost badge; tell the Authority of a change of name or home address or loss of
  eligibility; add or remove classes of work; annual returns for licence holders; renewal only while
  active; current badge stays valid while a renewal is processed; giving up a COA.
- PSPLA decision [2023] NZPSPLA 041 (6 July 2023), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.justice.govt.nz/assets/Documents/Decisions/2023-NZPSPLA-041.pdf
  Quotes ss 66, 67 and 68 of the Act: certificate holders other than private investigators wear the
  Authority's ID in a readily visible position; the ID shows a unique identifier, photo, classes of
  work and expiry date (no name); armband, lanyard or retractable cord, never under a jacket or in a
  pocket; front displayed, nothing taped over; s 67(4) exemption only for a real or significant
  threat; s 66 produce the certificate on demand with no safety exemption; intentional breach is an
  offence and may be misconduct.
- NZ Police, "Citizen's Arrest" (Retail crime prevention hub, 2026), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.police.govt.nz/advice-services/businesses-and-organisations/retail-crime-prevention-hub/citizens-arrest
  Crimes Act ss 35 to 38 as amended by the Crimes Amendment Act 2026; only necessary,
  proportionate, minimum force; the safest course is usually to call 111 and observe; if you arrest,
  call 111 as soon as practicable, follow Police directions, do not question or search, check the
  person's welfare, never kneel or sit on them or press on the neck or head.
- Crimes Amendment Bill, Legislative statement J.17, first reading (December 2025), fetched
  2026-11-16: https://bills.parliament.nz/download/Paper/a1028ba9-ea2a-4370-a995-08de3851dbc0
  The bill amends ss 35 to 40, 52, 53 and 56 so citizen's arrest is justified at any time for any
  Crimes Act offence if the person contacts Police and follows directions, and so reasonable force
  may be used in making an arrest and defending property.
- NZ Police, "How to give someone a trespass notice", fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://police.govt.nz/node/27791
  Written or verbal notice; record date, time, reason and description for a verbal one; tell Police;
  the person must not return for two years; an invitation back from someone who lives or works
  there voids the notice.
- NZ Police Manual chapter "Trespass" (version 13.0, OIA release hosted by the independent
  policepolicy.nz; the text is the Police document), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://policepolicy.nz/policies/trespass/u-wzawg/drew%20l%20trespass%20chapter%2016%205605.pdf
  Trespass Act ss 3, 4, 5: occupier includes an employee or person acting under the occupier's
  authority; implied licence to enter shops; warning to leave versus warning to stay off; merely
  telling someone to leave does not stop a return; ticket holders have a licence coupled with a
  contract; service by handing over or putting the notice down in the person's presence; allow
  reasonable time to leave; HANZ notices up to 24 months; blanket notices often invalid.
- NZ Police, "Police emergency: Call 111" / 105, fetched 2026-11-16: https://Police.govt.nz/node/29388
  111 when someone is hurt or at risk, life or property is seriously threatened, or a crime is
  happening or the offenders have just left; 105 for things that have already happened and do not
  need urgent help, also online at 105.police.govt.nz.
- New Zealand Security Association, "New Zealand Security Services Good Practice Guidelines"
  (October 2026; developed with E tu, NZCTU and WorkSafe; named as a resource in all three skill
  standards), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://security.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NZSA-NZ-Security-Services-Good-Practice-Guidelines_Oct-2026-1.pdf
  Security staff have no Police powers; detect, deter, observe, report; not expected to risk injury;
  control order (eliminate, minimise by substitution, isolation and engineering, administrative,
  PPE); duress devices monitored 24/7; safe haven; site induction by risk level; escalation plans;
  offender on site: escape, hide, tell, observe, assist; protecting a scene; log books; green and
  blue COA; stewards versus crowd controllers; consent to search, refusal of entry; bag search,
  wanding and same-sex pat-down steps; public filming cannot be stopped and phones must not be
  seized. Its citizen's arrest paragraph predates the 2026 amendment and is not used.
- WorkSafe, "Introduction to the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015" (2nd edition), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://worksafe.govt.nz/dmsdocument/824-introduction-to-the-health-and-safety-at-work-act-2015
  PCBU primary duty; who is a worker; worker duties (s 45); eliminate then minimise risk (s 30);
  hazards include behaviour from fatigue, drugs or alcohol; notifiable events; right to cease or
  refuse unsafe work (s 83).
- Office of the Privacy Commissioner, "Privacy and CCTV", fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/privacy-and-cctv/
  Collect only what is necessary; clear signage naming who operates the system; audio is more
  intrusive and must be disclosed; principle 6 access to footage of yourself.
- Ministry of Justice, sale and supply of alcohol key points for the public, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.justice.govt.nz/justice-sector-policy/key-initiatives/sale-and-supply-of-alcohol/key-points-for-the-public/
  Intoxicated means affected by alcohol, drugs or other substances with two or more of appearance,
  behaviour, co-ordination and speech affected; intoxicated people are not served or allowed to
  stay; fake ID offences.
- Kiwi Access card FAQ for premises (the card issuer), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://kiwiaccess.co.nz/frequently-asked-questions-for-accepting-a-kiwi-access-card/
  Approved evidence of age: passport, current NZ driver licence, Kiwi Access card; security
  features (UV koru, embossed number and date of birth, transparent corner, Braille "ID").

## Could not be read
- legislation.govt.nz (the Act, the Minimum Training Regulations 2013, the Crimes Act, Trespass
  Act, Summary Offences Act, Sale and Supply of Alcohol Regulations, Oranga Tamariki Act) returned
  403 or an empty bot challenge to every fetch. Statute rules are asked only as the official pages
  above state them; section numbers are named only where the NZQA standard or a PSPLA decision
  names them. NZLII also returned 403.
- The Sale and Supply of Alcohol Amendment Regulations 2026 add digital identity credentials as
  approved evidence of age: no question says the list is complete.

## Left out on purpose
- Fines, fees, licence terms and PSPLA processing details beyond the official pages; the old
  "night time or three-year offence" citizen's arrest rule (changed by the Crimes Amendment Act
  2026; commencement of ss 35 to 39 is by Order in Council, so no question turns on the old or new
  time limits); the old "must not strike or do bodily harm" limit in ss 52, 53 and 56 (removed by
  the same Act); post-arrest searching (Police and NZSA advice differ); Oranga Tamariki Act and
  Summary Offences Act detail (text not readable); Privacy Act response deadlines; the practical
  role plays of 40147.
