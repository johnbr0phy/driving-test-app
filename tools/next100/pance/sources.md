# PANCE bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the sources below. No NCCPA practice
exam items, no past or recalled PANCE questions, and no prep-site or commercial question material
were used, not even reworded.

## Official: NCCPA (the exam owner)

- PANCE Content Blueprint (released 2025, effective January 2025), fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.nccpa.net/become-certified/pance-blueprint/
  Gave: the medical content categories (94% of the exam with professional practice 6%):
  Cardiovascular 11%, Dermatologic 4%, Endocrine 6%, Eyes, Ears, Nose and Throat 6%,
  Gastrointestinal/Nutrition 8%, Genitourinary 4%, Hematologic 5%, Infectious Diseases 7%,
  Musculoskeletal 8%, Neurologic 7%, Psychiatry/Behavioral Science 7%, Pulmonary 9%, Renal 5%,
  Reproductive 7%, Professional Practice 6%; the task categories (history and physical 16%,
  diagnostic and laboratory studies 10%, most likely diagnosis 18%, health maintenance 11%,
  clinical intervention 16%, pharmaceutical therapeutics 15%, basic science 8%, professional
  practice 6%); "up to 8% to 10%" general surgery and "12% to 15%" pediatric patients. The page
  names no newer blueprint.
- What to Expect on Test Day, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.nccpa.net/become-certified/test-day/
  Gave: PANCE is 300 multiple-choice questions in five blocks of 60 questions, 60 minutes each,
  with 45 minutes of total break time and a 15-minute tutorial (5 hours); a finished block cannot
  be reviewed; two IDs; arrive 30 minutes early.
- Exam Development and Scoring, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.nccpa.net/examdevelopmentscoring/
  Gave: PANCE scale range 200 to 800 with a passing standard of 350; scale scores put all forms on
  one scale, so the number correct needed varies; the passing standard is set by the modified
  Angoff method and approved by the NCCPA Board.
- "PANCE at a Glance" (NCCPA PA Outreach presentation, 2025), fetched 2026-11-16 (PDF text
  extracted):
  https://www.nccpa.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2025-NCCPA-PANCE.pdf
  Gave: computer based at Pearson VUE (200+ centers); score based on the number answered
  correctly with no deduction for wrong answers; no true/false, "all of the above" or "none of
  the above" items; one attempt per 90 days or three per calendar year, whichever is fewer, and up
  to six attempts within six years of program completion; apply no sooner than 180 days before
  graduation, with a 180-day exam window.
- https://www.nccpa.net/become-certified/pance/ returned HTTP 403 and was not used.

## Clinical sources read for specific facts

- USPSTF A and B Recommendations, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation-topics/uspstf-a-and-b-recommendations
  Used for: AAA ultrasound in men 65 to 75 who ever smoked; colorectal screening from 45; annual
  low-dose CT for lung cancer (50 to 80, 20 pack-years, quit within 15 years); cervical screening
  21 to 65 (cytology every 3 years before 30); low-dose aspirin after 12 weeks for high
  preeclampsia risk; PrEP.
- CDC STI Treatment Guidelines, 2021, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/default.htm
  https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/gonorrhea-adults.htm (ceftriaxone 500 mg IM under
  150 kg, 1 g at 150 kg or more; add doxycycline if chlamydia not excluded)
  https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/trichomoniasis.htm (women metronidazole 500 mg
  twice daily for 7 days; men 2 g once)
  https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/p-and-s-syphilis.htm (benzathine penicillin G;
  no azithromycin; desensitization when follow-up cannot be ensured; pregnancy)
- IDSA/SHEA Clostridioides difficile 2021 focused update, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/clostridioides-difficile-2021-focused-update/
  Used for: fidaxomicin suggested over vancomycin for an initial episode; vancomycin acceptable;
  metronidazole no longer recommended.
- CDC Treatment for Latent TB Infection, fetched 2026-11-16:
  https://www.cdc.gov/tb/hcp/treatment/latent-tuberculosis-infection.html
  Used for: preferred 3HP, 4R and 3HR regimens.

## Standard clinical facts

The other items test long-standing textbook facts that do not hinge on a recent guideline change
(murmurs, physical signs, classic imaging and lab patterns, first-line drugs such as SSRIs,
methotrexate for RA, levothyroxine, carbamazepine for trigeminal neuralgia, magnesium sulfate,
IVIG and aspirin for Kawasaki disease, the rule of nines, Light's criteria, the anion gap,
sensitivity and NNT arithmetic). Guideline statements named in explanations (AAP bronchiolitis
and ADHD, ATS/IDSA pneumonia, IDSA cystitis and meningitis, AHA/ACC/HFSA heart failure, ACC/AHA
cholesterol, GOLD oxygen criteria, AASLD HCC surveillance) are stated at the level that has been
stable for years; their documents were not re-read for this build, and the integrator's review
should spot-check them.

## Could not be loaded or confirmed

- The ADA Standards of Care 2026 (diabetesjournals.org, HTTP 403): no item depends on it beyond
  the A1c 6.5% diagnostic threshold.
- GINA 2026 report pages had no recommendation text; no asthma reliever item was written.
- CDC adult immunization schedule (July 2025 addendum) is in flux; no vaccine-age items.
- NCCPA's own page states no time or pass rate history; first-time pass rates are left out.

## Deliberately left out

Exam fees, pass rates, vaccine schedule ages, stroke thrombolysis windows and BP thresholds,
indexed or changing figures, named drugs' brand names (generic names only), and anything about
the exam itself. Kept clear of the nclex, nclexpn, paramedic, pmdc-nle (pknle), rorezid, kenck and
ccrn banks: a fact-and-answer overlap check against them led to 24 items being rewritten to test
a different fact.
