# Sources for `pesticide` (Pesticide applicator core exam, general standards)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the federal rules and the
EPA-supported core manual listed below. No state exam item, the EPA/PERC national core exam bank,
course workbook question, past paper or commercial practice question was used or reworded, and no
training provider is named. Checked 2026-11-18.

## Exam owner, outline and format (official)
- eCFR, 40 CFR Part 171, Certification of Pesticide Applicators (2017 revision),
  https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-40?part=171 (fetched 2026-11-18;
  the reader page at ecfr.gov/current/title-40/part-171 blocks automated reads, so its API
  rendering of the current text was read). Gave: 171.103(a) commercial applicators at least 18; written,
  proctored exams, government-issued photo ID, monitored candidates, only agency-supplied
  reference materials; 171.103(c) the ten core competency areas the bank follows (label and
  labeling comprehension, safety, environment, pests, pesticides, equipment, application methods,
  laws, supervision of noncertified applicators, professionalism) and the added certification for
  fumigation, aerial, sodium cyanide and sodium fluoroacetate; 171.105(a) private applicators and
  agricultural commodities; 171.107 five-year maximum certification and recertification by exam
  or continuing education; 171.201 direct supervision of noncertified applicators (training
  within 12 months, equipment instruction, age 18 with the 16-year family exception, labeling
  access, PPE, site-specific instructions, means to communicate, physical presence when labeling
  requires it, records kept two years at the principal place of business).
- US EPA, "Certification Standards for Pesticide Applicators",
  https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/certification-standards-pesticide-applicators
  (fetched 2026-11-18). Gave: the 2017 rule's changes (nationwide minimum age, 5-year maximum
  recertification, specialized fumigation and aerial certification, noncertified applicator
  training) and that states, tribes, territories and federal agencies certify applicators under
  EPA-approved plans.
- Michigan Department of Agriculture and Rural Development, "National Pesticide Applicator Core
  Exam Blueprint",
  https://www.michigan.gov/documents/mdard/National_Pesticide_Applicator_Core_Exam_Blueprint_505151_7.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-18). A state lead agency's copy of the national core exam blueprint built on an
  EPA job analysis: 12 chapters (pest management, federal laws, labeling, formulations, hazards
  and first aid, PPE, environment, transportation, storage and security, emergency response,
  planning the application, application, professional conduct) with their objectives. No weights
  or question counts are published, so the nine-category split and the 70% readiness line are
  Tigertest's.
- Pesticide Educational Resources Collaborative (PERC, UC Davis, under an EPA cooperative
  agreement), "Meeting Updated Competency Standards for Certified Pesticide Applicators" (2021),
  https://pesticideresources.org/ct/PERC_Updated_Competency_Guide_2021.pdf (fetched 2026-11-18).
  Gave: many states use the National Core Manual (2nd ed., 2014) and the EPA-housed core exam,
  while many write their own manuals and exams; there is no standard national private applicator
  manual.
- PERC, "Updated National Core Manual from PERC",
  https://www.pesticideresources.org/training-material/updated_core_manual (fetched 2026-11-18).
  Gave: the 3rd edition of the National Pesticide Applicator Certification Core Manual and its
  exam bank include the competencies added by the 2017 rule.

## Study content (official or agency-supported)
- National Pesticide Applicator Certification Core Manual, 2nd ed. (NASDA Research Foundation,
  2014, under an EPA cooperative agreement), hosted by EPA at
  https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2022-09/national-pesticide-applicator-cert-core-manual-2014.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-18). Used for: IPM terms (economic threshold, economic injury level, action
  threshold), control methods, FIFRA Sections 2(ee), 3, 18, 24(c), 25(b), skin absorption by body
  area and formulation, cholinesterase inhibition and baseline testing, heat stress and heatstroke
  signs, 15-minute eye flush, Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 and CHEMTREC, PPE (85% hand exposure,
  glove choice by solvent, sleeves and gloves for overhead work, pant legs over boots, leather and
  canvas, aprons to the knees, laundering, discarding soaked absorbent clothing), microencapsulated
  products and bees, drift (wind 3 to 10 mph, inversions, droplet size, boom height 20 to 24
  inches), storage (locked, posted, ventilated, nonporous floors, heavy items low, replacement
  labels), transport, the three Cs of spills and spill kits, calibration variables and the tank
  mix examples.
- eCFR, 40 CFR Part 156, Labeling Requirements,
  https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-40?part=156 (fetched 2026-11-18).
  Gave: 156.62 toxicity categories (oral LD50 50, 500, 5,000 mg/kg), 156.64 signal words
  (DANGER, POISON with skull and crossbones, WARNING, CAUTION; product as sold), 156.66 Keep Out of
  Reach of Children, 156.68 first aid statement, 156.70 precautionary statement headings, 156.10(j)
  Restricted Use Pesticide at the top of the front panel, 156.146 triple rinse (1/4 full, 10
  seconds), 156.208 restricted-entry intervals (48, 24 and 12 hours; 72 hours for some
  organophosphates where rainfall is under 25 inches).
- eCFR, 40 CFR Part 170, Worker Protection Standard,
  https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-40?part=170 (fetched 2026-11-18).
  Gave: handler training within the last 12 months, application exclusion zone (100 and 25 feet),
  respirator fit testing, training and medical evaluation, decontamination supplies, posting and
  record retention.
- US EPA, "Introduction to Pesticide Labels",
  https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-labels/introduction-pesticide-labels (fetched 2026-11-18): the
  label is the law; FIFRA 2(p) label and labeling.
- US EPA, "Introduction to Pesticide Drift",
  https://www.epa.gov/reducing-pesticide-drift/introduction-pesticide-drift (fetched 2026-11-18):
  drift definition, rinsing equipment over the treated area.
- US EPA, "What is EPCRA?", https://www.epa.gov/epcra/what-epcra (fetched 2026-11-18): SARA Title
  III emergency planning and community right-to-know reporting.

## Not confirmed and how the copy words it
- No national question count, time limit or passing score exists: each certifying authority sets
  its own. The landing and facts say so, and 70% is called Tigertest's readiness line.
- State rules (licence categories, fees, recordkeeping for private applicators, state-specific
  posting and notification) vary and are not asked.

## Left out on purpose
- Category exams (agricultural, ornamental and turf, structural and the rest) and the specialized
  fumigation and aerial exams; fees; state laws; USDA private applicator recordkeeping details;
  brand names of products.
