# Sources for `phcle` (Licensure Examination for Criminologists, PRC)

All checked 2026-11-05.

## Format (landing, FAQ, examFacts only; never in the bank)

1. PRC, Professional Regulatory Board of Criminology, "Program of the Licensure Examination for
   Criminologists to be given on August 1, 2, and 3, 2026" (dated April 7, 2026).
   https://www.prc.gov.ph/sites/default/files/Crim%20Program%20of%20Exam%20August%202026.pdf
   Read in full (pdftotext). Confirms: six subjects over three days, two a day;
   Criminal Law and Jurisprudence 20% (8:00 to 11:30 a.m.; CLJ 1 to 6: criminal justice system,
   human rights, Criminal Law Books 1 and 2, evidence, procedure and court testimony);
   Law Enforcement Administration 15% (1:30 to 4:00 p.m.; organization and administration,
   comparative policing, industrial security, operations and planning with crime mapping,
   character formation 1 and 2);
   Crime Detection and Investigation 20% (8:00 to 11:30 a.m.; investigation and intelligence,
   specialized crime investigation 1 with legal medicine, 2 with interview and interrogation,
   traffic and accident investigation, fire protection and arson investigation, vice and drug
   control, technical writing, cybercrime and environmental laws);
   Forensic Science 15% (1:30 to 4:00 p.m.; personal identification, photography, chemistry and
   toxicology, questioned documents, lie detection, ballistics, report writing and lab safety);
   Criminology 20% (8:00 to 11:30 a.m.; introduction, theories, human behavior and victimology,
   professional conduct and ethics, juvenile delinquency and justice, dispute resolution and
   crisis management, criminological research 1 and 2);
   Correctional Administration 10% (1:30 to 4:00 p.m.; institutional, non-institutional,
   therapeutic modalities). Total 100%. Calculators prohibited; an ordinary magnifying glass or
   horseshoe microscope is allowed.
2. PRC, Program of the Licensure Examination for Criminologists, February 4, 5 and 6, 2026.
   https://www.prc.gov.ph/sites/default/files/Program%20of%20Exam%20Feb%202026%20LE%20for%20Criminologists.pdf
   Same subjects, weights and times. So in 2026 the exam was given at least in February and
   August. Later 2026 or 2027 dates: not confirmed (a guide mentions December 2026).
3. PRC Board of Criminology Resolution No. 05, s. 2022, "Adoption and Promulgation of the Revised
   Tables of Specifications for the Subjects in the Licensure Examination for Criminologists",
   applied from the December 2022 LEC (published Business Mirror April 28, 2022; effective May 14, 2022).
   https://www.prc.gov.ph/sites/default/files/2022-published%20TOS.pdf
   The resolution text was read; the TOS annexes for three subjects were read as Word files:
   https://www.prc.gov.ph/sites/default/files/Criminal%20Law%20and%20Jurisprudence.docx
   (20%, 100 items: CJS 5, human rights 5, Book 1 20, Book 2 35, evidence 17, procedure 18)
   https://www.prc.gov.ph/sites/default/files/Crime%20Detection%20and%20Investigation.docx
   (20%, 100 items: fundamentals 10, SCI 1 with legal medicine 10, SCI 2 10, traffic 15, fire 15,
   vice and drugs 15, technical English 1 5, technical English 2 10, cybercrime and environment 10)
   https://www.prc.gov.ph/sites/default/files/Criminology.docx
   (20%, 100 items: introduction 15, theories 15, human behavior and victimology 15, ethics 10,
   juvenile 15, dispute resolution and crisis 10, research 20)
   So the TOS allots about five items per percentage point. The annexes for Law Enforcement
   Administration, Forensic Science and Correctional Administration did not load (guessed file
   names returned 404), so their item counts (75, 75, 50 if the same ratio holds) are NOT confirmed
   and are not stated on the landing.
4. R.A. 11131, the Philippine Criminology Profession Act of 2018 (Lawphil).
   https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11131_2018.html
   Section 17: weighted average of 75% with no grade below 60% in any subject; a candidate with
   the 75% average but a subject below 60% has the result deferred and retakes that subject once
   within two years, needing at least 80%; failing three or more subjects is failing the exam.
   Section 15 lists the subjects with weights (the statute's original split); the Board revised
   the subjects and weights by Resolution No. 01, s. 2021 (title and summary from search results:
   https://law.upd.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/PRC-Criminology-Resolution-No-01-S2021.pdf ,
   an image PDF not read). The 2026 programs (sources 1 and 2) show the weights in use.
   Section 14(e): an examinee who failed five times must complete a CHED-recognized refresher course.
   R.A. 11131 repealed R.A. 6506 (1972).

## Modelling decisions

- Each 50-question test: CLJ 10 (criminal law 6, evidence/procedure/human rights 4), LEA 8, CDI 10
  (investigation 5, traffic/fire/drugs 5), Forensic Science 7, Criminology 10 (theories and research
  6, ethics/juvenile/dispute resolution 4), Correctional Administration 5. The two 15% subjects round
  to 8 and 7. The within-subject splits are Tigertest's, guided by the TOS item counts above.
- Pass line 75% (the weighted average). The 60% subject floor and deferral rule are not modelled;
  the landing says so.
- Not modelled: the real length (six subjects of up to 100 items), Filipino-language items.

## Fact check, 2026-11-05

Each claim below was checked on 2026-11-05 against the source named. Lawphil pages were read directly.

Confirmed from primary text (kept):
- R.A. 8551 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8551_1998.html): the Commission exercises
  "administrative control and operational supervision" over the PNP; the Internal Affairs Service
  is created; compulsory retirement at 56. Local chief executives "direct the employment and
  deployment" of PNP units (Sec. 62). The LEA-003 explanation was reworded to that phrasing.
- R.A. 6975 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1990/ra_6975_1990.html): PLEB created in every
  city and municipality (Sec. 43); BJMP supervises city and municipal jails (Sec. 61) and district
  jails (Sec. 62); BFP and BJMP are DILG bureaus (Sec. 6).
- R.A. 11200: Police General is the highest rank and Patrolman/Patrolwoman the lowest
  (Senate legislative record https://ldr.senate.gov.ph/legislative-issuance/republic-act-no-11200
  and https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Republic_Act_No._11200).
- R.A. 11917, the Private Security Services Industry Act (2022), repeals R.A. 5487
  (https://ldr.senate.gov.ph/legislative-issuance/republic-act-no-11917).
- R.A. 7438 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1992/ra_7438_1992.html): Sec. 2(d) written,
  signed in the presence of counsel or on valid waiver before listed persons; Sec. 2 custodial
  investigation includes issuing an "invitation".
- R.A. 10640 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2014/ra_10640_2014.html): inventory witnesses
  are the accused, an elected public official and a representative of the National Prosecution
  Service or the media.
- R.A. 7659 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1993/ra_7659_1993.html): Art. 320 destructive
  arson covers trains, ships and aircraft for transport and buildings for public gatherings;
  reclusion perpetua is 20 years and 1 day to 40 years.
- R.A. 10592 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10592_2013.html): GCTA 20/23/25/30
  days, TASTM 15 days, Art. 98 one-fifth/two-fifths, Art. 29 full or four-fifths credit.
- R.A. 10707 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2015/ra_10707_2015.html): probation after an
  appeal reduces the penalty to a probationable one; Sec. 9(a) more than six years disqualifies.
- R.A. 10575 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10575_2013.html): BuCor under the DOJ;
  national inmates are those sentenced to more than three years.
- R.A. 9344 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2006/ra_9344_2006.html): diversion and CICL
  definitions, Sec. 57 status offenses. R.A. 10630
  (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10630_2013.html): 15 and below exempt, above 15
  below 18 liable only with discernment, Bahay Pag-asa built by provinces and HUCs.
- R.A. 8750 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1999/ra_8750_1999.html): drivers and front-seat
  passengers (and back-seat passengers of most private cars; TFD-002 explanation now says so).
- R.A. 11229 Sec. 5: no child 12 and below in the front seat, height exception
  (https://ldr.senate.gov.ph/legislative-issuance/republic-act-no-11229).
- R.A. 11053 Sec. 3: all forms of hazing prohibited
  (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11053_2018.html).
- P.D. 1612 Sec. 2 and 5 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1979/pd_1612_1979.html).
- R.A. 10175 Sec. 4(a)(1) illegal access and Sec. 6 one degree higher
  (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10175_2012.html).
- Rules of Court (https://lawphil.net/courts/rules/rc_110-127_crim.html): Rule 112 Sec. 1
  (4 years, 2 months and 1 day), Rule 113 Sec. 5(b), Rule 114 Sec. 7, Rule 117 Sec. 1,
  Rule 126 Sec. 10 (ten days).
- 2019 Amendments to the Revised Rules on Evidence (https://lawphil.net/courts/rules/am_19-08-15-sc_2019.html):
  original document rule; Rule 133 extrajudicial confession needs corroboration by the corpus delicti.
- Rule on Cybercrime Warrants, A.M. No. 17-11-03-SC
  (https://oca.judiciary.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/A.M.-No.-17-11-03-SC-dtd-07.03.18.pdf):
  WDCD, WICD, WSSECD and WECD.
