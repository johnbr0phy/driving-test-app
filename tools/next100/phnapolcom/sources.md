# Sources for `phnapolcom` (NAPOLCOM PNP Entrance Examination)

All 200 questions are original. Review-center "NAPOLCOM reviewer" pages and past-question
sites appeared in search results; none were opened for their questions and nothing was copied.
Checked 2026-11-05 unless noted. The national site www.napolcom.gov.ph returned a Cloudflare
challenge (HTTP 403) to every request this session, so the format comes from NAPOLCOM's own
announcement as posted on its Region 6 site, NAPOLCOM's Region 2 site, and government and national
news reports.

## Format (official)
- NAPOLCOM, "PNP Entrance and Promotional Examinations Announcement" for the June 30, 2024
  examination, signed by Commissioner Alberto A. Bernardo, Vice Chairperson and Executive Officer
  (scanned PDF, 11 pages, read page by page on 2026-11-05):
  https://r6.napolcom.gov.ph/Exam/june2024examannouncement.pdf (index page
  https://r6.napolcom.gov.ph/index-examination.php)
  - p.1: NAPOLCOM conducts the PNP Entrance (PNPE) nationwide "pursuant to its constitutional
    mandate to administer and control the PNP"; open to Filipino citizens meeting the age and
    education requirements and to Patrolmen/Patrolwomen with temporary appointments for lack of
    eligibility; passers are "conferred with police eligibility required for appointment to the
    rank of Patrolman/Patrolwoman". Exam time 8:00 A.M. to 11:00 A.M.
  - p.2: 35 examination centers; minimum qualification standards: civilian not less than 21 nor
    more than 30 years old on the day of the examination, bachelor's degree; temporary
    Patrolman/Patrolwoman, no age limit, bachelor's degree.
  - p.7: items to bring (Notice of Admission, valid government ID, Pencil No. 2, ball pen); attire
    (white shirt, dark pants, shoes).
  - p.8, "X. Examination Coverage": "Three (3) hours will be allotted". PNP Entrance Examination
    (Objective Type/Multiple Choice): I. General Information/Uprightness (Philippine Constitution;
    PNP Laws, R.A. 6975 and R.A. 8551 as amended; Criminal Justice System; Social Awareness and
    Value Judgment; Values). II. Verbal Reasoning (Vocabulary: antonyms, synonyms; Comprehension:
    paragraph organization, reading comprehension; Grammar: correct usage). III. Quantitative
    Reasoning (Basic concepts: percentage, fraction, number series; Problem solving skills / bar
    graph interpretation / analysis). IV. Logical Reasoning (Verbal; Non-verbal).
  - p.10: calculators, phones and review materials are prohibited in the exam room.
  - The announcement gives no number of items, no subject weights and no passing rate.
- PTV News (state broadcaster), 26 April 2019, "48.6K to take PNP entrance, promotion exams
  Sunday": "The PNP entrance examination consists of questions on general information, verbal
  reasoning, quantitative reasoning, and logical reasoning." https://ptvnews.ph/?p=116326
- NAPOLCOM Region 2, Examination page (read 2026-11-05): PNP Entrance, Promotional and PESE-WE
  examinations on April 19, 2026; previous on October 19, 2025.
  https://r2.napolcom.gov.ph/examination.php
- Daily Tribune, 19 April 2026, "NAPOLCOM holds nationwide PNP exams": held Sunday 19 April 2026,
  8 a.m. to 11 a.m., results expected within 45 days.
  https://tribune.net.ph/2026/04/19/napolcom-holds-nationwide-pnp-exams
- The Manila Times (NAPOLCOM release), 3 July 2026, "12,372 qualify in Napolcom-administered PNP
  eligibility examinations": 41,592 took the three April 19, 2026 examinations; 9,224 passed the
  PNPEE; it was for civilians holding a bachelor's degree aged 21 to 30 on exam day and temporary
  Patrolmen/Patrolwomen; passers may apply for appointment as Patrolman/Patrolwoman subject to
  Section 14 of R.A. 8551.
  https://test.manilatimes.net/2026/07/03/tmt-newswire/12372-qualify-in-napolcom-qdministered-pnp-eligibility-examinations/2377995

## Format (guides only, labelled so on the landing)
- Passing score 70%: review sites (thesummitexpress.com results pages, supertutor.ph, via search
  2026-11-05) say examinees need at least 70%. One source mentions 75% historically. NAPOLCOM's
  announcement does not state it. Labelled "guides say" and used as Tigertest's target.
- Item count: one guide says about 150 items; another says NAPOLCOM does not publish the count.
  Not stated on the landing beyond "not published".
- October 18, 2026 sitting: one review site lists it (registration August 17 to 21, 2026); not
  confirmed by NAPOLCOM or news this session, so the landing does not name it.

## Tigertest's model
- Four 50-question mock tests (200 items, no spares), 70% target. Weights are Tigertest's own,
  across the four official areas: General Information 14 (Constitution and criminal justice 6, PNP
  laws 5, values 3), Verbal 16 (vocabulary 6, paragraph organization and reading 5, grammar 5),
  Quantitative 13 (percentages, fractions and number series 7, problem solving and bar graphs 6),
  Logical 7. Bar graphs are described in words; non-verbal reasoning (figure series, mirror images,
  paper folding, cube counting, rotation) is written in text, since Tigertest v1 shows no images.
- No current affairs, no office-holders, no fees, no heights, no item about the exam itself.

## Law used in the general information questions
- 1987 Constitution, full text at the Lawphil Project (read 2026-11-05):
  https://lawphil.net/consti/cons1987.html
  Art. III s.2 (warrants), s.3(2) (exclusionary rule), s.4 (assembly and petition), s.6 (liberty of
  abode, lawful court order), s.11 (access to courts despite poverty), s.12 (counsel provided if
  the person cannot afford one; secret, solitary and incommunicado detention prohibited), s.13
  (bail), s.14 (presumption of innocence), s.15 (habeas corpus: invasion or rebellion), s.18(2)
  (involuntary servitude only as punishment for a crime after conviction), s.21 (double jeopardy),
  s.22 (ex post facto, bill of attainder); Art. XVI s.6 (one police force, national in scope and
  civilian in character, administered and controlled by a national police commission; authority
  of local executives provided by law).
- R.A. 6975 (DILG Act of 1990), Lawphil: https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1990/ra_6975_1990.html
  s.2 national in scope, civilian in character; s.6 DILG includes NAPOLCOM, PPSC, PNP, BFP, BJMP;
  s.24 PNP powers (including licensing security agencies, guards and private detectives); s.27
  ratio of one police officer per 500 persons (minimum one per 1,000); s.43 PLEB; s.51(b)(1)
  mayors' operational supervision and control, except 30 days before and after elections, when
  the COMELEC takes it; s.61 BJMP over district, city and municipal jails; s.66 to 67 PPSC.
- R.A. 8551 (PNP Reform and Reorganization Act of 1998), Lawphil:
  https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8551_1998.html
  s.13 of R.A. 6975 as amended (NAPOLCOM attached to the DILG for policy and program
  coordination; chairperson, four regular commissioners, Chief PNP ex officio member; three
  regular commissioners from the civilian sector, one from law enforcement, at least one a woman;
  DILG Secretary ex officio chairperson; vice chairperson as executive officer); s.16 (six-year
  term without reappointment); s.19 (12-month Field Training Program); s.28 (attrition by
  non-promotion, ten years); s.57 (women's desks); s.63 (mayor chooses the chief of police from a
  list of five eligibles); s.64 (governors and mayors automatically deputized as NAPOLCOM
  representatives); s.66 to 68 (PLEB as central receiving entity; one community member a woman
  and another a member of the Bar; three-year term).
- R.A. 11200 (2019) rank classification, Lawphil:
  https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2019/ra_11200_2019.html (Patrolman/Patrolwoman, then
  Police Corporal, Police Staff Sergeant, Police Master Sergeant...).
- R.A. 7438 (1992) rights of persons arrested, detained or under custodial investigation, Lawphil:
  https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1992/ra_7438_1992.html (s.2(f) visits by immediate
  family, doctor, priest or minister, counsel, accredited NGOs).
- R.A. 9344 (Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006), Lawphil:
  https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2006/ra_9344_2006.html (s.21(e) avoid handcuffs and
  restraints unless absolutely necessary). R.A. 10630 (2013), Lawphil
  https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10630_2013.html, read 2026-11-05: it amends other
  sections (s.22 and others) but not s.21, so the handcuff rule stands (PHNAP-CON-018).
- Revised Penal Code Art. 125 as amended by Executive Order No. 272 (1987), Lawphil
  https://lawphil.net/executive/execord/eo1987/eo_272_1987.html, read 2026-11-05: 12 hours (light
  penalties), 18 hours (correctional), 36 hours (afflictive or capital) (PHNAP-CON-016).
- Five pillars (law enforcement, prosecution, courts, corrections, community), PNP and NBI in the
  law enforcement pillar: UNAFEI, "Topic 2" of its joint seminar with the Philippines, citing a
  NAPOLCOM pamphlet (https://www.unafei.or.jp/activities/pdf/joint_philippines/topic2.pdf, via
  search 2026-11-05; reputable secondary, no primary statute lists the pillars) (PHNAP-CON-014).
- NBI and Parole and Probation Administration in the Department of Justice: Administrative Code of
  1987 (E.O. 292), Book IV Title III, s.4 (constituent units: NBI, Board of Pardons and Parole,
  Parole and Probation Administration...) and s.23(2) (the Administration shall "exercise general
  supervision over all parolees and probationers"), Lawphil
  https://lawphil.net/executive/execord/eo1987/eo_292_1987.html, read 2026-11-05 (PHNAP-CON-020,
  PHNAP-CON-021).
- Lupon Tagapamayapa: Local Government Code (R.A. 7160) s.399(a) (a lupon in each barangay,
  "composed of the punong barangay, as chairman and ten (10) to twenty (20) members") and s.402,
  Lawphil https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1991/ra_7160_1991.html, read 2026-11-05
  (PHNAP-CON-022).
- Rule on the Writ of Amparo, A.M. No. 07-9-12-SC (September 25, 2007), s.1: available to any
  person whose right to life, liberty and security is violated or threatened by an unlawful act or
  omission of a public official or employee, or of a private individual or entity; covers
  extralegal killings and enforced disappearances or threats thereof. Supreme Court PDF
  https://sc.judiciary.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/07-9-12-SC.pdf and ChanRobles
  https://chanrobles.com/scdecisions/jurisprudence2007/sep2007/am_07_9_12_sc_2007.php, via search
  2026-11-05 (PHNAP-CON-023).
- R.A. 9745, Anti-Torture Act of 2009, Lawphil
  https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2009/ra_9745_2009.html, read 2026-11-05: s.1 short title;
  s.2(b) no person under investigation or in custody shall be subjected to torture; s.6 freedom from
  torture an absolute right (PHNAP-CON-024).
- B.P. 880, Public Assembly Act of 1985, Lawphil
  https://lawphil.net/statutes/bataspam/bp1985/bp_880_1985.html, read 2026-11-05: s.3(c) maximum
  tolerance is "the highest degree of restraint" the police shall observe during a public assembly
  (PHNAP-VAL-011).
- PNP core values (Maka-Diyos, Makabayan, Makatao, Makakalikasan): Philippine National Police
  Academy, "The PNPA Logo", which says the PNPA adopts the core values of the PNP
  (https://pnpa.edu.ph/?p=196, read 2026-11-05). pnp.gov.ph returned no readable page this session;
  the PNPA is the PNP's own academy, and Devex's PNP profile gives the same four (via search
  2026-11-05) (PHNAP-VAL-001).
- Bayanihan (community cooperation, named for neighbours carrying a family's house on bamboo
  poles): general knowledge; USC Folklore Archives, "The Bayanihan Spirit"
  https://folklore.usc.edu/the-bayanihan-spirit/ (via search 2026-11-05) (PHNAP-VAL-012).

## Reading passage background
- PAGASA, "Tropical Cyclone Wind Signal" (https://pagasa.dost.gov.ph/learning-tools/tropical-cyclone-wind-signal,
  read 2026-11-05): a TCWS is a warning for a particular land area, "usually hoisted at the level of
  a province, independent component city, or highly urbanized city" (PHNAP-RDG-004).
- Philippine Constabulary and INP before 1991: R.A. 6975 s.23 (PNP formed from the INP police forces
  and the PC), s.86 (PNP absorbs the functions of the PC and INP) and s.90 (the PC "shall cease to be
  a major service of the Armed Forces of the Philippines"; the INP was its civilian component),
  Lawphil link above, read 2026-11-05 (PHNAP-RDG-019, PHNAP-RDG-020).
