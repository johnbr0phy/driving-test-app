- R.A. 10029, Philippine Psychology Act of 2009, approved March 16, 2010 (lawphil.net):
  https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_10029_2010.html
  - Sec. 3(d): a psychometrician may administer and score objective and structured personality
    tests, excluding projective tests; interpret them and write reports; conduct preparatory intake
    interviews; "at all times" under the supervision of a licensed professional psychologist.
  - Sec. 13: examinees need at least a bachelor's degree in psychology (landing only, not in bank).
  - Sec. 15: subjects named in the Act are Theories of Personality, Abnormal Psychology, Industrial
    Psychology and Psychological Assessment. The Board's current programs list Developmental
    Psychology in place of Theories of Personality; the bank follows the programs.
  - Sec. 18: to pass, a weighted general average of at least 75% with no rating below 60% in any
    subject.
  - Sec. 34: penalties, fine or imprisonment or both (amounts not used).
- Professional Regulatory Board of Psychology Resolution No. 11, s. 2017, adopting the Code of
  Ethics and Professional Standards for Psychology Practitioners (PAP code first adopted 2008,
  reviewed December 2015). Scanned PDF read page by page:
  https://www.prc.gov.ph/sites/default/files/PSYCHOLOGY-CodeEthics-2017-11.pdf
  - Declaration of Principles: the 2008 Universal Declaration of Ethical Principles for
    Psychologists (respect for dignity, competent caring, integrity, professional and scientific
    responsibilities).
  - II.A boundaries of competence and referral; II.B services in emergencies.
  - VII Assessment: A bases; B informed consent and its three exceptions (mandated by law, implied
    in routine institutional activity, decisional capacity), limits of confidentiality,
    interpreters; C tools; D obsolete and outdated results; E interpreting; F release of test data
    (raw and scaled scores, item responses, behavior notes are not released); G explaining results
    in non-technical language; H test security (qualified users only); I psychometricians administer
    objective and structured tests only, projective tests by trained psychologists, supervised by
    psychologists; J test construction.
  - VIII.A confidentiality: release without consent when there is imminent danger or when mandated
    by law.
- R.A. 11036, Mental Health Act, approved June 20, 2018 (lawphil.net): Sec. 4(t) "service user";
  Sec. 8 written informed consent.

## Secondary (labeled on the landing and in examFacts)

- Item counts per subject (Psychological Assessment 150, the others 100 each) come from the Board's
  2014 table of specifications as reposted on a reviewer blog, not from a PRC page:
  https://psychometricpinas.blogspot.com/2014/05/tables-of-specifications-for.html
  Its topic outline (A: psychometric concepts, test development and statistics, uses and
  limitations, evaluating tools by setting, administration and scoring of intelligence and
  objective personality tests, ethics; C: normal vs abnormal, recognizing disorders, theories of
  causes, sociocultural factors, ethics in diagnosis; D: selection, training, appraisal,
  motivation, teams, HRM, work-life balance) guided the category split.
- Board Resolution No. 02, s. 2023, "Adoption and Promulgation of the Enhanced Table of
  Specifications for the Subjects in the Psychologists and Psychometricians Licensure Examinations",
  is catalogued by UP Law NARO (https://naro.law.upd.edu.ph/documents/962, checked 2026-11-05) with
  no text available. Its existence is confirmed; its text and any changed weights within subjects are
  not, so the landing (FAQ 4) says the text is not posted and the bank keeps only the program weights.
- The split of each subject into categories (Developmental 5+5, Abnormal 6+4, I/O 5+5, Assessment
  8/6/6) is Tigertest's own, guided by the 2014 outline above; spec.json and the landing say only
  the 20/20/20/40 weights are official.
- The September 2026 exam was reported moved from August 19-20 to September 1-2, 2026 because of
  heavy rain (thesummitexpress.com news, not a PRC notice); not used in the copy.
- Panukat ng Ugali at Pagkatao (Enriquez and Guanzon-Lapena), an indigenous Filipino personality
  inventory: academic secondary sources (Del Pilar; UP Diliman journals), dates differ (1975 work
  begun, 1985 inventory). Rechecked 2026-11-05: Enriquez and Guanzon (1985) in the Philippine Journal
  of Educational Measurement, https://cem-inc.org.ph/sites/default/files/research/pjem_1985_vol04.pdf,
  and the PAP journal's Mapa ng Loob paper,
  https://www.pap.ph/assets/files/journals/the-development-of-the-masaklaw-na-panukat-ng-loob-mapa-ng-loob.pdf.
  TST-023 asks only that it is a Filipino-built personality test, not a date.
- Amok and latah (ABC-008, ABC-009), DSM-IV glossary of culture-bound syndromes, carried into
  DSM-5-TR's cultural concepts of distress. Checked 2026-11-05 through secondary sources: amok first
  reported in Malaysia, also Indonesia and Brunei, with Philippine forms (juramentado), and similar
  patterns in Laos, Polynesia and Puerto Rico (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_amok,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amok_syndrome); latah listed for Malaysia and Indonesia
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latah).
- Filipino values (DVL-020 utang na loob, ABC-012 hiya, IOO-012 pakikisama): Enriquez's accommodative
  surface values, glossed as gratitude/debt of gratitude (Kaut 1961), sense of propriety or shame,
  and smooth interpersonal relations (Lynch). Checked 2026-11-05 against Pe-Pua and Marcelino (2000),
  "Sikolohiyang Pilipino (Filipino psychology)", Asian Journal of Social Psychology 3,
  https://www.scribd.com/doc/313443077/PePua-Marcelino-2000-pdf (summary via search).

## Standard content (textbook knowledge, not checked against a single source this session)

DSM-5-TR criteria and durations (two weeks for a major depressive episode, six months for
schizophrenia, one to six months schizophreniform, under one month brief psychotic disorder);
developmental theorists (Erikson, Freud, Piaget, Vygotsky, Kohlberg, Bronfenbrenner, Ainsworth,
Thomas and Chess, Baumrind, Marcia, Elkind, Arnett, Sternberg, Levinson, Baltes, Carstensen, Peck,
Kubler-Ross, Horn and Cattell); I/O theories (Herzberg, Vroom, Locke, Hackman and Oldham, McClelland,
Alderfer, Fiedler, House, LMX, Meyer and Allen, Maslach, Hawthorne, Taylor, McGregor, Schein,
Karasek, Kirkpatrick, Taylor-Russell, Schmidt and Hunter, the four-fifths rule); psychometrics
(Spearman-Brown, KR-20, alpha, kappa, SEM, z and T scores, stanines, IRT); test facts (MMPI-2 567
items, MMPI-2-RF 338, PAI 344, MCMI-III 175, WAIS-IV indexes, Wechsler mean 100 SD 15). Every
computed key was recomputed by hand:
- Four-fifths rule: 0.8 x 50% = 40%.
- Selection ratio 12 / 240 = 0.05.
- Spearman-Brown, r = .60 doubled: 1.20 / 1.60 = 0.75.
- Maximum validity, r = .64: square root = 0.80.
- SEM = 15 x square root of .16 = 6.
- z = (71 - 56) / 6 = 2.50; T for z = -1.5 = 35.
- r = .60, r squared = 36%; IQR 58 - 42 = 16.
