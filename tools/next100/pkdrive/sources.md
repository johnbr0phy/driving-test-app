# Sources for `pkdrive` (Pakistan driving licence sign and theory test)

All 200 questions are original. Practice sites with "Pakistan driving test questions" were seen in
search results but not opened or copied. Signs are described in words (no images). Checked
2026-10-31 unless noted.

## Who runs the test
There is no single national test. Licences are issued by each province's licensing authority and
by the Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT); all recognise each other's licences.
- Wikipedia, "Driving licence in Pakistan" (read 2026-10-31): licences issued by each district,
  province or territory; rules broadly similar; all recognise each other's licences; minimum age
  18 for a permanent licence (20 for paid drivers; the Ordinance itself says 21, see below); valid CNIC and proof of residence; eyesight:
  read a number plate from 20.5 m; Punjab learner permit normally valid six months; Phase 1 a
  computerised written test then a road signs test, "at least 50%" to pass; Phase 2 practical test
  with driving and parking between cones.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_licence_in_Pakistan
- Punjab: learner permits moved from DLIMS to Dastak; first licence needs an in-person visit for
  biometrics, photo, e-sign test and driving test (gopunjab.pk guide, via search).
  https://gopunjab.pk/driving-license-online-punjab/
- Punjab e-sign test: a Lahore Traffic Police video shows ten multiple-choice sign questions on a
  computer at a Khidmat centre (PakWheels, gari.pk via search). Pass mark reported variously:
  PakWheels says at least five correct; other guides say 50% to 70%. NOT confirmed officially.
  https://www.pakwheels.com/blog/?p=184964
- Punjab retake wait for a failed e-sign or road test cut from 42 to 15 days by an amendment to the
  Punjab Motor Vehicle Rules 1969 (Dawn, ProPakistani Aug 2024, via search).
  https://propakistani.pk/2024/08/28/punjab-reduces-waiting-period-for-failed-driving-test-candidates/
- Punjab, from 17 September 2026 (reported): road test within 24 hours of passing the e-sign test
  (Minute Mirror, via search). Not found on the DLIMS FAQ by one guide; not used in the bank.
  https://minutemirror.com.pk/new-driving-licence-procedure-introduced-in-lahore-24-hour-deadline-for-road-test-632074/
- Islamabad (ITP): learner permit, then a theory test and a road sign test, then a driving test;
  wait of 42 days after the learner permit (PakWheels, ProPakistani guides via search). ITP
  stopped Learner Period Condonation in December 2025 (ProPakistani 18 Dec 2025).
  https://www.pakwheels.com/blog/how-to-get-driving-license-in-islamabad/
  https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/18/islamabad-stops-fast-track-driving-license-process/amp/
- Sindh: pre-licence driver training made compulsory (ARY News, 19 Feb 2026); learner's licence
  described as valid one year (Aaj News, Jan 2025). Computerised test details not published.
  https://arynews.tv/sindh-introduces-new-rule-for-obtaining-driving-licenses
- Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: computerised traffic sign test then a practical road test; learner permit
  45 days before the test (guides via search); digital licence management system launched 4 May
  2026 (Dawn). https://www.dawn.com/news/1997539

Conclusion used in the bank and landing: every authority sets its own question count, pass mark
and waiting period, so none of them is asserted as a rule. The sign test is the common core.
Tigertest's mock tests are 50 questions with an 80% target (Tigertest's own line, not an official
pass mark).

- Learner L plate: Punjab Police Motorway/traffic learning page (official, read via search
  2026-10-31): a learner holds a learner's permit, carries it while driving and shows a square
  white plate with a red letter L on the front and rear; a licensed driver sits beside the learner.
  https://mtwds.punjabpolice.gov.pk/node/29

## Signs
- Wikipedia, "Road signs in Pakistan" (read 2026-10-31): signs follow the Manual of Signs, Signals
  and Markings (National Transport Research Centre, 1989) and generally the Vienna Convention on
  Road Signs and Signals (Pakistan acceded 14 January 1980); bilingual English and Urdu; left-hand
  traffic. Regulatory series includes Stop (R-01), Give way (R-02), Give way to oncoming vehicles
  (R-03), No entry (R-06), No overtaking (R-23), Speed limit (R-25), Stop at police checkpoint
  (R-27), turn right/left, mandatory roundabout, mandatory routes for cycles. Warning series
  includes dangerous curve, steep ascent, pedestrian crossing, roundabout ahead, railway crossing,
  low-flying aircraft (W-24), sidewinds (W-25).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_signs_in_Pakistan
- Shapes and colours (Vienna Convention conventions): warning = red-bordered triangle; prohibition
  = red-bordered circle; mandatory = blue circle; stop = red octagon; give way = inverted
  triangle; no parking / no stopping = blue circle with red border and one bar / a cross; end of
  restriction = white circle with a black diagonal band.

## Rules of the road
- Left-hand traffic, overtake on the right, roundabouts clockwise giving way to the right
  (Wikipedia; Vienna Convention practice for left-hand traffic).
- Provincial Motor Vehicles Ordinance 1965 (W.P. Ordinance XIX of 1965), official consolidated copy
  "as amended up to 30-11-2023" from the Punjab Transport Department, read in full 2026-11-01:
  https://transport.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/The%20Provincial%20Motor%20Vehicles%20Ordinance%2C%201965%20%2830-11-2023%29_0.pdf
  (copies also on balochistan transport and kppolice.gov.pk). Confirmed from it:
  - s.4: 18 for a motorcycle or a private car; 21 for a car as a paid employee or a transport
    vehicle; 22 for a heavy transport vehicle.
  - Third Schedule Part II: the examiner checks the candidate knows ss.85-89, the Tenth Schedule,
    the traffic signs of the Ninth Schedule and "the general provisions of the Highway Code".
    Part III: read a registration plate with 3-inch figures from 25 yards (about 23 m), with or
    without spectacles; field of vision 130 degrees; tell red from green.
  - s.89: a motorcycle may carry one person besides the driver, on a proper seat behind.
    s.89-A (Punjab amendment 2005): driver and pillion rider must wear a crash helmet. s.89-B:
    seat belt for the driver of a car on a notified road.
  - Tenth Schedule (driving regulations): Reg. 1 keep as close to the left as expedient; Reg. 2
    pass to the right of traffic going the same way, except as in Reg. 3; Reg. 3 a driver "may pass
    to the left of a vehicle the driver of which having indicated an intention to turn to the right
    has drawn to the centre of the road" (confirms PKDRV-ROD-003); Reg. 4 no overtaking where it
    causes danger or a corner, hill or obstruction hides the road ahead; Reg. 5 do not speed up
    when being overtaken; Reg. 6 slow down at intersections and corners; Reg. 7 entering a main
    road, give way to its traffic, otherwise give way to all traffic approaching on your right;
    Reg. 9 turn left close to the left; turn right from near the centre, passing beyond the centre
    point and ending as near as may be to the left side of the road entered (PKDRV-JNC-020).
  - Eleventh Schedule: right arm extended horizontally, palm to the front = turning right (or
    moving right to pass).
  - The Ordinance has no rule on who gives way on a narrow hill road. The builder's item saying the
    downhill driver gives way (an Indian rule; the UK and Hong Kong codes say the opposite) was
    replaced by a Reg. 9 item on 2026-11-01.
- Punjab DLIMS (Driving License Information Management System, part of Dastak), official, read
  2026-11-01: learner licence applied for online with CNIC copies and a photo; a medical
  certificate from a government doctor if over 50. https://dlims.punjab.gov.pk/
- Third-party insurance compulsory: Motor Vehicles Ordinance 1965, chapter on insurance against
  third-party risks.
- NHMP (National Highways and Motorway Police): helpline 130; lane discipline enforced; sections
  closed in dense fog (APP, Zameen NHMP guide via search). nhmp.gov.pk returned 503 on 2026-10-31.
  https://www.app.com.pk/national/nhmp-striving-to-control-accidents-on-motorways-highways-by-strict-implementation-of-traffic-rules/
- Emergency numbers: police 15, NHMP 130, Rescue 1122 (Punjab and KP).

## Deliberately avoided
- Speed limit numbers: NHMP cut car limits from 120 to 100 km/h on motorways in March 2026 and
  restored them on 21 June 2026 (ARY News, APP, Dawn via search). Too changeable; no item asks a
  limit figure.
  https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1231141
- Fees, fines, challan amounts, pass marks of any one province, learner waiting periods, licence
  validity periods, pillion helmet rules (differ by province), alcohol limits.
