# pknle sources (checked 2026-11-05)

## Name of the exam
The brief calls it the PMDC National Licensing Examination (NLE). That was the Pakistan Medical
Commission's name (NLE syllabus of 4 June 2021). Since the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council Act,
2022 (Act IV of 2023) brought back the PM&DC, the exam is the National Registration Examination
(NRE), defined in section 18 of the Act. The landing says "NRE (formerly NLE)"; search terms keep both.
The PMDC results page is still at pmdc.pk/result/NLEStepOne.

## Read from PM&DC's own documents (all on pmdc.pk, read 2026-11-05)
- PMDC Act 2022 (https://pmdc.pk/Documents/law/PMDC%20Act.pdf), Gazette of Pakistan 16 January
  2023, Act No. IV of 2023, short title "the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council Act, 2022".
  Section 18: the Council "shall at least twice a year ... conduct the NRE for Pakistani foreign
  graduate students. Passing the NRE shall be mandatory for obtaining a full registration. The NRE
  shall be substantially based on objective computer based multiple choice questions and a
  practical component if determined by the Council"; no limit on attempts. 18(3): foreign-qualified
  practitioners registered abroad must also pass it.
- "National Registration Examination (Medical) Standard, Structure and Syllabus 2024"
  (https://pmdc.pk/Documents/Syllabus/National%20Registration%20Examination%20(Medical)%20Standard,%20Structure%20and%20Syllabus%202024.pdf),
  notified by PM&DC letter Exam/2024/13 of 30 April 2024 (National Medical & Dental Academic Board
  20 March 2024, Council 5 April 2024). Text extracted with pdftotext; the weightage page was also
  read as an image:
  - Eligibility: graduates of PM&DC approved foreign medical colleges (MBBS/MD or equivalent) with a
    provisional licence.
  - Two components: theory (MCQs) and a Clinical Skills Examination (CSE, OSCE format, 20 stations,
    about 3 h 30 min, 3 mandatory stations). Only theory passers may sit the CSE.
  - Theory: "200 MCQs in which 70% will be from clinical sciences disciplines and 30% from basic
    sciences disciplines", MCQs "targeting higher cognition".
  - Pass: 60% minimum in the theory and 60% in the CSE; Angoff's method "will also be adopted for
    standardization"; no negative marking in either component.
  - Theory pass valid 5 years for passing the CSE; three failed CSE attempts means sitting the whole
    NRE again (landing/FAQ only, never in the bank).
  - Basic sciences table (30%, "Total MCQs: 60"): Applied Pathology 11, Applied Anatomy 11, Applied
    Physiology 10, Clinical Pharmacology 8, Applied Biochemistry 6, Community Medicine 6, Forensic
    Medicine & Toxicology 4. NOTE: these rows add up to 56, not the 60 printed as the total.
  - Clinical sciences table (70%, 140 MCQs): Medicine and Allied 52, Surgery and Allied 48,
    Obstetrics & Gynaecology 14, Paediatrics 14, Ophthalmology 6, ENT 6 (adds up to 140).
  - CSE stations: Medicine 7, Surgery 7, O&G 2, Paediatrics 2, Eye 1, ENT 1.
- Press release "NRE is a standard examination" (https://pmdc.pk/Documents/press/NRE%20IS%20A%20STANDARD%20EXAMINATION.pdf),
  15 January 2026: NUMS (National University of Medical Sciences, Rawalpindi) has conducted the NRE
  "for the past two years" and is responsible for paper setting, conduct, evaluation and results of
  Step-I; "conducted in two steps, at least twice a year"; "on the curriculum at par with the MBBS/BDS
  in Pakistan"; "The passing criterion for the NRE is 60 percent, with no negative marking."
- Press release 1 June 2025 (https://pmdc.pk/Documents/press/PMDC%20Conducts%20National%20Registration%20Examination%20NRE-1%202025%20Across%20Major%20Cities.pdf):
  NRE-I held under section 18 of the PM&DC Act 2022 in Karachi, Islamabad, Lahore and Peshawar;
  "The theory examination began at 9:30 AM and lasted 03 hours and 30 minutes"; candidates must pass
  both components separately with at least 60%.
- Press release 13 December 2025 (https://pmdc.pk/Documents/press/PRESS-RELEASE-NRE-STEP-I-FOR-FOREIGN-MEDICAL-AND-DENTAL-GRADUATES-1412--2025.pdf):
  NRE Step I on 14 December 2025 through NUMS at four venues; 7,076 registered (6,989 medical, 87
  dental); no foreign graduate gets provisional or permanent registration to practise independently
  until they pass.
- Press release 6 August 2023 (https://pmdc.pk/Documents/press/NRE%20%20%206th%20August%2023.pdf):
  Step 1 is "a theoretical MCQ based basic & clinical sciences evaluation exam"; Step 2 is the
  clinical evaluation for a permanent licence.
- Public notice No. Exam-97 of 9 September 2026 (pmdc.pk/Documents/Others/Public Notice Regarding
  Extension of NRE Registration Portal .pdf): NRE Step-I on 11 October 2026 through NUMS.
- Public notice of 5 October 2026 (pmdc.pk/Documents/Others/Public Notice Regarding Re-Scheduled of
  NRE Step-I Exam.pdf): Step-I re-scheduled to Sunday 8 November 2026.

## Not confirmed (left out or labelled)
- Delivery mode of the current Step I (the Act and the 2024 standards say computer based; the press
  releases do not say whether recent sittings were on computer or paper). The landing says MCQs only.
- Question count of the most recent sittings: the 2024 standards say 200 MCQs; no later document
  changes it, but no 2025-2026 paper notice was read. The 3 h 30 min length is from the June 2025
  press release only.
- Single-best-answer format and the number of options per MCQ: not stated in the documents read.
  Tigertest's items have four options.
- Pass rates reported in the press (about 20% in one sitting) are not used.
- Search budget ran out before checking third-party guides; none are used.

## Blueprint used
Four 50-question mock tests, a quarter of the real 200, keeping the official 30/70 split:
Applied Anatomy & Physiology 5, Pathology & Biochemistry 5, Clinical Pharmacology 2, Community &
Forensic Medicine 3 (basic 15), Medicine & Allied 13, Surgery & Allied 11, Obstetrics & Gynaecology
4, Paediatrics 4, Eye & ENT 3 (clinical 35). The grouping is Tigertest's; Surgery is one short of a
straight quarter of 48 so the clinical side totals 35.

## Fact check of 2026-11-05 (web search budget was exhausted; direct fetches only)
Confirmed and kept (read 2026-11-05):
- PPC s. 337(3) shajjah-i-khafifah, -mudihah, -hashimah, -munaqqilah (CFM-011): Pakistan Penal Code
  (Act XLV of 1860), https://www.pakistani.org/pakistan/legislation/1860/actXLVof1860.html
- XDR typhoid began in Hyderabad district, Sindh, in November 2016; azithromycin the remaining
  reliable oral option (MED typhoid item): WHO Disease Outbreak News 27 December 2018,
  https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/27-december-2018-typhoid-pakistan-en ;
  carbapenem for complicated illness: CDC typhoid clinical guidance,
  https://www.cdc.gov/typhoid-fever/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html
- Sofosbuvir with daclatasvir is "the most widely used and low-cost pangenotypic DAA regimen", DAAs
  cure over 95%, 12 to 24 weeks (MED-015, stem reworded off "national programme"): WHO hepatitis C
  fact sheet, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hepatitis-c
- Rapid diagnostic tests (molecular assays) as the initial TB test (MED-014, stem reworded to WHO):
  WHO tuberculosis fact sheet, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/tuberculosis
- CCHF via contact with animal blood during and after slaughter; ribavirin given off label with
  unproven benefit (explanation softened): https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/crimean-congo-haemorrhagic-fever
- Brucellosis doxycycline 45 days with streptomycin, or with rifampicin: WHO brucellosis fact sheet,
  https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/brucellosis
- Cutaneous leishmaniasis by female phlebotomine sandflies, ulcers on exposed skin, lifelong scars:
  https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/leishmaniasis (antimonial treatment not on that
  page, so removed from the explanation)
- Vivax: chloroquine where still sensitive, primaquine added to prevent relapse:
  https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/malaria (14-day duration not on that page; kept as
  "usually 14 days", standard WHO guideline regimen)
- Chlorine needs at least 30 minutes contact; residual 0.2 to 0.5 mg/L at the collection point:
  WHO Technical Notes on WASH in Emergencies, note 11,
  https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/wash-documents/who-tn-11-measuring-chlorine-levels-in-water-supplies.pdf
  (CFM-006 rewritten to test the contact time; the GDWQ "0.5 mg/L after 30 min" figure was not read)
- No specific antidote for aluminium phosphide: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_phosphide_poisoning
  (garlic odour not confirmed, removed from CFM-012)
- pmdc.pk home page (read 2026-11-05): the latest NRE notice is still the 5 October 2026 re-schedule
  (Step-I 8 November 2026).
