# Sources for `ppsc` (Punjab Public Service Commission general ability and GK screening test)

All 200 questions are original. Prep sites selling "PPSC MCQs" and "past papers" appeared in search
results; none were opened for their questions and nothing was copied. Checked 2026-11-05.

## The commission and its tests

- ppsc.gop.pk (home page and several syllabus PDFs under /Syllabus/) returned HTTP 503 or reset the
  connection on 2026-11-05 from this environment, by direct download and through WebFetch; the
  Wayback Machine could not be reached either. Nothing below is taken from the commission's own
  pages this session.
- Wikipedia, "Punjab Public Service Commission (Pakistan)", read 2026-11-05: oldest provincial
  public service commission in Pakistan, established April 1937, constituted under the Punjab Public
  Service Commission Ordinance 1978 and working under the Punjab Public Service Commission
  (Functions) Rules 1979, headquarters Lahore. (The article notes it relies on few sources.)
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Public_Service_Commission_(Pakistan)
- Search result snippets of PPSC syllabus PDFs (ppsc.gop.pk/Syllabus/Slb1G2024_240723152057.pdf,
  syllabus_23122916550.pdf, 4C2022.pdf and others, 2026-11-05; the PDFs themselves did not open):
  per-post syllabus notices describing a single MCQ paper of 100 marks in 90 minutes, a general
  ability part on general knowledge, Pakistan Studies, current affairs and Islamic Studies (Ethics
  for non-Muslim candidates), and a notice requiring 40 out of 100 in Paper-I to proceed to the next
  paper. Treated as guide-level (unconfirmed) because the PDFs could not be opened.
- mcqsdrive.com, "PPSC Syllabus 2026" (prep guide, read 2026-11-05): "100 marks, 100 MCQs, and 90
  minutes"; typical subject spread for general posts: general knowledge 20 to 25, Pakistan Studies
  15 to 20, current affairs 15 to 20, English 10 to 15, Islamiyat 10 to 12, mathematics 10 to 12,
  everyday science 8 to 10, computer science for relevant posts; check each advertisement.
  https://mcqsdrive.com/blog/ppsc-syllabus-2026/
- Other prep guides found by search (testpointpk, gotest, notesmcqs, skillzdunya; 2026-11-05) agree
  on 100 MCQs in 90 minutes (one older guide says 60 minutes) and disagree on negative marking
  (some say 0.25 per wrong answer, others none); one splits a Monitor paper 50% general ability and
  50% professional, another an Intelligence Officer paper 80% general ability and 20% computer.
  Conclusion: format varies by post; no fixed split, negative marking rule or pass mark can be
  confirmed for every sitting.

## What Tigertest models

- Four 50-question tests at Tigertest's own 70% target (35 of 50), labelled as a target on the
  landing, in the config comment and in examFacts. Weights are Tigertest's own: English 13, maths
  7, reasoning 5, Pakistan Studies 7, Constitution 6, geography 6, Islamiat 3, computers 3 per test.
- Left out: current affairs (date within months), everyday science (pakarmy/pkpaf lane in this
  batch), Urdu, the post's own subject, Punjab districts and policing (punjabpolice lane).

## Fact check (2026-11-05)

Second pass. ppsc.gop.pk and punjabcode.punjab.gov.pk still returned 503 or reset the connection;
no primary PPSC document could be read, so the format stays guide-level and is labelled so.

- Format: mcqsdrive.com (above, re-read 2026-11-05) states "100 marks, 100 MCQs, and 90 minutes" and
  says each advertisement sets the syllabus; it says nothing on negative marking, pass marks or the
  interview. Kept as "guides describe" on the landing and in examFacts.
- 40 out of 100 in Paper-I: seen only in search snippets of PDFs that would not open. Not
  confirmable, so removed from the landing FAQ and WIRING notes (the FAQ now says only that the
  qualifying mark and negative marking vary by post).
- Interview after the written test: not confirmed from a PPSC document this session; the landing
  and examFacts keep it hedged ("usually", "as the advertisement sets out").
- Founding April 1937, Lahore, PPSC Ordinance 1978 and Functions Rules 1979: Wikipedia only (above).
