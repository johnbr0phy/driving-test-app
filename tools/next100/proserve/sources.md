# Sources for `proserve` (AGLC ProServe Liquor Staff Training, Alberta)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from AGLC's own pages and handbooks, the
Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act and Alberta and Canadian government health and traffic pages.
No ProServe course content, exam item, sample question, commercial prep test or study-site item
was used (the ProServe course and its exam sit behind a paid AGLC training account). Checked
2026-11-19.

## The program (AGLC, official)
- AGLC, "ProServe Liquor Staff Training", https://aglc.ca/training/proserve-liquor-staff-training,
  fetched 2026-11-19. Gave: ProServe is Alberta's responsible liquor service and sales program;
  the course topics (duty of care, legal responsibilities when serving or selling liquor, how
  alcohol affects the body and behaviour, recognising apparent intoxication, teamwork, refusing
  minors and apparently intoxicated people, illegal activities on licensed premises); its aims
  (curb underage drinking, reduce over-consumption, impaired driving and violence); who must be
  certified (Class A, B, D, E, F and Duty-Free staff from owners who manage to bartenders, servers,
  greeters, hosts, retailers, delivery drivers, limousine and bus drivers and attendants,
  large-format bike operators, security staff; SEL Public Resale roles; liquor agency sampling
  staff); certification within 30 days of the employment start date; the exam needs 80% to pass,
  five attempts come with each registration, English only; the certificate expires five years
  from the completion date and is renewed by retaking the course and exam; proof is a printed
  certificate or a clear image on a phone, shown to an AGLC Inspector on request.
- AGLC, "AGLC Training Program Requirements", https://aglc.ca/training/program-requirements,
  fetched 2026-11-19: who needs ProServe, ProTect, SellSafe, Reel Facts and the other SMART
  programs (used for the program lines only).

  PDF https://aglc.ca/sites/aglc.ca/files/2026-09/26-09-10%20LH%20Handbook.pdf (169 pages,

  1.2 definitions (minor under 18; liquor over 1% alcohol by volume; licensed premises includes

  5.1 hours (6 a.m. to 2 a.m., one hour to finish drinks, cleared by 3 a.m., daylight saving rule,

  no drinking in back rooms; staff do not drink on duty); 5.4 food service; 5.5 minors (under

## Alcohol, health and impaired driving (government, primary)
- Alberta Health Services, "Alcohol and the Body" (Alcohol and Health Series, June 2022),
  https://albertahealthservices.ca/assets/info/amh/if-amh-alcohol-and-the-human-body.pdf,
  fetched 2026-11-19: about 20% absorbed through the stomach and 75 to 80% through the small
  intestine; food (fat especially) slows absorption; BAC rises with volume, strength and speed;
  lighter, higher body fat and older drinkers reach higher BAC; females have less alcohol
  dehydrogenase and less body water; the liver does most of the work at a fixed rate per hour;
  acetaldehyde; alcohol is a depressant; slurred speech, staggering, slowed reaction time;
  blood vessels dilate and body heat is lost; dehydration; some leaves in the breath; methanol
  and non-beverage alcohols.
- Health Canada, "Low-risk alcohol drinking guidelines",
  https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/substance-use/alcohol/low-risk-alcohol-drinking-guidelines.html,
  fetched 2026-11-19: a standard drink is 17.05 mL or 13.45 g of pure alcohol; 341 mL beer or
  cider at 5%, 142 mL wine at 12%, 43 mL spirits at 40%. The page's daily and weekly limits are the
  2011 guidelines that CCSA's 2023 guidance replaced, so no limit is asked.
- Health Canada, "About alcohol", https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/substance-use/alcohol/about.html,
  fetched 2026-11-19: alcohol circulates until the liver eliminates it; about 10% leaves through
  urine, sweat and breathing; food with protein and fat slows it; effects depend on amount, sex,
  age, body size and speed of drinking.
- Government of Alberta, "Impaired driving penalties", https://www.alberta.ca/impaired-driving-penalties,
  fetched 2026-11-19: IRS WARN at 0.05 to 0.079, IRS FAIL at 0.08 or more, zero tolerance for
  novice (Class 7 and Class 5-GDL) and commercial drivers.

## Not confirmed and how the copy words it
- The ProServe exam's question count and time limit are not published; the landing gives only
  the 80% pass mark, five attempts per registration and English, as AGLC states them, and says
  AGLC sets the rest.
- Fees, fines, licence suspension lengths and penalty amounts are left out of every question.
- Commercial host liability is common law, not in the handbook beyond "liquor service carries a
  risk of potential liability"; questions ask only about the duty of care AGLC names and the
  practical steps the handbook gives, never about court cases or damages.
