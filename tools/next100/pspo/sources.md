# PSPO I (`pspo`) sources

Every question in this bank is original. Nothing is taken or adapted from Scrum.org's Open
Assessments (Product Owner Open, Scrum Open), official sample questions, prep books or any other
question bank. Items were written fresh from the sources below, and checked against the `psm`
bank (`data/v2/psm/questions.json`) so no fact-and-answer pair is repeated.

## Assessment format (owner's page)

- **Professional Scrum Product Owner I (PSPO I) assessment**, Scrum.org,
  https://www.scrum.org/assessments/professional-scrum-product-owner-i-certification
  (fetched 2026-11-19). 80 questions, 60 minutes, 85% passing score; multiple choice, multiple
  answer and true/false; English and Japanese (Simplified Chinese through scrum.org.cn); USD 200
  per attempt, one attempt per password, passwords do not expire. Focus areas listed:
  Understanding and Applying the Scrum Framework (Empiricism, Scrum Team, Events, Artifacts,
  Done), Developing People and Teams (Self-Managing Teams), Managing Products with Agility
  (Forecasting & Release Planning, Product Vision, Product Value, Product Backlog Management,
  Business Strategy, Stakeholders & Customers). No per-area weighting is published, so the
  per-test split is Tigertest's.

## Focus area definitions (owner's pages)

- **Professional Scrum Competencies**, Scrum.org, https://www.scrum.org/professional-scrumtm-competencies
  (fetched 2026-11-19): the competency list and the line "A Product has a clear goal,
  stakeholders, and users. The value of the product is measurable."
- **Managing Products with Agility**, Scrum.org,
  https://www.scrum.org/professional-scrum-competencies/managing-products-with-agility
  (fetched 2026-11-19): one-paragraph definitions of Forecasting & Release Planning (small,
  incremental and frequent releases rather than big bang launches), Product Vision (the purpose of
  the product, the value it should deliver and to whom), Product Value, Product Backlog
  Management (adjusting and ordering items), Business Strategy (informed by mission and vision,
  informs product visions, inspected and adapted from feedback on Increments) and Stakeholders &
  Customers.

## Content sources

- **The Scrum Guide, November 2020**, Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland,
  https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html (fetched 2026-11-19). CC BY-SA 4.0, still the current
  edition. Product Owner accountability (one person, may delegate but remains accountable,
  organization must respect decisions, others change the backlog by convincing the Product
  Owner), product definition, Product Backlog and Product Goal, refinement (description, order,
  size; Developers size), Sprint (cancellation by the Product Owner only, forecasting practices),
  Sprint Planning (why, what, how), Sprint Review (working session, not a release gate), Increment
  and Definition of Done, multiple teams on one product share one Product Goal, Product Backlog
  and Product Owner. Paraphrased; no passage quoted at length.
- **Evidence-Based Management Guide, 2024 edition**, Scrum.org,
  https://www.scrum.org/resources/online-evidence-based-management-guide (fetched 2026-11-19),
  edition date from https://www.scrum.org/resources/evidence-based-management-guide (released
  7 May 2024). CC BY-SA 4.0. Four Key Value Areas (Current Value, Unrealized Value, Time-to-Market,
  Ability to Innovate) and their example measures, Strategic, Intermediate and Immediate Tactical
  Goals, and the empirical loop (set a goal, form a hypothesis, run an experiment, inspect,
  adapt). Used for the product value and business strategy items.

## Left out

- The fee, attempt rules and other assessment-admin facts (on the landing only, never in a question).
- Named outside techniques that Scrum does not prescribe (user stories, story points, MoSCoW,
  Kano) are asked only as optional practices, never as Scrum rules.
- Scoring here is all or nothing per item; Scrum.org does not document partial credit for
  multiple answer items, so no claim is made about the real assessment.
