# Sources: Serving It Right (British Columbia responsible beverage service), `servingitright`

All 200 questions in the bank are original, written by Tigertest from the rules and facts below.
No Serving It Right course material, self-study manual, final exam item, quiz or sample question,
and no prep-site or app question was read or used, even reworded: the course manual is the
Province's copyrighted work and was not opened, and only its public topic list was used to know
what the course covers. No public official question catalogue exists for this exam (questions are
randomly generated from a private pool), so there was nothing to run a similarity check against.
Tigertest is not affiliated with Responsible Service BC, the Liquor and Cannabis Regulation Branch
or the Province of British Columbia, and no stem names the program. All sources fetched 2026-11-23.

## Official: the program and its exam (Responsible Service BC, Province of British Columbia)

- Serving It Right FAQs: https://www.responsibleservicebc.gov.bc.ca/serving-it-right-faqs
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. Licensees, managers and retail liquor store workers need Serving It Right;
    servers moving from another province with a current certificate are exempt (employers may
    still ask for BC's). The course is free; the exam is paid and a purchase includes 3 attempts.
    Exam: multiple choice, 35 randomly generated questions, "The pass rate is 80%, or 28 questions
    answered correctly", no time limit. About 4 hours of study. Certificates valid 5 years from
    the final exam; certificates issued before September 15, 2015 expired September 15, 2020;
    recertification is not legally required but employers may ask for it. English only. No
    minimum age for the certificate. Minors 16 or older may serve liquor in food primary
    establishments but may not open, pour or mix it and must be supervised by adult staff; minors
    cannot work in liquor primary establishments without special permission and then cannot sell
    or serve. Certificates are printed from "My Certifications"; none are mailed. Landing and
    `examFacts` only for the format; the minors-at-work facts also back questions.
- Serving It Right Course: https://www.responsibleservicebc.gov.bc.ca/serving-it-right-course
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. Topics: signs of intoxication, legal liability, reducing alcohol
    over-consumption, reducing impaired driving, reducing the risk of violence, preventing sales
    to minors, duty of care on and off premises, creating and enforcing responsible beverage
    service policies. Learning outcomes: duty of care, legal responsibilities, how alcohol affects
    the body and behaviour, recognizing over-intoxication, handling minors, stopping service.
    Exam: 35 questions, 80% (28 correct), untimed, can log out and resume, certificate number on
    passing. The blueprint follows these topics; the split is Tigertest's (no weights published).
- What is Serving It Right: https://www.responsibleservicebc.gov.bc.ca/what-is-serving-it-right
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. BC's mandatory self-study course on the legal responsibilities of
    licensees, managers and servers; preventing problems from over-service; over-consumption can
    lower inhibitions and lead to sexualized violence, aggression and harassment. Related programs:
    Special Event Server and Selling It Right.
- Special Event Server FAQs: https://www.responsibleservicebc.gov.bc.ca/special-event-server-faqs
  - Search result read 2026-11-23: the Special Event Server exam is 25 randomly generated
    questions, 80% (20 correct). Landing only; not modelled.

## Primary law (BC Laws, King's Printer)

- Liquor Control and Licensing Act, SBC 2015, c 19:
  https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15019
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. s. 1 liquor is a product with more than 1% alcohol by volume; minor means
    under the age of majority. s. 42 inspections (the general manager may require employees and
    patrons to produce identification). s. 43 licensees must not obstruct an inspection. s. 51
    monetary penalties and licence suspensions on a prescribed schedule. s. 57 offences. s. 58
    officers and directors. s. 60 a licensee must not operate, and a person must not sell or serve
    liquor in a service area or liquor store, without the prescribed training. s. 61 no sale or
    service to an intoxicated person or a person showing signs of intoxication; must not permit a
    person to become intoxicated or an intoxicated person to enter or remain in a service area;
    no violent, riotous or disorderly conduct or unlawful activity; no person known to have a
    knife or weapon; may ask a person to leave or forbid entry; a person asked to leave must not
    remain or re-enter within 24 hours; self-protection is not a lawful excuse for a weapon. s. 62
    tied houses and inducements. s. 63 public education material. s. 64 advertising only as the
    regulations allow. s. 73 no drinking or open liquor outside a residence, private place, service
    area and other listed places; local governments may designate public places with set hours.
    s. 74 an intoxicated person must not be or remain in a public place; arrest without warrant.
    s. 75 no one may sell, serve or give liquor to an intoxicated person or a person showing signs
    of intoxication. s. 76 liquor in a motor vehicle (including off-road vehicles, moving or not)
    only sealed or not readily accessible. s. 77 no supplying liquor to a minor, no possessing
    liquor to supply one, no letting a minor drink in a place under your control; defence of
    asking for and examining prescribed identification. s. 78 minors must not buy, drink or
    possess liquor, enter or be in a liquor store or service area unless allowed, or present
    altered, borrowed or forged ID; exception for minors working with police or the general
    manager on compliance checks. s. 79 no minors in an establishment, service area or liquor
    store where the licence does not allow it; no employing minors except as allowed; the same ID
    defence. Fines are not used.
- Liquor Control and Licensing Regulation, BC Reg 241/2016:
  https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/241_2016
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. s. 1 adult means 19 or older. s. 9.1 and 16.1 liquor primary and food
    primary may sell unopened liquor for removal only with a meal. s. 10 liquor primary: not aimed
    at minors; food and non-alcoholic drinks at reasonable prices or free. ss. 16 to 19 food
    primary: the primary purpose is food, a varied menu from an on-site kitchen, the factors the
    general manager weighs. ss. 10 and 18 hours of liquor service from 9 a.m. to 4 a.m. at the
    latest. s. 59 and others retail store hours 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. s. 15(4) off-premises sales under
    a liquor primary endorsement not after 11 p.m. s. 72 person capacity. s. 80 record keeping. s. 82
    no unlimited or unspecified quantities for one price; no sales strategy likely to promote or
    encourage intoxication. s. 83 a list of drink quantities and prices on request. s. 84 minimum
    prices (amounts not used). s. 85 hotel guests may carry a drink to their room by a direct route,
    never when they appear intoxicated or have been violent or disorderly. s. 86 one drink at
    check-in. s. 88 golf course drinks by a direct route. s. 89 patrons cleared within half an hour
    after service ends. s. 90 liquor taken from patrons within half an hour (sealed unfinished wine
    excepted). s. 91 no drinking more than half an hour after service ends. s. 141 patrons drink
    only liquor sold or served in that service area; a sealed, unfinished bottle of wine may leave.
    s. 142 employees must not drink while working. s. 143 no one may sell, serve or supervise
    service without the training or recertification. s. 144 no diluting or adulterating, no
    refilling bottles. s. 158 prescribed identification: two pieces, one primary (passport, photo
    driver's licence with date of birth, or government photo ID card with date of birth) and one
    more with the person's name and a signature or a photo. ss. 161 to 166 employment of minors:
    direct and continual adult supervision; food primary minors 16 and over may sell or serve but
    not mix drinks or open containers; stadium the same and no hawking; liquor primary non-liquor
    jobs only. s. 184 SIR and the Special Event Server program; the SIR certificate expires 5 years
    after completion. s. 194 delivery services: deliverer must be an adult, gives a receipt, delivers
    only where liquor may be lawfully possessed, generally not between 11:30 p.m. and 9 a.m.
    Schedule 1 fees and Schedule 2 penalty amounts are not used.
- Age of Majority Act, RSBC 1996, c 7:
  https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96007_01
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. s. 1: the age of majority is 19.

## Official: alcohol and health

- HealthLinkBC (BC Ministry of Health), Alcohol use:
  https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/mental-health-substance-use/substance-use/alcohol-use
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. A standard drink is 341 mL (12 oz) of beer, cider or cooler at 5%, 142 mL
    (5 oz) of wine at 12%, 43 mL (1.5 oz) of spirits at 40%. Canada's guidance: 2 or fewer drinks a
    week likely avoids consequences; more than 2 per occasion raises injury and violence risk; 3 to
    6 a week raises the risk of several cancers; 7 or more significantly raises heart disease and
    stroke risk. BAC rises with amount and speed; food slows absorption; smaller bodies, women and
    older adults reach higher BAC; the liver works at about 0.016% BAC an hour for men and slower
    for women; only time brings BAC to zero; coffee, cold showers and exercise do not. 0.08% is 80
    mg per 100 mL of blood; the page gives 0.05% as the BC driving limit. No alcohol in pregnancy
    (FASD) and the safest choice while breastfeeding is none.
- HealthLinkBC, Intoxication: https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/healthwise/intoxication
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. Signs: slurred speech, clumsiness, problems with memory and attention,
    passing out; belligerence or aggression including sexual aggression, becoming unusually quiet,
    impaired judgment.
- HealthLinkBC, Alcohol poisoning: https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/healthwise/alcohol-poisoning
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. Vomiting (choking risk when unconscious), confusion, passing out or
    unable to wake, seizures, fewer than 8 breaths a minute, 10 seconds or more between breaths,
    low temperature, bluish or pale skin; call 9-1-1; coffee, cold showers, walking or sleeping it
    off do not help.
- Criminal Code, s. 320.14(1)(b): https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-46/section-320.14.html
  - Fetched 2026-11-23. 80 mg of alcohol in 100 mL of blood within two hours of operating a
    conveyance.

## Could not be read

- www2.gov.bc.ca (the LCRB's own pages, including the Food Primary and Liquor Primary Terms and
  Conditions guides and "Preventing contraventions") timed out or hung up on every request from
  this build (WebFetch and curl). No question rests on those guides: rules on minors accompanied by
  a parent in a liquor primary, signage, happy hour wording and licence-specific terms are left
  out. Everything legal comes from the Act and Regulation on bclaws.gov.bc.ca.
- Older sources name go2HR as the program's administrator; the current site is run on a
  gov.bc.ca domain and names no other body, so the copy says Responsible Service BC and the LCRB.
- The Motor Vehicle Act's roadside prohibition sections were not read, so no question asks the
  administrative roadside ranges or prohibition lengths; driving items use the Criminal Code 80 mg
  limit and HealthLinkBC's statement only.
