# SHSAT (`shsat`): sources

Every question in this bank is original, written for Tigertest from the New York State Next
Generation Learning Standards (2017) for English Language Arts and Mathematics. No item, passage,
sample question or explanation from the NYC Guide to the SHSAT, the NYC SHSAT Portal practice
tests, released SHSAT forms or any commercial SHSAT prep book or site was used or reworded. Every
reading and revising passage is original.

## Format and outline (NYC Public Schools, the test owner)

- NYC Guide to the Specialized High Schools Admissions Test (SHSAT) for 2027 Admissions.
  https://pwsblobprd.schools.nyc/prd-pws/docs/default-source/default-document-library/nyc-guide-to-the-shsat-for-2027-admissions3fd494198373477d9445875f399cd8ab.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-14, 31 pages). Gave: "Beginning in Fall 2026, the SHSAT will be a
  computer-adaptive test (CAT)"; three hours of testing time (180 minutes; 360 with extended
  time); 50 items in the ELA section and 50 in the Math section; the student chooses which
  subject to start with and divides the time; a subject cannot be revisited once submitted; every
  item must be answered before moving on; ELA passage sets (Reading Comprehension and
  Revising/Editing Part A) can be revisited until the set is submitted, stand-alone items and
  every Math item cannot. ELA parts: Reading Comprehension (literary and informational texts,
  including poetry, fiction, memoir, journalism, argument and accounts of science and history),
  Revising/Editing Part A (a text with numbered sentences; errors such as missing or unnecessary
  supporting details, missing or inappropriate transitions, a missing or unclear introductory or
  concluding statement, confusing organization, language misuse) and Part B (a sentence, list of
  sentences or paragraph; best correction for an error, identifying a sentence with an error,
  combining sentences or revising part of a sentence; based on the Grade 7 Language standards).
  Math: "Math Fill in the Blanks and Math Multiple Choice", word problems and computation from
  the NYS Next Generation Mathematics Learning Standards; Grade 8 forms use standards through
  Grade 7, Grade 9 forms through Grade 8; formulas are not given; no calculators; scrap paper is
  provided; answers to some items are entered with an equation editor.
- NYC Public Schools, Specialized High Schools Admissions Test page.
  https://www.schools.nyc.gov/learning/testing/specialized-high-school-admissions-test/
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Gave: the SHSAT is the sole admissions factor for eight Specialized High
  Schools (LaGuardia uses an audition); current NYC 8th graders and first-time 9th graders may
  take it; two sections of 50 questions each in a standard 180 minutes; math content areas are
  ratios and proportional relationships, the number system and expressions, equations,
  inequalities, statistics and probability, and geometry; item types are multiple choice,
  fill-in-the-blank and technology-enhanced items; adaptive from fall 2026 with every student
  answering the same number of questions; each subject includes 10 unscored field-test items;
  scaled subtest scores are summed into a composite used for rank ordering; DOE computers only.

## Content standards (primary text the items are written from)

- New York State Next Generation Mathematics Learning Standards (2017).
  https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/nys-next-generation-mathematics-p-12-standards.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Grade 6 and Grade 7 domains used: NY-7.RP (unit rates, proportional
  relationships, multistep ratio and percent problems including tax, markups, markdowns,
  commission, percent increase and decrease, simple interest), NY-7.NS (operations with rational
  numbers, repeating decimals), NY-7.EE (equivalent expressions, px + q = r and p(x + q) = r,
  inequalities px + q > r), NY-7.G (scale drawings, circles, angle relationships, area of
  triangles and trapezoids, surface area and volume of right prisms), NY-7.SP (box plots,
  interquartile range, measures of center and variability, compound probability), plus the
  Grade 6 standards they build on (ratios, factors and multiples, exponents, mean and median).
- New York State Next Generation English Language Arts Learning Standards (2017).
  https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/nys-next-generation-ela-standards.pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Grade 7 Reading standards 7R1 to 7R9 (evidence and inference, theme and
  central idea, development of ideas, word meaning and tone, structure, point of view, argument),
  Writing standards on organization and transitions, Language standard 7L3a (precise, concise
  language) and the Grades 6-8 Core Conventions and Core Punctuation skills in Appendix A
  (pronoun case and shifts, unclear antecedents, misplaced and dangling modifiers, sentence
  types, verbals, active and passive voice, verb shifts, commas, parentheses, dashes, hyphens).
- NYSED pages listing those documents:
  https://www.nysed.gov/curriculum-instruction/new-york-state-next-generation-mathematics-learning-standards
  and https://www.nysed.gov/curriculum-instruction/new-york-state-next-generation-english-language-arts-learning-standards
  (fetched 2026-11-14).

## Could not load or confirm

- The schools.nyc.gov "fall 2026 SHSAT guide" and Specialized High Schools enrollment pages
  returned 404 on fetch; the 2027 admissions guide PDF and the SHSAT test page above cover the
  format. The guide's task statement mentioned 57 + 57 questions; that was the paper format
  before fall 2026. The current guide says 50 + 50, which this bank follows.
- Neither source gives the split of ELA items between reading and revising/editing, or of Math
  items by content area, or how many Math items are fill-in-the-blank. The per-test counts here
  (ELA: 10 Part A, 10 Part B, 30 reading; Math: ratios 10, number and expressions 11, equations
  and inequalities 11, geometry 10, statistics and probability 8, with about 5 typed answers) are
  Tigertest's own and the landing says so.
- How raw answers become scaled scores, and each school's cutoff score, are not published as a
  fixed table; the copy never predicts a score or an offer.

## Deliberately left out

- Computer adaptivity: each practice test is one fixed form of 50 + 50 mixed difficulty.
- The 10 unscored field-test items per subject: every practice item counts.
- Grade 9 forms (standards through Grade 8): the bank follows the Grade 8 forms (through Grade 7).
- Technology-enhanced drag-and-drop and equation-editor items other than typed numbers.
- Test-day rules, registration, dates and accommodations: on the landing only, never in items.
