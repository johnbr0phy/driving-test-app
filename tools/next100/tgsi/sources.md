# Telangana Police SI Preliminary Written Test (`tgsi`) sources

Every question in this bank is original, written from the syllabus and the official documents below. No previous
TSLPRB paper, official key, coaching-institute question, question bank or practice test was used, not even reworded.
All pages fetched 2026-11-13.

## Who sets the exam, the format and the syllabus (official)

- **Telangana State Level Police Recruitment Board (TSLPRB)**, DGP Office Complex, Lakdi-ka-pul, Hyderabad.
  Site: https://www.tgprb.in (www.tslprb.in redirects there; fetched 2026-11-13). The board still signs itself
  TSLPRB in its 2026 documents; searchers also call it TGLPRB / TGPRB.
- **Notification for SCT SI (Civil) and equivalent posts, Rc. No. 225 / Rect. / Rect.1 / 2026 dated 29 July 2026**,
  https://doc.tgprb.in/SI+(Civil+et+al)+2026+Notification+dated+29-07-2026.pdf (57 pages, fetched 2026-11-13).
  What it gave:
  - Para 1: 275 vacancies (SI Civil 148, RSI AR 14, RSI SAR CPL 3, RSI TGSP 12, SPF SI 44, Station Fire Officer 39,
    Deputy Jailor 14 + 1).
  - Para 15(C): a degree is required.
  - Para 16(A), Preliminary Written Test: one paper of three hours, "Arithmetic and Test of Reasoning / Mental
    Ability (100 Questions) and General Studies (100 Questions)", 200 marks, objective, OMR sheet with a blue or
    black ball-point pen. Qualifying marks 30% for OCs including EWS, 25% for BCs, 20% for SCs / STs /
    Ex-Servicemen. Full marks for darkening only the one right bubble, zero for no bubble, and "in all other
    cases, 20% of full marks shall be awarded as a negative mark". Questions set in English-Telugu and
    English-Urdu; the English version is final where a translation is unclear. Deleted questions are compensated
    pro rata.
  - Para 16(B) and (C): PMT / PET (1600 m in 7 min 15 s for men, 800 m in 5 min 20 s for women), then a Final
    Written Examination of four 3-hour papers (English and Telugu/Urdu, qualifying, with 25% negative marking in
    their objective parts; Arithmetic and Reasoning 200 questions; General Studies 200 questions; 40% / 35% / 30%
    to qualify). Landing copy only; not modelled.
  - Annexure II, "Syllabus for Preliminary Written Test (200 questions - 200 marks)": Arithmetic (number system,
    simple interest, compound interest, ratio and proportion, average, percentage, profit and loss, time and work,
    work and wages, time and distance, clocks and calendars, partnership, mensuration); Test of Reasoning (verbal
    and non-verbal: analogies, similarities and differences, spatial visualization, spatial orientation, problem
    solving, analysis, judgment, decision making, visual memory); General Studies: 1. General Science and the
    environment, 2. Current events of national and international importance, 3. History of India (including the
    Indian National Movement), 4. Principles of Geography and Geography of India, 5. Indian Polity and Economy
    (political system, rural development, planning and economic reforms), 6. Telangana Movement and State
    Formation: the idea of Telangana (1948-1970), mobilisation phase (1971-1990), towards formation of Telangana
    State (1991-2014). The board prescribes no textbook.

- **Press note dated 8 September 2026**, https://www.tgprb.in/SI_PC_2026/Press%20Note%20dated%2008-09-2026.pdf

## Telangana Movement and State Formation (primary sources)

- **Report of the Committee for Consultations on the Situation in Andhra Pradesh (Justice B. N. Srikrishna),
  Ministry of Home Affairs, December 2010**,
  https://www.mha.gov.in/files/commissions_committees_documents/CCSAP_REPORT_060111.pdf (listed on
  https://www.mha.gov.in/ccsap-justice-retd-b-n-srikrishna-reports, fetched 2026-11-13). Chapter 1 (history of
  the region, 1948-2010) gave: Police Action 13-18 September 1948; M. A. Vellodi made Chief Minister January 1950;
  Burgula Ramakrishna Rao's first popular ministry after the 1952 elections; first Mulki agitation 1952-53; the
  States Reorganisation Commission (Justice S. Fazal Ali, H. N. Kunzru, K. M. Panikkar; appointed 29 December 1953)
  and its recommendation on the residuary Hyderabad State (unite with Andhra after the elections due about 1961
  if two-thirds of its legislature agreed); the Gentlemen's Agreement of 20 February 1956 at Hyderabad House,
  New Delhi, eight signatories, 14 points including the 60:40 cabinet and a Telangana Regional Council; Andhra
  Pradesh formed 1 November 1956; Regional Committee 1958; Telangana Safeguards Day 10 July 1968; the 1969
  agitation sparked by a student's hunger strike in Khammam (January 1969), the Eight-Point Plan (11 April 1969),
  the Bhargava Committee (22 April 1969), the Telangana Praja Samithi; Presidential Order of 7 March 1970;
  P. V. Narasimha Rao Chief Minister September 1971 as the first Telanganite; the High Court's Mulki ruling of
  14 February 1972 and the Supreme Court's reversal of 3 October 1972; the Jai Andhra agitation; the Six-Point
  Formula (21 September 1973), the 32nd Amendment inserting Article 371D, Mulki Rules Repeal Act (31 December
  1973) and abolition of the Telangana Regional Committee from 1 January 1974; Presidential Order 1975
  (G.O. Ms. No. 674, 20 February 1975); G.O. Ms. No. 610 of 30 December 1985; the Girglani Commission
  (25 June 2001, report 30 September 2004, 126 findings); the TRS formed in 2001; K. Chandrasekhar Rao's fast from
  29 November 2009; the students' Joint Action Committee of Osmania and Kakatiya universities; the Union Home
  Minister's announcements of 9 and 23 December 2009; the Committee constituted 3 February 2010; its six options,
  with a united state and a statutory Telangana Regional Council the "most workable" and bifurcation with
  Hyderabad as Telangana's capital the "second best".
- **The Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014 (No. 6 of 2014)**, Gazette of India Extraordinary, 1 March 2014,
  copy hosted by the Tripura High Court: https://thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Acts/Andhra%20Pradesh%20Reorganisation%20Act,%202014..pdf
  (fetched 2026-11-13; India Code at https://www.indiacode.nic.in timed out and refused WebFetch). Gave: assent
  1 March 2014; s.3 (Telangana's ten districts); s.5 (Hyderabad common capital for a period not exceeding ten
  years, then capital of Telangana); s.12 (7 Rajya Sabha seats); s.14 (17 Lok Sabha seats to Telangana, 25 to
  Andhra Pradesh); s.15 (Assemblies of 175 and 119); s.22-23 (Telangana Legislative Council of 40); s.30-31 (High
  Court); s.84-85 (Apex Council; Godavari River Management Board headquartered in Telangana, Krishna board in
  Andhra Pradesh); s.90 (Polavaram a national project); s.97 (Article 371D now covers Andhra Pradesh or
  Telangana).
- **Government of Telangana, State Profile**, https://www.telangana.gov.in/about/state-profile/ (fetched
  2026-11-13): 29th State, formed 2 June 2014; part of Hyderabad State from 17 September 1948 to 1 November 1956;
  area 1,12,077 sq km; borders (Maharashtra and Chhattisgarh north, Karnataka west, Andhra Pradesh south and east);
  119 MLAs.
- **Government of Telangana, Districts**, https://www.telangana.gov.in/about/districts/ (fetched 2026-11-13): 33
  districts; Bhadradri Kothagudem the largest, Hyderabad the smallest.
- **Government of Telangana, Language & Culture**, https://www.telangana.gov.in/about/language-culture/ (fetched
  2026-11-13): Deccan plateau uplands; Satavahanas the earliest known rulers; the Kakatiyas (11th to 14th
  centuries, capital Warangal), then the Qutb Shahis and Asaf Jahis.
- **Government of Telangana, State Symbols**, https://www.telangana.gov.in/about/state-symbols/ (fetched
  2026-11-13): read for context; no symbol questions were written.

## General science, history and geography

- Static textbook facts at the level the syllabus sets ("a well educated person who has not made a special study
  of any scientific discipline"): vitamins, ozone, refraction, SI units, blood, vectors of disease, acids, the
  atmosphere; the national movement, ancient, medieval and modern India; latitudes, rivers, soils, rocks, borders.
  Each was written from settled school-level knowledge and checked by a second read; none is a figure that
  changes. The Nalgonda fluorosis item rests on the long-documented high fluoride in that district's groundwater.

## Modelling decisions

- Real paper: 200 questions in 3 hours. Modelled at half length at the same pace: 100 questions in 90 minutes,
  Part A (Arithmetic and Reasoning) 50 then Part B (General Studies) 50, one module. Two tests, Test 2 held out of
  the drills; 4 drill-only items per domain; 236 in all.
- Per test (Tigertest's split; the board publishes none within each part): arithmetic 13 + 12, reasoning 13 + 12,
  and 10 each for general science, history, geography, polity and economy, and the Telangana Movement.
- **Current events** (the syllabus's GS item 2) are not modelled: they change too fast to keep accurate. The
  landing and FAQ say so.
- **Non-verbal figure items and visual memory** need pictures and are not modelled; spatial orientation is
  written in text (dice, cubes, turns, mirror times, shadows).
- **Negative marking** (20% of the question's marks) is explained on the landing and in the score note, not
  applied: the app scores percent correct.
- **Language**: English only. The real paper is bilingual (English-Telugu, English-Urdu) with English final.
- No current office-holders, fees, salaries or cut-off marks in questions. Historic office-holders (P. V.
  Narasimha Rao in 1971, Burgula Ramakrishna Rao in 1952) are asked as history.
