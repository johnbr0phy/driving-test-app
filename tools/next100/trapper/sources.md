# Sources for `trapper` (Trapper education course test, AFWA North American Trapper Education)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the AFWA trapper education manual and
the AFWA Best Management Practices (BMP) documents listed below. No course quiz, unit review,
state exam, instructor test bank, past paper or commercial practice question was used or
reworded, and no training provider is named in a question. Checked 2026-11-19.

## Exam owner and course content (official)
- Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies, "Furbearer Management" page,
  https://www.fishwildlife.org/afwa-inspires/furbearer-management (fetched 2026-11-19). Gave: AFWA
  runs the North American Trapper Education Program and publishes the North American Trapper
  Education Manual (2005 and 2022 versions); trapping is regulated and trapper education is
  provided in all states; the BMP program evaluates traps with ISO standards; links to every
  document below.
- AFWA, "Trapper Education Manual: A Guide for Trappers in the United States (2022)",
  https://www.fishwildlife.org/download_file/view/4323/1213 (fetched 2026-11-19, all 18 chapters,
  the species pages and Appendix A read). Updated in 2022 to the trapper education standards AFWA
  developed with IHEA-USA. Every chapter is a content standard with learning objectives, and the
  bank follows them: introduction (values of furbearers, benefits of trapping, privilege not
  right, NTA and FTA), history (fur trade from the 1500s, Champlain at Quebec in 1608, Beaver Wars,
  steel traps not mass produced until after 1823, attitudes, animal welfare and animal rights, the
  seven principles of the North American Model, Migratory Bird Treaty of 1916), furbearer
  management (renewable resources, habitat, biological and cultural carrying capacity,
  compensatory and additive mortality, extirpation, three harvest requirements, three management
  issues, license and excise tax funding), regulations (brochures, change triggers, permission,
  nuisance rules, penalties, TIP lines), BMPs (AFWA coordinates, USDA funding, 70% injury and 60%
  efficiency thresholds, practicality and safety reviews), traps (kill-type and live-restraining,
  long-spring, coil-spring, guarded and enclosed footholds, offset, padded, double and laminated
  jaws, submersion depths of 24 inches for muskrat and 42 for beaver, colony and cage traps, cable
  devices and locks, stakes of 18 to 24 inches, cross-staking, shock springs, swivels, jaw spread,
  pan level, pan tension and pan throw, cleaning, rusting, dyeing and waxing, setting tongs from
  size 220), equipment, bait, lure and urine, selective techniques (pan tension of 1, 2 and 4
  pounds, dirt-hole spacing of 7 and 12 inches, trigger shapes), water sets, land sets (4-point
  bedding check, dirt-hole, flat, post and cubby sets, enclosed footholds, bucket and box sets,
  leaning poles), cable devices (stops, breakaways, baking soda boil, loop sizes and heights),
  safety (hypothermia, frostbite at 102 to 105 degrees F, 4 inches of clear ice, wading upstream,
  diseases, 1 cup of bleach per gallon, the rope release from a body-gripping trap, firearm use),
  running a trapline (early summer permission, scouting, daily checks at first light, dispatch,
  releasing non-targets), using furbearers (four ways to sell, grading, meat, glands, disposal),
  handling fur (40 degree rule, cased and open, fur in and fur out, tails, stretchers, drying at
  55 to 60 degrees F, freezing, boarding beaver) and responsible trapping.
- AFWA, "Best Management Practices for Trapping in the United States: Introduction" (updated
  2022), https://www.fishwildlife.org/download_file/view/3835/1213 (fetched 2026-11-19). Gave: the
  five criteria; injury scales from ISO 10990-5 (average score of 55 or less, 70% with no, mild or
  moderate injury); 60% efficiency; killing traps must cause irreversible unconsciousness in 70%
  of animals within 300 seconds and let a trapper release himself or herself without help;
  submersion systems must keep the animal from surfacing; testing by experienced trappers with
  technicians in regulated seasons with daily checks; two coyote BMPs (Eastern and Western);
  BMPs are voluntary and educational.
- AFWA, "BMP FAQ 2024", https://www.fishwildlife.org/download_file/view/5531/1213 (fetched
  2026-11-19). Gave: BMP traps meet ISO-based criteria; BMPs are implemented through outreach and
  education, not legislation; supported by The Wildlife Society and veterinary associations.
- AFWA, "Trapping BMPs: Did You Know?" fact sheet,
  https://www.fishwildlife.org/download_file/view/1522/1213 (fetched 2026-11-19). Gave: BMPs are
  recommendations, not laws, and each state agency decides how they are used.
- AFWA and USFWS, "How to Avoid Incidental Take of Lynx While Trapping or Hunting Bobcats and
  Other Furbearers", https://www.fishwildlife.org/download_file/view/881/1213 (fetched
  2026-11-19). Gave: lynx versus bobcat tail tips and ear tufts; avoiding lynx in bobcat, marten
  and fisher sets.
- AFWA, "How to Avoid Incidental Take of Wolverine During Regulated Trapping Activities" (revised
  December 2024), https://www.fishwildlife.org/download_file/view/5577/1213 (fetched 2026-11-19).
  Read for background; nothing in the bank depends on it.

## Format (state courses)
- North Carolina Wildlife Resources Commission, "Trapper Education",
  https://www.ncwildlife.gov/trapping/trapper-education (fetched 2026-11-19). An example of how a
  state delivers the AFWA course: the online North American Trapper Education course followed by
  a 4-hour field day, with the AFWA manual keeping content consistent across states.
- AFWA publishes no question count, time limit or pass mark: each state that requires trapper
  education runs its own course, field day and test. The 80% line on Tigertest is a readiness
  line, said so in the copy, and four 50-question tests are Tigertest's format.

## Left out on purpose
- Every state rule: seasons, legal trap types and sizes, setback distances, check intervals,
  tagging, license ages, nuisance permits, penalties, possession rules. The manual itself leaves
  these blanks for each state to fill in; the bank says "varies by state" where it touches them.
- BMP program counts that differ between AFWA documents (22 or 23 species, 600 or 700 trap
  types, 41 or 43 states) and money totals.
- Specific trap brand recommendations, and the photo-based trigger shape figures.
- Facts the `hunter` and `bowhunter` banks already ask (habitat elements, carrying capacity
  definition, limiting factor, public trust ownership, funding by license fees and excise tax,
  Pittman-Robertson, recognising hypothermia, trip plans, map and compass backup).
