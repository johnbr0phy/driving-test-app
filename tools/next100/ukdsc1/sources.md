# ukdsc1 sources (Deer Stalking Certificate Level 1, checked 2026-11-16)

Every question in this bank is original, written from the primary law, official guidance and the owner's syllabus below. No DMQ test paper, question bank, assessment day material or past question was used, and no commercial or club practice quiz was read. The Deer Management Qualifications (DMQ) question bank is not public and never used.

## Owner, format and syllabus (Deer Management Qualifications)
- DMQ, Deer Stalking Certificate 1 page: https://dmq.org.uk/qualifications/deer-stalking-qualification-1/ (fetched 2026-11-16). Five assessments: Written (50 multiple-choice questions from a DMQ question bank, pass 40), Visual (20 images of the six wild deer species, species and sex both right to score, pass 16), Game Meat Hygiene (40 multiple-choice questions, pass 32; passing completes the theory element of trained hunter status), Safety (a walk through a safety path with 4 deer targets, shoot or do not shoot, plus 9 further safety questions, all correct), Shooting (two shots at 100 m, two at 70 m, two at the head from 10 to 20 m). Syllabus topics listed: deer biology and ecology, legislation, stalking techniques and taking the shot, deer identification, safety, shooting, large game meat hygiene. Three years from registration to complete the modules; only failed modules are re-sat. The page links to the Deer Initiative guides (England and Wales), bestpracticeguides.scot (Scotland) and the FSA Wild Game guidance. No handbook edition or version number is stated.
- DSC1 Guidance to Candidates (document last modified 8 March 2022, hosted by the British Deer Society as a DMQ assessment centre): https://bds.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/DSC1-Info-2022-v2.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Gives the seven knowledge areas: deer biology and ecology (life and reproductive cycles, habitat, behaviour of the six species), the law (legal basis for firearms and culling), stalking techniques and taking the shot (points of aim, reaction to shot placement), deer identification (six species, male and female, summer and winter coat), safety, shooting, large game (inspection of carcasses and legal requirements). The 2022 page gives the written paper as 50 questions at 80% (the same 40 of 50 as DMQ's current page), so passPct is 80.

## Law, England and Wales
- Deer Act 1991, contents: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/contents (fetched 2026-11-16). Up to date for changes known on or before 13 November 2026.
- Section 1, poaching: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/section/1 (fetched 2026-11-16). Entering land to search for or pursue deer without consent; taking, killing or injuring without consent or lawful authority; removing a carcase; the honest belief defence; an authorised person may require name, address and departure.
- Section 2, close seasons: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/section/2 and Schedule 1: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/schedule/1 (fetched 2026-11-16). Close seasons for Chinese water deer (both sexes 1 April to 31 October), fallow, red and sika stags and bucks 1 May to 31 July, hinds and does 1 April to 31 October, red/sika hybrids as their sex, roe buck 1 November to 31 March, roe doe 1 April to 31 October. Muntjac are not listed. Keeping deer by way of business on land enclosed by a deer-proof barrier is outside section 2.
- Section 3, night: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/section/3 (fetched 2026-11-16). Taking or killing deer is an offence from the expiry of the first hour after sunset to the beginning of the last hour before sunrise.
- Section 4, prohibited weapons and Schedule 2: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/section/4 and https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/schedule/2 (fetched 2026-11-16). Traps, snares, poisoned or stupefying bait, nets; firearms and ammunition in Schedule 2 (any smooth-bore gun; any rifle under .240 inch calibre or under 2,305 joules (1,700 foot-pounds) muzzle energy; any air gun, air rifle or air pistol; any cartridge for a smooth-bore gun; any rifle bullet other than soft-nosed or hollow-nosed); arrows, spears, poison missiles; firing at deer from a moving vehicle or one with its engine running, and using a motor vehicle to drive deer.
- Section 6, exceptions: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/section/6 (fetched 2026-11-16). To prevent suffering of an injured or diseased deer; orphaned dependent deer (2007 addition); "any reasonable means" to kill a deer too injured to survive as an act of mercy; smooth-bore slaughtering instrument at least 12 bore, barrel under 24 inches, shot not smaller than AAA; for Chinese water deer and muntjac only, a rifle of at least .220 inch and 1,356 joules (1,000 foot-pounds) with a soft or hollow-nosed bullet of at least 50 grains.
- Section 7, occupiers and others: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/section/7 (fetched 2026-11-16). Defence to preventing serious crop or property damage on cultivated land, pasture or enclosed woodland; 12 bore smooth-bore with AAA shot or a single non-spherical projectile of at least 22.68 grammes (350 grains); conditions and who is covered, written authority for household members and employees.
- Section 8, licences: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/section/8 (fetched 2026-11-16). Natural England and Natural Resources Wales licences (close season, night, scientific); licence term up to two years.
- Firearms Act 1968, section 1: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/27/section/1 (fetched 2026-11-16). Certificate needed for every firearm except shotguns and ordinary air weapons; offences for possessing a firearm or ammunition without a certificate or breaching a condition.
- Deer Initiative, Close Seasons guide (29.10.07, England and Wales): https://thedeerinitiative.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/close-season-shooting.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Deer may be shot on any day of the week including public holidays. Muntjac have no close season because they breed all year.

## Law, Scotland
- Deer (Scotland) Act 1996, contents: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/58/contents (fetched 2026-11-16). Part III offences ss. 17 to 26, 17A register of persons competent to shoot deer, Schedule 3 penalties. Legislation.gov.uk notes outstanding changes by 2026 asp 6 to s. 5 (close seasons) and s. 18 (night shooting) that were not yet applied: nothing from those pending changes is asked.
- Section 5, close seasons: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/58/section/5 (fetched 2026-11-16). The Secretary of State must fix a close season for females of every species and may do so for males, by order; NatureScot authorisations may allow close season culling.
- Section 18, night: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/58/section/18 (fetched 2026-11-16). Same night period as England and Wales; occupiers of agricultural land or woodland may be authorised by NatureScot to take deer at night where necessary to prevent damage or for public safety and no other reasonable means would do.
- Section 21, firearms and ammunition: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/58/section/21 (fetched 2026-11-16). Power to make orders on firearms, ammunition and sights; breach is an offence.
- The Deer (Close Seasons) (Scotland) Order 2011, Schedule as made: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2011/417/schedule/made (fetched 2026-11-16). Female red, sika, fallow and red/sika hybrid 16 February to 20 October; female roe 1 April to 20 October.
- The Deer (Close Seasons) (Scotland) Amendment Order 2023 (SSI 2023/184), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2023/184/made (fetched 2026-11-16): removes the close seasons for male deer from 21 October 2023; female close seasons unchanged.
- The Deer (Firearms etc.) (Scotland) Amendment Order 2023 (SSI 2023/332), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2023/332/made (fetched 2026-11-16): minimum bullet weight in the 1985 Order cut from 100 grains (6.48 grams) to 80 grains (5.3 grams) from 3 November 2023, allowing non-lead ammunition; sighting devices for night shooting (light-intensifying and heat-sensitive) allowed.
- NatureScot deer authorisations page (https://www.nature.scot/professional-advice/protected-areas-and-species/licensing/species-licensing-z-guide/deer/deer-authorisations) returned 403 to automated fetching, so Scottish muzzle energy, velocity and roe-specific minima were not confirmed from an official page and are not asked. Only the 2023 Order text above is used for Scottish firearms.

## Deer biology, behaviour and identification
- Scottish Best Practice Guides (a partnership of ADMG, BASC, BDS, FLS, NatureScot, SGA and others) species pages, all fetched 2026-11-16: Red deer https://bestpracticeguides.scot/deer-ecology/red-deer/ ; Roe deer https://bestpracticeguides.scot/deer-ecology/roe-deer/ ; Fallow deer https://bestpracticeguides.scot/deer-ecology/fallow-deer/ ; Sika deer https://bestpracticeguides.scot/deer-ecology/sika-deer/ ; Muntjac https://bestpracticeguides.scot/deer-ecology/muntjac/ . They gave shoulder heights, antler cycles, rut and calving or fawning dates, delayed implantation in roe, palmate antlers in fallow, no defined rut in muntjac, the sika metatarsal gland and alarm calls. Their shot placement, humane dispatch, safety and firearms pages are scanned images that could not be read, so the England and Wales Deer Initiative guides below are used for those topics.
- GB Non-native Species Secretariat identification sheet, Chinese water deer: https://www.nonnativespecies.org/assets/Uploads/ID_Hydropotes_inermis_Chinese_Water_Deer.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). No antlers, tusks, large rounded erect ears, 50 to 60 cm at the shoulder, hind quarters higher than the shoulders, stumpy tail with no white rump patch, rut November to January with a whistling call from males, a bounding gait, Schedule 9 listing in England and Wales, native to East China and Korea, escapes from Woburn and Whipsnade.

## Shot placement, follow up, rifles and carcass handling (Deer Initiative, England and Wales Best Practice Guides)
- Shot placement (July 2023 V4): https://thedeerinitiative.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/RHMC0711_Shot-Placement_Guide_Deer_July-2023_V4.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Heart and lung area is the ideal shot; broadside aim point halfway up the chest in line with the foreleg; shot slightly further forward at the shoulder blade usually drops the deer; chest shot is the largest and least mobile target; head and neck shots are small, mobile targets and not the first choice; brain sits high and towards the back of the skull so the shot must strike above the level of the eyes; bullet strikes lower than the aim point at extreme close range; a neck shot may still give a blink reflex for minutes; rumen risk outside broadside angles; humane death is the key objective, not meat value.
- Follow up of shot deer: https://thedeerinitiative.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Follow-up.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Assume every deer shot at was hit; reload immediately; second shot must be safe; wait at least 30 minutes when the position of a wounded deer is not known; do not pursue a mobile wounded deer; keep to one side of the trail; dog on a lead; confirm death by touching the eye; blood, hair and tissue evidence table and reaction table.
- Rifles and ammunition (July 2023 V3): https://thedeerinitiative.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/RHMC0722_Rifles_and_Ammo_BP_Guide_Deer-July-2023-V3.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Hollow-nosed or soft-nosed bullets; .240 and 1,700 foot-pounds for red, fallow, sika and roe; .220, 50 grains and 1,000 foot-pounds for muntjac and Chinese water deer; accuracy standard of 3 rounds into a 10 cm circle at 100 m; wind and distance effects.
- Gralloching: https://thedeerinitiative.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/RHMC0822_Gralloching-Guide-V3.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Check for death first (blink reflex), bleeding by thoracic sticking, tie the oesophagus, open from the inside out, avoid puncturing the gut, rectum is a major contamination source, an intact carcass keeps good condition for about an hour in cool conditions and 15 minutes in sun, greening.
- Larder hygiene and safety and Larder design: https://thedeerinitiative.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/RHMC0819_Larder-Hygiene-and-Safety-Guide-V3.pdf and https://thedeerinitiative.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/RHMC0823_-Larder-Design-Guide-V3.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Waste containers marked not fit for human consumption, knives sterilised at 82 degrees C, no eating drinking or smoking, chiller checked at least twice a day, carcass stored ideally at 1 to 2 degrees C and not frozen, 7 degrees C ceiling for large game, space around carcasses.
- Supply of venison: https://thedeerinitiative.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/RHMC0708_Supply_of_Venison_Guide_July-2023_V4.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Routes of supply, local meaning the county, a neighbouring county or 50 km / 30 miles, game meat after skinning, deer killed other than by hunting (road traffic accidents) must not be supplied for human consumption.

## Food hygiene (Food Standards Agency)
- The Wild Game Guidance, published 3 October 2024, next review October 2026 (England, Wales, Northern Ireland): https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69bc1c944c478213a823ce84/Wild_Game_Guidance__1.pdf (fetched 2026-11-16). Gralloch as soon as possible; a trained person examines the body and viscera; numbered declaration with date, time and place of killing; head and viscera need not accompany the body when no abnormality; if abnormal or no trained person, head (not antlers) and all viscera except stomach and intestines accompany the body; chill to not more than 7 degrees C throughout the meat, not frozen; do not heap carcasses; transport at 7 degrees C large game meat, 4 degrees C small game, 3 degrees C offal; private domestic consumption exemption; supply of small quantities; training areas for a trained person; notifiable disease reporting to APHA; lead shot advice.
- FSA guidance landing page: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-for-primary-producers-and-food-business-operators-on-the-hygienic-production-of-wild-game (fetched 2026-11-16).
- Scotland's Food Standards Scotland Wild Game Guide (December 2021) exists as a separate document; it was downloaded but not relied on. The hygiene questions use the FSA guidance for England, Wales and Northern Ireland and say so where the rule is country-specific.
