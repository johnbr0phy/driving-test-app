# WAEC WASSCE English Language (`wassceeng`): sources

All 270 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the WAEC English Language syllabus topic lists below.
No WAEC past question, specimen paper, marking scheme, prep-site question or commercial practice book was used or
paraphrased. WASSCE past questions are widely posted online; none was opened for content.

## Exam owner (checked 2026-11-14)
- West African Examinations Council (WAEC), Nigeria national office, FAQ page:
  https://www.waecnigeria.org/index.php/node/9?page=0 (fetched 2026-11-14 by the build coordinator; it refused this
  session's connection on 2026-11-15). It confirms that English Language and General Mathematics are the two core
  subjects every WASSCE candidate takes for the 2026 and 2027 entries.
- WAEC's own sites do not serve the English Language syllabus itself, so the syllabus text below is read from the
  WAEC syllabus as reproduced by two sites that agree with each other and with the hosted PDF. The copy says the
  format is "from WAEC's syllabus as published by these sites".

## Syllabus and format (fetched 2026-11-14, re-read 2026-11-15)
- WAEC English Language syllabus PDF (11 pages), hosted at
  https://waecsyllabus.com/files/ssce/ENGLISH%20LANGUAGE.pdf (fetched 2026-11-14). Read with pdftotext. Gave:
  - Scheme: three papers, all to be taken; Papers 1 and 2 a composite paper at one sitting.
  - Paper 1 (The Gambia, Nigeria, Sierra Leone and Liberia): 80 multiple-choice questions in 1 hour for 40 marks,
    forty lexical and forty structural, four options lettered A to D.
  - Lexis: vocabulary of everyday usage (home, social relationships, common core school subjects) and the general
    vocabulary of sixteen fields: building and building construction; agriculture; fishing; stock exchange; health;
    environment; culture, institutions and ceremonies; law and order; motor vehicles and travelling; government and
    administration; sports; religion; science and technology; animal husbandry; advertising; human internal body
    system and function. Idioms and collocations; structural elements (sequence of tenses, pronouns matching their
    antecedents, prepositions); figurative usage; sense relations (synonyms, antonyms, homonyms). "General"
    vocabulary means words most educated people know, not specialist terms.
  - Structure: word-form changes for number, tense and degree; how words combine into groups and sentences;
    structural words (conjunctions, determiners, prepositions).
  - Paper 1 for Ghana is different: 30 lexical, 20 structural and 30 literature questions. Not modelled.
  - Paper 2: essay (one of five topics, 450 words minimum, 50 marks), comprehension (20 marks) and summary
    (30 marks) in 2 hours for 100 marks. Not modelled (written answers).
  - Paper 3: 60 multiple-choice items in 45 minutes for 30 marks: Test of Orals in Nigeria and Liberia, Listening
    Comprehension in Ghana, The Gambia and Sierra Leone. Test of Orals areas: vowels (pure vowels and diphthongs),
    consonants and clusters, rhymes, word stress and syllable structure, emphatic stress and intonation patterns,
    phonetic symbols. The syllabus lists sample contrasts (seat/sit, pool/pull/pole, ship/chip, play/pray), word
    stress shifts between nouns and verbs ('increase/in'crease, 'record/re'cord), emphatic stress for contrast, and
    falling tunes for statements, wh- questions and commands, rising tunes for yes/no questions and statements said
    as questions. Those examples taught the rules; the items here use other words.
- waecsyllabus.com, "WAEC English Language Syllabus 2026/2027", https://waecsyllabus.com/english-language/
  ("Last updated: September 5, 2026", fetched 2026-11-14). Same scheme and lists; states the total is 170 marks,
  that WAEC has issued no newer syllabus so it applies to the May/June 2027 WASSCE for School Candidates, and that in
  Nigeria Paper 2 is written first, then Paper 1 at the same sitting, with Paper 3 in the afternoon.
- MySchool, "WAEC Syllabus: English Language", https://myschool.ng/classroom/waec-syllabus/english-language
  (labelled 2027, fetched 2026-11-14). Same text as the PDF.

## Grading
- WASSCE results are reported as grades A1 to F9. No raw-to-grade conversion for English Language is published by
  WAEC or by the sites above, so Tigertest reports percent correct only and the copy says the grade boundaries are
  not published.

## Language model
- Oral items follow the Received Pronunciation model used in West African school English, with British spelling.
  Each key follows the standard RP transcription of the word; a word with more than one common British
  pronunciation (garage, temperature, vegetable, poor) is not used as a key.

## How the facts are used
- Paper 1 is modelled at full length: 80 four-option questions in 60 minutes, 40 lexis and 40 structure.
- Paper 3 (Test of Orals) is modelled at half length at the real pace: 30 questions in 23 minutes (60 in 45).
  Listening comprehension, which needs audio, is not modelled.
- Paper 2 and Ghana's literature section are not modelled; the landing says so.
- Left out: fees, dates of the next series, grade cut-offs and anything only a news report states.

## Grades
- WAEC grades WASSCE subjects on the nine-point scale A1 to F9 (WAEC circular reported by GNA: https://www.ghanaweb.com/GhanaHomePage/NewsArchive/WAEC-explains-new-grading-system-under-WASSCE-114576). No WAEC table of marks to grades was found; that is Tigertest's observation.
