# Water Treatment Operator Class I bank sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the exam owner's Need-to-Know
Criteria and formula table and from the federal regulations and government guidance below.
None is copied, adapted or reworded from WPI/ABC sample items, past exams, the AWWA or CSUS
study guides, or any prep site, app or question pool. The two sample items printed in the
Need-to-Know Criteria were not reused. The bank was checked against Tigertest's EPA 608
(`data/epa608-questions.json`) and OSHA 10 (`data/osha-questions.json`) banks: no fact and
answer pair is repeated (the 19.5 percent oxygen-deficient line, the permit-space definition,
the attendant's duty, who removes a lock, a tag used alone and the SDS section numbers are
asked there and left out here).

## Outline and format (Water Professionals International, formerly the Association of Boards of Certification)

ABC renamed itself Water Professionals International (WPI, gowpi.org); its exams are still
called the ABC standardized exams. abccert.org and gowpi.org refused automated connections from
this machine on 2026-11-19, and the Wayback Machine copies of abccert.org were blocked as bot
traffic, so the owner's documents were read from state agencies that host them for candidates.

- WPI, Need-to-Know Criteria, Water Treatment Operator Class I (copyright 2025), hosted by the
  Alabama Department of Environmental Management, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://adem.alabama.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/NTK%20WT2.pdf
  (the file is named WT2 but its title page and content are Water Treatment Operator Class I).
  The edition in force. Exam content: Treatment Process 31 questions (10 recall, 21
  application, 7 calculation items); Laboratory Analysis 16 (9 recall, 7 application, 0
  calculation); Equipment Operation and Maintenance 26 (7, 19, 2); Source Water
  Characteristics 15 (9, 6, 1); Security, Safety, and Administrative Procedures 12 (5, 7, 0);
  100 scored questions, up to 10 unscored pre-test questions; 40% recall, 60% application;
  10% calculation questions; calculations given in US and metric units. Job task lists for each
  area (used for the categories here). Primary references: AWWA WSO Water Treatment Grade 1 and
  CSUS Water Treatment Plant Operation Volume 1 (not used: paid books).
- ABC, Need-to-Know Criteria, Water Treatment Operator Class I, 2017 edition, hosted by the
  Montana DEQ as its Class 3B study packet, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://prod-deq.mt.gov/files/Water/WQInfo/Images/opcert/3BStudy.pdf
  The previous edition: Treatment Process 30%, Laboratory Analysis 13%, Equipment Operation &
  Maintenance 27%, Source Water Characteristics 10%, Security, Safety, Compliance, &
  Administrative Procedures 20%. Used only to confirm the job tasks carried into 2025; the
  weights here follow the 2025 counts.
- WPI, Formula/Conversion Table, Water Treatment, Distribution, & Water Laboratory Exams
  (copyright 2024), hosted by the Virginia Department of Health, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/content/uploads/sites/14/WPI-FCT-2024-WT.pdf
  Every calculation uses its formulas and constants: feed rate lb/day = dose x MGD x 8.34 /
  purity; detention time = volume / flow; filter loading gpm/ft2; backwash rise rate
  in/min = gpm/ft2 x 12 / 7.48; drop test velocity; surface overflow and weir overflow rates;
  CT = residual x time; hypochlorite strength; C1V1 = C2V2; % stroke; fluoride saturator
  18,000 mg/L; water, brake and motor horsepower with 3,960; alkalinity and EDTA hardness
  titrations; threshold odor number; area 0.785 D2; 7.48 gal/ft3; 8.34 lb/gal; 0.433 psi/ft;
  2.31 ft/psi; 694 gpm per MGD; 17.1 mg/L per grain per gallon; 0.746 kW/hp; the P and T
  alkalinity table.
- North Carolina DEQ, Drinking Water Operator Certification FAQs, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://deq.nc.gov/about/divisions/water-resources/operator-certification/drinking-water-operator/dw-operator-certification-faqs
  "A score of 70 percent or higher is required to pass." Who certifies, the exam used and the
  pass mark vary by state; the landing gives WPI's format as the reference and says to check
  the state program. 70% is used here as the readiness line.

## Federal regulations (eCFR, current text read 2026-11-19)

- 40 CFR part 141, National Primary Drinking Water Regulations, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-141
  141.2 definitions (MRDL, GWUDI, CT and the inactivation ratio, diatomaceous earth filtration,
  Level 1 and 2 assessments); 141.33 record retention (microbiological and turbidity 5 years,
  chemical 10 years); 141.62 inorganic MCLs (fluoride 4.0, nitrate 10 and nitrite 1 as N,
  arsenic 0.010 mg/L; the 6-month infant note in 141.11); 141.64 DBP MCLs (TTHM 0.080, HAA5
  0.060, bromate 0.010, chlorite 1.0 mg/L); 141.65 MRDLs (chlorine and chloramines 4.0 as Cl2,
  chlorine dioxide 0.8); 141.70 to 141.73 (3-log Giardia and 4-log virus removal and
  inactivation; conventional and direct filtration CFE 0.3 NTU in 95% of monthly readings and
  never above 1 NTU); 141.72 (entry point residual not below 0.2 mg/L for more than 4 hours;
  distribution residual detectable in 95% of samples); 141.76 (recycled backwash returned
  through the existing filtration processes); 141.174 and 141.560 (individual filter turbidity
  recorded every 15 minutes); 141.852 and 141.858 (repeat samples within 24 hours, at least
  three per positive; 30-hour sample holding time); 141.201 to 141.204 (Tier 1 notice within
  24 hours, Tier 2 within 30 days, Tier 3 within one year); 141.152 (CCR by July 1 each year).
  The lead action level is in transition (0.015 to 0.010 mg/L under the 2024 Lead and Copper
  Rule Improvements, compliance date in 2027), so no lead numbers are asked.
- 40 CFR part 143, National Secondary Drinking Water Regulations, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-143
  143.3 levels (iron 0.3, manganese 0.05, color 15 color units, odor 3 TON, pH 6.5 to 8.5,
  fluoride 2.0, TDS 500 mg/L); secondary levels are reasonable goals, not federally enforceable.
- 40 CFR 136.3 Table II, preservation and holding times, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-136/section-136.3
  Total residual chlorine and pH: analyze within 15 minutes; hardness and metals: acid to
  pH below 2.
- 29 CFR 1910.146, Permit-required confined spaces, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-J/section-1910.146
  Hazardous atmosphere (flammable gas over 10% of LFL; oxygen-enriched above 23.5%); test
  first for oxygen, then combustible gases, then toxic gases; retrieval systems for non-entry
  rescue.
- 29 CFR 1910.147, Control of hazardous energy, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-J/section-1910.147
  Verification of isolation before work.
- 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-Z/section-1910.1000
  Chlorine PEL ceiling 1 ppm.
- 29 CFR 1910.151(c), eyewash: quick drenching or flushing facilities where corrosive
  materials are handled (checked 2026-11-19 with the part 1910 text above).

## Government guidance used for operating practice

- US EPA, Alternative Disinfectants and Oxidants Guidance Manual, EPA 815-R-99-014, April 1999,
  copy hosted by the US Bureau of Reclamation, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/programs/quagga/docs/EPAGuidanceManual.pdf
  HOCl and OCl- by pH, chloramine formation below a 5:1 Cl2:N ratio and breakpoint, ozone and
  bromate, chlorine dioxide and chlorite, UV, permanganate for iron and manganese, gaseous
  chlorine feed by ejector vacuum, hypochlorite decay, separate chlorine and ammonia rooms.
- US EPA, Optimizing Water Treatment Plant Performance Using the Composite Correction Program,
  EPA/625/6-91/027, 1998 edition, hosted by ADEM, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.adem.alabama.gov/programs/water/drinkingwater/files/CPEHandbook.pdf
  Jar testing, mudballs from poor backwash, filter loading rates, chemical feed calibration.
- US EPA, AWOP Goals and Operational Guidelines, EPA 815-B-24-012, 2024, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-06/goals-and-operational-guidelines-for-pursuing-optimization.pdf
  IFE and CFE 95th percentile 0.10 NTU; post-backwash spike 0.30 NTU and back to 0.10 within
  15 minutes; settled water goals.
- US EPA, Guidance Manual for Compliance with the Filtration and Disinfection Requirements
  (SWTR Guidance Manual), 1991, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-10/documents/guidance_manual_for_compliance_with_the_filtration_and_disinfection_requirements.pdf
  Slow sand and diatomaceous earth filtration, T10 contact time, baffling.
- US DOE, Improving Pumping System Performance: A Sourcebook for Industry, 2nd ed., fetched
  2026-11-19: https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2014/05/f16/pump.pdf
  Cavitation and vapor pressure, packing and mechanical seals, wear rings, best efficiency
  point and recirculation, affinity laws, parallel pumps, adjustable speed drives, deadheading
  a positive displacement pump.
- Montana Water Center and Montana DEQ, Groundwater Manual for Small Water Systems, October
  2016, fetched 2026-11-19:
  https://deq.mt.gov/files/Water/WQInfo/Documents/opcert/Study%20Materials/GroundwaterManual.pdf
  Static and pumping levels, drawdown, cone of depression, specific capacity, grouting,
  wellhead protection, iron bacteria, air gaps, chlorine gas heavier than air.
- CISA, EPA and FBI, Top Cyber Actions for Securing Water Systems, February 2024, FBI copy,
  fetched 2026-11-19: https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2024/240221.pdf
  Reduce internet exposure of OT devices, change default passwords, MFA.

2025 WPI sample items, figure and drawing items, metric-only versions of the calculations
(the real exam prints US and metric units side by side; the bank uses US units), and the
operator's own state reporting forms.
