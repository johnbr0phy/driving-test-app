# Wonderlic cognitive ability test sources

All 200 questions are original, written for Tigertest from the content description below. None is taken
or reworded from Wonderlic's sample assessment, its blog's sample questions, a released test or any prep
site. Wonderlic's downloadable sample assessment was opened only for its instructions and its items were
not used.

## Format and content (the test owner, Wonderlic)

- Wonderlic, "Cognitive Ability Assessment" (science page):
  https://wonderlic.com/science/cognitive-ability/ (fetched 2026-11-14). The Wonderlic Select cognitive
  ability test "consists of 50 questions that candidates have 12 minutes to answer"; items cover "simple
  math, basic logic, language comprehension, spatial reasoning, pattern identification, and more";
  difficulty rises through the test; the score is estimated from the overall response pattern, each
  question weighted by its difficulty and the effect of guessing, not a plain count of right answers.
- Wonderlic, "Cognitive Ability Test for Hiring" (Wonderlic Select product page):
  https://wonderlic.com/select-hiring-assessment/cognitive/ (fetched 2026-11-14). Repeats 50 questions in
  12 minutes, the same item areas, rising difficulty and response-pattern scoring; the weight of the
  cognitive score against personality and motivation varies by job.
- Wonderlic, "Wonderlic Personnel Test (WPT-R)" product sheet (2015):
  https://wonderlic.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/WPT-R_Description_MSP.pdf (fetched 2026-11-14).
  "12 minute timed assessment", "50 questions", online administration and scoring, measures general
  mental ability: the ability to understand instructions, learn, adapt and solve problems.
- Wonderlic, "Wonderlic Test Prep" (test-taker hub): https://wonderlic.com/test-takers/
  (fetched 2026-11-14). The cognitive section is short and timed, often about 12 minutes; question types
  named are word problems, number series, logic puzzles, vocabulary and analogies, with no single topic
  dominating; if a question takes too long, make your best guess and move on; the classic 50-question
  cognitive test averages a score of around 20; there is no universal pass or fail, employers compare
  results with the profile for the role; retake rules are set by the employer.
- Wonderlic, "Cognitive ability" in the WonScore product: https://wonderlic.com/wonscore/cognitive-ability/
  (fetched 2026-11-14). Cognitive ability is one of three parts of the full assessment (with personality
  and motivation), scored against the factors that matter for the role.
- Wonderlic, "The Story of Wonderlic": https://wonderlic.com/about/history/ (fetched 2026-11-14).
  E. F. Wonderlic created the Wonderlic Personnel Test in the 1930s (work dated to 1932).

## How the bank follows it

Seven drill domains cover the item areas Wonderlic names: vocabulary, verbal reasoning and analogies
(language comprehension), arithmetic (simple math), word problems, number and letter series (pattern
identification), logic (basic logic and logic puzzles) and spatial reasoning. Wonderlic publishes no
per-area counts, so the per-test split (8/7/8/8/7/7/5) is Tigertest's own. Spatial items are written in
words (directions, folding, cubes, clock hands), since the bank carries no pictures. Each practice test is
50 questions in 12 minutes, ordered from easier to harder like the real test.

## Could not be confirmed, and how the copy words it

- Calculator: a search result summarising Wonderlic's sample assessment says work is done without a
  calculator, but the PDF text could not be extracted here, so the facts table does not state it; the
  practice test simply offers no calculator.
- Wonderlic's own score (response-pattern weighted) has no published conversion from a raw count, so
  Tigertest reports percent correct and never a "Wonderlic score". The landing quotes only Wonderlic's
  statement that the classic 50-question test averages about 20.
- Countries and languages: Wonderlic's pages fetched name none, so the exam is listed for the US.

## Left out

Sample questions from any source; NFL or sports score anecdotes (not on Wonderlic's pages fetched);
personality and motivation sections (not modelled); the 30-question remote Quicktest of the Scholastic
Level Exam (an admissions product).
