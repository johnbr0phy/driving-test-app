# Sources for `zak53moto` (South Africa K53 learner's licence test, Code 1, motorcycle)

All 200 questions are original. No official or commercial question bank was opened or copied, and
no stem is shared with the Code 2 (`tools/countries/zak53/`) or Code 3 (`tools/countries/zak53c3/`)
banks: stems were compared by normalised text and fuzzy ratio, and every near match was reworded
into a motorcyclist's situation. Rules come from the National Road Traffic Act 93 of 1996 and the
National Road Traffic Regulations, 2000; signs and markings follow the SADC Road Traffic Signs
Manual classes listed in `../../countries/zak53/sources.md`. Signs are described in words.
Checked 2026-11-05 unless noted. Web search ran out of budget early in this session, so several
facts rest on the sibling banks' sources (checked 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-03) and are listed as
unconfirmed in the builder's report.

## Code 1 and the learner's licence (checked this session)
- gov.za, "Apply for a learner's licence"
  (https://www.gov.za/services/driving-licence/apply-learners-licence), fetched 2026-11-05:
  Code 1 is "a motor cycle with or without a sidecar, motor tricycle or quadrucycle"; minimum age
  16 if the engine does not exceed 125 cc, 18 if it exceeds 125 cc, measured on the date of the
  test; Code 2 up to 3,500 kg GVM from 17; Code 3 over 3,500 kg from 18; the learner's licence is
  "valid for 24 months and cannot be extended"; study road signs, motor vehicle controls and road
  rules; an eye test comes first. The page states supervised driving in general and lists no
  separate conditions for motorcycle learners.
- Wikipedia, "Driving licence in South Africa"
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_licence_in_South_Africa), fetched 2026-11-05: Code A1
  motorcycles of 125 cc or less, Code A over 125 cc; A1 from 16. It says Code 1 learners need not
  be accompanied, which gov.za's general wording does not say, so no item asks about supervision.
- Learner may not carry a passenger: only an Arrive Alive article quoting a rider trainer was found
  (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/the-learner-motorcyclist-do-we-test-effectively, re-read 2026-11-05;
  no regulation cited, regulation text unreachable). Fact-check 2026-11-05: LIC-008 was replaced by
  an eye-test item (gov.za page above), and no item now asks about learner passengers.

## Motorcycle rules (checked this session)
- Helmets, reg 207: Arrive Alive, "Compulsory Protective Helmets"
  (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/compulsory-helmets), fetched 2026-11-05: no person may drive or be
  a passenger on a motor cycle, tricycle or quadricycle, or be a passenger in its sidecar, on a
  public road without a protective helmet that fits properly with the chin strap fastened under the
  chin; the driver must ensure passengers under 14 comply.
- Duties relating to motor cycles: Arrive Alive, "Motorbike Safety and Children as Pillion"
  (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/motorbike-safety-and-children-as-pillion), fetched 2026-11-05,
  quoting the regulation: rider seated astride with feet on the front footrests; a passenger only
  on a motorcycle over 50 cc, seated in a sidecar or astride a pillion with feet on footrests; not
  more than two persons on a motorcycle (sidecar excluded); not more than two adults in a sidecar;
  nothing carried in front of the rider except a non-bulky object secured or in a carrier, not
  blocking view or control.

## Test format (checked 2026-11-05)
- Wikipedia, "Driving licence in South Africa" (above), re-read 2026-11-05: the learner's test is
  multiple choice on rules of the road, road signs and markings, and vehicle controls; pass marks
  22 of 28 (rules), 23 of 28 (signs, markings and signals) and 6 of 8 (controls), so 64 questions.
  The RTMC figure of 64 random questions is recorded in `../../countries/zak53/sources.md`. Secondary
  source only; the landing says "study guides give". No Code 1 specific format is published;
  Tigertest uses 22 rules / 22 signs and markings / 6 controls per 50-question mock, 40 of 50 to
  pass (Tigertest's own target).

## Rules shared with the Code 2 bank (sources in ../../countries/zak53/sources.md, not re-checked)
- Alcohol: ordinary drivers under 0.05 g per 100 ml blood, 0.24 mg per 1,000 ml breath; professional
  drivers 0.02 g (NRTA s65).
- Crash duties: stop, give name and address, report an injury crash within 24 hours and a
  damage-only crash by the next working day (NRTA s61).
- Speed limits 60 / 100 / 120 km/h (reg 292); two-second following distance.
- Stopping and parking (regs 304, 305): 9 m before a pedestrian crossing, 6 m of a bridge or tunnel,
  alongside a vehicle on a roadway under 9 m, 5 m of an intersection, 1.5 m of a hydrant, on the
  roadway or within 1 m of it outside urban areas.
- (No item relies on this since 2026-11-05.) Headlamps sunset to sunrise and when persons and vehicles are not clearly visible at 150 m; dip
  when main beam could dazzle. Hand-held phone ban (reg 308A).
- Sign classes and names (control, command, prohibition, reservation, comprehensive, selective
  restriction plates, warning, temporary signs on yellow), route prefixes N, R, M and brown tourism
  signs: Wikipedia "Road signs in South Africa" (SADC manual summary).

## Riding practice
- Controls layout (clutch lever left, front brake lever right, rear brake pedal right foot, gear
  lever left foot, throttle twistgrip, kill switch, choke, side stand cut-out) and riding advice
  (both brakes, blind spots, gravel, wind, wet paint) are standard motorcycle practice; Arrive Alive
  "Motorcycle Safety" (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/motorcycle-safety, fetched 2026-11-05) advises
  never riding between lanes, so no item endorses filtering and none asserts it is illegal.

## Deliberately avoided
- Supervision rules for Code 1 learners (sources disagree), daylight-only riding for learners and
  any daytime headlamp law for motorcycles (not confirmed), lane-splitting legality, freeway engine
  size limits, helmet standards, fines, fees, AARTO points and the licence card renewal rule.

## Fact-check 2026-11-05 (official RTMC chart and Arrive Alive, Department of Transport road safety site)
- RTMC, "Road Traffic Signs" chart 5, Sign Descriptions (2007, printed by the RTMC)
  (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/downloads/traffic_signs/chart%205.pdf), read 2026-11-05. Confirms:
  stop R1, 4-way stop R1.4, stop/go R1.5, yield R2, yield to pedestrians R2.1, yield at mini-circle
  R2.2, no entry R3, one-way R4, pedestrian priority R5, yield to oncoming traffic R6; command signs
  minimum speed R101, keep left/right R103/R104, proceed straight only R107, turn right R109, motor
  cycles only R116, pay toll R132, switch head lamps on R133; prohibition signs speed limit R201,
  height limit R204, excessive noise (hooting) R206, hitch-hiking R207, left/right turn R211/R212,
  U-turn R213, overtaking R214, parking R216, stopping R217, pedestrian R218, motor cycle R222, goods
  vehicle R229, agricultural vehicle R236, animal-drawn vehicle R237; bus and pedal cycle lane
  reservation R301-R304; freeway begins R401/R402, woonerf (living street) R403; time-limit plates
  (R)501-(R)506; warning signs crossroad, curves, T-junction, side road, roundabout, two-way traffic,
  traffic signals, stop and yield control ahead, pedestrians, children, cyclists, cattle, railway
  crossing, steep descent, narrow bridge W326, road narrows, speed humps, slippery road, falling
  rocks, sharp curve chevron signs W405-W408; temporary scholar patrol ahead TW305 and roadworks
  TW336; tourism signs, hospital (with name), filling station, accommodation and cul-de-sac
  information signs; markings RM1 no overtaking, RM4.1 left edge line, RM5 painted island, RM8
  mandatory direction arrow, stop line, yield line, pedestrian crossing lines; traffic signals:
  steady yellow disc "prepare to stop vehicle behind line", flashing red disc "act as for 4-way stop
  & yield right of way to pedestrians", flashing yellow disc "proceed with caution & yield right of
  way to pedestrians", steady green disc and green arrow (proceed, yielding to traffic and
  pedestrians lawfully within the junction), steady red arrow (stop, turn left only if safe),
  flashing left red arrow (turn left after stopping, yielding), flashing red man, overhead lane
  signals S16 green arrow / S17 red cross / S18-S19 yellow arrow (lane closed, leave it), traffic
  officer hand signals SS1 and road construction flag signals SS2.
- Wikipedia, "Road signs in South Africa" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_signs_in_South_Africa),
  read 2026-11-05: temporary signs are largely the same as permanent ones with a yellow background
  instead of white. Sign shapes and colours (red octagon stop, red-bordered triangles, blue command
  discs, red-ringed prohibition discs) follow the SADC manual's images on that page and are not
  stated in its text.
- Wikipedia, "Numbered routes in South Africa" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbered_routes_in_South_Africa),
  read 2026-11-05: N national routes, R provincial and regional routes, M metropolitan routes.
- Arrive Alive, "How much may a person drink" (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/how-much-may-a-person-drink),
  read 2026-11-05: 0.05 g per 100 ml blood and 0.24 mg per 1,000 ml breath (all drivers); professional
  drivers 0.02 g and 0.10 mg. Wikipedia "Drunk driving law by country" agrees (0.05%, 0.02%).
- Arrive Alive, "Legal duties after a road crash" (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/legal-duties-after-a-road-crash),
  read 2026-11-05: stop immediately; give name and address to anyone with reasonable grounds; report
  an injury crash at a police station or traffic office within 24 hours; after damaging someone
  else's property, stop and report at the nearest police station. It gives no separate "next working
  day" deadline, so LIC-017 no longer states one.
- Arrive Alive, "Road legislation and using your phone when driving"
  (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/Road-Legislation-and-using-your-phone-when-driving), read 2026-11-05:
  reg 308A bans holding a phone in either hand or with any other part of the body while driving,
  also at a robot with the engine running (LIC-019, LIC-020).
- Arrive Alive, "Speed limits and the law" (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/speed-limits-and-the-law),
  read 2026-11-05: 60 km/h urban, 100 km/h outside urban areas (not freeway), 120 km/h freeway.
- Arrive Alive, "Following distances and road crashes"
  (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/following-distances-and-road-crashes), read 2026-11-05: 2-3 seconds
  only in ideal conditions, three seconds better, at least 5-6 seconds in adverse weather and on
  slippery roads (SPD-005, SPD-006).
- Arrive Alive, "Safe overtaking" (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/safe-overtaking-and-road-safety),
  read 2026-11-05: pass on the offside (right) with the right indicator; do not overtake in blind
  areas such as bends, corners, junctions, crossroads and intersections or near a blind rise; never
  overtake on the wrong side in the emergency lane (SPD-009, SPD-010, SPD-011).
- Arrive Alive, "Motorcycle safety" (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/motorcycle-safety), re-read
  2026-11-05: "Never ride in between lanes in traffic or share a lane with another vehicle" (SPD-019).
- Arrive Alive, "Lights on" (https://www.arrivealive.co.za/lights-on), read 2026-11-05: the Department
  of Transport urged motorists to keep dipped headlamps on during the day (PRK-009).
- Arrive Alive, "Where are you not allowed to stop or park" (https://carinsurance.arrivealive.co.za/where-are-you-not-allowed-to-stop-or-park-under-the-national-road-traffic-regulations/),
  read 2026-11-05: regs 304 and 305, 9 m of a pedestrian crossing on its approach side, on or within
  6 m of a bridge, tunnel or subway, alongside a vehicle on a roadway under 9 m wide, 5 m of an
  intersection, 1.5 m either side of a hydrant, and outside urban areas on the roadway or within 1 m
  of its edge.

  chart) replaced by animal-drawn vehicle prohibited, hitch-hiking prohibited and motor cycles only.
- WRN: dip (not in the chart) replaced by narrow bridge; chevrons named as sharp curve chevron signs.
- GDE: brown colour, H and bed symbols not confirmed: replaced by tourism sign, hospital sign with
  name, filling station sign and cul-de-sac sign.
- MRK: amber "too close to stop", solid/broken combined line and officer's raised arm not confirmed:
  replaced by steady amber, overhead red X and construction flag signal; yellow edge line now "left
