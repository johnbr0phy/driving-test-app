# Sources: PSIRA security officer grade assessment (Grades E to C), `zapsira`

All checked 5 November 2026. Web search was exhausted for this session's workflow, so sources are
the documents that could be fetched directly.

## Official (read this session)

- Private Security Industry Regulation Act 56 of 2001, as published in Government Gazette 23051,
  25 January 2002 (gov.za copy): https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a56-010.pdf
  (landing page https://www.gov.za/documents/private-security-industry-regulation-act lists later
  amending Acts, including the Private Security Industry Regulation Amendment Act 18 of 2014; the
  amended text was not read, so the bank avoids anything that Act may have changed, such as business
  ownership rules). Used:
  - s. 2: PSIRA established as a juristic person (kept by the Amendment Act; the head office clause
    it deletes is no longer asked).
  - s. 3: objects (Constitution and law, minimum standards of occupational conduct, high training
    standards, rights of security officers).
  - Definitions of "security officer", "security service" (protecting or safeguarding a person or
    property in any manner, reaction services, etc.) and "security service provider".
  - s. 20(1): no person may render a security service for remuneration, reward, a fee or benefit
    unless registered; s. 20(2): executives, directors, members and partners of a security business
    must be registered.
  - s. 21(1): application with fingerprints, the fee and other documents.
  - s. 23(1): natural person: fit and proper; citizen or permanent resident; at least 18; prescribed
    training; no Schedule offence within 10 years; no improper conduct within 5 years; clearance
    certificate for former members of official military, security, police or intelligence forces;
    mentally sound.
  - s. 25: certificate of registration and certificate of identification; return on withdrawal or
    suspension.
  - s. 28: code of conduct binding on all security service providers, registered or not, and to the
    extent the Act provides on in-house security; s. 28(3)(b) minimum wages and anti-exploitation
    standards.
  - s. 43: Security Officers Act 92 of 1987 repealed (the old board's name is not asked).
  - Schedule (table of offences), including fraud and theft.
- Criminal Procedure Act 51 of 1977, Juta consolidation as on justice.gov.za:
  https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/1977-051.pdf . Used: s. 23 (private arrestor may seize
  articles and must deliver them to a police official forthwith; safe custody of objects that could
  cause harm), s. 42 (arrest by a private person without warrant: Schedule 1 offence in presence or
  reasonably suspected; fresh pursuit; affray; owner, occupier or person in charge and anyone
  authorised by them may arrest a person found committing any offence on the property), s. 48
  (audibly demand entry before breaking open premises), s. 49 (force reasonably necessary and
  proportional; deadly force only for a threat of serious violence or a crime involving serious
  bodily harm with no other reasonable means), s. 50 (arrested person to a police station as soon
  as possible; court within 48 hours), Schedule 1 (theft, robbery, fraud, malicious injury to
  property, housebreaking with intent, assault with grievous bodily harm, etc.).
- Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993 (gov.za copy):
  https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a85-93.pdf . Used: s. 8 (employer
  provides and maintains a safe working environment), s. 14 (employee duties: reasonable care,
  cooperate, obey lawful safety rules, report unsafe situations as soon as practicable, report an
  injury incident not later than the end of the shift), s. 15 (no interfering with or misusing
  safety items), s. 17 (health and safety representatives where more than 20 employees).

## Known law used without reading the text this session

- Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996: s. 9 equality, s. 10 dignity, s. 12 freedom
  and security of the person, s. 14 privacy (searches), s. 35(1) arrested persons (remain silent,
  court within 48 hours). Well known; not fetched this session.
- Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (information adequate, relevant, not excessive;
  kept secure). Not fetched this session.
- Code of Conduct for Security Service Providers, 2003 (PSIRA). Not fetched (gov.za returned 500,
  psira.co.za unreachable); items rely only on the Act's s. 28 and on general obligations (honesty,
  no unlawful force, confidentiality, professional conduct) stated generally, without regulation
  numbers.
- Emergency numbers 10111 (SAPS), 10177 (ambulance), 112 (from a cellphone): long-standing South
  African numbers, not confirmed on saps.gov.za this session (site unreachable).
- First aid figures follow current international resuscitation guidance (30:2 is not used; AED,
  choking, recovery position, cooling burns under running water about 20 minutes).

## Exam format (not confirmed)

- psira.co.za could not be loaded this session (TLS certificate chain error through the proxy and
  HTTP 503 through the fetch tool), and web search was unavailable. No official source for the
  Grade E, D and C course content, assessment length, time or pass mark was read.
- Widely known structure, unconfirmed this session: security officers train in grades E (entry) to
  A at PSIRA-accredited training centres, each grade a short course ending in an assessment set by
  the centre; registration with PSIRA requires at least Grade E (guards usually Grade E to C).
- Tigertest's choices: four 50-question mock tests, 70% as Tigertest's target, labelled as such on
  the landing and in examFacts. Training sets grouped by grade are Tigertest's own mapping of
  the course topics, not PSIRA's syllabus.

## Fact-check pass, 5 November 2026

- Private Security Industry Regulation Amendment Act 18 of 2014, Government Gazette 45295,
  8 October 2021 (assented and published; s. 43 says it comes into operation on a date fixed by
  presidential proclamation, and gov.za lists it as "To be gazetted", so commencement is not
  confirmed): https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202110/45295privatesecurityindustryregulationamendmentact18of2014.pdf
  (listing: https://www.gov.za/documents/private-security-industry-regulation-act), checked
  2026-11-05. It amends s. 2 (deletes "head office in Pretoria"), s. 3, s. 20(1) and (2)(a), s. 21(1)
  (adds ID copy, insignia and uniform sketches; (a) fingerprints unchanged), s. 23(1)(c), (d) and
  (h) (removes the 10-year window for Schedule offences), s. 28(2) (drops "whether registered or
  not") and adds offences to the Schedule. Not amended: s. 23(1)(a), (b), (e), (f), (g), s. 25,
  s. 43, the "protecting or safeguarding a person or property" paragraph of "security service".
  Changes made: LAW-009 now asks why a recent theft conviction counts (true under both texts),
  LAW-016 explanation drops "whether or not registered", LAW-022 (Pretoria head office) replaced
  by "juristic person" (both texts), and the landing and examFacts note the 10-year change.
- Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996, Chapter 2 (Bill of Rights):
  https://www.gov.za/documents/constitution/chapter-2-bill-rights , checked 2026-11-05. Confirmed
  s. 9(1) and (3) grounds, s. 10 dignity, s. 12(1)(a) freedom not deprived arbitrarily or without
  just cause, s. 14 privacy (person or home searched; property searched; possessions seized),
  s. 35(1)(a) remain silent, (b) informed promptly of that right, (d) court within 48 hours (or the
  end of the first court day after). ROL-011 explanation corrected to the s. 14 wording.
- Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013, s. 10 (adequate, relevant and not excessive)
  and s. 19(1) (secure integrity and confidentiality by reasonable technical and organisational
  measures): https://popia.co.za/section-10-minimality/ and
  https://popia.co.za/section-19-security-measures-on-integrity-and-confidentiality-of-personal-information/
  (reproduction of the Act's text), checked 2026-11-05. ACC-003, ACC-004 confirmed.
- Emergency numbers 10111 (police), 10177 (ambulance), 112 (mobile phones): saps.gov.za returned
  503, so confirmed from the secondary list https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_emergency_telephone_numbers
  (South Africa row), checked 2026-11-05.
- First aid, checked 2026-11-05 (no South African official page could be fetched; UK first aid
  bodies following the same ILCOR/ERC guidance used):
  - CPR straight away for unresponsive and not breathing normally, 5-6 cm at 100-120 a minute:
    https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/cpr/
  - AED: switch on, follow its prompts, CPR continues except during analysis and shock:
    https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/how-to-use-a-defibrillator/
  - Choking: if they can cough, encourage coughing; back blows when coughing fails:
    https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/choking/
  - Recovery position for unresponsive, breathing normally (airway open, vomit drains, monitor):
    https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/recovery-position/
  - Heart attack signs (chest pain, sweating, breathlessness), call for help, rest comfortably:
    https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/heart-attack/
  - Burns: cool running water for 20 minutes; no creams, oils or butter:
    https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/
  - Snake bite: keep still and calm, do not cut or suck: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/snake-bites/
  - Seizure: the five-minute threshold could not be confirmed, so EMG-026's explanation now says
    to call for an ambulance if the seizure goes on a long time, another follows or the person is
    injured (https://www.epilepsy.org.uk/info/firstaid/what-to-do: status epilepticus is an
    emergency). Key (clear space, protect the head) unchanged.
  - Fracture (keep still) and heat exhaustion (rest, shade, water) remain general first aid
    practice, not fetched.
- Fire: fire triangle (heat, fuel, oxygen; removing one stops a fire; a fire blanket smothers):
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_triangle , checked 2026-11-05. The "feel the door with the back
  of the hand" item (EMG-015) could not be confirmed from a fetched source and was replaced by a
  fire blanket / oxygen item. Evacuation, assembly point and closing doors remain general practice.
- Radio: "Over" expects a reply, "Out" needs none, never together:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procedure_word ; "Bravo" for B in the ICAO alphabet:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_phonetic_alphabet , both checked 2026-11-05.
- Code of Conduct for Security Service Providers, 2003: still not fetched (gov.za 404, psira.co.za
  503). ROL-024 and POW-020 explanations no longer cite the code; role and conduct items (bribes,
  confidentiality, uniform, alcohol on duty, non-security tasks, media) are scenario judgement
  items whose keys do not depend on any code wording.
- Still unconfirmed (psira.co.za unreachable, web search unavailable): the grade structure (E to
  A at accredited centres), the assessment format and pass mark. The landing and examFacts say
  the format is set by the training centre and 70% is Tigertest's target; the grade-to-topic
  grouping of the training sets is labelled Tigertest's own. General guarding practice items
  (access control, key registers, seals, patrols, occurrence books, scene protection, chain of
  custody) are standard practice, not tied to a PSIRA text.
