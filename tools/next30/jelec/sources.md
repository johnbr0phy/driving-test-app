# Sources for `jelec` (journeyman electrician exam, 2023 NEC)

All 200 questions are original, written by Tigertest from the code rules themselves. No commercial
question bank, exam-prep book or NFPA text was copied; options and explanations paraphrase the rules.
Checked 2026-10-30.

## Code
- NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 edition (published 2022). Articles used: 90, 100, 110,
  200, 210, 215, 220, 230, 240, 250, 300, 310, 314, 334, 344, 348, 352, 358, 400, 404, 406, 408,
  410, 422, 430, 440, 450, 500/501, 511, 517, 590, 680, 690, 700, 701, and Chapter 9 Table 1 plus
  Informative Annex C (EMT fill counts). Explanations cite articles and tables by name; specific
  section numbers appear only on the landing study guide for tables (310.16, 250.66, 250.122,
  314.16, 300.5) whose numbers did not change in 2023.
- 2023-specific points used: dwelling GFCI list including laundry areas and 125-250 V receptacles;
  Type 1 or Type 2 SPD at dwelling services; outdoor emergency disconnect for one- and two-family
  dwellings; working-space table now headed 151-1000 V; transformer primary-only protection for
  "1000 V or less"; all definitions consolidated in Article 100.

## Exam format (varies by state)
- No single national exam. Many states and cities use the ICC National Standard Journeyman
  Electrician exam (Pearson VUE: about 80 questions, 4 hours, open book) or a PSI/Prometric
  state exam; Oklahoma's PSI bulletin, for example, lists 100 scored items including 10 on
  grounding and bonding. 70% is the most common pass mark; some states use 75%. Secondary sources
  only (third-party summaries of the ICC bulletin); the ICC candidate bulletin itself was not
  fetched. The landing says "varies by state" and "about 80".
- Blueprint here (5/5/6/5/7/8/4/5/5 of 50) is a typical journeyman weighting, heaviest on wiring
  methods and grounding, not any one state's published outline.

## Not covered
- State or local licensing law, business law, and state amendments to the NEC.
- Figure-dependent items (none written).
- Facts that differ in the 2020 or 2026 NEC were avoided where possible; the bank is 2023-based.
