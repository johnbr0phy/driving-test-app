# Sources for `pcoc` (Transport Canada Pleasure Craft Operator Card test)

All 200 questions are original, written from the official federal sources below. No course
provider's test, practice exam or question bank was copied, and no item was taken from the US
boating bank (`data/boating-questions.json`). Checked 2026-10-30.

## Test format
- SPEC.md row said "36 questions, 75%". That was the original 1999-2010 test. Transport Canada
  replaced it with a 50-question test (Boating Industry, "Canada toughens exam for pleasure boat
  operators", 15 April 2011: "has 50 questions, up from 36"). Accredited providers describe the
  current test as 50 multiple-choice questions, 75% to pass (38 of 50), up to 1 hour 15 minutes
  online (BOATsmart, boatsmartexam.com/ca, checked 2026-10-30). Transport Canada's own pages give
  the 75% minimum (archived backgrounder) but do not publish the count; modelled here as 50 / 75%.
- Standard for online testing: Transport Canada TP 15080E (cited by providers; text not checked).

## Rules (official)
- Transport Canada, "Proof of competency for recreational boaters", modified 2024-05-30:
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/marine-transportation/marine-safety/proof-competency-recreational-boaters
  Any motor incl. electric trolling motors, even when not in use; PCOC valid for life; copies not
  accepted; rental checklist only for the rental period; visitors fewer than 45 consecutive days;
  under 12: 10 hp / 7.5 kW, 12 to 15: 40 hp / 30 kW without direct supervision (16+ in the boat);
  no PWC under 16; not required in Nunavut or NWT; replacement from the issuing provider.
- Transport Canada, "Pleasure Craft Operator Card (PCOC)":
  https://tc.canada.ca/en/marine-transportation/preparing-operate-your-vessel/pleasure-craft-operator-card-pcoc
- Small Vessel Regulations, SOR/2010-91 (Justice Laws, current to 2026):
  https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2010-91/FullText.html
  Licensing at 7.5 kW aggregate engine power (s. 100), copy of licence on board (s. 101);
  up to 6 m: PFD each, reboarding device over 0.5 m, 15 m heaving line (s. 204), flashlight or
  3 pyrotechnic signals with at most one smoke (s. 205), 6 to 9 m: flashlight and 6 signals, at
  most 2 smoke; paddle or anchor with 15 m (s. 206), bailer or manual pump; navigation equipment
  incl. sound device, lights, compass (s. 207), compass exemption up to 8 m within sight of
  seamarks (s. 215); 5B:C extinguisher for inboard engine, fixed fuel tank or fuel-burning
  appliance (s. 208); flare exemption within 1 nautical mile on a river, canal or lake (s. 213);
  PWC reduced list when all wear PFDs (s. 218); sailboards (s. 219); signals expire 4 years after
  manufacture (s. 20); inherently buoyant PFDs for under 16 (s. 202); inflatables worn in an open
  vessel (s. 10).
- Collision Regulations (C.R.C., c. 1416, International Regulations with Canadian modifications):
  Rules 5, 6, 9, 13 to 18, 23, 25, 30, 34, 35 (lookout, safe speed, narrow channels, overtaking,
  head-on, crossing, stand-on, sail, lights, anchor light, oars, manoeuvring and fog signals).
- Vessel Operation Restriction Regulations, SOR/2008-120 (as amended by SOR/2025-158): 10 km/h within
  30 m of shore in the waters listed in s. 2(7), with exceptions in s. 2(8) (towing straight out from
  shore, rivers under 100 m wide, canals, buoyed channels). Re-read by the reviewer 2026-10-30.
- Small Vessel Regulations s. 1005 (Water Sports): towing a person needs someone other than the
  operator keeping watch, seating for every person towed, a PFD for each, and is banned in restricted
  visibility and from one hour after sunset to sunrise. Re-read by the reviewer 2026-10-30.
- Transport Canada, Safe Boating Guide (TP 511E). The 2026 PDF link on tc.canada.ca returned
  404 on 2026-10-30; content used is stable across editions: compliance notice, float plans,
  fuelling (blower about 4 minutes), carbon monoxide, cold water 1-10-1, HELP and huddle,
  person overboard, Mayday / Pan-Pan / Securite, VHF 16, ROC(M), distress signals.
- Canadian Aids to Navigation System (TP 968, Canadian Coast Guard): lateral buoys (red right
  returning, odd green / even red), bifurcation, fairway, isolated danger, cardinal buoys and
  topmarks, control buoys (keep out, danger, control, information), swimming, mooring,
  anchorage, cautionary buoys, ranges, daybeacons.
- Environment and Climate Change Canada marine warnings: strong wind 20-33 kn, gale 34-47,
  storm 48-63, hurricane force 64+.

## Not verified against a current primary text
- Exact current test length (50) is from providers and a 2011 trade report, not a TC page.
- Pleasure craft licence validity is left out: TC proposed cutting it from 10 to 5 years with fees, and a secondary source says that took effect 2026-01-06; not confirmed on a TC page. The 7.5 cm character height IS confirmed (TC "After you apply", modified 2026-01-06).
- Swimming buoy light colour (yellow) and port bifurcation / daybeacon shapes from TP 968 memory.
