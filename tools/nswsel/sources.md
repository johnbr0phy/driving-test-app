# NSW Selective High School Placement Test: sources

All questions, passages and poems are original. Nothing is copied or adapted from NSW past papers,
the department's practice tests or commercial prep material.

## Official (checked 2026-10-29)
- NSW Department of Education, "Get ready for the Selective High School Placement Test":
  https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/students/oc-and-shs/going-to-a-selective-high-school/get-ready-for-the-selective-high-school-placement-test
  - Computer-based. Four tests, each weighted 25%:
    Reading 17 questions (3 with multiple parts, 38 answers), 45 min, multiple choice;
    Mathematical Reasoning 35 questions, 40 min, five options; Thinking Skills 40 questions,
    40 min, four options; Writing 1 open response, 30 min.
  - No calculators, dictionaries or rulers; working out on paper.
  - Reading texts: fiction, non-fiction, poetry, magazine articles, reports; one example question
    type compares 2 short extracts on the same theme.
- NSW Department of Education, "Selective high school practice tests":
  https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/parents-and-carers/choosing-a-school-setting/selective-high-schools/placement-test/selective-high-school-practice-tests
  - Same counts; "45 minutes to provide 38 answers across 17 questions"; some common items do
    not count towards final scores. Not used for item content.

## Secondary (format detail only, checked 2026-10-29)
- Braintree Coaching, "NSW Selective School Test: The 4 Components Explained (2026)":
  maths content areas (number and algebra, measurement and geometry, statistics and
  probability, multi-step problems drawn from the Year 5 to 6 curriculum); thinking skills
  covers critical thinking and problem solving.
- Search summaries of a 2023 NSW test information document (Cambridge-produced, now 404) and
  prep sites describing the reading parts: a fiction extract, poetry, a factual text with six
  sentences removed, and extracts on a related theme. Test Academy reports an inline-cloze
  reading item from late 2025 (could not open the page to confirm).

## Modelling decisions
- Reading is 38 items, one per answer, so the score matches the 38 marks: narrative 6, poem 5,
  article 5 + 1 inline cloze, sentences removed 6 (one item per gap, 5 options from a pool of 7),
  four extracts A to D 10 ("Which extract ...", locked order), two texts 5.
- Maths 35 per test (number and algebra 18, measurement and space 11, statistics and probability
  6; our split, the department publishes none), five options. Drill-only items include 3 numeric
  entry items; the real test is multiple choice only, so numeric items stay out of the tests.
- Thinking Skills 40 per test, critical thinking 20, problem solving 20 (our split), four
  options, text only (the real test also has spatial and pattern figures; not modelled).
- Writing not modelled. Results are percent correct; the overall is the plain average of the
  three section percents (equal weighting, like the real 25% each). No pass mark exists.

## Facts in passages, checked against general reference knowledge (not re-verified online)
- Platypus: electroreceptors in the bill, closes eyes, ears and nostrils underwater, eats up to
  about a fifth of body weight a night, grinding pads instead of adult teeth, eastern Australia.
- Overland Telegraph Line: Charles Todd, work from 1870, about 3,200 km Darwin to Adelaide,
  ends joined at Frew's Ponds in August 1872, repeater stations, termites and iron poles,
  Alice Springs began as a repeater station.
- Light pollution: more than 80% of the world's people under light-polluted skies and more than a
  third unable to see the Milky Way (Falchi et al., 2016).
- Bogong moths: migrate to the Australian Alps, navigate using the stars and Earth's magnetic
  field (Dreyer et al.).
- Sydney Opera House: Utzon won in 1957, spherical solution about 1961, over a million tiles,
  Utzon left in 1966, opened 1973; the orange-peel story framed as popular legend with no proof.
- Platypus is "native only to" eastern Australia (an introduced population lives on Kangaroo Island).
