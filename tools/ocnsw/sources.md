# NSW Opportunity Class Placement Test (OC, Year 4): sources

All questions, stories, poems and articles are original and written for Year 4 readers (age 9 to 10).
Nothing is copied or adapted from NSW past papers, the department's practice tests or commercial prep
material. Australian spelling, metric units, AUD.

## Official (checked 2026-10-30)
- NSW Department of Education, "Get ready for the Opportunity Class Placement Test":
  https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/students/oc-and-shs/going-to-an-opportunity-class/get-ready-for-the-opportunity-class-placement-test
  - Computer-based, multiple choice, three tests one after another at a test centre, each weighted 33.3%:
    Reading 14 questions (3 with multiple parts, 33 answers), 40 min;
    Mathematical Reasoning 35 questions, 40 min, five options; Thinking Skills 30 questions, 30 min, four options.
    No writing test.
  - No calculators or rulers; paper for working out.
  - Reading texts: fiction and non-fiction, including poetry, magazine articles, reports and extracts
    from novels or non-fiction books.
  - Thinking Skills: critical thinking and problem solving, no prior knowledge needed.
- NSW Department of Education, "Going to an opportunity class":
  https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/students/oc-and-shs/going-to-an-opportunity-class
  - OCs are for gifted and high potential students in Years 5 and 6 in public primary schools (Aurora
    College runs online OCs for rural and remote students); parents apply at the end of Year 3 or start of
    Year 4; test in mid-May; results in September; test results decide placement.

## Secondary (format detail only, checked 2026-10-30)
- Web search summaries of the department's OC test information PDF (schoolsequella.det.nsw.edu.au, the
  PDF itself would not open): a gap-match task on one factual text with six sentences removed (seven
  sentences offered, one unused), other multi-part items on several short extracts. One prep guide
  describes 8 questions on four short extracts. The exact split of the 33 reading answers is not
  published; ours is a modelling choice (below).

## Modelling decisions
- Reading 33 items per test, one per answer: story extract 5, poem 3, article 3 (the 11 single
  questions), then three multi-part sets: sentences removed 6 (one item per gap, five options from the
  pool of seven), four extracts A to D 8 (locked "Extract A..D" options) and two texts compared 8 (locked
  Text A only / Text B only / Both texts / Neither text). Comprehension singles have four options.
- Maths 35 per test, five options: number and patterns 18, measurement and space 11, data and chance 6
  (our split; the department publishes none). Year 4 (Stage 2) content with some Stage 3 stretch, as the
  OC test is designed for high-potential students. Two numeric-entry items are drill-only; the real test is
  multiple choice, so numeric items stay out of the tests.
- Thinking Skills 30 per test: critical thinking 15, problem solving 15 (our split), four options,
  text only (the real test also has spatial and pattern figures; not modelled).
- Scores are percent correct; the overall is the plain average of the three section percents (equal
  weighting, like the real one-third each). No pass mark exists.

## Facts in texts, checked against general reference knowledge (not re-verified online)
- Lyrebird: superb lyrebird in forests of south-eastern Australia, mimics other birds and some man-made
  sounds (camera shutters, car alarms widely reported), males sing and display on mounds in winter
  breeding season, named for the lyre-like tail, on the Australian 10c coin.
- Honey: nectar carried in the honey stomach, passed to house bees, fanned to evaporate water, cells capped
  with wax; one worker makes about 1/12 teaspoon in her life (commonly cited figure); bees have five eyes.
- Wombats: marsupials, backward-opening pouch, burrows, mainly nocturnal/crepuscular grazers, joey;
  common wombat range south-eastern Australia including Tasmania (hairy-nosed wombats live elsewhere, hence
  "mainly").
- Echidna: monotreme (with the platypus and other echidna species), found across Australia, toothless,
  long sticky tongue for ants and termites, digs down or curls up when threatened, egg in a pouch hatches
  after about 10 days, baby called a puggle, on the 5c coin.
- Seeds: seed coat, imbibition, temperature for germination, root first then shoot; coco de mer seeds can
  weigh well over a bowling ball (around 7 kg).
- Beach safety: swim between the red and yellow flags; in a rip, stay calm, float and raise an arm (Surf
  Life Saving Australia advice). Shell Beach, Western Australia, is made of tiny cockle shells.
- Bike helmets: required by law in most of Australia (exemptions exist, e.g. some NT paths), so the text says
  "most of Australia".
