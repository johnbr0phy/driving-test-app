## Licensing authority, outline and format

US state driver licensing agencies administer the commercial driver license school bus endorsement. FMCSA regulates the baseline knowledge and skills they test. The federal outline requires passenger endorsement qualification, S-specific knowledge, and a skills test in a school bus of the relevant vehicle group. The written practice bank focuses on the school bus knowledge component. [49 CFR 383.123(a)(1)-(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-383/subpart-G/section-383.123) and [FMCSA, endorsements and restrictions](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/commercial-drivers-license/drivers)

The federal minimum passing score is 80% for each knowledge test. The regulations describe FMCSA-approved state testing methods and prescribed numbers of items in each knowledge area. They refer to a standardized system with three possible responses per test item. The cited public sections do not provide the S endorsement's numeric item count or domain quotas. Tigertest's four practice forms each offer 50 original four-option items, with 40 correct corresponding to 80%. Their lengths and allocations are editorial. [49 CFR 383.135(a)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-383/subpart-H/section-383.135) and [49 CFR 383.133(b)(1)-(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-383/subpart-H/section-383.133)

Appendix D sets the entry-level school bus training curriculum. Theory units D1.1 through D1.11 and behind-the-wheel units D2.1 through D2.6 help identify operating subjects. The present materials do not provide or certify that training. FMCSA identifies first-time S endorsement holders among the groups subject to its entry-level training rules. The eCFR displayed Title 49 current through 2026-11-25 when checked. [Appendix D to 49 CFR Part 380](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-380/appendix-Appendix%20D%20to%20Part%20380) and [FMCSA, Entry-Level Driver Training](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/commercial-drivers-license/entry-level-driver-training-eldt)

## Scope limits

The two state manuals illustrate why distances require a source check. At section 10.2.1, California describes 200 feet or approximately five to ten seconds for amber warning lights, subject to state law. New York specifies at least 300 feet. Section 10.1.1 also gives different danger-zone dimensions. California's pupil escort rule in section 10.2 is jurisdiction-specific. The national practice bank omits uniform claims about these distances and escort obligations. It also leaves divided-road passing rules, strobe activation thresholds, district discipline policy, local emergency contacts and driver certification paperwork to the responsible jurisdiction. [California, sections 10.1, 10.2 and 10.7](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/california-commercial-driver-handbook-pdf/) and [New York, sections 10.1, 10.2 and 10.7](https://dmv.ny.gov/brochure/cdl10sec10.pdf)

The downstream railroad storage allowance is identified as a handbook guideline. It is distinct from the approach stopping distance. NHTSA confirms that the bus must have space beyond the farthest rail before proceeding. [NHTSA, Railroad Crossings, Storage Area](https://www.nhtsa.gov/planning-safer-school-bus-stops-and-routes/planning-safety)

The forms assess knowledge. Behind-the-wheel skills are examined separately. State booking details, test lengths and local operating policies vary.
